This post is about the screen: feature films, television series and documentaries in which hacking is the subject or the engine of the plot, from the summer of 1983 to November 2026. Two companion posts cover the rest of the shelf: games, Capture the Flag (CTF) competitions and cyber ranges in **[Hackers at play](/blog/hackers-at-play-games-ctfs-and-cyber-ranges)**, and the books, including several the films below were adapted from, in **[Hackers on the page](/blog/hackers-on-the-page-books-from-cuckoos-egg-to-now)**. The history the films draw on is told in the Learn article **[Hacking in the 20th century](/learn/hacking-in-the-20th-century)**, and where a work retells a real case, the case has its own Learn article, linked in place.

Two rules apply throughout. Plot descriptions are short and spoiler-light, and they say so where an ending matters. And every judgement about realism or influence below is somebody's, named and linked: the makers, the people depicted, practitioners, critics, courts and governments. The post itself keeps no score of taste.

## How the realism score works

Each fiction work gets up to four checks, and a check counts only when a source in the list at the end establishes it:

| Check | What it means | What counts as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tools (T) | Real software, commands or protocols identifiable on screen | The tool's maker (for example nmap.org's page on films), the production, or the press |
| Adviser (A) | A technical adviser named in the credits, by the makers or by the press | A credit list, an interview with the makers or the adviser |
| Method (M) | The central intrusion uses a technique that exists in the real world, at a pace someone with knowledge calls plausible | A source on the film plus a source on the technique outside it |
| Record (R) | Only for works drawn from a real case: the case is followed, and the makers or the people involved have said what was changed | Interviews, statements, letters from the people depicted |

The score is the number of checks met over the number that apply: three for an invented story, four for a real case. A question mark means that no source read settles the point, and it counts as not met, so a low score can mean "not documented" as much as "shown to be false"; the reasons column says which. Documentaries are not scored, because they are records rather than depictions: for them the tables list who made them, what case they cover and what is documented about their reception. A work with no source on any of the four checks is marked "not scored".

## The film that changed a directive: WarGames (1983)

**What it is.** *[WarGames](/glossary/wargames)* was directed by John Badham from a screenplay by Lawrence Lasker and Walter F. Parkes, produced by United Artists and distributed by MGM/UA, the Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer and United Artists company; it opened in United States cinemas on 3 June 1983 after a screening at the Cannes Film Festival in May, and was nominated for three Academy Awards, for sound, cinematography and screenplay ([American Film Institute Catalog, WarGames](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67139)). In Brazil it is *Jogos de Guerra* ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/wargames/umc.cmc.4n8grrnb4vq7tgygwcd1cxzcq)). In the American Film Institute (AFI) synopsis, a Seattle teenager changes his school grades over the telephone with a password he saw written on a slip of paper in a desk, while at the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) a computer called the War Operation Plan Response, or WOPR, is put in charge of nuclear war planning ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67139)); the boy then reaches that computer thinking it is a game ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)). The ending matters, and it is not described here.

**Who advised it.** Lasker has said that he met the RAND Corporation computer scientist Willis Ware in RAND's library, that Ware became an adviser, and that Ware told him NORAD's claim of isolation was wrong because "A lot of these guys will leave an open door so that they can work from home over the weekend" ([MEL Magazine, oral history, 17 March 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)). In the same oral history, David Scott Lewis, a member of the computer club at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), says the hero was modelled in many ways on him ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)); when DEF CON screened the film for its 25th anniversary in 2008, the organizers quoted Lewis saying that the library scene in which the hero hunts for a backdoor password "is clearly a reference to many of my antics" ([DEF CON Forums, 26 June 2008](https://forum.defcon.org/node/10547)). The AFI credits list Duncan Wilmore as technical adviser and Steve Grumette as computer video consultant ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67139)). Lasker adds that what made the plot "click" was learning that computers were connected over telephone lines ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)).

**What it gets wrong, by its own record.** The AFI's note records that unnamed Air Force officers, quoted in the press on the day of release, called many facts about the military chain of command inaccurate: the film merges the Strategic Air Command and the Navy into NORAD's warning system and gives NORAD powers it does not have ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67139)).

**What it changed.** On Saturday 4 June 1983 at Camp David, the White House's daily diary records: "The President and the First Lady watched the movie "War Games."" ([Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation, Daily Diary of President Ronald Reagan, 4 June 1983](https://www.reaganfoundation.org/cms/assets/1716570167-presidents-schedule-06-04-1983.pdf)). The following Wednesday, 8 June, Reagan met the bipartisan leadership of both houses of Congress, by his own diary ([Reagan Foundation, White House Diaries, 8 June 1983](https://www.reaganfoundation.org/ronald-reagan/white-house-diaries/diary-entry-06081983/)). The journalist Fred Kaplan's account of that meeting, excerpted in an oral history, is that Reagan summarized the film's plot and asked the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, General John Vessey, whether it could happen, and that the answer, drafted by the Pentagon's Donald Latham, came back as "Yes, the problem is much worse than you think" ([MEL Magazine, excerpting Kaplan's Dark Territory, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity); [Medill News Service, 7 March 2016](https://medillonthehill.medill.northwestern.edu/2016/03/new-book-aims-to-tell-secret-history-of-cyberwar)). In the oral history's telling, drawn from Kaplan's book, the question set off "a string of interagency memos, working groups, studies and meetings" that ended in National Security Decision Directive 145 (NSDD-145) ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)).

The directive itself, signed on 17 September 1984, says that telecommunications and automated information processing systems "are highly susceptible to interception, unauthorized electronic access, and related forms of technical exploitation", names the Secretary of Defense as executive agent, and designates the Director of the National Security Agency (NSA) as the National Manager for the security of those systems ([Reagan Library, NSDD-145, 17 September 1984](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/archives/reference/scanned-nsdds/nsdd145.pdf); [Federation of American Scientists, NSDD-145 transcription](https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/nsdd145.htm)). The directive does not mention the film; the link between the two rests on Kaplan's reporting.

The law came by a separate road. The Congressional Research Service records that congressional inquiry into computer crime began "no later than 1976", with hearings in successive Congresses, and that the original federal computer crime section, 18 U.S.C. 1030, passed as part of the Comprehensive Crime Control Act of 1984 and was rewritten in 1986 (Public Law 99-474) into the statute now cited as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) ([Congressional Research Service, Charles Doyle, Report 97-1025, updated 28 June 2005](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20050628_97-1025_a930ee431f0b4982f520249f48e0b72004b98232.html)). The story of the same summer, the 414s, the hearings and the 1986 law, is told in the Learn article **[The 414s and the summer of 1983](/learn/the-414s-and-the-summer-of-1983)**.

How much the film moved policy is disputed, and the people who were there disagree. Kevin Bankston, a lawyer specializing in technology privacy, said in 2020 that clips of the film were screened in committee hearings and that "no single text, movie or anything has had a more direct impact on cybersecurity policy in the U.S. than WarGames" ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)). Mark Weinberg, then assistant White House press secretary, who watched the film with the Reagans, said in the same piece that "Ronald Reagan saw movies essentially to be entertained, not to have his thinking changed or to have a life-altering experience" and that his views on nuclear weapons predated the film ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)). Kevin Poulsen, a former hacker turned journalist, wrote in 2015 that Congress passed the original CFAA "in direct response" to the film ([Wired, Kevin Poulsen, 16 January 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/01/why-i-hope-congress-never-watches-blackhat/)). The social scientist David Kirby's name for the pattern, a film made with scientists' help that frightens the public into supporting a cause, is "The WarGames Effect" ([MEL Magazine, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/wargames-ronald-reagan-cybersecurity)).

**Careers.** Kor Adana, later a writer and producer on *Mr. Robot*, says he was "inspired by films like "WarGames" and "Hackers."" ([The Hill, 25 October 2017](https://digital-release.thehill.com/policy/cybersecurity/356991-qa-with-mr-robots-kor-adana-and-ryan-kazanciyan)). The film is not on the Library of Congress's National Film Registry, which held 900 films after the 2024 selection and added 25 more announced in January 2026, none of them a hacking film ([Library of Congress, complete registry listing](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-film-preservation-board/film-registry/complete-national-film-registry-listing/); [Library of Congress, 17 December 2024](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2024/12/announcing-the-2024-national-film-registry/); [infoDOCKET, 29 January 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/01/29/library-of-congress-names-25-films-to-the-national-film-registry-selections-include-clueless-the-karate-kid-philadelphia-and-brooklyn-bridge/)).

**Score: 2 of 3.** T ?: no source read names software or commands on screen; Lasker's account confirms only that the hero connects over telephone lines. A yes: Ware, Lewis and the credited technical adviser. M yes: a password copied from a slip of paper, Ware's open door and Lewis's research for passwords are techniques of the period; the military side, by the Air Force officers' account, is wrong. (The phone-system side of the period is in **[Phreaking: the network that whistled its own passwords](/learn/phone-phreaking-and-the-blue-box)**.)

## The cryptographer's cameo: Sneakers (1992)

*[Sneakers](/glossary/sneakers-film)*, directed by Phil Alden Robinson for Universal Pictures, is *Quebra de Sigilo* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/sneakers/umc.cmc.2ymtppe6e6ks0kki70y5o2es8)). Lawrence Lasker, by then producing his own films, was behind it, and the cryptographer [Leonard Adleman](/glossary/leonard-adleman) has told how it came about: Lasker first called him about a film on cryptography, went away to make *Awakenings* instead, and came back asking Adleman to prepare the slides and words for a lecture scene about a breakthrough in factoring ([Leonard Adleman, The story of Sneakers, University of Southern California, Laboratory for Molecular Science](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/?p=2041)). Adleman writes that the scene mentions the number field sieve, "the fastest factoring algorithm currently known", alongside "a fantasy about towers of number fields and Artin maps", that when Lasker offered money he asked instead that his wife could meet Robert Redford, that his phrase "a breakthrough of Gaussian proportions" made it into the film, and that he was credited, in his recollection, as "mathematical consultant" ([Adleman, The story of Sneakers](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/?p=2041)). The crew redrew his computer-made slides by hand because, Lasker told him, hand-drawn slides looked more realistic ([Adleman](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/?p=2041)). The social side of the trade the film shows is the subject of **[Social engineering: pretexting, vishing and baiting](/learn/social-engineering-pretexting-vishing-and-baiting)**.

**Score: 1 of 3.** T ?: no source read on software shown. A yes: Adleman. M no for the central device: the code-breaking breakthrough the plot turns on is, in its own consultant's words, a fantasy dressed in real mathematics.

## Rollerblades and a real defacement: Hackers (1995)

*[Hackers](/glossary/hackers-film)*, directed by Iain Softley and released by United Artists (MGM), is *Hackers - Piratas de Computador* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/hackers/umc.cmc.4ug4r0vwr7og0d35al431d6zc)). Softley said in 2020 that the screenwriter Rafael Moreu had researched with people in the New York hacking community, including the circle of *2600* magazine and its editor Emmanuel Goldstein, some of whom "became advisors on the film", and that the cast spent about three weeks in a "hackers' boot camp" before shooting ([Collider, 30 September 2020](https://collider.com/hackers-director-interview-iain-softley-25th-anniversary/)); an oral history lists Omar Wasow as hacking consultant ([/Film, 30 November 2015](https://www.slashfilm.com/541326/hackers-oral-history)). Chris Wysopal, of the Boston group the L0pht, recalled hearing that "some guy making a movie about hackers came to a 2600 meeting", and judged that the film got the hacker mentality right but not the clothes: "No one ever saw a hacker on rollerblades" ([Christian Science Monitor, Passcode, 16 September 2015](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/2015/0916/Hackers-at-20)). Softley's own summary, in the same piece: "I wasn't trying to make a technically accurate movie. I was trying to make an urban fairy tale." ([Christian Science Monitor, 2015](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/2015/0916/Hackers-at-20)). The plot's investigation uses phone taps and dumpster diving ([Christian Science Monitor, 2015](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/2015/0916/Hackers-at-20)); the Nmap project's page on films calls its cyberspace scenes "ridiculous 3D animated eye-candy" ([Nmap, Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/)).

**What happened in the real world.** Weeks before release, the film's promotional website was altered by intruders who signed "Hacked by ILF" and added "Go see 'The Net' instead of this dog"; MGM decided to keep the altered page online and said it was "thoroughly impressed by their creativity and ingenuity" ([The Spokesman-Review, 22 August 1995](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1995/aug/22/real-hackers-computer-savvy-intruders-give-united)). Rob Vincent, of the hacker radio show *Off The Hook*, remembers the scene's first fear, that the film would bring in "posers", and adds that it also brought in people who went on to contribute ([Christian Science Monitor, 2015](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/2015/0916/Hackers-at-20)). Dave Kennedy, founder of TrustedSec and developer of the Social-Engineer Toolkit (SET), put it more personally: "“Hackers” turned me into who I am today." ([Christian Science Monitor, 2015](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/2015/0916/Hackers-at-20)). Softley says that Jake Davis, whom he describes as one of the most prominent members of [Anonymous](/glossary/anonymous), told him at a festival that the film had inspired him ([Collider, 2020](https://collider.com/hackers-director-interview-iain-softley-25th-anniversary/)); the movement's story is in **[Anonymous, LulzSec and the hacktivist decade](/learn/anonymous-lulzsec-and-the-hacktivist-decade)**.

**Score: 2 of 3.** T no, by the Nmap project's account. A yes: Moreu's research and the named consultant. M yes: phone taps and dumpster diving are real techniques, and the opening of the plot rests on them.

The same year produced *The Net*, directed by Irwin Winkler for Columbia Pictures, *A Rede* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/the-net/umc.cmc.4065u9algwu4wns55pepqgfne)); no source read documents its technical side, so it is not scored.

## A Hanover hacker and a hunted one: 23 (1998) and the Mitnick films (1998 to 2002)

**23.** *23: Nichts ist so wie es scheint*, directed by Hans-Christian Schmid, retells "auf fiktive Weise" (in a fictional way) the life of the Hanover hacker Karl Koch, who spied for a secret service, by the account of the city of Hanover's own portal ([Hannover.de, updated 2 May 2018](https://www.hannover.de/Kultur-Freizeit/Geschichte-Geschichten/Echt-hann%C3%B6versch/Zehn-Dinge/Zehn-Filme,-in-denen-Hannover-die-Hauptrolle-spielt/23-%E2%80%93-Nichts-ist-so-wie-es-scheint-1998)). A review describes Koch and a friend getting into the networks with a Commodore computer and an acoustic coupler ([Filmstarts, 25 February 2010](https://www.filmstarts.de/kritiken/18992.html)), and the Austrian daily *Der Standard* gave its budget as 6 million marks, produced by Claussen und Wöbke of Munich ([Der Standard](https://www.derstandard.at/story/16188/nichts-ist-wie-es-scheint)). The real case, the Chaos Computer Club's world and the sale of data to the KGB, is in **[The Chaos Computer Club, the Btx heist, and the hack that ended in a forest](/learn/chaos-computer-club-btx-and-the-kgb)**. **Score: 1 of 4.** T yes (the period's hardware, per the review); A ?; M ?; R ?: the city's portal calls the reconstruction fictional, and no source read lists the changes.

**Takedown and the protest it drew.** In July 1998 a small group led by Emmanuel Goldstein protested at Miramax's offices in New York against *Takedown*, the studio's planned film about [Kevin Mitnick](/glossary/kevin-mitnick) based on the 1996 book by Tsutomu Shimomura and the journalist John Markoff ([Wired, 16 July 1998](https://www.wired.com/1998/07/a-low-key-mitnick-protest/)). Goldstein, who had read a draft, said that it depicted Mitnick as violent and as altering medical records, and that "Miramax is going to get major things wrong, when the facts of this case are far more interesting than the fiction" ([Wired, 1998](https://www.wired.com/1998/07/a-low-key-mitnick-protest/)). The magazine's answer was its own documentary, *Freedom Downtime*, on the "Free Kevin" movement, presented at the H2K2 hacker conference on 13 July 2002 ([InfoconDB, H2K2: Freedom Downtime](https://infocondb.org/con/hope/h2k2/freedom-downtime)). The case itself, with what the courts actually decided, is in **[Kevin Mitnick: the myth, the man, and what the case actually decided](/learn/kevin-mitnick-the-myth-and-the-man)**. *Takedown* is not scored: no source read describes the finished film's hacking or credits, and the 1998 criticism concerns a draft. The book it was based on is covered in **[Hackers on the page](/blog/hackers-on-the-page-books-from-cuckoos-egg-to-now)**.

## Nmap becomes a film star: The Matrix Reloaded (2003) and its successors

The Nmap project keeps a page of films that show its network scanner, and it opens: "For reasons unknown, Hollywood has decided that Nmap is the tool to show whenever hacking scenes are needed" ([Nmap, Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/)). The first famous case was *The Matrix Reloaded*, by Lana and Lilly Wachowski for Warner Bros. (*Matrix Reloaded* in Brazil), in which, by the project's account, Trinity runs Nmap version 2.54BETA25 to find a vulnerable Secure Shell (SSH) server and breaks in with the SSH1 CRC32 exploit from 2001 ([Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/); [Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/the-matrix-reloaded/umc.cmc.5b3qv27alugfj95rrag186slh)). The flaw is real: the CERT Coordination Center (CERT/CC) describes a remote integer overflow in the code that several SSH1 implementations used to detect CRC32 attacks, allowing an attacker to run code "with the privileges of the SSH daemon, typically root", and the United States National Vulnerability Database lists it as CVE-2001-0144 ([CERT/CC, VU#945216, 2001, revised 20 May 2003](https://www.kb.cert.org/vuls/id/945216); [NIST NVD, CVE-2001-0144](https://services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/json/cves/2.0?cveId=CVE-2001-0144)). Nmap's page records that Scotland Yard's Computer Crime Unit and the British Computer Society issued a joint warning that viewers "should not be tempted to emulate the realistic depiction of computer hacking" ([Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/)). The first film, *[The Matrix](/glossary/the-matrix)* (1999), was added to the National Film Registry in 2012 ([Library of Congress, complete registry listing](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-film-preservation-board/film-registry/complete-national-film-registry-listing/)). **Score for Reloaded: 2 of 3.** T yes; A ?; M yes (a real exploit of the period against a real service).

Nmap's author, [Fyodor](/glossary/fyodor), adds on the same page that he has been "pleased to help out" to make some films "slightly more realistic" and invites scriptwriters to ask ([Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/)). The project's list, as read on 16 November 2026 (its last stated update is of 21 April 2020), includes:

## The adviser who warned Congress: Blackhat (2015)

*Blackhat*, directed by Michael Mann for Universal Pictures, is called simply *Hacker* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/blackhat/umc.cmc.3b81dxvan920ao8s9k8uyq2er)). Its "hacker adviser", in his own words his "actual screen credit", was Kevin Poulsen, who went over the screenplay line by line with Mann; Mann later brought in a second consultant, Chris McKinley, "to write code for the movie" and to train the lead actor in Linux basics ([Wired, Kevin Poulsen, 16 January 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/01/why-i-hope-congress-never-watches-blackhat/)). Poulsen's own story is in **[Kevin Poulsen: the man who won a Porsche by owning the phone company](/learn/kevin-poulsen-dark-dante)**.

The same article is a documented effect in its own right. Writing in the week the White House proposed to broaden the CFAA and raise its penalties after the Sony breach, Poulsen asked lawmakers not to take the film's opening, in which malware makes a Chinese nuclear plant explode, as a description of the world: "nuclear plants are not exploding" ([Wired, 16 January 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/01/why-i-hope-congress-never-watches-blackhat/)). The British Film Institute (BFI) placed the film in its 2024 list of ten key technothrillers ([BFI, 10 great technothrillers, 5 September 2024](https://www.bfi.org.uk/lists/10-great-technothrillers)).

**Score: 2 of 3.** T yes: code written for the screen by a working programmer, by the adviser's account. A yes: Poulsen and McKinley. M no for the opening: the adviser himself says the event does not happen.

## The most-praised: Mr. Robot (2015 to 2019)

*[Mr. Robot](/glossary/mr-robot)*, created by Sam Esmail for USA Network with Universal Cable Productions as the studio, follows Elliot Alderson, a cyber-security engineer drawn into the hacker group fsociety ([Comcast, 22 April 2016](https://corporate.comcast.com/news-information/news-feed/usa-networks-universal-cable-productions-mr-robot-wins-prestigious-peabody-award)); in Brazil it is *Sociedade Hacker* ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/show/mr-robot/umc.cmc.306sepo05euzg2r68zzsa02up)). It won a George Foster Peabody Award in the 75th competition, in April 2016 ([Comcast, 22 April 2016](https://corporate.comcast.com/news-information/news-feed/usa-networks-universal-cable-productions-mr-robot-wins-prestigious-peabody-award)).

**The people.** Kor Adana, a former penetration tester and forensic investigator in the automotive industry, was a writer and producer on the show; Ryan Kazanciyan, an incident responder, was a technical consultant ([The Hill, 25 October 2017](https://digital-release.thehill.com/policy/cybersecurity/356991-qa-with-mr-robots-kor-adana-and-ryan-kazanciyan)); Michael Bazzell, for 18 years a government computer crime investigator, mostly with the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Cyber Crimes Task Force, was its technical adviser ([Syracuse University, 12 July 2016](https://onlinegrad.syracuse.edu/?p=5302)).

**The tools.** At the end of the first season *Wired* listed the real tools the show had used: DeepSound to hide files inside audio, ProtonMail, a Raspberry Pi planted to reach a heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) system, Bishop Fox's Tastic RFID Thief to copy radio-frequency identification (RFID) badges, RSA SecurID tokens, Rapid7's Metasploit Framework with the Meterpreter payload, Short Message Service (SMS) spoofing in the Social-Engineer Toolkit, and the FlexiSPY monitoring app ([Wired, 26 August 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/08/peek-inside-mr-robots-toolbox/)). Adana said in 2016 that "every single attack we showcase is based on a real attack", and that the show varies its attacks more than real attackers do, who reuse what works ([Vice, 20 June 2016](https://www.vice.com/en/article/where-mr-robot-gets-inspiration-for-its-hacks)).

**What it gets wrong, by its own team.** Bazzell named the compromise: "While we are accurate about the details of the hack, we must fudge the time element." ([Syracuse University, 2016](https://onlinegrad.syracuse.edu/?p=5302)). The Brazilian blog of the security vendor ESET makes the same point in Portuguese, that the show compresses timelines, and calls these "concessões dramáticas, não erros técnicos" (dramatic concessions, not technical errors) ([ESET Brasil, Filmes sobre hackers: o que é real e o que é exagero?](https://www.eset.com/br/blog/cultura/filmes-sobre-hackers-o-que-e-real/)).

**Score: 3 of 3.** T yes; A yes; M yes, with the time compression the advisers themselves acknowledge.

## Real leaks, real people: Citizenfour, The Fifth Estate and Snowden (2013 to 2016)

**Citizenfour (2014).** Laura Poitras's documentary, filmed while Glenn Greenwald first met [Edward Snowden](/glossary/edward-snowden) to decide how to publish the documents, won the Academy Award for documentary feature in February 2015, shared with the producers Mathilde Bonnefoy and Dirk Wilutzky; on stage Poitras said "Thank you to Edward Snowden for his courage, and for the many other whistleblowers" ([TheWrap, 23 February 2015](https://www.thewrap.com/citzenfour-director-thanks-journalists-for-exposing-truth-during-best-documentary-acceptance-speech/)). The same report noted that the United States Department of Justice had charged Snowden in 2013 under the Espionage Act and with theft of government property ([TheWrap, 2015](https://www.thewrap.com/citzenfour-director-thanks-journalists-for-exposing-truth-during-best-documentary-acceptance-speech/)). The site's entry is *[Citizenfour](/glossary/citizenfour)*, and the policy questions are in **[Surveillance capability and restraint](/learn/surveillance-capability-and-restraint)**.

**The Fifth Estate (2013).** DreamWorks' film about WikiLeaks, directed by Bill Condon and *O Quinto Poder* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/the-fifth-estate/umc.cmc.6zc4zztns8eno1ragemzhx2ll)), was opposed by its subject before it opened: [Julian Assange](/glossary/julian-assange) refused to meet Benedict Cumberbatch and wrote to him that the film was based on "the two most discredited books on the market" and that "I believe it will distort events and subtract from public understanding" ([The Register, 10 October 2013](https://www.theregister.com/2013/10/10/assange_refused_cumberbatch_meeting/)). Cumberbatch said the letter "winded me quite a bit" and that "there was a certain amount of naivety about what he thought a film's impact could be" ([Christian Science Monitor, 19 October 2013](https://www.csmonitor.com/The-Culture/Movies/2013/1019/Benedict-Cumberbatch-discusses-The-Fifth-Estate-and-Julian-Assange-s-reaction)). Not scored on tools or method; its record is disputed by the person it depicts.

**Snowden (2016).** Oliver Stone's film for Open Road Films is *Snowden: Herói ou Traidor* in Brazil ([Apple TV Brasil](https://tv.apple.com/br/movie/snowden/umc.cmc.3j73epo0sky4mob3jog0xxw5r)), and one early scene shows Nmap ([Nmap in the movies](https://nmap.org/movies/)). After its first public screening, at Comic-Con in July 2016, Snowden, speaking by video from Moscow, said there was not a lot of fictionalization in it; the Associated Press noted that the film takes liberties with facts not yet in the public record, including the moment the files leave the NSA, and Stone said the way that scene is dramatized was Snowden's idea ([Associated Press, on Shoot, 22 July 2016](https://www.shootonline.com/news/edward-snowden-gives-support-oliver-stone-film-after-comic-con-screening)). **Score: 2 of 4.** T yes; A ?; M ?; R yes, with the change named by the director.

## The law on trial: The Internet's Own Boy (2014)

Brian Knappenberger's documentary about [Aaron Swartz](/glossary/aaron-swartz), who died by suicide in January 2013 after he was indicted for computer fraud over the alleged download of nearly four million academic articles from JSTOR, premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2014 and was acquired for North America by Participant Media and FilmBuff ([The Hollywood Reporter, 27 February 2014](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/participant-filmbuff-buy-us-rights-670573)). Knappenberger said that from the start he wanted "to use the film to advance the principles he cared about" ([The Hollywood Reporter, 2014](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/participant-filmbuff-buy-us-rights-670573)), and it was released on 27 June 2014 on Vimeo On Demand and other services ([Daily Dot, 27 June 2014](https://dailydot.com/entertainment/aaron-swartz-internets-own-boy-vimeo-online/)). The Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) commissioned its own review of its role in the case, delivered to its president on 26 July 2013 ([MIT, Report to the President: MIT and the Prosecution of Aaron Swartz](https://swartz-report.mit.edu/)).

The film arrived a year after a bill named for Swartz: "Aaron's Law Act of 2013", introduced on 20 June 2013 by Representative Zoe Lofgren and four co-sponsors, would have redefined "access without authorization" in the CFAA as obtaining information by "knowingly circumventing one or more technological or physical measures" ([United States Government Publishing Office, H.R. 2454, 113th Congress](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-113hr2454ih/html/BILLS-113hr2454ih.htm)). By January 2015, Poulsen wrote, the proposal had "floated through the halls of Congress and out a window, never to be seen again" ([Wired, 16 January 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/01/why-i-hope-congress-never-watches-blackhat/)).

## Stuxnet on film: Zero Days (2016)

Alex Gibney's *Zero Days*, on the [Stuxnet](/glossary/stuxnet) malware, premiered in competition at the Berlin International Film Festival in February 2016; the festival lists David Sanger and Yossi Melman as both protagonists and consultants ([Berlinale 2016, Zero Days](https://berlinale.de/en/2016/programme/201608480.html)). At the press conference, Gibney said that "the trend and momentum towards greater and greater secrecy in the U.S. administration is appalling" and that "When the government keeps secrets, sometimes it's important for filmmakers to keep secrets, too"; Magnolia Pictures and Showtime had bought the United States rights ([The Hollywood Reporter, 17 February 2016](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/berlin-alex-gibney-angry-cyber-866603/)). The case is in **[Stuxnet: the first cyberweapon, and the day code broke a machine](/learn/stuxnet-and-the-first-cyberweapon)**, and the book that shaped later fiction about it, Kim Zetter's *[Countdown to Zero Day](/glossary/countdown-to-zero-day)*, belongs to the books post.

## Data as the target: The Great Hack (2019)

Karim Amer and Jehane Noujaim's documentary on [Cambridge Analytica](/glossary/cambridge-analytica) went out on Netflix on 24 July 2019; in Brazil it is *Privacidade Hackeada*, described as the story of how the firm "se tornou o símbolo do lado sombrio das redes sociais" (became the symbol of the dark side of social networks) after the 2016 United States election ([Netflix Brasil, Privacidade Hackeada](https://www.netflix.com/br/title/80117542)). ESET's Portuguese-language publication WeLiveSecurity singled out the testimony of the firm's former business development director, Brittany Kaiser ([WeLiveSecurity, 29 February 2024](https://www.welivesecurity.com/pt/seguranca-digital/documentarios-sobre-hacking-e-ciberseguranca-para-maratonar/)). The trade it describes is in **[Influence for hire](/learn/influence-for-hire-the-firms-that-sell-influence-operations)**.

## Cyberwar as drama in the 2020s

**The Undeclared War (2022).** Channel 4 and Peacock commissioned Peter Kosminsky's six-part drama in April 2021 from Playground and NBC International Studios; it follows analysts at Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) in a post-pandemic 2024 working against an attack on the British electoral system, and Channel 4 said that during a three-year research process Kosminsky's team "gained access to the cyber security industry on both sides of the Atlantic" ([Channel 4, 27 April 2021](https://www.channel4.com/press/news/channel-4-and-peacock-commission-seven-time-bafta-winner-peter-kosminskys-undeclared-war)). Kosminsky: "The Undeclared War is fiction but based very heavily on research carried out over a number of years"; he would not be specific about the research, and said the code is shown as "Codeworld", a world of everyday actions standing for operations inside the computer, so as not to show "endless scenes of people sitting at keyboards" ([Channel 4, interview, 8 July 2022](https://www.channel4.com/press/news/interview-peter-kosminsky-creator-writer-director-executive-producer)). **Score: 1 of 3.** T no, by the maker's own design; A yes (the research access Channel 4 describes); M ?: the realism of the central threat is the maker's claim, not checked in a source read.

**Leave the World Behind (2023).** Sam Esmail, the creator of *Mr. Robot*, directed this Netflix film about a family on holiday when a cyberattack seems to take the country down; in Brazil it is *O Mundo Depois de Nós*, and the newspaper *O Povo* reported that it was Netflix's most-watched film of the second half of 2023 ([O Povo, 19 July 2024](https://www.opovo.com.br/vidaearte/2024/07/19/ataques-e-apagoes-ciberneticos-confira-4-producoes-para-assistir.html)). Not scored.

**Zero Day (2025).** Netflix's limited series, *Dia Zero* in Brazil, was created by Eric Newman, Noah Oppenheim and Michael S. Schmidt and stars Robert De Niro as a former president investigating a lethal cyberattack ([Netflix Brasil, Dia Zero](https://www.netflix.com/br/title/81598435)). Netflix's own magazine says the producers consulted Clint Watts, a former FBI special agent, and that Glatter, who directed all six episodes, read Kim Zetter's book on Stuxnet; Schmidt said the aim was "to show what a catastrophic cyberattack could look like" ([Netflix Tudum, 20 February 2025](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/zero-day-true-story-facts)). Practitioners asked by the press were divided: Martin Jartelius of Outpost24 called the simultaneous disruption of several industries highly improbable, and Roger Grimes of KnowBe4 called its scale questionable while praising the terminology ([SecurityBrief, 4 March 2025](https://securitybrief.co.nz/story/experts-discuss-netflix-miniseries-zero-day-cyber-themes)). **Score: 1 of 3.** T ?; A yes; M no, by the practitioners quoted.

**Jamtara (2020).** Netflix's Indian series about young people running a lucrative scam in a small town is *Jamtara - Você é o próximo* in Brazil ([Netflix Brasil, Jamtara](https://www.netflix.com/br/title/81183491)). Not scored.

**Midnight in the War Room (2026).** A documentary produced by Semperis Studios, of the cyber resilience company Semperis, and featuring Chris Inglis, Jen Easterly, Marcus Hutchins and Mary Aiken among more than 50 contributors, was announced for a world premiere at Black Hat USA in Las Vegas on 5 August 2026 ([Cybersecurity Ventures, 18 May 2026](https://cybersecurityventures.com/world-premiere-of-midnight-in-the-war-room-documentary-at-black-hat-vegas/)). Its executive producer is Semperis's chief marketing officer ([Cybersecurity Ventures, 2026](https://cybersecurityventures.com/world-premiere-of-midnight-in-the-war-room-documentary-at-black-hat-vegas/)), a fact a viewer may want to weigh.

## Brazil on screen

### O Rei da Internet (2026)

*[O Rei da Internet](/glossary/o-rei-da-internet)*, directed by Fabrício Bittar from a script he wrote with Vinícius Perez, stars João Guilherme as Daniel Nascimento and Marcelo Serrado as the mentor who leads the gang; it opened in Brazilian cinemas on 14 May 2026, distributed by Manequim Filmes, a label of Vitrine Filmes, as a coproduction with Telecine and Clube Filmes ([Jovem Pan, 12 March 2026](https://jovempan.com.br/?p=3443); [Ingresso.com, 14 May 2026](https://www.ingresso.com/noticias/conheca-daniel-nascimento-hacker-inspirou-filme-o-rei-da-internet)). The distributor's synopsis describes a teenager who breaks into national and international servers, joins a criminal organization moving millions of reais, and becomes the target of the "primeira grande operação da Polícia Federal contra crimes virtuais" (the Federal Police's first major operation against online crime), all before turning 17 ([Jovem Pan, 2026](https://jovempan.com.br/?p=3443); [Omelete, 11 March 2026](https://www.omelete.com.br/filmes/o-rei-da-internet-joao-guilherme-filme-poster-data)). The film is rated for over-18s ([Diário do Nordeste, 19 May 2026](https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/entretenimento/zoeira/nos-cinemas-com-o-rei-da-internet-joao-guilherme-comenta-estilo-do-filme-tudo-parece-surreal-1.3765042)).

**How it was made.** Bittar told *Omelete* that besides reading "o livro" (the book) he interviewed Nascimento at length, that Nascimento answered technical questions with long audio messages, and that "esse filme também é um pouco documental" (this film is also somewhat documentary), with archive footage mixed in ([Omelete, interview, 14 May 2026](https://www.omelete.com.br/filmes/o-rei-da-internet-entrevista-influencias/)). João Guilherme said he met Nascimento during the process ([Diário do Nordeste, 2026](https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/entretenimento/zoeira/nos-cinemas-com-o-rei-da-internet-joao-guilherme-comenta-estilo-do-filme-tudo-parece-surreal-1.3765042)).

**What critics said about its hacking.** *Omelete*'s critic placed the story in 2004 and 2005 and wrote that it retells the real story "com bastante liberdade criativa e um narrador bem pouco confiável" (with a great deal of creative freedom and a very unreliable narrator) ([Omelete, review, 20 May 2026](https://www.omelete.com.br/filmes/criticas/o-rei-da-internet-joao-guilherme-critica)). Itatiaia's critic wrote that the film bets less on technical explanations of hacking than on the experience of a boy consumed by power, recreating Windows XP, the UOL chat rooms and Counter-Strike, and that some moments seem "absurdos demais para serem reais" (too absurd to be real), such as exploits found by a young Russian inside Counter-Strike ([Itatiaia, 14 May 2026](https://www.itatiaia.com.br/cultura-e-lazer/critica-o-rei-da-internet-transforma-caos-digital-dos-anos-2000-em-cinema-pop/)).

**Where it went next.** It opened in Portugal on 6 July 2026 with NOS Lusomundo Audiovisuais ([DN Brasil, Diário de Notícias, 12 June 2026](https://dnbrasil.dn.pt/entretenimento-dn-brasil/filme-brasileiro-sobre-o-maior-hacker-do-pas-estreia-em-portugal-em-julho)), and in August 2026 Magenta Light Studios acquired it for the United States and Canada ([Omelete, citing Variety, 22 August 2026](https://www.omelete.com.br/filmes/o-rei-da-internet-distribuicao-estados-unidos-canada)).

The real fraud scene of the period, the Brazilian banking trojans that later went global, is in **[Brazilian cybercrime: from the boleto to the Pix](/learn/brazilian-cybercrime-from-boleto-to-pix)**. The case behind the film is a matter for police and court records that were not found for this post; the facts above are the film's and its makers' account.

**Score: 2 of 4.** T ?: critics say it avoids technical explanation; A yes: the subject himself, interviewed at length; M ?: one critic finds a key episode implausible; R yes: a real case, with the creative liberty and the narrator's unreliability named by the critic and the documentary intent named by the director.

## Where to start

The list below sorts by what each work offers on the record above, not by taste.

- **For the policy story:** *WarGames*, then the directive it is linked to, then the Learn article on the 414s; *The Internet's Own Boy* for the same law thirty years later.
- **For the highest realism score on the rubric:** *Mr. Robot*, season one, with *Wired*'s list of its tools beside it.
- **For real exploits on screen:** *The Matrix Reloaded*'s Nmap scene, then the CERT/CC note on the flaw it used.
- **For real cases told by the people in them:** *Citizenfour*; *Zero Days* for Stuxnet; *The KGB, the Computer and Me* for incident response as it looked in 1990.
- **For the Brazilian angle:** *O Rei da Internet*, read with its critics, then *Onisciente* for surveillance as Brazilian science fiction.
- **For the argument about realism itself:** *Hackers* and its director's "urban fairy tale", against *Blackhat* and its adviser's warning.

<ReaderQuestions>

- Of the works in this post, WarGames has the most documentary evidence of a film reaching a president, yet the directive does not name it and the computer crime hearings had begun years before: how much credit should a work of fiction get for a policy that was already under way?
- Mr. Robot's own advisers say they compress time to fit the screen: is a realistic tool on screen still realistic if the attack takes seconds instead of weeks?
- Some documentaries here were made with an advocacy aim their makers state openly, and one 2026 documentary is produced by a security vendor: what should a viewer check before treating a documentary as a record?
- O Rei da Internet tells a living person's criminal past through his own present-day voice: what do films owe the victims and the investigators of the cases they dramatize?
- If films can push lawmakers, as Poulsen feared about Blackhat, should security advisers to film productions say publicly what the film gets wrong, as he did?

</ReaderQuestions>
