## What actually degrades

The folk model of tiredness is a speed penalty: same thinking, slower. If that were true it would be manageable — you would take longer and arrive at the same place.

What happens instead is that the **content** of your reasoning changes.

**The set of live explanations shrinks.** Early on you can hold four possibilities loosely and move between them. By hour eleven you can hold one, and the others are not rejected — they have quietly stopped being available. Nothing announces their departure.

**The one you are on hardens.** Every additional hour invested makes abandoning it more expensive, and fatigue removes the surplus capacity that abandoning requires. This is [the assumption you cannot see](/practice/the-assumption-you-cannot-see) arriving by a different route: not a belief you never examined, but one you examined at hour three and can no longer re-open.

**Contrary evidence gets absorbed rather than weighed.** A measurement that does not fit gets explained in passing — *"that is probably the monitoring"* — with a fluency that at hour three would have prompted a check.

**Risk tolerance moves, and not consistently.** Some people become reckless, wanting it finished. Others become paralysed, unwilling to touch anything. Both feel like caution from the inside.

## The part that makes it dangerous

> **The faculty that would notice all of the above is itself degraded, and it degrades before anything visible does.**

You can still type accurately, still read a capture, still explain your reasoning coherently to somebody on a call — which is precisely why the room believes you, and why you believe yourself. **Nothing in your own experience feels wrong.** The sensation of being tired is not the same as the sensation of reasoning badly, and only the first one is available to you.

This is why *"I will stop when I notice I am not thinking straight"* fails. The noticing is downstream of the thing that broke.

## So the countermeasures have to be external

Every rule here is set **while rested**, by the same person, in advance — because a rule that requires an assessment at hour eleven requires exactly the capacity that is missing.

**A clock, not a feeling.** *"At 03:00 I hand over or escalate"* works. *"I will stop when I am too tired"* does not, and it is the same argument as [the go/no-go time](/practice/change-windows-and-rollback-arithmetic) in a change window, applied to a person instead of a change.

**No irreversible actions after a stated hour.** Decide in advance which class of action you will not take late — the destructive restore, the reformat, the config wipe. **Not because you would be careless, but because the one thing you cannot do at hour eleven is accurately estimate whether you are being careless.** Reversible work can continue; that distinction is from [deciding with incomplete information](/practice/deciding-with-incomplete-information) and it carries the weight here.

**Somebody else's read.** Not a review — a single question, asked of a rested person: *"here is what I think and why."* Their first reaction is worth more than another hour of yours, and its main value is that they can still hold the alternatives you have lost.

**Write the hypothesis down when you form it.** At hour eleven you can compare what you believe against what you believed, which is the only self-check that survives, because it does not require you to assess your current state — only to read.

## For whoever is running the room

The person eleven hours in will not put their hand up, and asking them to is asking for a judgement they cannot make.

**Make it the room's decision, not theirs.** *"You have been on this since four. Talk Ana through it and go home"* removes the need for them to admit anything, and it should be routine rather than remarkable. Where it happens only in extremis it reads as a rebuke, and people avoid triggering it.

And watch for the tell that is visible from outside even when it is invisible inside: **the same explanation, restated with rising confidence and no new evidence.** From within, that feels like conviction consolidating. From outside it is the clearest signal available that the set of live explanations has collapsed to one.

## The rules, set while rested

Written once, kept where you will see them at three in the morning:

- **My stop time is _____.** A clock time. At it, I hand over or escalate.
- **After _____ I do not do:** the irreversible list, named in advance.
- **I write my hypothesis down when I form it**, with the time.
- **When I have restated the same theory twice with no new evidence, I say so out loud** — to somebody, not to myself.
- **If I have been on this more than _____ hours, a rested person hears it before I act on it.**

Five lines, decided on a good afternoon. **At hour eleven you will not write these, and you will not need to — you will only need to read them**, which is the one cognitive act still reliably available.
