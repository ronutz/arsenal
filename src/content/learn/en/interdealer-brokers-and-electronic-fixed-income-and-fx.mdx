Most of the bonds and currencies in the world never trade on an exchange. The staff of five United States (US) agencies wrote in 2015 that Treasury securities "are traded over the counter, and trades are executed by voice or on electronic trading platforms" ([staffs of the US Treasury and four other agencies, Joint Staff Report, 13 July 2015](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)), and the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) puts the average foreign exchange (FX) turnover of April 2025 at about $9.5 trillion a day in its revised tables ([BIS, Triennial Survey 2025, FX annex tables, revised June 2026](https://www.bis.org/publications/triennial-survey/rpfx25-fx-annex-2606.pdf)). Between the banks that make those markets sits a kind of firm most investors never see: the interdealer broker (IDB), which lets dealers trade with one another without revealing who they are.

This part of the series opened by [Electronic trading: from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) follows that hidden layer from the voice brokers of London and New York to the screens that replaced them. After reading, you will be able to explain what an interdealer broker does and why anonymity matters to dealers, trace ICAP, BrokerTec, Electronic Broking Services (EBS) and Reuters Matching to their current owners from the companies' own filings, retell the Treasury flash rally of 15 October 2014 from the official report and work out what its price swing meant in dollars, weigh both sides of the dispute over last look in foreign exchange, and describe how Brazil trades government bonds and dollars electronically. The Financial Information eXchange (FIX) messages that carry these trades are covered in [The life of an order in FIX](/learn/the-life-of-an-order-in-fix), and the binary protocols of the platforms in [FIX encodings and successors](/learn/fix-encodings-and-successors-fixml-fast-sbe-and-exchange-binary-protocols); this part is about the market structure around them.

## The middlemen between dealers

### Two markets in one

Bond and currency markets have long had two layers. A study group of the BIS Markets Committee wrote that the fixed income market "has historically been marked by a segmentation between the inter-dealer segment, in which dealers trade with one another, and the dealer-customer segment, in which dealers trade with their clients", and recalled that in the first of them "dealers traditionally traded either bilaterally over the phone or multilaterally (and anonymously) via voice brokers" ([BIS Markets Committee, Electronic trading in fixed income markets, January 2016](https://www.bis.org/publications/electronic-trading-fixed-income-markets.pdf)). The US report on 15 October 2014 draws the same line: "In contrast to the interdealer market, where brokers facilitate anonymous trades between counterparties, dealer-to-customer trades occur directly between broker-dealers and end investors" ([Joint Staff Report, 2015, footnote 31](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). Two BIS economists described FX in the same terms, as "long characterised by a two-tier structure, with clearly delineated inter-dealer and dealer-customer segments" ([Schrimpf and Sushko, BIS Quarterly Review, December 2019](https://www.bis.org/publications/fx-trade-execution-complex-and-highly-fragmented_0.pdf)).

### What a broker sells

An IDB does not take the other side of a trade for its own book; it finds the other side. ICAP, today a brand of the TP ICAP group, lists what it offers financial institutions: help to "find counterparties for financial transactions quickly and efficiently", "a level of anonymity for clients, which can be crucial when trading sensitive instruments or large positions", and price discovery "by aggregating buy and sell orders and providing indicative pricing information", which it calls particularly important in over-the-counter (OTC) markets "where prices may not be readily available" ([ICAP, Financial Markets, undated page](https://icap.com/financial-markets)). Anonymity is the core of the service: a bank that shows a large bid to its competitors by telephone tells them what it needs, while a bid shown through a broker reveals only that someone wants to buy.

### Licensed intermediaries

The role was formalized when governments restructured their bond markets. TP ICAP's history of its Tullett Prebon brand says that in 1985 the Bank of England named six groups, Fundamental and Marshall Brokers, Charles Fulton, Mabon Nugent and Tullett and Tokyo among them, as eligible to operate as interdealer brokers, "a new form of intermediary in the restructured British government securities market", ahead of London's deregulation of 1986 ([TP ICAP, Tullett Prebon Brasil, History, undated](https://tpicap.com/tpbrasil/history)); [Open outcry: trading floors and the pregão](/learn/open-outcry-trading-floors-and-the-pregao) tells the story of that Big Bang. The same history says that Liberty Brokerage, founded in 1986 by most of the primary US government securities dealers, was one of the two largest IDBs in Treasuries "with a share of just under 35% of the market" before it merged with Tullett and Tokyo Forex in 1999 ([TP ICAP, History, undated](https://tpicap.com/tpbrasil/history)). Those are the company's accounts of its own lineage.

## ICAP, Tullett Prebon and TP ICAP

### From Intercapital to ICAP

ICAP's own page dates its origin to Intercapital, "set up in May 1986, initially to concentrate on the new interest rate swaps market", which merged with Garban plc in September 1999; the combined Garban-Intercapital plc was renamed ICAP plc in July 2001 ([ICAP, About Us, undated](https://icap.com/about-us)). The page lists the electronic steps that followed: in 2006 ICAP "bought EBS, a major trading platform for foreign exchange", and in December 2007 it acquired Traiana, a provider of post-trade processing ([ICAP, About Us](https://icap.com/about-us)). Before EBS came the bond platform: a competitor's annual report records that "On May 7, 2003, ICAP, an interdealer broker in the financial markets, completed its acquisition of the global trading operations of BrokerTec Global, a global electronic bond trading platform" ([eSpeed, Form 10-K for 2003, filed 15 March 2004](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1094831/000095013604000735/file001.htm)). Euromoney reported on 1 May 2006 that ICAP paid "a cash consideration of $775 million" for EBS, which it called "still the FX spot market's predominant broker", and that EBS's 13 shareholders had refused approaches from venture capitalists ([Euromoney, 1 May 2006](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh1h5lzrm/foreign-exchange/brokers-icap-pulls-off-coup-with-ebs-purchase/)).

### The split of 2016

In November 2015 ICAP "agreed terms for the disposal of its global hybrid voice broking and information business to Tullett Prebon" ([ICAP, About Us](https://icap.com/about-us)); TP ICAP says the deal "completed on 30 December 2016 and Tullett Prebon plc was renamed TP ICAP Group" ([TP ICAP, History](https://tpicap.com/tpbrasil/history)). What remained of the old ICAP, the electronic platforms, took a new name: Thomson Reuters' annual report for 2016 refers to "NEX Group's (formerly ICAP) EBS platform" as a competitor of its FX business ([Thomson Reuters, Annual Report 2016, filed with Form 40-F, 9 March 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075124/000119312517076946/d252082dex991.htm)). On 2 November 2018 CME Group, the parent of the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), and its subsidiary CME London completed the acquisition of NEX Group by a scheme of arrangement under the United Kingdom's Companies Act, issuing 16,926,582 shares of Class A common stock to NEX shareholders and appointing NEX's chief executive, Michael Spencer, to CME's board ([CME Group, Form 8-K, filed 8 November 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000119312518322722/d644644d8k.htm)). CME's next annual report put it plainly: "In connection with the acquisition of NEX, we acquired the BrokerTec and EBS trading platforms, which will eventually be migrated to CME Globex" ([CME Group, Form 10-K for 2018, filed 28 February 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637519000010/cme-2018123110k.htm)).

### TP ICAP now

The broking half grew into the largest firm of its kind by its own account: TP ICAP's annual report for 2025 calls the group "the world's largest inter-dealer broker" and describes its origins as "a predominantly voice-driven interdealer broker" ([TP ICAP, Annual Report and Accounts 2025, April 2026](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/sites/g/files/escbpb106/files/2026-04/ARA_Full%20report_2025.pdf)). Its results for the first half of 2026 report revenue of £1,292 million, of which the Global Broking division, the old interdealer business, contributed £783 million, or 61%, beside Energy and Commodities (£233 million), Liquidnet (£194 million) and the data division Parameta Solutions (£102 million); the group says "Our targeted investment into electronic platforms continued to drive higher levels of client engagement and trading activity" and that it is investing in its Fusion platform with Amazon Web Services ([TP ICAP, Interim results for H1 2026, 5 August 2026](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/sites/g/files/escbpb106/files/2026-08/TP%20ICAP%20H1%202026%20Interim%20results.pdf)). The ICAP brand's own page says it operates "in 22 countries, from 33 locations", and that its voice broking "is fully integrated with our technology - Fusion - giving customers electronic connectivity to our order books and trading venues" ([ICAP, About Us](https://icap.com/about-us); [ICAP, Financial Markets](https://icap.com/financial-markets)).

## Treasuries on screens: eSpeed and BrokerTec

### Two platforms, and a date in dispute

The interdealer Treasury market went electronic around the turn of the century. BGC, the descendant of Cantor Fitzgerald's broking business, says "Cantor started our wholesale intermediary brokerage operations in 1972", that "In 1996, Cantor launched its eSpeed system, which revolutionized the way government bonds are traded in the inter-dealer market by providing a fully electronic trading marketplace", and that eSpeed went public in 1999 ([BGC Partners, Form 10-K for 2021, filed 28 February 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1094831/000162828022004275/bgcp-20211231.htm)). The BIS study group dates the launch differently: "eSpeed and BrokerTec for US Treasury securities both launched in 1999" ([BIS Markets Committee, January 2016](https://www.bis.org/publications/electronic-trading-fixed-income-markets.pdf)). The two documents do not say what each date measures, so both are given here.

BrokerTec was the banks' own answer. The Chicago Mercantile Exchange's annual report for 2002 described BrokerTec Global as "an electronic interdealer fixed-income broker whose members include Citigroup, Credit Suisse First Boston, Deutsche Bank AG, Goldman Sachs Group, J.P. Morgan Chase, Lehman Brothers, Merrill Lynch & Co., Morgan Stanley and UBS Warburg", active in US Treasuries, euro-denominated sovereign debt and repurchase agreements, which had launched its own futures exchange in 2002 and agreed in January 2003 to sell most of its trading operations to ICAP ([Chicago Mercantile Exchange Holdings, Form 10-K for 2002, filed 21 March 2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000104746903009665/a2104660z10-k.htm)). The rivalry reached court: eSpeed sued BrokerTec, ICAP and BrokerTec's technology supplier for patent infringement in July 2003, and in January 2004 the court refused a preliminary injunction on the ground that "a critical public interest in maintaining a fluid, competitive market for trading treasury securities" outweighed eSpeed's private interest ([eSpeed, 10-K for 2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1094831/000095013604000735/file001.htm)).

eSpeed's platform later changed hands twice. BGC sold "the Fully Electronic portion of the Company's former benchmark on-the-run U.S. Treasury brokerage, market data and co-location service businesses" to Nasdaq on 28 June 2013 ([BGC Partners, 10-K for 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1094831/000162828022004275/bgcp-20211231.htm)), and Nasdaq announced on 25 June 2021 that it had completed the sale of that business, Nasdaq Fixed Income, to Tradeweb Markets, announced on 2 February 2021 ([Nasdaq, news release filed with Form 8-K, 25 June 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1120193/000119312521200449/d139731dex991.htm); [Nasdaq, 2 February 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1120193/000119312521025308/d89849dex991.htm)).

### How the screens work

The interdealer screens are central limit order books (CLOBs), but with a twist. The 2015 report explains that "BrokerTec offers a 'workup' mechanism that is initiated by a regular order book trade. The workup provides an opportunity for all traders to transact additional quantity on either side of the initial trade at the price of the initiating trade", and that workups made up a majority of BrokerTec's volume in the most volatile minutes of 15 October 2014 ([Joint Staff Report, 2015, footnote 8](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). The same report says trading in the most recently issued, "on-the-run", securities concentrates on the electronic platforms, while interdealer trading in older "off-the-run" issues still takes place "primarily through traditional voice-assisted brokers" ([Joint Staff Report, section 4](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)).

Who may use the screens changed more than how they work. "Until 1992, for example, the interdealer brokers only allowed primary dealers, as designated by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, to access their trading platforms"; access then widened to netting members of the Government Securities Clearing Corporation, now the Fixed Income Clearing Corporation (FICC), then to clients of prime brokers, and "PTFs gained access to the platforms in the mid-2000s", PTFs being principal trading firms, which trade only their own money, usually with automated strategies ([Joint Staff Report, section 4](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). By 2016 the BIS group could write that "The inter-dealer market for on-the-run US Treasury securities is almost entirely electronic, with a CLOB and a large presence of PTFs" ([BIS Markets Committee, January 2016](https://www.bis.org/publications/electronic-trading-fixed-income-markets.pdf)). The high-frequency strategies behind that presence, and the argument about them, are in [High-frequency trading: what it is and the dispute](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute).

### BrokerTec at CME today

CME Group's annual report for 2025 says its cash markets business, BrokerTec and EBS, is now "primarily operated on CME Globex" and generated $283.7 million of clearing and transaction fees in 2025 ([CME Group, Form 10-K for 2025, filed 26 February 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637526000009/cme-20251231.htm)). BrokerTec offers "a dealer-to-dealer electronic trading platform operated on CME Globex" for US Treasuries, European government bonds and repurchase agreements; in 2025 CME launched BrokerTec Chicago, "a second CLOB for cash U.S. Treasuries co-located with our U.S. Treasury futures and options markets", and it offers request-for-quote (RFQ) trading in repo through BrokerTec Quote ([CME Group, 10-K for 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637526000009/cme-20251231.htm)). The same filing describes BrokerTec Americas as a FICC member running a "matched principal platform", on which anonymous trades between FICC members "are expected to be novated promptly to FICC, which will be their ultimate counterparty", and names a competitive threat: newer venues that "compete on price, offering their services at significantly lower rates than the established EBS and BrokerTec platforms" ([CME Group, 10-K for 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637526000009/cme-20251231.htm)). CME's order entry for these markets runs over the same binary iLink protocol as its futures, as [the part on FIX encodings](/learn/fix-encodings-and-successors-fixml-fast-sbe-and-exchange-binary-protocols) describes.

## 15 October 2014: the Treasury flash rally

### What happened

On 15 October 2014 the yield of the benchmark 10-year Treasury note "experienced a 37-basis-point trading range, only to close 6 basis points below its opening level", and between 9:33 and 9:45 a.m. Eastern Time it fell 16 basis points and came back "without a clear cause" ([Joint Staff Report, 13 July 2015, executive summary](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). The staffs of the Treasury, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) wrote that "For such significant volatility and a large round-trip in prices to occur in so short a time with no obvious catalyst is unprecedented in the recent history of the Treasury market" ([Joint Staff Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). Their data came largely from BrokerTec, eSpeed and CME, the venues where interdealer price discovery happens ([Joint Staff Report, section 1](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)).

| 9:30 to 9:45 | Market depth in the 10-year note about 20% of its year-to-date average for that quarter-hour | [Joint Staff Report, section 2](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf) |

| 9:44:35 | 10-year yield back at 1.99% | [Joint Staff Report, footnote 16](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf) |

### What the staff found

The report found no single cause. Principal trading firms "represented more than half of traded volume, followed by bank-dealers", and in the first six minutes of the window their share rose to about 73.5% of 10-year volume in cash and 68.4% in futures, while bank-dealers fell to 21.4% and 14.1% ([Joint Staff Report, section 3](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). Bank-dealers widened their spreads and for a time showed few or no offers in the cash order book, while PTFs reduced the size of their orders "but maintained tight bid-ask spreads"; volumes reached six to ten times their average levels, and no trade on the platforms analysed was broken ([Joint Staff Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). At the futures exchange, "a peak message rate of around 40 messages per millisecond results in a gradual slowing down of the response time of the matching engine", a latency the report defines as the time between an order's generation by a firm and its addition to the book; the report adds that "some market participants monitor latency and include it as a variable in their trading strategies" ([Joint Staff Report, section 3 and footnote 27](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). How firms watch that latency on the wire is the subject of [Network-derived analytics: watching trading on the wire](/learn/network-derived-analytics-and-trading-monitoring).

The report also measured how tightly Chicago and New Jersey were bound: trades by the ten most active PTFs at CME were "frequently followed by trades by the same group of firms in the cash market with a lag of roughly 5 milliseconds", just above the theoretical minimum latency between the two matching engines ([Joint Staff Report, appendix B](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)); [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency) measures that same route.

### Worked example: what 16 basis points means in dollars

A yield is easier to grasp as a price. Take an illustrative 10-year note paying a 2% coupon twice a year, priced on a coupon date (not the actual benchmark issue of that day, whose terms are not used here), and price it at the three yields of footnote 16 with the standard present-value formula: each half-yearly coupon of 1 per 100 and the final 100 are discounted at half the yield per period. The report itself uses "a quantity of $100 million at the 10-year maturity point" as an example of a large but not unusual trade ([Joint Staff Report, section 2](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)), so the table applies the moves to $100 million of face value.

### What changed afterwards

The report's broader conclusion was a question, not a verdict: by many metrics liquidity was "as robust as they have ever been", but the changes in structure raised the issue of "whether an improvement in average liquidity conditions may come at the cost of rare but severe bouts of volatility that coincide with significant strains in liquidity" ([Joint Staff Report, executive summary](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)). One concern it named was clearing: PTFs trading on the interdealer platforms stayed outside FICC and cleared bilaterally or through prime brokers, so their trades might not get the same margin and default protections ([Joint Staff Report, appendix C](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/276/joint-staff-report-the-us-treasury-market-on-10-15-2014.pdf)).

The SEC answered that point eight years later. On 13 December 2023 it adopted rules requiring clearing agencies for Treasuries to make their direct participants submit eligible secondary market trades for central clearing, citing an industry estimate that only 13% of cash Treasury volume was centrally cleared in the first half of 2017 and that a further 19% was "hybrid" clearing, "executed on an interdealer broker platform" where one side cleared centrally and the other bilaterally with the broker ([SEC, Release 34-99149, December 2023](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/34-99149.pdf)). The SEC later extended compliance by a year, to 31 December 2026 for cash trades and 30 June 2027 for repurchase agreements ([SEC, final rule, extension of compliance dates, Federal Register, 4 March 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-03-04/html/2025-03351.htm)), dates repeated by Commissioner Mark T. Uyeda in a statement of 23 December 2025 ([SEC, Commissioner Uyeda, statement, 23 December 2025](https://sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-statement-update-continuing-work-toward-treasury-clearing-implementation-122325)).

## Foreign exchange: from Reuters Dealing to EBS and Matching

### Messaging, then matching

FX went electronic in two steps. The London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG), which now owns the business, says that "In 1981, at a time when all FX business was conducted over the phone", Reuters launched the Reuters Dealing System, which let traders negotiate and confirm deals by electronic text, and that "Eleven years later" it launched Matching ([LSEG, global FX trading ecosystem, undated page](https://www.lseg.com/en/fx/global-forex-trading-ecosystem)). WatersTechnology reported on 4 May 1992 that Reuters' Dealing 2000-2 electronic order-matching system "went live last Wednesday", with "some 70 trades executed on the first day" by 19 banks at 22 sites in London and New York ([WatersTechnology, 4 May 1992](https://www.waterstechnology.com/sell-side-technology/feature/1626269/reuters-opens-dealing-2000-to-thin-volume-trader-raves)). Its rival, EBS, "went live in 1993", according to Euromoney ([Euromoney, 1 May 2006](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh1h5lzrm/foreign-exchange/brokers-icap-pulls-off-coup-with-ebs-purchase/)).

The central banks of the Group of Ten described how both worked in 2001. "Participation in both systems is limited to dealers, who anonymously enter bids and/or offers into their terminals"; each bank allocates credit lines to the counterparties it will accept, so the best price a dealer can hit may differ from the best price in the market; when two orders match, the counterparties are revealed to each other and settle two days later; and "If several banks are offering the same best price, their offers are met on the basis of time (first in, first out)" ([Committee on the Global Financial System, BIS, The implications of electronic trading in financial markets, January 2001, box D](https://www.bis.org/publications/cgfs-paper-16-implications-electronic-trading-financial-markets.pdf)). Four years earlier the New York Foreign Exchange Committee had surveyed its members: "Since its introduction in 1992, electronic broking has helped accelerate the pace of change in the industry", and electronic systems already handled 40% of brokered transactions, though only 33% of brokered value ([Foreign Exchange Committee, Federal Reserve Bank of New York, survey of December 1997](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/microsites/fxc/files/elecbrok.pdf)).

### The primary market, and its decline

Economists at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York wrote in 2024 that "In the late 1990s, two electronic brokers, Reuters (now Refinitiv) and Electronic Broking Services (EBS), established themselves in the interdealer market as the clear sources of price discovery, becoming known jointly as the 'primary market'" ([Chaboud, Chung, Goldberg and Nordstrom, Liberty Street Economics, 11 January 2024](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2024/01/towards-increasing-complexity-the-evolution-of-the-fx-market)). Their importance then shrank. Dealer-to-dealer trading fell from 63.0% of turnover in 1998 to 46.1% in 2022, because "Dealers in major trading centers currently internalize about 80 percent of spot orders", matching opposite customer flows in house instead of hedging with other banks, while "Nearly 60 percent of trading now takes place electronically" ([Liberty Street Economics, 2024](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2024/01/towards-increasing-complexity-the-evolution-of-the-fx-market)). The BIS found that electronic interdealer spot trading had fallen 7% between 2016 and 2019, to $368 billion a day, and that interdealer brokerage systems "now only account for a small fraction of the entire market", while adding that "primary venues (eg EBS Market and Reuters (Refinitiv) Matching) are still vital for FX market functioning" because dealers need them when volatility makes internalization hard ([Schrimpf and Sushko, BIS Quarterly Review, December 2019](https://www.bis.org/publications/fx-trade-execution-complex-and-highly-fragmented_0.pdf)).

### The owners now

Reuters' FX venues passed through three owners. Thomson Reuters notes "the 2008 acquisition of Reuters"; in October 2018 it sold 55% of its Financial and Risk business "to private equity funds managed by Blackstone for approximately $17 billion" and kept 45% of "the new company, which is now known as Refinitiv" ([Thomson Reuters, Annual Report 2018, filed with Form 40-F, 13 March 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075124/000119312519073387/d669805dex991.htm)); LSEG completed its all-share acquisition of Refinitiv on 29 January 2021 ([LSEG, 29 January 2021](https://lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2021/lseg-all-share-acquisition-refinitiv-transaction-completion-and-admission-trading)). LSEG describes FX Matching as an "Anonymous central limit order book for spot and FX swaps trading for the dealer-to-dealer community, with over 1,000 subscribers and spot matching in over 80 currency pairs" ([LSEG FX, UK MTF factsheet, August 2023](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/fx/lseg-fx-mtf-factsheet.pdf)), while an LSEG article of the same year says it covers "more than 70 currency pairs in total" ([LSEG, Tick History PCAP article, September 2023](https://lseg.com/en/insights/fx/revolutionising-fx-price-transparency-with-tick-history-pcap)); the two counts are LSEG's own. LSEG also says that from May 2021 it began moving Matching and its dealer-to-client platform FXall onto Millennium Exchange, the software that runs the London Stock Exchange's trading ([LSEG, FX ecosystem](https://www.lseg.com/en/fx/global-forex-trading-ecosystem)).

EBS went from its bank shareholders to ICAP, NEX and CME, as the table above shows. CME now describes "EBS Market" as "a CLOB electronic trading platform for spot FX, Spot Precious Metals and non-deliverable forwards" and "EBS Direct" as "a relationship-based trading platform", and in 2025 it launched FX Spot+, connecting OTC spot FX with its FX futures ([CME Group, 10-K for 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637526000009/cme-20251231.htm)). A non-deliverable forward (NDF) is a forward contract without physical delivery of the currency; because the Brazilian real is not convertible, foreign investors use NDFs offshore to take positions in it, according to the BCB ([Banco Central do Brasil (BCB), Estudo Especial 41/2019](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE41_O_mercado_de_cambio_brasileiro_e_o_desenvolvimento_do_mercado_de_derivativos_cambiais.pdf)). Speed reached these venues too: the BIS records that EBS raised the frequency of its price updates "from every 100 milliseconds (ms) to every 20ms in September 2016, and further to every 5ms for select participants in February 2017" ([BIS Markets Committee, Monitoring of fast-paced electronic markets, September 2018](https://www.bis.org/publications/monitoring-fast-paced-electronic-markets.pdf)). The same report records a counter-move by dealer banks in 2013, the ParFX platform with deliberate random delays, which belongs to [Fairness by design](/learn/fairness-by-design-speed-bumps-batch-auctions-and-iex).

## Last look: risk control or free option?

### The rule

Most electronic FX does not trade on a central book. It trades on prices streamed by liquidity providers (LPs) to their clients, the liquidity consumers (LCs), and many of those prices are subject to last look. The FX Global Code, the industry's code of conduct, defines it: "Last look is a practice utilised in Electronic Trading Activities whereby a Market Participant receiving a trade request has a final opportunity to accept or reject the request against its quoted price" ([Global Foreign Exchange Committee, FX Global Code, principle 17, updated December 2024](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/fx_global.pdf)). The Code was published complete in May 2017 after about two years of work by central banks from 16 jurisdictions under the BIS Markets Committee, and the Global Foreign Exchange Committee (GFXC) of central banks and market participants maintains it ([GFXC, Establishment of the FX Global Code](https://www.globalfxc.org/fx-global-code/establishment-of-the-fx-global-code/)). It "does not impose legal or regulatory obligations on Market Participants nor does it substitute for regulation" ([FX Global Code, foreword](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/fx_global.pdf)).

Principle 17 allows only two checks: a validity check, that the request is operationally sound and that "there is sufficient available credit", and a price check, "to confirm whether the price at which the trade request was made remains consistent with the current price that would be available to the Client" ([FX Global Code, principle 17](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/fx_global.pdf)). The GFXC tightened the text in December 2017 and in a report of August 2021 explained the two ways a price check can work: a symmetric check rejects any request whose price has moved beyond a tolerance in either direction, while an asymmetric check "tends to lead to trades being accepted when the price has moved in favor of the LP, while trade requests are rejected if the price moves beyond the tolerance level against the LP" ([GFXC, Execution Principles Working Group Report on Last Look, August 2021](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/gfxc_report_last_look-1.pdf)). The report adds that "Incorporating any delay that is additional to what is required to complete the price and validity check is considered contrary to Principle 17", and that "Principle 17 explicitly disallows the use of last look for the purposes of information gathering with no intention to accept the Client's request to trade" ([GFXC, 2021](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/gfxc_report_last_look-1.pdf)).

### Worked example: four requests, two kinds of check

An LP streams an offer to sell euros at 1.10000 dollars and applies a price tolerance of half a pip, 0.00005. A client clicks to buy. During the last look window the market moves; the table shows what each kind of check does with four outcomes. The rules are those described by the GFXC; the prices and tolerance are invented for the example and were computed by two independent programs.

| LP's current price at the end of the window | Move against the quote | Symmetric check | Asymmetric check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.10000 | 0.0 pip | accept | accept |
| 1.10003 | +0.3 pip (against the LP, within tolerance) | accept | accept |
| 1.10008 | +0.8 pip (against the LP, beyond tolerance) | reject | reject |
| 1.09992 | −0.8 pip (in the LP's favour, beyond tolerance) | reject | accept |

The last row is the whole dispute in one line: under an asymmetric check the client never wins a move beyond the tolerance, because a move in the client's favour is rejected and a move in the LP's favour is accepted. The GFXC therefore asks LPs to disclose whether their check is symmetric and the minimum and maximum length of their window ([GFXC, 2021, recommendation 2](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/gfxc_report_last_look-1.pdf)).

### The case for, and the case that was sanctioned

<Claim status="attributed" by="by the GFXC">The case for last look starts from the way FX is quoted: LPs "simultaneously stream many indicative FX quotes", so an LP is exposed to many requests arriving at once, or to requests that reach it "significantly later than the time of the quote", and the validity and price checks are how it manages that risk</Claim> ([GFXC, 2021](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/gfxc_report_last_look-1.pdf)). The GFXC also notes that "there are venues in the FX industry that use firm quotes and do not utilize last look", so clients can choose ([GFXC, 2021](https://www.globalfxc.org/uploads/gfxc_report_last_look-1.pdf)); LSEG says "there is no last look in FX Matching" and "All orders in Spot Matching are firm" ([LSEG, September 2023](https://lseg.com/en/insights/fx/revolutionising-fx-price-transparency-with-tick-history-pcap)).

The case against was made by a regulator. On 18 November 2015 the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) announced that Barclays would pay an additional $150 million penalty, bringing its FX penalties with that regulator to $635 million, over the last look system of its BARX platform ([NYDFS, press release of 18 November 2015, as reprinted by Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/new-york-state-department-of-financial-services-announces-barclays-to-pay-additi-20151118/)). The NYDFS said Barclays had built last look to protect itself from "toxic order flow", fast clients exploiting stale prices, but applied it to all clients using its FIX interface, rejecting orders when the price moved against the bank beyond an undisclosed threshold during a hold period, so that "Barclays instead used it as a general filter to reject customer orders that Barclays predicted, based on price movements during the hold period, would be unprofitable to the Bank"; clients received only a rejection code, "NACK", for "Not Acknowledged" ([NYDFS, 2015](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/new-york-state-department-of-financial-services-announces-barclays-to-pay-additi-20151118/)). The bank made its filters symmetric in September and October 2014, after the investigation began, though one platform carrying 7% of its volume stayed asymmetric until August 2015, according to the regulator ([NYDFS, 2015](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/new-york-state-department-of-financial-services-announces-barclays-to-pay-additi-20151118/)). The Code's later text, disclosure of symmetry and of window length, and the ban on delay beyond the checks themselves, answers exactly those practices.

## Brazil: brokers, the Treasury's dealers, Trademate and the dollar

### The interdealer brokers in Brazil

The global brokers have Brazilian arms. TP ICAP's list of subsidiaries includes Tullett Prebon Brasil Corretora de Valores e Câmbio, wholly owned, and ICAP do Brasil Corretora de Títulos e Valores Mobiliários in Rio de Janeiro ([TP ICAP, Annual Report and Accounts 2025](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/sites/g/files/escbpb106/files/2026-04/ARA_Full%20report_2025.pdf)). Tullett Prebon says it entered Brazil in 2011 by buying Convenção, a broker founded by Eduardo da Rocha Azevedo, a former president of the São Paulo stock exchange (Bovespa) and founder of the Brazilian Mercantile and Futures Exchange (BM&F) ([TP ICAP, Tullett Prebon Brasil, About Us, undated](https://tpicap.com/tpbrasil/about-us)). Its Brazilian desks describe themselves in its own words: the FX desk's brokers are "primarily specialized in the USD futures market", "The Fixed Income desk is one of the market leaders in Treasury securities", and its direct market access (DMA) offers "Connectivity with most trading platforms worldwide through the FIX Gateway" ([TP ICAP, Tullett Prebon Brasil, Asset Classes, undated](https://tpicap.com/tpbrasil/product)). The other IDB group with a Brazilian history, Compagnie Financière Tradition and CM Capital, appears in [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access).

Competition for brokers' teams reaches the courts here too. TP ICAP discloses that in December 2012 Flow Participações and Brasil Plural Corretora sued Tullett Prebon's Brazilian companies <Claim status="alleged" by="by the plaintiffs">for unfair competition, including the recruitment of Flow's former employees and the use of systems and confidential information developed by the plaintiffs</Claim>, claiming R$478 million; TP ICAP says it "intends to vigorously defend itself" and that there is no certainty as to the outcome ([TP ICAP, Annual Report and Accounts 2025, note 34](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/sites/g/files/escbpb106/files/2026-04/ARA_Full%20report_2025.pdf)).

### Government bonds: the Treasury's dealers and Trademate

Brazil's federal debt is sold through dealers, which the federal audit court, the Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU), defines as financial institutions authorized by the BCB and accredited by the National Treasury "com o objetivo de promover o desenvolvimento dos mercados primário e secundário de títulos públicos", to develop the primary and secondary markets for government bonds ([TCU, 7 May 2025](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/imprensa/noticias/tesouro-nacional-aplica-com-regularidade-normas-relacionadas-ao-sistema-de-dealer)). The bonds are registered and settled in the Sistema Especial de Liquidação e Custódia (Selic), which the TCU names as the system where dealers' special operations are recorded ([TCU, 2025](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/imprensa/noticias/tesouro-nacional-aplica-com-regularidade-normas-relacionadas-ao-sistema-de-dealer)). The BIS study group of 2016 cited Brazil among the markets where electronic interdealer trading in sovereign bonds "successfully developed with the benefit of public sector support: Selic in Brazil" ([BIS Markets Committee, January 2016](https://www.bis.org/publications/electronic-trading-fixed-income-markets.pdf)).

The screen where those bonds now trade between institutions is B3's. B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão) describes Trademate as "a plataforma de negociação eletrônica de produtos de renda fixa da bolsa do Brasil", its electronic trading platform for fixed income, built natively in the cloud and integrated over the FIX protocol ([B3, Trademate, undated page](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/solucoes/plataformas/interface-de-negociacao/trademate/com-o-trademate-e-possivel-negociar.htm)). B3 dates its launch to July 2023, replacing the earlier Trader platform, in two announcements ([B3, 29 October 2024](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/trademate.htm); [B3, 7 January 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/trademate-8AE490C99B99E56A019B99F966A64E5C.htm)) and to June 2023 in a third ([B3, 20 August 2026](https://b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/trademate-8AE490C8A012158601A020A688A87098.htm)). B3 reports R$4.2 trillion traded in secondary fixed income on Trademate in 2025, 49% more than in 2024, of which R$2.9 trillion in federal government bonds, with 5,178,737 trades against 873,768 a year earlier and 650 active institutions ([B3, 7 January 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/trademate-8AE490C99B99E56A019B99F966A64E5C.htm)). On 17 August 2026 it recorded 1,431 trades in government bonds in one day, worth R$16.57 billion, about 93% of them in inflation-linked notes, and B3 says the Treasury's dealers, "nove bancos e três corretoras", nine banks and three brokers, chose Trademate for the 24th consecutive half-year as one of the official platforms for buying and selling government bonds ([B3, 20 August 2026](https://b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/trademate-8AE490C8A012158601A020A688A87098.htm)). Changes to Trademate's FIX messages are covered in [The life of an order in FIX](/learn/the-life-of-an-order-in-fix).

The plumbing behind a trade is short. For dealer trades to count in the Treasury's dealer evaluation, B3 told participants in 2023, the cycle "inicia-se com o lançamento dos negócios na plataforma de Renda Fixa Trademate", starts with the trade on Trademate, then goes through capture and allocation to the generation of a command number used "para o registro e liquidação nos Sistemas do Selic", for registration and settlement in the Selic systems ([B3, Comunicado Externo 120/2023-VNC, 28 November 2023](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/15/E6/11/3A/1971C8107E854EB8AC094EA8/CE%20120-2023-VNC%20Dealeranca_B3_STN_PT.pdf)).

### The dollar: futures first, spot second

Brazil's FX market has an unusual shape, and the central bank and academics describe it from two angles. A special study of the BCB explains that the spot market has a primary segment, open to anyone importing, exporting or moving money, and an interbank segment in which only institutions authorized by the BCB operate, 187 of them in April 2019 including every bank with an FX portfolio; together they traded about US$7 billion a day ([BCB, Estudo Especial 41/2019](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE41_O_mercado_de_cambio_brasileiro_e_o_desenvolvimento_do_mercado_de_derivativos_cambiais.pdf)). Granger causality tests, the study says, show that "na maior parte do tempo, o preço se forma no mercado futuro e é, então, repassado para o mercado à vista", that most of the time the price forms in the futures market and passes to spot, the reverse of developed markets, because listed dollar futures on B3 trade about twice the volume of deliverable FX ([BCB, EE 41/2019](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE41_O_mercado_de_cambio_brasileiro_e_o_desenvolvimento_do_mercado_de_derivativos_cambiais.pdf)). Yet the reference rate Ptax, used to settle many derivatives, is formed in the interbank spot market ([BCB, EE 41/2019](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE41_O_mercado_de_cambio_brasileiro_e_o_desenvolvimento_do_mercado_de_derivativos_cambiais.pdf)).

Why it is so is argued. <Claim status="attributed" by="by Ventura and Garcia">André Ventura and Márcio Garcia, using every bid, offer and trade in BM&F's dollar futures and its interbank spot dollar trading from February 2006 to May 2007, found the futures market far more liquid and the exchange rate formed there first, and concluded that "os mercados cambiais no Brasil possuem uma configuração absolutamente atípica no mundo", that Brazil's FX markets have an entirely atypical configuration, which they explain by the history of the market's rules</Claim> ([Ventura and Garcia, Revista Brasileira de Economia 66(1), 2012, IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/fgv/epgrbe/v66y2012i1a1907.html)). Some studies, the BCB notes, attribute the migration of liquidity to the requirement of BCB authorization to trade interbank and carry FX positions; <Claim status="attributed" by="by the BCB">the BCB's own view is that "a maior liquidez dos derivativos não decorre de restrições de acesso ao mercado interbancário de câmbio", that the derivatives' greater liquidity does not come from access restrictions, since every bank with an FX portfolio is authorized and banks are the ones that form prices elsewhere too</Claim> ([BCB, EE 41/2019](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE41_O_mercado_de_cambio_brasileiro_e_o_desenvolvimento_do_mercado_de_derivativos_cambiais.pdf)).

The spot interbank market also has its brokers. Ventura and Garcia describe trades made directly between dealers or through intermediary brokers, which "não podendo carregar posições em moeda estrangeira", cannot carry FX positions, and date BM&F's open-outcry ring for spot dollars, the Roda de Dólar Pronto, to 1 February 2006 ([Ventura and Garcia, 2012, author's copy](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/mgarcia/Artigos/111228_AV_MG_RBE.pdf)); the PUMA Trading System's first stage went live in spot FX in August 2011, as [The screens arrive](/learn/the-screens-arrive-electronic-exchanges-from-nasdaq-to-globex) records. B3's FX clearinghouse, created in 2002, acts as central counterparty for interbank spot dollar trades; about 100 banks were enabled to use it, average daily volume in 2023 was about R$10 billion, and it moved to a cloud platform from 22 January 2024 ([B3, 17 January 2024](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/b3-moderniza-clearing-de-cambio-para-desenvolver-novos-produtos-e-fomentar-o-mercado.htm)). Its rules give brokers the same limited role: "Os Intermediadores não podem, em qualquer hipótese, praticar Operações de Câmbio em nome próprio", intermediaries may never trade FX in their own name ([B3, Manual de Operações da Câmara de Câmbio B3, 2 May 2024](https://b3.com.br/data/files/5B/03/FD/26/F68A09105FE89209AC094EA8/Manual%20de%20Operacoes%20da%20Camara%20de%20Cambio%20B3.pdf)). The FIX rules of B3's FX segment, a blind screen open only to banks, are in [The life of an order in FIX](/learn/the-life-of-an-order-in-fix).

### Worked example: a spot dollar trade on B3's electronic screen

B3's manual sets out how a trade on its electronic spot FX screen, Câmbio Pronto Eletrônico, becomes a registered FX contract at the central bank, with every step carried by messages of the Brazilian Payments System (Sistema de Pagamentos Brasileiro, SPB).

| Step | What happens | Message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agents (banks or authorized brokers) send buy and sell orders to the electronic system, directly or through an intermediary | orders |
| 2 | The closed trade is registered at the FX clearinghouse | |
| 3 | The clearinghouse formalizes the operation at the BCB | CAM0053 |
| 4 | The BCB confirms the registration to the clearinghouse | CAM0053R1 |
| 5 | The clearinghouse asks the agents to formalize the FX contract at the BCB | BMC0015 |
| 6 | The agents formalize the FX contract at the BCB | CAM0054 |
| 7 | The BCB confirms the formalization | CAM0054R1/R2 |
| 8 | The clearinghouse tells the agents the process is complete | BMC0005 |

Source: [B3, Manual de Operações da Câmara de Câmbio B3, section 9.1, 2 May 2024](https://b3.com.br/data/files/5B/03/FD/26/F68A09105FE89209AC094EA8/Manual%20de%20Operacoes%20da%20Camara%20de%20Cambio%20B3.pdf). If an agent cannot formalize the contract or fails a margin call, the manual lets the clearinghouse substitute Banco B3 for it at the defaulter's cost; an agent that used an intermediary remains responsible for every later step ([B3, manual, section 9.1](https://b3.com.br/data/files/5B/03/FD/26/F68A09105FE89209AC094EA8/Manual%20de%20Operacoes%20da%20Camara%20de%20Cambio%20B3.pdf)).

## What to remember

- Interdealer brokers let banks trade with one another anonymously; they find the other side rather than taking it, first by voice and since the late 1990s on screens.
- ICAP bought BrokerTec in 2003 and EBS in 2006, sold its voice broking to Tullett Prebon in 2016 (now TP ICAP), and as NEX sold BrokerTec and EBS to CME Group in November 2018; Reuters' Matching passed to Thomson Reuters, Refinitiv and, in January 2021, LSEG.
- The interdealer market for on-the-run Treasuries is almost entirely electronic, with principal trading firms providing most of the volume; on 15 October 2014 the 10-year yield fell 16 basis points and came back within twelve minutes without a clear cause, and the US Treasury clearing mandate now reaches interdealer trades.
- FX became electronic with Reuters in 1992 and EBS in 1993; dealers now internalize most customer flow, but the primary venues still matter for price discovery, and about 58% of FX turnover was electronic in April 2025.
- Last look lets a liquidity provider accept or reject a trade after the click; the FX Global Code limits it to validity and price checks, asks for disclosure of symmetry and window length, and the NYDFS fined Barclays in 2015 for using it as a profit filter.
- In Brazil the Treasury's dealers trade government bonds on B3's Trademate and settle in Selic, while the dollar's price forms first in B3's futures market; why that is so is argued between academics and the central bank.
