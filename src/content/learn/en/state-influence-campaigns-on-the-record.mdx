<MethodNote />

A state that runs a covert influence campaign does not sign it. What the public knows comes from others: a rival government's report, an indictment, a parliamentary inquiry, a platform's takedown notice, a researcher's analysis, an archive opened after a regime fell, and very occasionally an admission. Each of those sources can show some things and not others, and each accusation has usually drawn a denial. This part of the [series](/learn/psychological-operations-from-the-small-lie-to-the-campaign), after [the anatomy of an influence operation](/learn/anatomy-of-an-influence-operation-abc-disarm-and-kill-chains), lays out the record for campaigns attributed to the Soviet Union, Russia, China, Iran, Israel, the United States and France, one entry at a time: who made the attribution, what evidence they published, when, what the accused party said, and the evidence status that follows. After reading, you can place a claim about a state campaign on that scale and say what the studies of its effects measured.

## How to read an attribution

Four kinds of document recur below, and they carry different weight.

**Charging documents** are allegations. A grand jury indictment states what prosecutors say they can prove at trial; an affidavit for a seizure warrant, in the words of the one filed against the Doppelganger domains in September 2024, sets out facts to establish probable cause ([Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) affidavit, 2024](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-09/doppelganger_affidavit_9.4.24.pdf)). Neither is a verdict.

**Platform takedowns** are a company's finding about its own service. Meta says that when it investigates and removes such networks "we focus on behavior rather than content, no matter who's behind them, what they post, or whether they're foreign or domestic" ([Meta, Third Quarter 2022 report](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/11/metas-adversarial-threat-report-q3-2022/)). Its attributions are graded in its own wording, typically "links to individuals associated with" an institution, which is not the same as saying the institution ordered the work.

**Researchers' assessments** come with stated confidence. Mandiant, for example, says it assessed "with high confidence" that certain accounts belonged to the campaign it calls DRAGONBRIDGE ([Mandiant, 2022](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/dragonbridge-targets-rare-earths-mining-companies)), and the researchers who examined a pro-Western network in 2022 wrote that the platforms had not shared the technical details of their investigations ([Graphika and Stanford Internet Observatory, 2022](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_stanford_internet_observatory_report_unheard_voice.pdf)).

**Official findings and admissions** sit at the far end. A parliamentary committee's finding, a court's judgment, a document in an opened archive or a confession by the person accused changes the status of a claim, though each has limits of its own, as the cases show. On this page, the label after a claim says which kind of support it has.

## The Soviet AIDS story: INFEKTION, or Denver (1983 to 1992)

**The accusation at the time.** In a report dated August 1987 and released that October, the United States (US) Department of State set out Soviet influence activities, with a chapter on what it called the Soviet Union's AIDS disinformation campaign, the story that the virus behind acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) was made in a US military laboratory ([State Department, 1987](https://sspconline.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/soviet-influence-activities-1987_0.pdf)). This was the target government's account, published during the Cold War; the [short history of propaganda](/learn/a-short-history-of-propaganda-and-psychological-warfare) follows the story's spread, including its Brazilian placements.

**The denial, then a retreat.** After Secretary of State George Shultz told Mikhail Gorbachev in October 1987 that the Soviet Union had peddled "bum dope" on AIDS, the Soviet Academy of Sciences disavowed, in Izvestia, the thesis that the virus was artificially created; the historian Thomas Boghardt notes that Moscow had still never publicly acknowledged authorship of the campaign ([Boghardt, 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)).

**The admission.** In 1992, Boghardt writes, Yevgeny Primakov, director of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR), <Claim status="reported" by="by Boghardt, citing the Boston Globe and Izvestiya">"confirmed the KGB's participation"</Claim> ([Boghardt, 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)); National Public Radio (NPR) summarized in 2018 that "Russia finally acknowledged, in 1992, a KGB disinformation campaign about AIDS" ([NPR, 2018](https://www.npr.org/2018/08/22/640883503/long-before-facebook-the-kgb-spread-fake-news-about-aids)). The newspapers of 1992 that carried Primakov's words were not read for this part.

**The archives.** After the Eastern bloc fell, historians found the paperwork. In the files of Bulgaria's communist-era State Security, Christopher Nehring found a telegram of 7 September 1985 from the Soviet Committee for State Security (KGB) asking its Bulgarian counterparts to help spread the thesis; a 1986 plan by the active-measures division of East Germany's foreign intelligence (Hauptverwaltung Aufklärung, HVA) promising a study to prove that AIDS was "a product of the USA's bioweapons research"; and a KGB telegram of 1987 saying that a complex of measures "has been carried out since 1985 in cooperation with the [East] German and to some extent the Czech colleagues" ([Selvage and Nehring, 2019](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/operation-denver-kgb-and-stasi-disinformation-regarding-aids)). The same 1986 plan named the aims as strengthening "anti-American sentiments in the world" and sparking "domestic political controversies in the USA", and recorded the code name the HVA had registered on 17 July 1986: Denver.

**What remains open.** <Claim status="established" by="by Bulgarian and East German archival documents and the 1992 Russian acknowledgement">The KGB's leading role</Claim> rests on documents and on an acknowledgement by its successor. The East German Ministry for State Security's share, its relation to the biologist Jakob Segal and the code name itself (INFEKTION in the earlier literature, Denver in the files) are <Claim status="disputed">contested among historians</Claim>, as the [history part](/learn/a-short-history-of-propaganda-and-psychological-warfare) sets out; Douglas Selvage and Christopher Nehring add that 90 percent of the HVA's files were destroyed or lost in 1989 and 1990 ([Selvage and Nehring, 2019](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/operation-denver-kgb-and-stasi-disinformation-regarding-aids)). The story outlived the admission: the same authors found Segal's Fort Detrick thesis cited by the French edition of Sputnik in February 2018.

## The Internet Research Agency (2014 to 2023)

**The indictment.** On 16 February 2018 a federal grand jury in Washington charged the Internet Research Agency (IRA) of Saint Petersburg, two companies called Concord Management and Consulting and Concord Catering, the businessman Yevgeniy Prigozhin and twelve other Russian nationals ([United States v. Internet Research Agency, indictment, 2018](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J-PURL-gpo89499/pdf/GOVPUB-J-PURL-gpo89499.pdf)). The indictment alleges that the organization <Claim status="alleged" by="by the grand jury indictment">"had a strategic goal to sow discord in the U.S. political system, including the 2016 U.S. presidential election"</Claim>, that it employed hundreds of people, and that by September 2016 its monthly budget for a Concord-funded effort called "Project Lakhta" exceeded 73 million roubles, over 1,250,000 US dollars.

**The Special Counsel.** The report of Special Counsel Robert Mueller (March 2019) opens: "The Russian government interfered in the 2016 presidential election in sweeping and systematic fashion" ([Special Counsel, Volume I](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco/file/1373816/dl)). It describes a campaign that <Claim status="attributed" by="by the Special Counsel">"evolved from a generalized program designed in 2014 and 2015 to undermine the U.S. electoral system, to a targeted operation that by early 2016 favored candidate Trump and disparaged candidate Clinton"</Claim>, cites a Facebook representative's testimony that 470 IRA accounts made 80,000 posts between January 2015 and August 2017 and that the IRA reached as many as 126 million people, and Twitter's announcement of 3,814 IRA accounts and about 1.4 million people notified. It adds that "The investigation did not identify evidence that any U.S. persons conspired or coordinated with the IRA". Large parts of the IRA section are redacted as "Harm to Ongoing Matter".

**The Senate committee.** Volume 2 of the US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) report, on Russia's use of social media (2019), found that the Russian government "tasked and supported" the IRA's interference and that Prigozhin's ties to the Kremlin, "Despite Moscow's denials", pointed to significant Kremlin support ([SSCI, Volume 2](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Report_Volume2.pdf)). It found that no group of Americans was targeted more than African Americans, and that on Instagram 133 IRA accounts published over 116,000 posts and drew 187 million engagements, against 76 million on Facebook. It also recorded its dependence on the companies: "the full scope of this activity remains unknown to the Committee, the social media companies, and the broader U.S. Government".

**The replies.** The day the indictment was unsealed, Prigozhin told the RIA Novosti agency, as The Moscow Times translated it, "The Americans are really impressionable people, they see what they want to see"; Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova asked "Thirteen people intervened in the elections in the U.S.?!"; and Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said "Until we see facts, everything else is just empty talk" ([The Moscow Times, 2018](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2018/02/19/russia-reacts-charges-election-meddling-mueller-investigation-a60553)). When the Special Counsel's report appeared, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said "The report contains no proven evidence that Russia allegedly interfered in the election process in the United States" and "As before, we do not accept such charges" ([TASS, 2019](https://tass.com/politics/1054567)).

**The case that was never tried.** Concord Management and Consulting contested the charges in court. In March 2020, weeks before the trial was due to begin, prosecutors moved to dismiss the case against the two Concord companies, saying "A trial of this case risks publicizing sensitive law enforcement information" and that Concord had been "eager and aggressive in using the judicial system to gather information about how the United States detects and prevents foreign election interference" ([NPR, 2020](https://www.npr.org/2020/03/17/817215010/citing-security-feds-drop-case-against-russians-linked-to-election-interference)). The allegations against the Concord companies were therefore never tested before a jury.

**The admission.** On 7 November 2022, in comments posted by the press service of his catering company, Prigozhin said: "We have interfered, we are interfering and we will continue to interfere", "carefully, accurately, surgically and in our own way, as we know how to do" ([Al Jazeera, 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/11/7/wagner-group-founder-admits-to-us-election-interference); [NPR, 2022](https://www.npr.org/2022/11/07/1134878028/yevgeny-prigozhin-russia-election-interference-putin)). In February 2023 he told journalists from Der Spiegel, as The Moscow Times reported, "I was never just a financier of the Internet Research Agency. I invented it, I created it, I ran it for a long time", adding that he had set it up to protect the Russian information space from Western propaganda ([The Moscow Times, 2023](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2023/02/14/wagner-founder-prigozhin-admits-he-was-behind-russias-infamous-troll-farms-a80228)). The admission concerns his own role; it is not a statement by the Russian state, whose spokesmen had denied the charges.

## Spamouflage, also called DRAGONBRIDGE (2019 to 2024)

**The first reports.** Graphika says it uncovered the network in September 2019, and in 2020 it reported that Twitter attributed a dataset of it to operators linked to Chinese state actors, "although Graphika has not to date been able to establish a connection between Spamouflage Dragon and government entities" ([Graphika, 2020](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_report_spamouflage_dragon_goes_to_america.pdf)). Mandiant says it has reported since June 2019 on a pro-People's Republic of China (PRC) campaign of thousands of inauthentic accounts it calls DRAGONBRIDGE; in 2022 it described accounts posing as Texan residents protesting a rare-earths plant, and judged that the activity "does not appear to have been particularly effective" ([Mandiant, 2022](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/dragonbridge-targets-rare-earths-mining-companies)).

**The criminal complaint.** On 17 April 2023 the DOJ announced charges against officers of China's Ministry of Public Security (MPS) assigned to a "912 Special Project Working Group"; <Claim status="alleged" by="by US prosecutors">"The complaint alleges how members of the Group created thousands of fake online personas on social media sites"</Claim> ([DOJ press release, print hosted by Poder360, 2023](https://static.poder360.com.br/2023/04/DOJ-policiais-china-presos.pdf)). The next day, asked about the arrests in the same set of cases, foreign ministry spokesperson Wang Wenbin said "China firmly opposes the US's slanders and smears, its political manipulation" and the "blatant prosecution of Chinese law enforcement and cyber administration officials" ([PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 18 April 2023](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/nanhai/eng/fyrbt_1/202304/t20230418_11061179.htm)).

**The platform finding.** In August 2023 Meta removed 7,704 Facebook accounts, 954 Pages, 15 Groups and 15 Instagram accounts and reported that <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">"our investigation found links to individuals associated with Chinese law enforcement"</Claim> ([Meta, Second Quarter 2023 report](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). Asked by Reuters about it, Wang said "I'm not aware of what you mentioned", that "Some people and institutions have launched one 'rumor campaign' after another against China", and that the company should "avoid applying double standards" ([Consulate-General of the PRC in Adelaide, transcript of 30 August 2023](https://adelaide.china-consulate.gov.cn/eng/wjbfyrth/202308/t20230830_11135713.htm)).

**The volume and the reach.** Google's Threat Analysis Group (TAG) calls DRAGONBRIDGE the most prolific influence actor it tracks: it disrupted over 65,000 instances in 2023 and over 175,000 in the network's lifetime, and of the 57,000 YouTube channels disabled in 2023, 80% had no subscribers ([Google TAG, 26 June 2024](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/google-disrupted-dragonbridge-activity-q1-2024/)). When Graphika reported in September 2024 that accounts in the network were posing as American voters, Chinese embassy spokesperson Liu Pengyu rejected its findings as full of "prejudice and malicious speculation" and said that "China has no intention and will not interfere" in the election ([Associated Press, via the Anchorage Daily News, 2024](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/09/03/china-linked-spamouflage-network-mimics-americans-online-to-sway-us-political-debate/)).

**Status.** <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta and, in part, the FBI">A link to Chinese law enforcement</Claim>; the role of the MPS group is <Claim status="alleged" by="by US prosecutors">alleged in a criminal complaint</Claim>; China denies interfering.

## The same record, other states

The platforms' quarterly reports and official statements name many more states than the four above. Four cases from governments allied with the United States, and from the United States itself, show that the record is not confined to its adversaries.

### Iran (2024)

In early August 2024 Microsoft reported that groups connected with the Iranian government had launched covert news sites aimed at opposite ends of the US electorate, one called Nio Thinker for left-leaning readers and another, Savannah Time, posing as local conservative news, and that a group connected with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) had sent a spear-phishing email to a high-ranking campaign official ([Microsoft, 2024](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2024/08/08/iran-targeting-2024-us-election/)). On 16 August OpenAI said it had banned accounts of the same operation, which Microsoft calls Storm-2035, and that most of its social media posts drew few or no likes, shares or comments ([Voice of America, 2024](https://www.voanews.com/amp/openai-blocks-iranian-group-from-chatgpt-says-it-targeted-us-election/7745899.html)). On 19 August the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), the FBI and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) said that the intelligence community <Claim status="attributed" by="by ODNI, the FBI and CISA">attributes to Iran the reported compromise of the Trump campaign</Claim> and that "Iran seeks to stoke discord and undermine confidence in our democratic institutions" ([joint statement, CISA bulletin, 2024](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCISA/bulletins/3afd156)). The agencies did not say how they reached the conclusion, the Associated Press noted ([Associated Press, via the Anchorage Daily News, 2024](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/08/19/us-intelligence-officials-say-iran-is-to-blame-for-hacks-targeting-trump-biden-harris-campaigns/)). Iran's mission to the United Nations denied the hacks, said Iran had neither the motive nor the intention to interfere and challenged the United States to provide evidence; earlier it had told the agency that "The U.S. presidential election is an internal matter in which Iran does not interfere" ([PBS NewsHour, from the Associated Press, 2024](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/iran-ramping-up-cyber-activity-apparently-meant-to-influence-u-s-election-microsoft-says)).

### Israel (2024)

On 29 and 30 May 2024 Meta and OpenAI reported, independently, operations run by STOIC, a political marketing and business intelligence firm in Tel Aviv. Meta removed 510 Facebook accounts, 11 Pages, one Group and 32 Instagram accounts, banned the firm and sent it a cease-and-desist letter; the accounts posed as locals, "including as Jewish students, African Americans and 'concerned' citizens", and drew little authentic engagement, Meta wrote, as quoted by NBC News ([NBC News, 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/meta-openai-say-disrupted-israeli-companys-influence-campaign-rcna154774)). OpenAI described STOIC as a commercial company in Israel generating content about the Gaza conflict and nicknamed the operation "Zero Zeno" for its low engagement ([OpenAI, 2024](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/kftzwdyauwt9/5IMxzTmUclSOAcWUXbkVrK/3cfab518e6b10789ab8843bcca18b633/Threat_Intel_Report.pdf)). On 5 June The New York Times reported, as NPR summarized it, <Claim status="reported" by="by The New York Times">that STOIC was being paid $2 million by Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs to influence members of the US Congress</Claim>; the same day the Israeli watchdog FakeReporter tied five websites to the firm ([NPR, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/06/05/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign-gaza)). A ministry spokesman told JNS that the ministry "does not engage in disinformation campaigns" and that "Any claim about a connection between the firm and the Stoic company is without foundation" ([The Jewish Chronicle, 2024](https://www.thejc.com/news/israel/israeli-minister-denies-reports-the-government-ran-covert-influence-campaign-on-us-politicians-uy5gj4fq)); Minister Amichai Chikli accused FakeReporter of "slander against IDF soldiers and the State of Israel", and STOIC did not respond to NPR. <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta and OpenAI">The firm's operation</Claim> is a platform attribution; <Claim status="denied" by="by Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs">the ministry's commissioning of it</Claim> rests on the newspaper's sources and documents.

### The United States (2022 and 2024)

On 24 August 2022 Graphika and the Stanford Internet Observatory published an analysis of datasets that Twitter and Meta had removed, which they called "the most extensive case of covert pro-Western IO on social media to be reviewed and analyzed by open-source researchers to date"; the platforms gave the presumptive countries of origin as the United States and Great Britain (Twitter) and the United States (Meta), without naming any organization ([Graphika and Stanford Internet Observatory, 2022](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_stanford_internet_observatory_report_unheard_voice.pdf)). In November Meta added that <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">"our investigation found links to individuals associated with the US military"</Claim> ([Meta, Third Quarter 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/11/metas-adversarial-threat-report-q3-2022/)). The Washington Post had reported in September that the Pentagon ordered an audit of its clandestine information warfare; press secretary Patrick Ryder said its information operations "support our national security priorities" and "We are committed to enforcing those safeguards" ([Stars and Stripes, from The Washington Post, 2022](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2022-09-19/pentagon-clandestine-psychological-operations-7388658.html)). In June 2024 Reuters reported a military campaign in 2020 and 2021 against China's Sinovac vaccine, identifying at least 300 accounts on X aimed at the Philippines ([Al Jazeera, from Reuters, 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/6/14/pentagon-ran-secret-anti-vax-campaign-to-undermine-china-during-pandemic)); a Pentagon document sent to Manila and reviewed by Reuters said <Claim status="reported" by="by Reuters, from a document it reviewed">"It is true that the (Department of Defense) did message Philippines audiences questioning the safety and efficacy of Sinovac"</Claim> ([Interaksyon, from Reuters, 2024](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/politics-issues/2024/07/29/280555/u-s-told-philippines-it-made-missteps-in-secret-anti-vax-propaganda-effort/)).

### France (2020)

On 15 December 2020 Meta announced the removal of three networks aimed at Africa: two linked to people associated with past IRA activity and with Prigozhin's operations, and one in which <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">"our investigation found links to individuals associated with French military"</Claim>, posting about France's policies in Francophone Africa and the security situation in the Central African Republic and Mali ([Meta, 2020](https://about.fb.com/news/2020/12/removing-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-france-russia/)). France's defence ministry told Agence France-Presse (AFP) it was not surprised by the conclusions but "we are not at this stage in a position to attribute any responsibility" ([The Moscow Times, from AFP, 2020](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2020/12/16/troll-crackdown-exposes-france-russia-rivalry-in-africa-a72382)); Prigozhin told Reuters that he considered Facebook a tool of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) ([Al Jazeera, 2020](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/12/15/french-and-russian-trolls-fight-it-out-in-central-africa-election)).

## What attribution can and cannot show

**It can identify the operators without proving who gave the order.** Meta's standard phrase, "links to individuals associated with", appears in the Chinese, US and French cases above ([Meta, 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/11/metas-adversarial-threat-report-q3-2022/); [Meta, 2020](https://about.fb.com/news/2020/12/removing-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-france-russia/)). It ties the accounts to people; it does not by itself show that their institution commissioned the work.

**The evidence is often not public.** Graphika and the Stanford Internet Observatory noted that Twitter and Meta "did not share the technical details of their investigations" ([2022](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_stanford_internet_observatory_report_unheard_voice.pdf)); the authors of the main IRA exposure study wrote that they relied on Twitter's identification of the accounts and could not validate the process, "which has not been detailed publicly by Twitter" ([Eady et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9)); and the US agencies' Iranian attribution came without stated method ([Associated Press, 2024](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/08/19/us-intelligence-officials-say-iran-is-to-blame-for-hacks-targeting-trump-biden-harris-campaigns/)).

**Legal process tests claims, when it happens.** Indictments and affidavits are allegations; the case against the Concord companies ended before trial ([NPR, 2020](https://www.npr.org/2020/03/17/817215010/citing-security-feds-drop-case-against-russians-linked-to-election-interference)), and defendants abroad are rarely in court.

**Even official findings carry stated limits.** The SSCI, with access unavailable to the public, wrote that the full scope of the activity "remains unknown" even to the companies ([SSCI, Volume 2](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Report_Volume2.pdf)).

**Admissions are rare and late.** The Soviet AIDS campaign was acknowledged five years after it was denied ([Boghardt, 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)), and Prigozhin spoke more than four years after his indictment ([NPR, 2022](https://www.npr.org/2022/11/07/1134878028/yevgeny-prigozhin-russia-election-interference-putin)). Replies to attributions, by contrast, came quickly: denials from Moscow, Beijing, Tehran and the Israeli ministry, and statements that neither confirmed nor denied from the Pentagon and the French defence ministry, as set out above. Some denials were later contradicted; others still stand against the attribution.

**Commercial intermediaries blur the picture.** Meta warned in 2021 that firms selling influence operations "could also be used by sophisticated actors to hide their involvement behind private firms, making attribution more challenging" ([Meta, The State of Influence Operations 2017-2020](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/IO-Threat-Report-May-20-2021.pdf)); the [next part, on influence for hire](/learn/influence-for-hire-the-firms-that-sell-influence-operations), follows those firms.

**Exposure can itself be used.** Meta describes "perception hacking", in which operators try "fostering the perception that they are everywhere, playing on people's fear of widespread deception itself", citing a 2018 IRA website that claimed thousands of fake accounts when Meta found only a small network ([Meta, 2021](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/IO-Threat-Report-May-20-2021.pdf)). A 2025 study for Sweden's Psychological Defence Agency, based on leaked SDA documents, assessed with medium confidence that <Claim status="reported" by="by its authors">"Discourse in Western target countries around the exposure of Doppelgänger contributed to SDA receiving more funding"</Claim> ([Pamment and Tsurtsumia, 2025](https://mpf.se/download/18.7cffbee41969f6d83e115221/1747230166207/Beyond%20Operation%20Doppelganger.pdf)).

## What the effect studies measured

**The IRA on Twitter, 2016.** Gregory Eady and five co-authors linked a three-wave survey of 1,496 US respondents, run by YouGov from April to October 2016, to the timelines of the accounts each respondent followed, about 1.2 billion posts ([Eady et al., Nature Communications, 9 January 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9)). Their abstract reports that "only 1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures", that exposure was concentrated among users who strongly identified as Republicans, that it was "eclipsed by content from domestic news media and politicians", and: <Claim status="reported" by="by the study's authors (peer-reviewed)">"we find no evidence of a meaningful relationship between exposure to the Russian foreign influence campaign and changes in attitudes, polarization, or voting behavior"</Claim>. In the last month of the campaign, respondents saw about 4 posts a day from Russian accounts against 106 from national news media and 35 from politicians.

**What the authors say it does not show.** The design is observational, "a near best-case observational design", so the estimated relationships "cannot be confidently said to be causal"; the study is limited to Twitter and to posts, and "does not speak to the impact of similar campaigns on other social media platforms, nor to the possibility of foreign election interference via other channels, such as hacking" ([Eady et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9)). They add that such campaigns may have second-order effects and that "Russia's foreign influence campaign on social media may have had its largest effects by convincing Americans that its campaign was successful". The co-author Joshua Tucker stressed on the Tech Policy Press podcast that "We did not have a commensurate data set from Facebook", so "we don't know what the impacts were on Facebook" ([Tech Policy Press, 2023](https://techpolicy.press/examining-the-impact-of-internet-research-agency-tweets-in-the-2016-u-s-election)).

**An earlier study, the same direction.** Christopher Bail and colleagues followed 1,239 Republican and Democratic Twitter users in late 2017 and found "no evidence that interaction with IRA accounts substantially impacted 6 distinctive measures of political attitudes and behaviors over a 1-mo period", while noting their "inability to determine whether IRA accounts influenced the 2016 presidential election" ([Bail et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), 2020, PubMed abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=31767743&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)).

**The opposing argument.** Kathleen Hall Jamieson's Cyberwar (Oxford University Press, 2018) argues from intelligence reports, indictments, media coverage and communication research that Russian trolls and hackers shaped the agenda of the 2016 campaign; reviewing it in Nature, Alexander Klimburg read its implicit conclusion as <Claim status="disputed">that Russian interference "very probably" had a major role in the result</Claim> ([Klimburg, Nature, 2018](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-018-06942-9/d41586-018-06942-9.pdf)). Tucker described Jamieson's work on hacking and leaking as outside what his study could test ([Tech Policy Press, 2023](https://techpolicy.press/examining-the-impact-of-internet-research-agency-tweets-in-the-2016-u-s-election)). The two lines of work measure different things: individual attitudes on one platform, and the agenda of news coverage.

**What the platforms measured.** In the pro-Western network, the average tweet received 0.49 likes and 0.02 retweets, and only 19% of the covert assets had more than 1,000 followers ([Graphika and Stanford Internet Observatory, 2022](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_stanford_internet_observatory_report_unheard_voice.pdf)); DRAGONBRIDGE's engagement was "almost entirely inauthentic, coming from other DRAGONBRIDGE accounts" ([Google TAG, 2024](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/google-disrupted-dragonbridge-activity-q1-2024/)); and Meta wrote of Spamouflage that it "consistently struggled to reach beyond its own (fake) echo chamber" ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). These are counts of engagement on one service, not measures of belief, and the companies that publish them are also the ones whose enforcement they reflect.

## What to remember

- An indictment or an affidavit is an allegation; a platform takedown is a company's finding about its own service; an archive document, a court finding or an admission changes a claim's status.
- The KGB's leading role in the AIDS story is documented in Bulgarian and East German files and was acknowledged by Russia in 1992; the Stasi's share and the code name remain disputed.
- The IRA was indicted in 2018 and described by the Special Counsel and the Senate committee; the case against its funders' companies was dropped before trial in 2020, and Prigozhin said in 2022 and 2023 that he had interfered and had founded the agency.
- Doppelganger was attributed to Russian companies by Meta, the EU and the US Treasury, and Germany assigned a 2024 wave of fake accounts to it; the Kremlin calls the accusations nonsense.
- Spamouflage was linked by Meta to Chinese law enforcement and an MPS group was charged in the United States; China denies interfering.
- Platforms and governments have also attributed campaigns to Iran, to an Israeli firm reportedly paid by a ministry that denies it, to individuals associated with the US military and to individuals associated with the French military.
- Measured effects on individual attitudes, in the studies of the IRA on Twitter, were small or undetectable; the authors say this does not cover other platforms, hacking or second-order effects.
