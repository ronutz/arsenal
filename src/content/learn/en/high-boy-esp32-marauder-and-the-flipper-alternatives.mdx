The previous part, [The Flipper Zero: sub-GHz, RFID, NFC, infrared and the panic](/learn/the-flipper-zero-sub-ghz-rfid-nfc-infrared-and-the-panic), ended with a device that reads radio remotes, radio-frequency identification (RFID) badges and near-field communication (NFC) cards, and that Brazil's regulator refused to homologate in 2023. This part follows what grew around it. In April 2025 a Brazilian team opened a project page whose title calls its device "The Brazilian Answer to the Flipper Zero" and explained that "in some countries especially in Brazil the Flipper has become restricted" ([High Code, High Boy, Hackaday.io, created 15 April 2025](https://hackaday.io/project/202872)). Most of the alternatives, Brazilian or not, run on the same family of cheap Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chips from a Shanghai company. After reading, you will be able to explain why such radios multiplied, trace the main ESP32 projects (Marauder, Pwnagotchi, Bruce and their relatives) from their own repositories, follow the High Boy from its first board to its crowdfunding and its changing specification, say who the Brazilian hardware hacker Julio Della Flora is and what he made, describe what Anatel would require before a Brazilian device of this kind could be sold, and name what defenders should change because these tools exist. ## Why cheap radios multiplied

**The chip.** Espressif Systems was founded in April 2008 with its headquarters in Shanghai's Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park. Its timeline, as the company publishes it ([Espressif, Milestones](https://www.espressif.com/en/company/about-us/milestones)):

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Apr 2008 | Espressif Systems founded in Shanghai |
| Jun 2013 | First product, the ESP8089 Wi-Fi system on chip (SoC) for tablets and set-top boxes |
| May 2014 | ESP8266EX, its first Internet of Things (IoT) SoC, "a low-power, highly-integrated Wi-Fi solution that requires a minimum of 7 external components" |
| May 2016 | Named a Gartner "Cool Vendor", for Wi-Fi chips "that have enabled the creation of postage-stamp-sized wireless computers costing less than $3" |
| Sep 2016 | ESP32, a 32-bit microcontroller combining Wi-Fi and Bluetooth |

| 22 Jul 2019 | Initial public offering on the Sci-Tech Innovation Board (STAR) of the Shanghai Stock Exchange |
| Apr 2020 | Research and development centre established in Brazil |
| Dec 2020 | ESP32-S3, a dual-core Xtensa LX7 microcontroller with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) 5.0 |
| Jun 2022 | ESP32-C5 announced, "the industry’s first RISC-V SoC solution that supports 2.4 and 5 GHz dual-band Wi-Fi 6" |
| Jan 2023 | ESP32-P4, a high-performance microcontroller with many input and output interfaces and security features |
| Sep 2023 | Cumulative shipments pass one billion since the ESP8266 |
| Apr 2024 | Majority stake in M5Stack |

The founder and chief executive, Teo Swee Ann, said when the billion was announced that "we sold almost 200 million chips in the past year alone" ([Espressif, "Espressif Leads the IoT Chip Market with Over 1 Billion Shipments Worldwide", September 2023](https://www.espressif.com/en/news/1_Billion_Chip_Sales)); in November 2026 the company's own page claims "1.5 Billion+ Global IoT SoC Shipments" ([Espressif, About Espressif](https://www.espressif.com/en/company/about-espressif)). These are the company's figures. A chip sold in such volumes, with an open software kit and a large hobby community, is the base layer of everything in this part.

**The radio module.** For the sub-GHz bands used by gate remotes, the alternatives reach for the same part as the Flipper: Texas Instruments' CC1101, which the manufacturer lists as active, covering 300 to 348, 387 to 464 and 779 to 928 MHz ([Texas Instruments, CC1101](https://www.ti.com/product/CC1101)).

**The kit makers.** M5Stack, "Founded in China in 2017", received investment from Espressif in 2018, released the M5StickC in 2019 and was acquired by Espressif in 2024; it says it has sold more than three million products ([M5Stack corporate overview, PDF of July 2025, published on Espressif's server](https://dl.espressif.com/public/10-00-Allen-Chao-M5Stack-Corporate-Overview.pdf)). Espressif announced its majority stake on 22 April 2024, describing M5Stack's ecosystem as "built around its flagship main control module, powered by Espressif’s all ESP32 series of chips" ([Espressif, "Espressif Acquires Majority Stake in M5Stack, Enhancing AIoT Ecosystem", 22 April 2024](https://www.espressif.com/en/news/Espressif_Acquires_M5Stack)). Its pocket M5StickC PLUS2, built on the ESP32-PICO-V3-02 with an infrared emitter, a 1.14-inch screen and a 200 mAh battery, was listed at US$19.95 and marked end of life on 16 November 2026 ([M5Stack store, M5StickC PLUS2](https://shop.m5stack.com/products/m5stickc-plus2-esp32-mini-iot-development-kit)). LILYGO's T-Embed CC1101 pairs an ESP32-S3 with a CC1101, 13.56 MHz RFID, an infrared transmitter and receiver and a 1.9-inch colour screen for US$49.38; its product page describes the radio with the same sentence Flipper Devices uses for its own, "the maximum range is 50 meters" ([LILYGO, T-Embed CC1101](https://lilygo.cc/products/t-embed-cc1101); [Flipper Docs, Sub-GHz](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz)).

**The Brazilian price of a dollar device.** Julio Della Flora, introduced below, wrote in his free gear guide of 2024 that in Brazil "we have a 92% tax on imported products" and that "It's not uncommon for certain devices to cost as much as a car to import" ([Julio Della Flora, The Ultimate Hardware Hacking Gear Guide, PDF dated 19 July 2024](https://github.com/jcldf/ultimate-hardware-hacking-gear-guide-/releases/download/zine/hackers.toolbox.1-2.pdf)). That is his account; the import rules themselves are read in a later part of this series, on owning hacking gear legally. Combined with a refused homologation for the best-known product, it explains why Brazilian makers looked at boards they could buy or build.

## The Wi-Fi line: Deauther, Marauder and Pwnagotchi

**ESP8266 Deauther.** The oldest of the lineage runs on the 2014 chip. Its README says the firmware tests "802.11 networks using an ESP8266", that "The deauthentication attack is the main feature, which can be used to disconnect devices from their WiFi network", and that "Although this denial-of-service attack is nothing new, a lot of devices are still vulnerable to it. Luckily this is slowly changing with more WiFi 6 enabled devices being used"; its disclaimer reads "Use it only against your own networks and devices!" ([SpacehuhnTech, esp8266_deauther, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SpacehuhnTech/esp8266_deauther/v2/README.md)). The 802.11 frames involved, and the defence, are in [The WiFi Pineapple: rogue access points and the Karma attack](/learn/the-wifi-pineapple-rogue-access-points-and-the-karma-attack).

**ESP32 Marauder.** "A suite of WiFi/Bluetooth offensive and defensive tools for the ESP32", released under the MIT License, copyright 2020 Just Call Me Koko ([ESP32Marauder, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/master/README.md); [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/master/LICENSE)). Its wiki groups the features into reconnaissance and wardriving with satellite positioning, Wi-Fi sniffers that scan access points and stations, capture packets and handshake material and detect deauthentication, Wi-Fi attacks described as "active transmission tools for authorized testing", Bluetooth sniffers listed as "AirTag/Find My, Flipper, skimmer, Flock, and Meta detection tools", Bluetooth advertisement generators, and a "Flipper Zero companion" mode for compatible boards ([ESP32 Marauder wiki, Applications; read again 17 November 2026 in a browser](https://github.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/wiki/applications)). Its home page tells users to run it "only on systems and radio environments you own or are explicitly authorized to assess" ([ESP32 Marauder wiki, Home](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/Home.md)). Official hardware went through versions 4, 6, 6.1, 7 and 8 and a Mini, with on-board satellite positioning from v6.1, plus a Flipper backpack board that draws its battery from the Flipper ([ESP32 Marauder wiki, Official Marauder hardware, read through an automated extract](https://github.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/wiki/marauder-versions)); the latest release on 16 November 2026 was v1.20.0, of 28 October 2026 ([ESP32Marauder, releases; read again 17 November 2026 in a browser](https://github.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/releases)).

The README contains a lesson in how radio compliance works for such boards. It lists the identifier issued by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) for the radio module fitted to each official board (an ESP32-C5-WROOM-U module for v8 and the C5 Wardriver, an ESP32-WROOM-32U module for v4 to v7) and warns: "This is the module identifier, not a claim that every carrier-board or enclosure configuration has a separate end-product grant" ([ESP32Marauder, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/master/README.md)). A certified module inside a device does not by itself certify the device.

**Pwnagotchi.** On 19 October 2019 the researcher known as evilsocket presented version 1.0.0 of an artificial intelligence (AI) for Wi-Fi hacking as "the story of a summer project that started out of boredom" ([evilsocket, "Weaponizing and Gamifying AI for WiFi Hacking: Presenting Pwnagotchi 1.0.0", 19 October 2019](https://www.evilsocket.net/2019/10/19/Weaponizing-and-Gamifying-AI-for-WiFi-Hacking-Presenting-Pwnagotchi-1-0-0/)). It is not an ESP32 project: it runs on a Raspberry Pi, and its README describes an agent trained with the advantage actor-critic (A2C) method that drives the bettercap tool and "learns from its surrounding WiFi environment" to collect as much crackable Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA) key material as it can, passively or through authentication and association attacks, stored as packet captures of full and half handshakes and of the identifiers known as PMKIDs; nearby units talk "using a parasite protocol I've built on top of the existing dot11 standard" and learn to divide the channels between them ([evilsocket/pwnagotchi, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evilsocket/pwnagotchi/master/README.md)). The maintained fork supports the Raspberry Pi Zero W in 32 bits and the Zero 2 W and Raspberry Pi 3, 4 and 5 in 64 bits ([jayofelony/pwnagotchi, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jayofelony/pwnagotchi/master/README.md)). Pwnagotchi also gave a hacking tool a character: its states are "represented on the display as different moods, expressions and sentences" ([Pwnagotchi, Introduction](https://pwnagotchi.ai/intro/)). Flipper Devices, as quoted by Ars Technica, wrote that "Flipper was inspired by pwnagotchi project" ([Ars Technica, 2 November 2023](https://arstechnica.com/security/2023/11/flipper-zero-gadget-that-doses-iphones-takes-once-esoteric-attacks-mainstream/)).

## The multitool line: NEMO, Evil-M5, Bruce and CapibaraZero

When M5Stack's sticks and the Cardputer reached hobbyists, firmware appeared that turned them into Flipper-like multitools. The projects credit each other, which makes the lineage traceable from their own pages:

| Project | First target | How it describes itself | Licence and status, 16 November 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| M5Stick-NEMO (n0xa) | M5StickC and Cardputer | "Firmware for high-tech pranks and digital self-defense on M5Stack ESP32 Devices"; it "stands in contrast to another high-tech gadget that's associated with certain sea-dwelling creatures" | active ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n0xa/m5stick-nemo/main/README.md)) |
| Evil-M5Project (7h30th3r0n3) | M5Cardputer and M5Stack/ESP32 family | "a pocket red-team lab: 90+ offensive and defensive modules for WiFi, Bluetooth, NFC, Sub-GHz, LoRa and full network attacks" | active ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/7h30th3r0n3/Evil-M5Project/main/README.md)) |
| Bruce (pr3y and contributors) | M5Stack, LILYGO, Elecrow and others | "a versatile ESP32 firmware packed with offensive-security tools, built to make Red Team operations fast and portable" | GNU Affero General Public License (AGPL) 3.0; hardware under the permissive CERN Open Hardware Licence (CERN-OHL-P-2.0); release v1.17 announced ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pr3y/Bruce/main/README.md); [bruce.computer](https://bruce.computer/)) |
| CapibaraZero | ESP32-S3 boards | "CapibaraZero aim to be a cheap alternative to FlipperZero™" | GNU General Public License (GPL) 3.0; organization archived on 28 August 2025; README: "THIS PROJECT IS DEPRECATED, SWITCH TO BRUCE FW" ([fw, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CapibaraZero/fw/main/README.md); [GitHub organization; read again 17 November 2026 in a browser](https://github.com/CapibaraZero)) |

Bruce's README names its ancestors: "ESP32Marauder by @justcallmekoko", "Launcher by @bmorcelli", "Evil-M5Project by @7h30th3r0n3" and "M5Stick-Nemo by @n0xa" ([Bruce, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pr3y/Bruce/main/README.md)). Its feature list shows how far a few dollars of hardware now reaches: Wi-Fi beacon spam, targeted deauthentication and an "EvilPortal + Deauth"; BLE "Bad BLE" keyboard scripts and pairing spam for iOS, Windows, Samsung and Android; sub-GHz scanning, copying and replay with a CC1101; 125 kHz and 13.56 MHz tag reading and cloning with a PN532 module; infrared with a TV-B-Gone port; a Joint Test Action Group (JTAG) scanner and Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter (UART) terminal among its "Wire Tools"; and items labelled "Jammer Full" and "NRF24 Jammer" (same source). Its disclaimer says it "is intended for legal and authorized security testing purposes only". NEMO, at the other end, ships "Wireless Attack Detection: BLE Hunter, Deauth Hunter and PineAP Hunter modes" and a "BadUSB Hunter" that warns about suspicious Universal Serial Bus (USB) devices ([M5Stick-NEMO, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n0xa/m5stick-nemo/main/README.md)): the same cheap boards serve defence as readily as attack.

The jammer items deserve a plain statement of the law, because jamming is treated more strictly than any other function in the rules read for this series. In the United States, "the operation, marketing, sale, importation, distribution, or shipment of RF jammers is a violation of federal law", with "no exceptions for personal, commercial, educational, or vehicular use" ([FCC, Jammer Enforcement, updated September 2026](https://www.fcc.gov/general/jammer-enforcement)). In Canada, subsection 4(4) of the Radiocommunication Act prohibits "the installation, use, possession, manufacture, importation, distribution, lease, offer for sale, or sale of jammers", with ministerial exemptions for bodies such as the police ([Canada Gazette, Part I, 10 October 2026, regulatory impact analysis statement](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-10-10/html/reg2-eng.html)). In Brazil, Anatel's 2023 note, as Minha Operadora reported it, said that the criterion applied to the Flipper Zero "também é aplicado a bloqueadores de sinais de radiocomunicações", which are not homologated for personal use ([Minha Operadora, 29 March 2023](https://www.minhaoperadora.com.br/2023/03/anatel-divulga-nota-sobre-certificacao-do-flipper-zero.html)). Flipper Devices drew the same line for its official firmware in 2020; open firmware for generic boards does not.

## The High Boy

**Who makes it.** The Hackaday.io page, created on 15 April 2025, lists a team of two accounts, High Code and Emanuel Magalhães, and links to highboy.com.br and the GitHub organization HighCodeh ([High Code, High Boy, Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/202872)). The firmware's licence header reads "Copyright (c) 2025 HIGH CODE LLC" ([TentacleOS, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HighCodeh/TentacleOS/dev/README.md)). The Brazilian site Desbugados described the creators as four Brazilian students who founded High Code in the United States to ease international logistics ([Desbugados, 27 November 2025](https://www.desbugados.com.br/noticias/2025/11/27/o-canivete-suico-hacker-brasileiro-rival-do-flipper-zero-arrecada-20x-a-meta-no-kickstarter)); that is the publication's account.

**Why it exists.** The team's log of 28 April 2025 says the project started "out of pure curiosity", that "in some countries especially in Brazil the Flipper has become restricted", and that the question became "Why not make my own?"; when photos spread and people asked for a product, "the story changed" ([High Code, "Why the high boy", Hackaday.io log, 28 April 2025](https://hackaday.io/project/202872)). The project page calls it "Inspired by the spirit of the Flipper Zero but with a 100% Brazilian soul" and says it is "open source, open hardware" (same source). Its mascot is an octopus called Octobit, because "an octopus has 8 tentacles, and 8 bits make 1 byte" (same source), a theme the firmware's name, TentacleOS, carries on.

## Anatel homologation for a Brazilian device

A Brazilian maker faces the same rules that stopped the Flipper Zero, and some that a foreign maker can avoid by not selling in Brazil. As read on 16 November 2026:

1. **Certification is required to use a transmitter at all.** "É vedada a utilização de equipamentos emissores de radiofreqüência sem certificação expedida ou aceita pela Agência" ([Lei 9.472 of 16 July 1997, article 162, paragraph 2, at the Senado](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/551348/publicacao/15754971)).
2. **Low-power devices are "restricted radiation" equipment.** They have no right to protection from interference, must not cause it, "devem possuir certificação emitida ou aceita pela Anatel", and must carry, on the product or in the manual, the sentence "Este equipamento não tem direito à proteção contra interferência prejudicial e não pode causar interferência em sistemas devidamente autorizados" ([Anatel, Resolução 680, 27 June 2017, articles 3 to 5](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2017/936-resolucao-680)).
3. **The technical requirements are published.** For a "Transceptor de Radiação Restrita", Anatel's certification table lists Resolução 680, the requirements of Ato 14448 of 4 December 2017 (general conditions, specific conditions of use, spread-spectrum and digital modulation equipment) and the test procedures of Ato 237 of 7 January 2022 ([Anatel, requisitos técnicos e procedimentos de ensaio, Categoria II, updated 8 November 2024](https://sistemas.anatel.gov.br/anexar-api/publico/anexos/download/c19c8d97b00863097e7e4bc44d9c315e)). The band limits that matter for a High Boy-like device are the ones shown in the previous part: periodic operation above 70 MHz for remotes, 433–435 MHz at up to 10 mW of equivalent isotropically radiated power (EIRP), and 902–907.5 and 915–928 MHz for several classes ([Anatel, Ato 14448](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/atos-de-requisitos-tecnicos-de-gestao-do-espectro/2017/1139-ato-14448)).
4. **Who applies and how.** The manufacturer, the commercial representative of a foreign company, or "qualquer pessoa física ou jurídica, quando para uso próprio" may apply, and the applicant must be resident in Brazil or a company under Brazilian law ([Anatel, Resolução 715, 23 October 2019, article 20](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2019/1350-resolucao-715)). Conformity is assessed either by certification, in which an "Organismo de Certificação Designado" attests conformity, or by a declaration of conformity made by the applicant itself (same source, article 4, items IV and VII, and article 29). A certificate gives the right to use and sell nationwide; a declaration homologates the product only for the holder's own use (article 64). Anatel publishes a manual for the personal-use declaration ([Anatel, Manual de Orientações, Homologação de Produtos, Uso próprio/pessoal, Declaração de Conformidade, version 2.0, 1 March 2018](https://sistemas.anatel.gov.br/anexar-api/publico/anexos/download/6b6b7fbe4c10d3369afbefe22a125180)).
5. **The door that closed on the Flipper.** Article 60 orders refusal when "o produto se prestar a fins ilícitos, ou concorrer à facilitação de crime ou contravenção penal" or when it "puder prejudicar a prestação dos serviços de telecomunicações legalmente constituídos" ([Resolução 715, article 60](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2019/1350-resolucao-715)). In 2023 Anatel held that a declaration of conformity was not appropriate for the Flipper Zero, while leaving institutional homologation open to security bodies and public agencies ([Itatiaia, 30 March 2023](https://www.itatiaia.com.br/ciencia-e-tecnologia/anatel-esclarece-proibicao-do-flipper-zero/)), and its inspection superintendent described the adjustments under discussion with the maker, including lower power and sensitivity ([Minha Operadora, citing TeleSíntese, 7 April 2023](https://www.minhaoperadora.com.br/?p=113265)). A device whose firmware advertises jamming or whose marketing leads with capturing other people's signals walks into the same article.

Where does the High Boy stand? The project told Desbugados in November 2025 that it planned to handle the Brazilian regulatory side in early 2026 ([Desbugados, 27 November 2025](https://www.desbugados.com.br/noticias/2025/11/27/o-canivete-suico-hacker-brasileiro-rival-do-flipper-zero-arrecada-20x-a-meta-no-kickstarter)); a year later its site says "regulatory certification has not been completed" ([High Boy, official site](https://highboy.com.br/)). Anatel's public certificate database was not searched for this article; that check is left for the integrator. The tension is visible in the documents: a Brazilian device created because the Flipper was restricted in Brazil must clear the same article 60 that restricted it.

## What defenders should take from it

- **Assume the adversary has it.** When a radio with Wi-Fi, BLE and sub-GHz costs tens of dollars and the firmware is a browser flash away (Bruce's README points to an official web flasher), the question is not whether someone nearby can run these attacks but whether your systems survive them ([Bruce, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pr3y/Bruce/main/README.md)).
- **Wi-Fi.** Deauthentication abuses management frames, and Protected Management Frames (PMF), in the Wi-Fi Alliance's words, "extends security protections to unicast (protection from eavesdropping and forging) and multicast (protection from forging) management action frames". The Alliance says that WPA3 networks "Require use of Protected Management Frames (PMF)" and that "PMF is required for all new certified devices"; WPA3-Personal adds "increased protections from password guessing attempts", which is what Pwnagotchi's captured handshakes are for ([Wi-Fi Alliance, Security](https://www.wi-fi.org/discover-wi-fi/security)). See [WPA3](/glossary/wpa3), [802.1X](/glossary/802-1x) and [KRACK and the Wi-Fi handshake](/learn/krack-and-the-wifi-handshake). Legacy Internet of Things gear is where the Deauther's own README says the attack still lands.
- **Captive portals.** The "Evil Portal" in Marauder and Bruce, and NEMO's portal that "tries to social engineer email credentials", are phishing pages on a fake access point ([M5Stick-NEMO, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n0xa/m5stick-nemo/main/README.md)). Teach users that a [captive portal](/glossary/captive-portal) asking for an email password is a red flag.
- **Bluetooth.** The pairing-spam modes in these firmwares are the same trick that crashed iPhones on iOS 17; keep devices updated and turn off proximity pairing prompts where the platform allows, as described in the previous part.
- **Use the same tools to watch.** Marauder detects deauthentication, card skimmers, Flippers and AirTags; NEMO hunts BLE spam, deauthentication and Pineapple-style access points; a cheap board running them is a usable sensor for an office, an event or a home ([ESP32 Marauder wiki, Applications](https://github.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/wiki/applications); [M5Stick-NEMO, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n0xa/m5stick-nemo/main/README.md)).
- **Supply chain.** The counterfeit wallet described above passed the eye and failed the cryptographic check. Use the vendor's attestation where it exists and buy security hardware from the vendor or authorized channels ([Ciência Embarcada, 28 April 2026](https://cienciaembarcada.com.br/publicacoes/pesquisadores-da-high-code-descobrem-ataque-de-ledger-falsa/)).
- **Inventory what trusts radio.** Gates, badges, alarms and Internet of Things devices bought years ago are the targets these tools reach; the remedies for remotes and cards are in [Radio for hackers](/learn/radio-for-hackers-sdr-hackrf-rtl-sdr-and-rolling-codes) and [Badges and cards](/learn/badges-and-cards-rfid-nfc-the-proxmark-and-mifare).

## What to remember

- Espressif's ESP8266 (2014) and ESP32 (2016), close to 200 million chips a year by the company's own count in 2023, plus Texas Instruments' CC1101, are why Flipper-like tools now cost tens of dollars.
- The lineage is documented by the projects themselves: Deauther and Marauder for Wi-Fi, Pwnagotchi for handshake collection, NEMO and Evil-M5 on M5Stack, Bruce absorbing them, CapibaraZero archived in 2025 in favour of Bruce.
- The High Boy was made by High Code (High Code LLC in its firmware header), began because the Flipper was restricted in Brazil, raised far more than its US$7,000 goal on Kickstarter in November 2025 according to the press, and by 16 November 2026 had moved to an ESP32-P4 and ESP32-C5 design whose regulatory certification had not been completed.
- Julio Della Flora is a Londrina-based teacher, researcher and YouTuber with two CVE records and a free gear guide; in the sources read he reviewed the High Boy rather than built it.
- A Brazilian device must pass Resolução 680, Ato 14448 and Resolução 715, including the article 60 that refused the Flipper; jammers are prohibited in the United States and Canada and are not homologated for personal use in Brazil.
