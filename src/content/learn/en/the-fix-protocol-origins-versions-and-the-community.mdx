Since 2003 the specification of the Financial Information eXchange (FIX) protocol has called itself "the inter-firm messaging protocol of choice" ([FIX 4.4 specification, volume 1, preface, June 2003](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)). It is a "global, open standard" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)), and its custodian, the FIX Trading Community, says it is used by "well over 10,000 firms sending billions of messages a day" ([FIX Trading Community, Who we are](https://fixtrading.org/who-we-are/)). This part of the series opened by [Electronic trading: from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) tells where FIX came from and how it is governed. After reading, you will be able to date each version from its own release notes, explain why the session layer was separated from the business messages in 2006, say what FIX Latest, extension packs and FIX Orchestra are, describe who decides what goes into the standard, and recognize which version a given exchange speaks. The companion part, [Anatomy of a FIX message and the session layer](/learn/anatomy-of-a-fix-message-and-the-session-layer), takes a message apart byte by byte.

## Before FIX: telephones and one-off links

The FIX Trading Community describes the world before FIX as one in which firms "generally had to build custom point‑to‑point interfaces for each counterparty, maintain multiple proprietary message formats and pay vendor‑specific licensing or integration fees" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). The specification itself promised institutions and brokers "a means of reducing the clutter of unnecessary telephone calls and scraps of paper" ([FIX 4.4 specification, volume 1, preface, June 2003](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)). The work it replaced was, in the words of the 1994 preface, traders "on the phone placing routine orders and recording fills" ([FIX 2.7a specification, preface, July 1994](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/)).

## Fidelity and Salomon Brothers, 1992 to 1994

The earliest account comes from the preface signed by Christopher Morstatt, a vice president of Salomon Brothers, in July 1994: "In mid-1992 Fidelity Investments and Salomon Brothers began work on a project to link the two firms' trading systems", to automate equity indications and executions ([FIX 2.7a specification, preface](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/)). The same preface gives each side's motive. Fidelity wanted its traders to spend less time on the telephone, and Salomon's motive was "the desire to leverage its investment in technology into increased service for its clients" (same source).

The link went live in early 1993 "and has been in production each trading day since", according to the preface ([FIX 2.7a specification](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/)). The two firms decided early that other buy-side and sell-side firms should join the specification work, and throughout 1993 "a group of large financial firms met regularly to refine the specification" (same source). Participation was limited until the protocol had proved itself in production and change requests had slowed; by early 1994 most committee members had implemented it, and the result was offered to the industry "as a starting point towards the establishment of a standard for broker/institution communication" (same source).

The release notes give the first dates. Version 2.6 was published on 14 February 1994 and version 2.7 on 15 July 1994, in a memo addressed to the "FIX User Group" ([FIX 2.7 release notes, 15 July 1994](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-release-notes/)). The FIX Trading Community's archive calls 2.7 "the first version of the FIX Protocol used in production" ([FIX Trading Community, Legacy FIX specifications](https://fixtrading.org/legacy-fix-specifications/)).

Several design choices from 1994 are still visible today. Version 2.7a defined FIX at "two levels; session and application", the session level being "concerned with the delivery of data" and the application level defining "business related data content", and declared the protocol independent of the telecommunications protocol (it named X.25, asynchronous lines and the internet) ([FIX 2.7a specification, introduction](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/)). It already used `tag=value` fields separated by the non-printing American Standard Code for Information Interchange (ASCII) character Start of Heading (SOH), sequence numbers re-initialized "each day starting at 1", heartbeats, a body length and "a simple checksum of characters" (same source). Some choices did not last: in 2.7 "All connections between a broker and institution are initiated from the institution's site" (same source), a restriction removed in version 3.0 ([FIX 3.0 release notes, August 1995](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-3-0-specification-release-notes/)). The scope in 1994 was "Equity and Fixed Income transactions" between brokers and institutions ([FIX 2.7a specification](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/)).

## Why a common language mattered

The economic case, as the FIX Trading Community presents it today, is that FIX replaced bespoke interfaces with "one standard protocol that works across thousands of counterparties and venues" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). It sets the comparison out as a table, which is the custodian's own argument rather than a measured study:

| Cost or risk | FIX, as the FIX Trading Community describes it | Proprietary interfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Up-front build | Lower (shared standard, reusable tooling) | Higher (custom per venue or vendor) |
| Adding counterparties | Very low marginal cost | Linear, or worse, cost growth |
| Regulatory change | Shared across the industry | Borne entirely by the firm |
| Vendor lock-in | Low | High |

Source: [FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/), read 15 November 2026.

The 2003 preface split the benefit three ways: business users could cut telephone traffic, technologists gained "an open standard that leverages the development effort", and vendors gained "ready access to the industry" ([FIX 4.4 specification, volume 1, preface](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)). The same preface named two principles. FIX "has remained vendor neutral", and it "avoids over-standardization": it does not demand a single carrier or "a single security protocol", leaving those choices to the firms (same source).

That second principle has a cost, which the community acknowledges. Its Orchestra page notes that "It is common for parties to encode their FIX messages slightly differently, possibly using user-defined tags" ([FIX Trading Community, Orchestra](https://fixtrading.org/standards/orchestra/)), and the session standard defines "rules of engagement" as the document, "usually provided in document form", that describes one firm's specific use of FIX ([FIX Session Layer technical standard, November 2020, section 3.1.8](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-session-layer-technical-standard/)). A common language, in other words, still arrives in local dialects; the later sections on FIX Orchestra and on Brazil's exchange, B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão, as its documents sign it: [B3, Comunicado Externo 004/2026-VNC](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/22/90/30/AD/D67EB9109B5E99B9AC094EA8/CE%20004-2026-VNC%20ENTRYPOINT%20FIX%20E%20BINARIO%20INTERFACE%20DE%20ENVIO%20DE%20ORDENS_PT.pdf)), show what that looks like.

## The versions from 2.7 to 4.4

Each early version came with a release-notes memo, and the memos are the best primary record of what changed. The archive page heads its sections with other dates (1 April 1998 for FIX 4.0 and 1 May 1998 for FIX 4.1, for example), which do not match the memos; the table uses the dates the memos themselves carry ([FIX Trading Community, Legacy FIX specifications](https://fixtrading.org/legacy-fix-specifications/)).

| Version | Date on the primary document | What changed, in the document's words or close to them |
|---|---|---|
| 2.6 | Published 14 February 1994 | The version 2.7 replaced ([FIX 2.7 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-release-notes/)) |
| 2.7 | 15 July 1994 | Sequence numbers to initialize to one each day; BodyLength clarified; new fields such as OrdRejReason, ExDestination and StopPx ([FIX 2.7 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-release-notes/)) |
| 3.0 | Memo dated 3 August 1995 (the 4.0 memo gives 27 September 1995 as the publication date) | Corrected checksum calculation; HeartBtInt(108) made mandatory in the Logon message; the delimiter clarified; no restriction on which party initiates a connection; date and time values "expressed in GMT" ([FIX 3.0 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-3-0-specification-release-notes/); [FIX 4.0 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-0-specification-release-notes/)) |
| 4.0 | Draft announced 13 January 1996 | "A major overhaul of the session layer": every message increments the sequence number, some administrative messages are replaced by a gap fill instead of being resent, Logon and Logout formalized; four-digit years; the Don't Know Trade message; quotes; user-defined tags 5000 to 9999; sequence numbers up to 999,999 ([FIX 4.0 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-0-specification-release-notes/)) |
| 4.1 | Memo dated 1 April 1998, which says 4.0 was published "in the Spring of 1997" | ResetSeqNumFlag added to Logon; all identifier fields become character data; Cancel/Replace used exclusively to reduce quantity; futures, options and forex support expanded ([FIX 4.1 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-1-specification-release-notes/)) |
| 4.2 | Cover dated 1 March 2000, errata of 1 May 2001 (the 4.3 memo gives 1 March 2001) | Still supported today alongside 4.4 ([FIX 4.2 with errata 20010501](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-2-specification-with-errata-20010501/); [FIX 4.3 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-3-specification-release-notes-original-aug-24-2001/); [The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)) |
| 4.3 | 24 August 2001 | Seven volumes; dual syntax, `tag=value` and FIX Markup Language (FIXML), based on the Extensible Markup Language (XML); fixed income; collective investment vehicles; derivatives; multileg orders; "Streetside" trade capture; Asia-Pacific and Japanese requirements ([FIX 4.3 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-3-specification-release-notes-original-aug-24-2001/)) |
| 4.4 | Errata of 18 June 2003, replacing an original date of 30 April 2003 still visible as a tracked change | Used by exchanges and other markets as well as between buy side and sell side; equities plus four other products ([FIX 4.4 specification, volume 1](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)) |

Three details in this record are worth keeping. First, the date conflicts in the table are in the primary documents themselves, so a single "release date" for FIX 3.0 or FIX 4.2 depends on which document one reads. Second, the name drifted: the FIX 4.2 preface calls the effort the "Financial Interface eXchange", while 2.7a and 4.4 use "Financial Information Exchange" and "Financial Information eXchange" ([FIX 4.2 with errata 20010501, preface](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-2-specification-with-errata-20010501/); [FIX 2.7a](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-2-7-specification-document-pdf/); [FIX 4.4, volume 1](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)). Third, the product range widened steadily: the 2003 preface lists collective investment vehicles (CIVs), derivatives, fixed income and foreign exchange besides equities ([FIX 4.4, volume 1, preface](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-4-specification-with-20030618-errata/)).

FIX 4.2 and FIX 4.4 are the two old versions that the FIX Trading Community still supports; "Other versions are no longer supported but are available in our archive for reference" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)).

## FIX 5.0, the service packs and FIX Transport 1.1

The largest structural change came in 2006. The FIX Transport (FIXT) 1.1 specification, published by FIX Protocol Limited (FPL), records that "In October 2006, FPL's Global Technical Committee introduced a new framework which separated the FIX Session Protocol from the FIX Application Protocol", so that application messages could "use any suitable session transport technology" ([FIXT 1.1 specification, errata March 2008](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-transport-fixt-v1-1-technical-standard/)). FPL is the company behind the community, and the Global Technical Committee (GTC) is its standards committee (see the governance section below). The session protocol took a new name, "FIXT.x.y, with the first version being FIXT 1.1" (same source).

The archive describes the same change for FIX 5.0, dated 30 December 2006: the GTC introduced a "Transport Independence (TI) framework", the application layer kept the "FIX.x.y" moniker, the session layer took "FIXTx.y", and "the FIX Session and Application layers have been decoupled and are now independent" ([FIX Trading Community, Legacy FIX specifications](https://fixtrading.org/legacy-fix-specifications/)). FIX 5.0 also brought 11 extension packs for listed derivatives post-trade processing, foreign exchange traded as a security, support for the United States and European regulatory requirements of the day, and an Execution Acknowledgement message for the buy side to confirm receipt of an execution report (same source).

Two service packs followed. The release notes of FIX 5.0 Service Pack 1 (SP1) are dated March 2008 and cover the extension packs approved "since the publication of FIX Protocol Version5.0 in December 2006" ([FIX 5.0 SP1 release notes, March 2008](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-5-0-sp1-release-notes/)). The archive says SP1 includes 25 extension packs and Service Pack 2 (SP2) 19, the latter adding, among other things, trade capture for the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/)), position limits at The Options Clearing Corporation (OCC) ([OCC](https://www.theocc.com/company-information/about-occ)), foreign exchange non-deliverable forwards, standardized credit default swaps for clearing and "a FIX interface by the London Stock Exchange" ([FIX Trading Community, Legacy FIX specifications](https://fixtrading.org/legacy-fix-specifications/)). SP2 was reissued with errata in August 2011 ([FIX 5.0 SP2 errata release notes, 18 August 2011](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-5-0-service-pack-2-with-20110818-errata-release-notes/)).

Splitting the layers changed how a message announces its version. Under FIX 4.x the first field, BeginString(8), carries the version itself; under FIXT the first field says `FIXT.1.1` and the application version travels separately, in DefaultApplVerID(1137) on the Logon or ApplVerID(1128) on a message ([FIX Session Layer technical standard, sections 4.2.1 and 4.3.7](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-session-layer-technical-standard/)). The code set for that field runs from 0 for FIX 2.7 to 9 for FIX 5.0 SP2 and 10 for FIX Latest ([FIXimate, ApplVerIDCodeSet](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/codeSets/ApplVerIDCodeSet)). Today's session standard names four session profiles, FIX.4.2, FIX4, FIXT and Lightweight FIXT (LFIXT):

| Session profile | BeginString(8) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| FIX.4.2 | `FIX.4.2` | With the FIX 4.2 application layer only |
| FIX4 | `FIX.4.4` | Backward compatible with FIX 4.4; recommended when one application version, such as FIX Latest, is used |
| FIXT | `FIXT.1.1` | Required when several application versions share one session |
| LFIXT | `FIXT.1.1` | "Lightweight FIXT", with restricted session-level recovery, by counterparty agreement |

Source: [FIX Session Layer technical standard, November 2020, table 1](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-session-layer-technical-standard/).

## FIX Latest and the extension packs

After SP2 the community stopped issuing numbered versions. "From 2009, the protocol ("FIX Latest") has been upgraded incrementally through extension packs", which are "backwards compatible and can even be used on legacy versions of the Protocol" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). An extension pack (EP) gets "a unique, sequential number at the point they are approved by the GTC", packs are cumulative, a user may implement only part of one, and the supported subset is set by bilateral rules of engagement ([FIX Latest specification, introduction, as of EP284, November 2023, section 4.2](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-latest-specification-introduction/)). The session standard adds that once an extension pack is published "it becomes the FIX Latest version of FIX" ([FIX Session Layer technical standard, section 3.1.17](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-session-layer-technical-standard/)).

The packs range from a single new value to whole new message families. The newest on the extension-pack page on 15 November 2026 was EP312, "FIX Latest Session Layer Corrections", last updated on 11 September 2026 ([FIX Trading Community, EP312](https://fixtrading.org/packages/ep312/)); just before it come error corrections for FIX 4.2 and FIX 4.4 (EP310, EP311), equity issuance (EP309), a "New Market Order Type" (EP304), outage communication (EP305) and the "EU Consolidated Tape for Bonds and Equities" (EP300), for the European Union ([FIX Trading Community, Extension packs](https://fixtrading.org/standards/extension-packs/)). The browsable reference, FIXimate, now opens on a repository described as "FIX Latest as of Extension Pack 312" and served through Orchimate ([FIXimate](https://fiximate.fixtrading.org/)).

### User-defined fields

Not every need waits for an extension pack. Tags 5000 to 9999 were set aside for user-defined fields in FIX 4.0 ([FIX 4.0 release notes](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-4-0-specification-release-notes/)); today all of them "have all been allocated", 8000 to 8499 are reserved by the GTC for regulatory needs in legacy versions, and since December 2009 tags 20000 to 39999 may be used bilaterally without registration ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). The GTC's stated policy is that firms should prefer tags from the latest extension pack "as opposed to customised extensions through user defined fields" (same source). B3's order gateway shows the bilateral range in use: its Logon message accepts CancelOnDisconnectType(35002) and CODTimeoutWindow(35003) ([B3, EntryPoint message specification, version 2.40, 27 November 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/A7/77/7B/FE/5789491029BEEC39AC094EA8/EntryPointMessageSpecs.pdf)).

## The family of standards around FIX

FIX is defined as "an application‑layer protocol", which specifies what business information is exchanged "rather than how that information is physically sent across a network" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). The same business messages can therefore travel in several encodings and over several session protocols, each a separate technical standard ([FIX Latest specification, introduction, table 1](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-latest-specification-introduction/)):

| Layer | Standards |
|---|---|
| Application | FIX Latest; FIX Algorithmic Trading Definition Language (FIXatdl), an XML-based standard for algorithmic-order user interfaces |
| Encoding | `tag=value` (the original, "the most widely used one"); FIX Markup Language (FIXML); FIX Adapted for STreaming (FAST); Simple Binary Encoding (SBE); Google Protocol Buffers; JavaScript Object Notation (JSON); Abstract Syntax Notation One (ASN.1) |
| Session | FIX session protocol with the FIX.4.2, FIX4, FIXT and LFIXT profiles; FIX Performance Session Layer (FIXP); Simple Open Framing Header (SOFH); FIX-over-TLS (FIXS) |

The international standards body ISO published two of these in April 2022: ISO 3531-1, the `tag=value` encoding, and ISO 3531-2, the session layer, both from its subcommittee for information exchange in financial services, ISO/TC 68/SC 9 ([ISO, ISO 3531-1:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/81510.html); [ISO, ISO 3531-2:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/81511.html)). The community's technical-standards page adds that SBE "became an ISO/IEC standard in 2025" as ISO/IEC 25390:2025 ([FIX Trading Community, Technical standards](https://fixtrading.org/standards/technical-standards/)). The binary encodings and FIXP are the subject of [FIX encodings and successors](/learn/fix-encodings-and-successors-fixml-fast-sbe-and-exchange-binary-protocols).

## FIX Orchestra: rules of engagement a machine can read

Because every firm documents its own use of FIX, onboarding a new counterparty has long meant reading a specification document and coding to it. FIX Orchestra is "a standard for creating machine-readable definitions of messaging protocols": which messages exist, which fields they carry with what validation rules, alternative layouts, permitted workflows and "network details, session and encoding settings" ([FIX Trading Community, Orchestra](https://fixtrading.org/standards/orchestra/)). The page lists the uses: communicating a FIX service to counterparties, normalizing inbound flows that differ slightly, generating compliance tests, validating one's own implementation and provisioning sessions (same source). Version 1.0 of the standard was published as final on 19 February 2021, version 1.1 is at release candidate 3, and Orchestra is licensed under the Apache License 2.0 ([FIX Trading Community, FIX Orchestra standard](https://fixtrading.org/standards/orchestra/fix-orchestra-standard/)). The FIX Latest repository itself is distributed as an Orchestra file ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)), and the session standard suggests Orchestra for documenting session settings such as the session profile and the test-request threshold ([FIX Session Layer technical standard, footnotes 1, 3 and 9](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-session-layer-technical-standard/)).

## The FIX Trading Community: who decides what goes in

### The legal entity

The company behind the standard is FIX Protocol Ltd, number 03760285 on the register of England and Wales, incorporated on 23 April 1999 as a "Private company limited by guarantee without share capital" and listed as active ([Companies House, FIX PROTOCOL LTD](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/03760285)). The FIX Latest introduction calls "FIX Trading Community™" "a brand of FIX Protocol Ltd, a non-profit UK registered entity", and says the company "is managed under a special" purpose trust "which owns the assets", so that FIX remains "a non-proprietary, free and open protocol" ([FIX Latest specification, introduction, section 2](https://fixtrading.org/packages/fix-latest-specification-introduction/)). The trade site Finance Magnates reported on 19 August 2013 that "FIX Protocol Ltd has today announced a name change to FIX Trading Community and a new website", quoting a co-chair of the Global Steering Committee who put the membership at "over 275 members" ([Finance Magnates, 19 August 2013](https://financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/technology/fix-protocol-rebrands-to-fix-trading-community-to-reflect-its-recent-expansion-of-resources/amp)).

### Committees and working groups

The community "is managed by its Global Steering Committee (GSC)", made up of the co-chairs of its committees, which "reports to the FIX Protocol Ltd (FPL) board" ([FIX Trading Community, Committees](https://fixtrading.org/committees/)). Under it sit three kinds of committee: product committees for asset classes (digital assets and technology; fixed income, currencies and commodities; equities; post-trade), regional committees (Americas, Asia Pacific, Europe Middle East Africa and Japan) and service committees, among them the Global Technical Committee, which "manages FIX's business and technical standards" except the Market Model Typology (MMT) (same source). Participation is for member firms; guests from non-member firms may join working groups at the co-chairs' discretion but "cannot participate in voting" (same source).

The working groups are where requirements start. The list on 15 November 2026 ran from 24x7 markets and artificial intelligence (AI) workflows to carbon trading, European consolidated tapes, MiFID transaction reporting, outage communications, prediction markets and securities lending, alongside technical subcommittees for the protocol, the standards and Orchestra ([FIX Trading Community, Working groups](https://fixtrading.org/working-groups/)).

### How a change becomes part of FIX

The community describes a four-step path ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)):

1. Market participants in a working group define the business problem and the workflows and data needed, "documented in a formal gap analysis".
2. The technical standards group turns the proposal into detailed specifications.
3. The Global Technical Committee reviews them "to ensure consistency, quality and alignment with existing standards".
4. A "two to four week public review" precedes inclusion in the standard, as an extension pack.

### Intellectual property

Two policies keep the standard open. Participants in FIX meetings must disclose patents with claims that the specification would infringe and either disclaim them or offer a licence ([FIX Trading Community, Policies](https://fixtrading.org/policies/)). Patents arising from FIX work are assigned to FIX Protocol Ltd, which grants members a "royalty and fee free" licence on reasonable and non-discriminatory (RAND) terms, revocable if a patent is misused "to create competing specifications or protocols" (same source). The specification documents are published under the Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 licence, so they cannot be adapted "with a view to creating a new right, title, license or interest" in an altered FIX (same source).

### Members

The member directory read on 15 November 2026 counted 37 associate members, 25 premier global members and 281 regular members, grouped as 88 buy-side institutions, 60 sell-side broker-dealers, 115 vendors, 49 venues, 29 associations and 2 regulators ([FIX Trading Community, Members](https://fixtrading.org/member/)). The standards page speaks of a ">300 firm member base" ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)).

## Adoption by asset class and by venue

From its equity origins FIX now covers, by the community's account, "Equities, fixed income, FX, listed derivatives, OTC derivatives, digital assets", with messages for pre-trade (market data, reference data, quoting, requests for quote), trade (orders and executions, program trading, blocks, multi-leg) and post-trade (allocations, confirmations, settlement instructions, collateral, margin, positions) ([FIX Trading Community, The FIX Protocol](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fix-protocol/)). Here FX stands for foreign exchange and OTC for over the counter. The page adds that FIX is used for regulatory reporting to the Trade Reporting and Compliance Engine (TRACE) of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA), to the CFTC, to the OCC, to the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) and to the Australian Securities & Investments Commission (ASIC), and that the providers of Europe's consolidated tapes support it (same source; the names as each body gives them: [FINRA, TRACE](https://www.finra.org/filing-reporting/trace), [CIRO](https://www.ciro.ca/), [ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/)).

## Brazil: from MegaDirect and BELL to EntryPoint

### The exchange's interfaces

B3 is the result of the merger, completed on 29 March 2017, of BM&FBOVESPA (Bolsa de Valores, Mercadorias e Futuros) and Cetip; BM&FBOVESPA had itself been "created in 2008, from the merger of BM&F and Bovespa Holding" ([B3 Investor Relations, history](https://ri.b3.com.br/en/?p=42)). B3's account dates the launch of the PUMA Trading System, "created in partnership by BM&FBOVESPA and the CME Group", to 2011 in derivatives and currency, and its arrival in equities to 2013 (same source).

FIX was the vehicle for that migration. A frequently-asked-questions document of 28 September 2011 describes EntryPoint as "the evolution of BM&FBovespa current order entry APIs", built on the experience of the BELL and MegaDirect interfaces but offering a unified specification for equities, derivatives and foreign exchange ([BM&FBOVESPA, EntryPoint frequently asked questions, version 1.0, 28 September 2011](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/42/E0/21/B9/B289E410AB87A7E492D828A8/EntryPointFAQ.pdf)). It states that "EntryPoint is based on the 4.4 version of the Financial Information eXchange ("FIX") Protocol. MegaDirect is based on FIX.4.2", and that "EntryPoint is the specification, MBEP and PUMA are the implementations", MBEP being Megabolsa EntryPoint, a wrapper that gave early EntryPoint access to the equities system before PUMA (same source). The same document ties the end of BELL to the derivatives segment and the end of MegaDirect to equities, and requires separate FIX sessions for each trading flow, the four direct market access (DMA) models and the desk (same source; B3 uses the same expansion on its [EntryPoint page](https://www.b3.com.br/en_us/solutions/platforms/puma-trading-system/for-developers-and-vendors/entrypoint-order-entry)).

B3's developer page in November 2026 still describes "FIX Order Entry" as "the order management interface for the PUMA Trading System, based on FIX 4.4 protocol", with a drop copy service and a TCP Replayer for market data recovery that answers "over a TCP connection (a FIX 4.4 session)" ([B3, EntryPoint FIX Order Entry](https://www.b3.com.br/en_us/solutions/platforms/puma-trading-system/for-developers-and-vendors/entrypoint-order-entry)). Its message specification, version 2.40 of 27 November 2025, sets BeginString(8) to "FIX.4.4" ([B3, EntryPoint message specification](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/A7/77/7B/FE/5789491029BEEC39AC094EA8/EntryPointMessageSpecs.pdf)).

The binary gateway came next. B3 completed phase 1, the derivatives market, on 4 December 2023 and moved equities in three waves on 29 January, 19 February and 4 March 2024, keeping the throttle limits of the FIX gateways ([B3, Circular Letter 015/2024-PRE, 24 January 2024](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/56/46/59/0D/3664D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/OC%20015-2024%20PRE%20%20Lan%C3%A7amento%20do%20Gateway%20Binario%20Segmento%20Bovespa%20(Binary%20EntryPoint)%20-%20EN.pdf)). The binary guidelines say the interface "is based on FIXP protocol" and "intended to represent all FIX semantics", and map FIX names to binary ones, NewOrderSingle (35=D) to SimpleNewOrder among them ([B3, Binary EntryPoint messaging guidelines 8.4.2.3](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/69/35/F1/AB/94711A103BBB511AAC094EA8/BinaryEntryPoint-MessageSpecificationGuidelines-8.4.2.3-enUS.pdf)). The two gateways run side by side: in January 2026 B3 announced new versions of the specifications of "EntryPoint FIX e Binário", FIX and binary EntryPoint, with the same new value in tag 378 of the ExecutionReport for both ([B3, Comunicado Externo 004/2026-VNC, 22 January 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/22/90/30/AD/D67EB9109B5E99B9AC094EA8/CE%20004-2026-VNC%20ENTRYPOINT%20FIX%20E%20BINARIO%20INTERFACE%20DE%20ENVIO%20DE%20ORDENS_PT.pdf)).

FIX also reaches B3's over-the-counter fixed income platform: in April 2026 B3 told participants in the balcão segment that changes to the "catálogos de mensageria FIX de Order Entry (OE) e Drop Copy (DC)" of the Trademate platform would go live on 27 April 2026 and were mandatory for participants with those sessions ([B3, Comunicado Externo 016/2026-VNC, 16 April 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/25/C6/03/D6/0D69D9107DD5C8D9AC094EA8/CE%20016-2026-VNC%20ATUALIZACAO%20NO%20CATALOGO%20DE%20MENSAGERIA%20FIX%20ORDER%20ENTRY%20OE%20E%20DROP%20COPY%20DC_PT.pdf)). The detail of B3's access models belongs to the part [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access).

## What to remember

- FIX began in mid-1992 as a link between Fidelity Investments and Salomon Brothers, went live in early 1993, and was offered to the industry in 1994; version 2.7 (15 July 1994) was the first used in production.
- From the start FIX separated a session level (delivery) from an application level (business content), used `tag=value` fields with the SOH delimiter, and was independent of the network underneath.
- FIX 4.0, drafted in 1996, overhauled the session layer; FIX 4.2 and 4.4 are the two old versions still supported; dates for some versions differ between the primary documents.
- In October 2006 the GTC split session from application: FIXT 1.1 carries the session, and the application version travels in ApplVerID; since 2009 FIX grows by numbered extension packs, cumulatively called FIX Latest (EP312 in September 2026).
- FIX Protocol Ltd, a UK company limited by guarantee incorporated in 1999 and branded FIX Trading Community since 2013, maintains the standard through a steering committee, a technical committee and member working groups, with royalty-free patent terms.
- Venues speak different generations: NYSE's Pillar gateway uses FIX 4.2, London uses FIXT 1.1 with FIX 5.0 SP2, B3 runs a FIX 4.4 gateway beside a binary one built on FIXP.

<ReaderQuestions>

- If a common language still arrives in local dialects, each venue with its own rules of engagement, how much of the saving that FIX promised survives the onboarding of a new counterparty?
- Should a standard that grows by small cumulative extension packs ever publish a new numbered version again, and what would be gained or lost?
- FIX's custodian is a non-profit funded by member fees and protected by a purpose trust: is that model enough to keep a protocol open when the largest members are also its largest users?
- When an exchange keeps a 2000-era version such as FIX 4.2 in production in 2026, is that a sign of a stable standard or of a cost of migration that nobody wants to pay?
- Brazil's exchange built its order entry on FIX, yet Brazilian firms are barely visible in the community's committees: what would a stronger Latin American voice change in the standard?
