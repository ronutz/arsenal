Two facts have to be held together in this part. The sexual abuse of children is common, is usually committed by people the child knows, and is mostly never reported. And in the 1980s and 1990s, accusations of abuse that never happened, many of them involving Satanic rituals, sent innocent people to prison, closed schools and broke families, until courts, inquiries and journalists took them apart. Each fact has been used to deny the other. This part of the series opened by [the map of mass hysteria](/learn/mass-hysteria-how-groups-come-to-believe-anything) follows the second without losing the first: the ritual-abuse scare in the United States (US) and the reports that examined it, the McMartin and Kern County cases, their counterparts in Britain, New Zealand and Paraná, the experiments on false and recovered memories and the dispute that still divides psychologists, and the Escola Base case in São Paulo, with the judgments that followed and what changed afterwards. After reading, you can say what each court and inquiry found, what the memory research does and does not show, and how the [witch hunts of earlier centuries](/learn/witch-hunts-and-moral-panics) and these cases resemble each other.

No person is described here as guilty of anything a court did not find. English quotations are given as published; Portuguese-language sources are quoted verbatim with an English gloss. Names of children and of private accusers are left out.

## Real abuse, mostly hidden

The World Health Organization (WHO) states that "1 in 5 women and 1 in 7 men report having been sexually abused as a child" and that "Child maltreatment is often hidden. Only a fraction of child victims of maltreatment ever get support from official support services" ([WHO, Child maltreatment, fact sheet, 8 May 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/child-maltreatment)).

In Brazil, the Fórum Brasileiro de Segurança Pública (FBSP) counted 84,388 police records of rape and rape of a vulnerable person in 2025, a rate of 39.5 per 100,000 inhabitants and the first fall since the pandemic; its researchers warn that the fall may mean "não menos violência, mas menos denúncia" (not less violence, but fewer reports), recalling that the 2012 national victimization survey estimated that only 7.5% of victims of sexual offences told the police and that health data suggest police records capture about 10% of rapes ([FBSP, Anuário Brasileiro de Segurança Pública 2026](https://forumseguranca.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/anuario-2026.pdf)). Among victims aged 0 to 13, the same yearbook found that family members were the perpetrators in 59.2% of cases, other acquaintances in 36.9% and strangers in 3.9% ([FBSP, 2026](https://forumseguranca.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/anuario-2026.pdf)). The danger children face is mostly at home and among people they know, which is part of why the stories of strangers in robes, examined below, misdirected attention.

## The 1980s: how a ritual-abuse scare took shape

### A book, then the cases

In 1980 a Canadian woman, Michelle Smith, and her psychiatrist, Lawrence Pazder, published *Michelle Remembers*, an account, in the description of the Times Higher Education, of "sexual abuse, torture, mutilation, the sacrifice of babies, and her ceremonial marriage to Satan" ([Times Higher Education, The Satanic disabuser, 25 August 1995](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/5156)). Kenneth Lanning, a supervisory special agent at the Behavioral Science Unit of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), later cited the book as typifying the adult-survivor accounts that reached law enforcement, and quoted Pazder's definition of ritualized abuse as "repeated physical, emotional, mental, and spiritual assaults combined with a systematic use of symbols and secret ceremonies designed to turn a child against itself, family, society, and God" ([Lanning, Investigator's Guide to Allegations of "Ritual" Child Abuse, FBI, January 1992](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/136592NCJRS.pdf)). Those who shared La Fontaine's scepticism about satanic abuse, the Times Higher Education reported, "link the surge of 'recovered memories' to the publication in the early 1980s of Michelle Remembers" ([Times Higher Education, 1995](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/5156)).

### What the FBI's specialist found

Lanning's 1992 guide is the most detailed contemporary assessment by an investigator. He wrote that in 1983 and 1984, when he first heard such stories, "I tended to believe them", and that he was first contacted about one in early 1983; in October 1984 the problems of a case in Jordan, Minnesota, became public, and in February 1985 the FBI held the first national seminar on "these kinds of cases" ([Lanning, 1992](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/136592NCJRS.pdf)). The cases shared four dynamics: multiple young victims, usually from birth to six years old; multiple offenders, sometimes dozens, "perhaps as many as 40-50 percent" of them reported to be women; fear as the controlling tactic; and bizarre or ritualistic activity.

Then he set out why he had come to doubt part of them, without dismissing the rest:

- "Some of what the victims in these cases allege is physically impossible", some "possible but improbable (human sacrifice, cannibalism, vampirism)", some "possible and probable (child pornography, clever manipulation of victims)", and "some is corroborated (medical evidence of vaginal or anal trauma, offender confessions)".
- In none of the cases he knew had bodies been found, "in spite of major excavations", nor physical evidence that a murder had taken place.
- The believers were using "the long-since-discredited numbers and rhetoric of the missing children hysteria in the early 1980s"; a 1989 Department of Justice bulletin estimated that between 52 and 158 children a year were kidnapped and murdered by non-family members, while those who accepted the stories of mass sacrifice "would have us believe that the satanists and other occult practitioners are murdering more than twice as many people every year in this country as all the other murderers combined", against about 23,000 murders a year.
- Believers cited the consistency of stories from all over America; Lanning answered that "The large number of people telling the same story is, in fact, the biggest reason to doubt these stories", since "It is simply too difficult for that many people to commit so many horrendous crimes as part of an organized conspiracy" ([Lanning, 1992](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/136592NCJRS.pdf)).

He found "no single, simple answer" to why victims alleged things that did not seem true and listed alternative explanations, from pathological distortion to traumatic memory and the influence of interviewers, and warned that the first step "is to admit the possibility that some of what the victims describe may not have happened. Some child advocates seem unwilling to do this" ([Lanning, 1992](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/136592NCJRS.pdf)). <Claim status="established" by="by the FBI's 1992 guide, for the cases its author had reviewed">No evidence of organized Satanic cults committing ritual murders was found</Claim>; that some children in those cases had been abused by someone was, in his words, sometimes corroborated.

## McMartin, Manhattan Beach, 1983 to 1990

### From one complaint to hundreds of children

The McMartin case began with one parent's complaint and grew through an official letter and a method of interviewing. The chronology compiled by Douglas Linder of the University of Missouri-Kansas City law school records that on 12 August 1983 a mother reported that her two-and-a-half-year-old son had been molested at the McMartin Preschool in Manhattan Beach, California, by Ray Buckey, the 25-year-old son of the owner; Buckey was arrested on 7 September; and on 8 September the police chief sent a letter to about 200 McMartin parents ([Linder, Chronology of the McMartin Preschool Abuse Trials, Famous Trials](https://famous-trials.com/mcmartin/904-chronology)). The letter, as Linder reproduces it, asked parents to "question your child to see if he or she has been a witness to any crime or if he or she has been a victim", listed the acts under investigation, and asked them to keep the investigation "strictly confidential" ([Linder, The McMartin Preschool Abuse Trial: An Account](https://famous-trials.com/mcmartin/902-home)). The complainant's later accusations, which involved a church, a beheaded baby and a man in a cape, grew "increasingly bizarre" by Linder's account, and Time magazine reported that in 1985 she was found to be an acute paranoid schizophrenic and that she died in 1986 ([Time, Six Years of Trial by Torture, 29 January 1990](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,969276,00.html)).

Children's Institute International (CII), a Los Angeles child-abuse centre, interviewed former pupils at the district attorney's request. Time reported that "By early 1984, investigators concluded that 369 of the 400 children interviewed had been abused"; Linder gives 360 by March 1984 ([Time, 1990](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,969276,00.html); [Linder, Chronology](https://famous-trials.com/mcmartin/904-chronology)).

### The verdicts and what they meant

The first trial, according to the Associated Press report carried by the Deseret News, was "a three-year, $15 million trial - the longest, costliest criminal trial in U.S. history"; Peggy McMartin Buckey had spent about two and a half years in jail awaiting trial, and her son five ([Deseret News, 20 January 1990](https://www.deseret.com/1990/1/20/18842085/acquitted-preschool-teacher-files-1-million-civil-rights-suit); [Seattle Times, 1990](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19900119/1051633/the-mcmartin-trial----child-abuse-case-mishandled-from-start-experts-say)). Time gave two years for the mother. The verdict was not a finding that nothing had happened to any child: Time reported that "seven of the twelve jurors said they believed the nine child witnesses were molested 'in some sense by someone'", but the prosecution could not show it happened at the school, and quoted the judge, William Pounders: "The case has poisoned everyone who had contact with it" ([Time, 1990](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,969276,00.html)). Legal experts quoted by the Seattle Times faulted the district attorney for an unmanageably large case and for "relying on therapists untrained in criminal investigative techniques to interview the alleged child victims"; one ten-year-old spent 16 days on the stand during the preliminary hearing, after which most parents withdrew their children from the case ([Seattle Times, 1990](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19900119/1051633/the-mcmartin-trial----child-abuse-case-mishandled-from-start-experts-say)).

The day after the verdict, Peggy McMartin Buckey filed a federal civil-rights suit seeking $1 million; <Claim status="alleged" by="by Peggy McMartin Buckey's lawsuit, January 1990">the defendants, including the county, the city, the former district attorney, CII, an interviewer and a television station and its reporter, had conspired "to conduct and publicize an investigation" so as to "predetermine the outcome"</Claim> ([Deseret News, 1990](https://www.deseret.com/1990/1/20/18842085/acquitted-preschool-teacher-files-1-million-civil-rights-suit)). The outcome of that suit is not in the sources read. The sources also disagree on details: the Seattle Times reports acquittals on 52 counts and a mistrial on 13 others, while Linder's chronology describes the jury as hung on "13 of the 52 counts" and the retrial as covering "eight counts involving three girls"; the complainant's son is said to have attended the school "about ten" times (Linder) or "14 times over three months" (Time).

### The press, judged by the press

In 1991 the Pulitzer Prize for Criticism went to David Shaw of the Los Angeles Times "For his critiques of the way in which the media, including his own paper, reported the McMartin Pre-School child molestation case" ([Pulitzer Prizes, David Shaw](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/david-shaw)). Linder's chronology notes that on 2 April 1984 the Los Angeles Times had reported that pupils were fondled, raped, photographed nude and forced to witness animals being slaughtered ([Linder, Chronology](https://famous-trials.com/mcmartin/904-chronology)).

### The opposite reading

Not every researcher accepts the now-common account of McMartin as a pure witch hunt. The criminologist Michael Salter, drawing on the political scientist Ross Cheit, argues that "The strong medical evidence of sexual abuse in some complainant children in McMartin was largely erased in media reporting", that journalists "reframed the case as lacking any evidentiary basis", and that when Cheit reviewed cases American journalists cited as fitting the McMartin template, "the majority included no allegation of organised and ritual abuse" and some involved "confessions, convictions and other compelling evidence of criminal activity" ([Salter, Organised child sexual abuse in the media, forthcoming in the Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Criminology, 2016, author's copy](https://www.memoiretraumatique.org/assets/files/v1/Documents-pdf/2016_Organised_child_sexual_abuse_in_the_media.pdf)). Salter sees the "witch hunt" framing as part of a backlash against child protection that, in his account, later revelations of clergy and celebrity abuse contradicted. <Claim status="disputed" by="by Cheit and Salter against the prevailing account">Whether the McMartin case was wholly baseless or a case of real abuse mishandled</Claim> is still argued; what is not disputed is that no court convicted anyone.

## Kern County, California: convictions that fell

The National Registry of Exonerations, a project of United States universities, records that from 1984 to 1986 "at least 30 defendants were convicted of child sex abuse and related charges and sentenced to long prison terms in a series of inter-related cases in Kern County, California", with eight more accepting plea bargains; "Over time, 20 of the defendants who were sentenced to prison were exonerated, the earliest in 1991 and the latest in 2008", in most cases after the children recanted, and "In all of the exonerations there was evidence that the complaining witnesses - some as young as four years old - had been coerced or persuaded by the authorities to make false accusations" ([National Registry of Exonerations, John Stoll](https://exonerationregistry.org/cases/11009)). The Registry calls the Kern County cases "the oldest and largest" of the prosecutions in "a wave of child sex abuse hysteria", adding that "Some (but not all) of these cases included allegations of satanic rituals".

John Stoll's case shows the mechanism and its limits. Accused in June 1984, he was convicted in September 1985 on 17 counts and sentenced to 40 years, on the testimony of six boys whom investigators had questioned "repeatedly and at length", promising that if they admitted being abused everything would be all right and they could go home; there was no other evidence. In 2004 four of the former child witnesses recanted, one said he did not remember being abused, and one, Stoll's son, stood by his testimony. On 30 April 2004 a Kern County Superior Court judge vacated the conviction, finding that the questioning had produced unreliable testimony; Stoll was released after 20 years, the California Attorney General and State Board of Control found in 2006 that he had not committed the crimes, and in 2009 the county agreed to pay him $5 million ([National Registry of Exonerations, John Stoll](https://exonerationregistry.org/cases/11009)). Other Registry entries describe the "Pitts Seven" and the Cox family cases in the same county, where authorities "continued to interview the children repeatedly, using highly suggestive questioning techniques now known to produce false accusations, especially among young children" ([National Registry of Exonerations, Teresa Cox](https://exonerationregistry.org/cases/10364); [Wayne Forsythe](https://exonerationregistry.org/cases/10452); [Gina Miller](https://exonerationregistry.org/cases/10692)).

## Beyond the United States

### Britain: 84 cases examined

In Britain the Department of Health commissioned the anthropologist Jean La Fontaine to study allegations of organized and ritual abuse reported to police, social services and the National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children (NSPCC) between 1988 and 1991. According to the Times Higher Education's account, "La Fontaine investigated 84 cases of alleged ritual abuse, and substantiates only three", none of which met her criteria for "satanic" abuse; the health secretary, Virginia Bottomley, said the report "exposed the myth of Satanic abuse", wording La Fontaine called "unfortunate and overdramatic": "My conclusion was that there was no evidence of satanic abuse in these cases" ([Times Higher Education, 1995](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/5156)). Her critics answered in the same article. The journalist Bea Campbell predicted she "may be destined to become the Freud of her generation"; the psychologist Ashley Conway said that amnesia after severe trauma is "completely consistent with what one would expect from genuinely traumatically abused children"; and the psychotherapist Valerie Sinason found it "disturbing that one anthropologist's readings of transcripts are being listened to more seriously than 40 senior health service clinicians". La Fontaine replied that she had not interviewed adult survivors because they would be "a completely unrepresentative sample of self-selected volunteers", and that "All this furore about satanism and satanists has distracted attention away from very seriously damaged children and what they need" ([Times Higher Education, 1995](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/5156)). The report itself could not be read for this part.

### New Zealand: a conviction quashed 29 years later

Peter Ellis, a teacher at the Christchurch Civic Childcare Centre, was convicted in 1993 on 16 counts of sexual offending against seven children; three convictions were set aside in 1994 after one child recanted, a further appeal failed in 1999, and a ministerial inquiry by a former Chief Justice in 2002 found he had not proven his convictions unsafe ([Supreme Court of New Zealand, Ellis v R, [2022] NZSC 115, 7 October 2022](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2022/2022-NZSC-115.pdf)). Ellis died on 4 September 2019, before his final appeal was heard; the Supreme Court allowed the appeal to continue after his death ([Ellis v R, [2022] NZSC 114](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2022/2022-NZSC-114.pdf)). On 7 October 2022 it unanimously quashed the convictions. It found that the expert evidence on child behaviour "lacked balance", "involved circular reasoning" and suggested that clusters of common behaviours could support a conclusion of abuse; that a chart given to the jury compounded this; and that fresh evidence from memory experts showed a risk of contamination through parents' questioning, meetings between parents and discussions among children, a risk on which the Crown's own expert, Professor Gail Goodman, "expressed substantial agreement" with the defence expert ([Ellis v R, [2022] NZSC 115](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2022/2022-NZSC-115.pdf)). The Court added that the case "posed difficult challenges for the parents of the complainants and the judgment should not be read as a criticism of them".

### Brazil: the Guaratuba case

In April 1992 a six-year-old boy, Evandro Ramos Caetano, disappeared in Guaratuba, on the coast of Paraná, and was found dead. Prosecutors accused Celina Abagge, the mayor's wife, and her daughter Beatriz of having ordered his abduction and murder, and five other people of taking part; <Claim status="alleged" by="by the prosecution, in the original case">the boy had been sacrificed in a ritual to "abrir os caminhos" (open the way) for the family's politics and business</Claim> ([TNH1, with Folhapress, 10 November 2023](https://www.tnh1.com.br/noticia/nid/caso-evandro-justica-anula-condenacoes-mais-de-30-anos-apos-sumico-de-menino/)). Davi dos Santos Soares and Osvaldo Marcineiro were convicted in 2004, as was Vicente de Paula Ferreira, who died in prison in 2011; Beatriz Abagge was sentenced in 2011 to 21 years and four months; the charge against Celina Abagge lapsed because of her age; two other defendants were acquitted in 2005.

Recordings of the confessions with previously unknown passages indicating torture were made public by the podcaster Ivan Mizanzuk, whose podcast Projeto Humanos investigated the case (TNH1 dates them to 2021, Tribuna do Paraná to 2020). On 9 November 2023 the First Criminal Chamber of the Tribunal de Justiça do Paraná (TJPR), by three votes to two, upheld a criminal review and acquitted Beatriz Abagge, Davi dos Santos Soares, Osvaldo Marcineiro and, posthumously, Vicente de Paula Ferreira, recognizing torture and the lack of evidence; Judge Adalberto Jorge Xisto Pereira, who led the majority, said: "Não há dúvida que houve tortura" (there is no doubt there was torture), while the rapporteur, Judge Miguel Kfouri Neto, voted against, arguing that the tapes should first undergo official forensic examination ([TNH1, 2023](https://www.tnh1.com.br/noticia/nid/caso-evandro-justica-anula-condenacoes-mais-de-30-anos-apos-sumico-de-menino/)). The prosecutor at the session supported the review, but the Ministério Público do Paraná later appealed; on 16 September 2025 the Superior Tribunal de Justiça (STJ) upheld the acquittals ([Tribuna do Paraná, 17 September 2025](https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/curitiba-regiao/caso-evandro-stj-mantem-decisao-que-anula-condenacoes-de-quatro-acusados/)). The government of Paraná, in a letter to Beatriz Abagge reported by the same newspaper, asked "perdão pelas sevícias indesculpáveis cometidas no passado contra a Senhora" (forgiveness for the inexcusable abuse committed against you in the past). "Depois de 31 anos e 131 dias, vencemos a luta da nossa vida: provarmos a nossa inocência", she said (after 31 years and 131 days we won the fight of our lives: proving our innocence) ([Tribuna do Paraná, 2025](https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/curitiba-regiao/caso-evandro-stj-mantem-decisao-que-anula-condenacoes-de-quatro-acusados/)). Who killed the boy is not established by the sources read.

## False memories: what the experiments show

### From misinformation to implanted events

The scientific side of the story began with experiments showing that information given after an event can distort memory of it, and moved, in the 1990s, to the question that mattered for these cases: can a whole event that never happened be implanted? Gillian Murphy and colleagues summarize the background: interest in the formation of false autobiographical memories "was sparked by concerns that therapists may be unwittingly planting misinformation in clients' minds using suggestive therapy techniques, particularly memories of abuse" ([Murphy et al., Lost in the mall again, Memory 31(6), 2023, accepted manuscript](https://cora.ucc.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/075c236a-356f-4694-94c5-9500b6c148d3/content)).

### Lost in the mall: the study and its descendants

In Elizabeth Loftus and Jacqueline Pickrell's 1995 study, participants were presented with three true events from their childhood and one fabricated event, being lost in a shopping mall, based on information from an older relative; after a written survey and two interviews, "25% of participants (n = 6) reported a false memory of getting lost in a shopping mall as a child", and the authors presented the result as "existence proof" rather than an estimate of how many people could be misled ([Murphy et al., 2023](https://cora.ucc.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/075c236a-356f-4694-94c5-9500b6c148d3/content)). The original paper, in Psychiatric Annals, could not be read for this part; its design is described here as its replicators report it.

### The dispute over what counts as a false memory

The numbers in the table differ because the studies define a false memory differently, and that definition is the heart of the dispute. Scoboria and colleagues built their coding scheme because estimates varied partly "due to false memories having been operationalised differently across studies" and called their 30.4% "the most valid estimate of false memory formation" in the implantation literature ([Scoboria et al., 2017](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=27892833)). Chris Brewin and Bernice Andrews concluded that "susceptibility to false memories of childhood events appears more limited than has been suggested" and cautioned "against accepting investigator-based ratings as necessarily corresponding to participants' self-reports" ([Brewin and Andrews, 2017](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=28163368)). Murphy and colleagues listed the main criticisms of the 1995 study as "the small sample size, the lack of a clear definition of 'full' and 'partial' memories, and lack of information about the coding process", citing Blizard and Shaw (2019), and noted that it has been cited by expert witnesses in important trials ([Murphy et al., 2023](https://cora.ucc.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/075c236a-356f-4694-94c5-9500b6c148d3/content)).

In 2025 the dispute reached the raw data. Andrews and Brewin reanalysed the Irish replication and reported that those judged to have false memories recalled on average one and a half of six core details with any confidence, 30% recalled none, and half had probably been lost on another occasion; Brewin said, "Experts need to be very careful in how they present research findings so as not to mislead the justice system" ([Royal Holloway, January 2025](https://www.royalholloway.ac.uk/research-and-education/departments-and-schools/psychology/news/new-research-interrogates-implanting-and-judgements-of-false-memory)). Kimberley Wade, Paul Riesthuis, Charlotte Bücken, Henry Otgaar and Loftus replied that Andrews and Brewin "posited that only 4% of Murphy et al.'s participants developed false memories", that "we should expect participants' reports to contain remnants of accurate memories, self or schematic knowledge, or speculation", and that "even low false memory rates can be practically important" ([Wade et al., Still Lost in the Mall, Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2025](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/189197/7/Applied%20Cognitive%20Psychology%20-%202025%20-%20Wade%20-%20Still%20Lost%20in%20the%20Mall%20False%20Memories%20Happen%20and%20That%20s%20What%20Matters.pdf)). Murphy and Ciara Greene answered with "Who Got Lost in the Mall? Challenges in Counting and Classifying False Memories" ([University College Cork, record of the reply, Applied Cognitive Psychology 39(2), e70044, 2025](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/who-got-lost-in-the-mall-challenges-in-counting-and-classifying-f/)), and Zsofia Szojka, Stephanie Block and David La Rooy asked "whether the definition and measurement of false memories within laboratory experiments can be meaningfully applied to real-life debates concerning justice for alleged victims and perpetrators of sexual abuse" ([Szojka, Block and La Rooy, Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2025](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/50722/8/50722%20SZOJKA_Is_It_Time_To_Leave_The_Shopping_Mall_Behind_Measurement_Flaws_Plausibility_And_External_Validity_%28OA%29_2025.pdf)). Andrews and Brewin's paper is dated 2024 by Szojka and colleagues and "in press" by Wade and colleagues; Royal Holloway announced it in January 2025. <Claim status="established" by="by every study in the table">Suggestion can produce some false recollection in some people</Claim>; <Claim status="disputed" by="by Andrews and Brewin against Wade, Loftus and others">how often it produces full, confident memories of events that never happened</Claim> is the open question.

### Repressed and recovered memories: the memory wars

A separate question is whether real trauma can be completely forgotten and later recovered. Lawrence Patihis, Elizabeth Loftus and colleagues found in 2014 "less belief in repressed memory among mainstream clinicians today compared with the 1990s", but "a substantial gap between the memory beliefs of clinical-psychology researchers and those of practitioners" ([Patihis et al., Psychological Science, 2014](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=24335599)). The exchange that followed carried its positions in its titles: Brewin and Andrews, "Why it is scientifically respectable to believe in repression", and Patihis and colleagues, "Unconscious repressed memory is scientifically questionable" ([Brewin and Andrews, 2014](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=25134717); [Patihis et al., 2014](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=25134716)). In 2019 Henry Otgaar and colleagues argued that the memory wars "have not vanished", reporting that belief in repressed memories "occurs on a nontrivial scale (58%)" and that "the scientifically controversial concept of dissociative amnesia, which we argue is a substitute term for memory repression, has gained in popularity" ([Otgaar et al., Perspectives on Psychological Science, 2019](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6826861/fullTextXML)). Proponents of dissociative amnesia point to its inclusion in the diagnostic manual of the American Psychiatric Association, as Otgaar and colleagues themselves note. The two sides agree that suggestive therapy can do harm; they disagree on how often memories recovered in therapy are true.

## Recovered memories and children's testimony in court

In 1994 a California court awarded Gary Ramona nearly $500,000 in a malpractice suit against two therapists who had treated his adult daughter, whose accusations of childhood abuse against him emerged during therapy for an eating disorder; The Christian Science Monitor reported it as "the first time a court has ruled that such therapists owe a 'duty of care' to a nonpatient", noted that Ramona "denies the allegations" and that his daughter "has not recanted her accusations", and quoted the False Memory Syndrome Foundation as saying it had received about 14,000 complaints from people who said they had been falsely accused since its founding in 1992 ([The Christian Science Monitor, 25 July 1994](https://www.csmonitor.com/1994/0725/25132.html)).

The child cases turned on interviewing more than on recovered memory. The Kern County exonerations and the New Zealand judgment above both rested on findings that repeated, suggestive questioning and contamination had made children's testimony unreliable. Brazil wrote such safeguards into law in 2017. Law 13.431 created the *depoimento especial*, a hearing of child victims and witnesses that "reger-se-á por protocolos e, sempre que possível, será realizado uma única vez" (shall follow protocols and, whenever possible, take place only once), in which "é assegurada à criança ou ao adolescente a livre narrativa sobre a situação de violência" (the child is guaranteed a free narrative of the violence), with complementary questions vetted by the judge and the session recorded on audio and video ([Lei nº 13.431, de 4 de abril de 2017, Senado Federal](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/17674073/publicacao/17674082)). The 2018 decree that regulates it requires that the special hearing "será regido por protocolo de oitiva" (shall follow a hearing protocol) and that professionals prefer "questionamentos mínimos e estritamente necessários" (minimal, strictly necessary questioning) ([Decreto nº 9.603, de 10 de dezembro de 2018, Senado Federal](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/30735080/publicacao/30735091)).

False accusations entered Brazilian family law by another route, and that route is contested. The 2010 law on *alienação parental* (parental alienation) lists among its examples "apresentar falsa denúncia contra genitor" (making a false accusation against a parent) to obstruct contact with the child ([Lei nº 12.318, de 26 de agosto de 2010, Senado Federal](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/585315/publicacao/15753615)). The United Nations Special Rapporteur on violence against women and girls, Reem Alsalem, called parental alienation a "pseudo-concept" in 2023, wrote that it is used against mothers who report abuse, and cited a Brazilian study in which women were accused of it in 66% of cases and men in 17% ([United Nations, A/HRC/53/36, 13 April 2023](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/HRC/53/36&l=en&t=pdf)). Brazil's Congress amended the law rather than repealing it: Law 14.340 of 18 May 2022 changed its procedures and required that children be heard under the 2017 law ([Lei nº 14.340, de 18 de maio de 2022, Senado Federal](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/35862908/publicacao/35863379)).

## The Escola Base case, São Paulo, 1994

### The accusation

The facts below are those set out by the São Paulo courts, as quoted in a 2013 judgment of the Superior Tribunal de Justiça (STJ). In March 1994 two mothers of pupils at the Escola de Educação Infantil Base, in the Aclimação district of São Paulo, told the police that their four-year-old children had been sexually abused. The accused were the owners, Icushiro Shimada and Maria Aparecida Shimada, their partners Paula and Maurício Alvarenga, and a couple whose child attended the school. "De fato, em 28 de março de 1994, toda a imprensa brasileira, incitada pelo delegado que cuidava do caso, divulgou uma série de matérias" (on 28 March 1994 the whole Brazilian press, incited by the police chief in charge, published a series of reports). What followed, in the words the STJ quoted, was "uma desenfreada credulidade nas 'denúncias' feitas pelas mães e nos depoimentos de crianças de 4 anos, tomando-se por verdadeiro tudo que era dito" (unbridled credulity in the mothers' accusations and in the statements of four-year-olds, taking everything said as true), with headlines about the "escolinha do sexo" (little sex school) ([Superior Tribunal de Justiça, REsp 1.215.294/SP, judged 17 December 2013, published 11 February 2014, copy published by Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf)).

The medical evidence did not support the accusation: the same account says the injuries found "poderiam ser atribuídas tanto à violência sexual como a problemas intestinais" (could be attributed to sexual violence or to intestinal problems). The accused were arrested and later released; the school was looted and vandalized; the owners went bankrupt and received anonymous death threats; and the police inquiry was closed "por absoluta falta de elementos de convicção desfavoráveis aos investigados" (for an absolute lack of evidence against those investigated) ([STJ, REsp 1.215.294/SP](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf)). One evening paper of the Folha group ran the front-page headline "Perua escolar carregava as crianças para a orgia" (school van carried the children to the orgy), according to the São Paulo court that later condemned its publisher ([Consultor Jurídico, 28 May 2008](https://www.conjur.com.br/2008-mai-28/jornal_condenado_indenizar_escola_base//?print=1)).

The journalist Alex Ribeiro, whose 1995 book on the case the court cited, described the press as driven by a "compulsão por denunciar" (compulsion to denounce) and concluded that "a simples retratação não corrige danos morais causados pela publicação de informações incorretas" (a simple retraction does not repair the moral damage done by publishing incorrect information) ([STJ, REsp 1.215.294/SP](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf)). The reporter who first broadcast the accusations, Valmir Salaro of TV Globo, said in 2022, when a documentary revisited the case: "Eu errei, assumi minha parcela do erro. Foi um erro em conjunto" (in English, "I made a mistake, I took my share of the blame. It was a collective mistake") ([Diário do Nordeste, 9 November 2022](https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/ultima-hora/pais/caso-escola-base-vira-tema-de-documentario-do-globoplay-relembre-historia-1.3298968)).

The judges disagreed about where the blame lay, and the defendants' arguments are part of the record. In the IstoÉ appeal one judge, Testa Marchi, found no damage because the reporting had not been exaggerated and argued that the blame for the "linchamento moral" (moral lynching) lay with the police authority ([Consultor Jurídico, 2006](https://www.conjur.com.br/2006-mai-23/istoe_condenada_pagar_120_mil_donos_escola_base//?print=1)). SBT (Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão) argued that "a mídia, seja radiofundida ou escrita, não inventou o caso" (the media did not invent the case) and that "Se a mídia noticiou, foi com base em informações prestadas pela Polícia" (if the media reported it, it was on the basis of information supplied by the police); Folha da Manhã said its headline merely reproduced official information ([STJ, REsp 1.215.294/SP](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf); [Consultor Jurídico, 2008](https://www.conjur.com.br/2008-mai-28/jornal_condenado_indenizar_escola_base//?print=1)). The first-instance judge in the SBT case, by contrast, found that the reports had attributed personal responsibility "de forma prematura e subjetiva, independentemente do término das investigações oficiais" (prematurely and subjectively, regardless of the end of the official investigations) ([Consultor Jurídico, 24 May 2006](https://www.conjur.com.br/2006-mai-24/sbt_condenado_pagar_900_mil_indenizacao//?print=1)).

Andrighi's vote in the SBT case, whose reduction the rapporteur then adopted, set out a rule of proportion: the press had not published a lie, since the story came from a police inquiry, but had committed "o abuso do direito de informar, transformando a ocorrência num espetáculo midiático" (abuse of the right to inform, turning the event into a media spectacle); since the state had been condemned for the "comportamento leviano e precipitado" (reckless and hasty behaviour) of the police chief, the broadcaster should not pay more than "o causador direto do dano" (the direct cause of the harm), and she fixed R$100,000 each, 40% of the state's award ([STJ, REsp 1.215.294/SP](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf)). The SBT suit had nearly been lost at the start: the first judge had dismissed it as out of time under article 56 of the press law of 1967, before the state court sent it back ([STJ, REsp 1.215.294/SP](https://www.migalhas.com.br/arquivos/2014/2/art20140219-07.pdf)).

### Payment, and the people

Winning was not being paid. In May 2006 Consultor Jurídico reported that after more than eleven years none of those involved had received any money, that the state had appealed at every level and had been fined by the STF for bad-faith litigation ([Consultor Jurídico, 23 May 2006](https://www.conjur.com.br/2006-mai-23/istoe_condenada_pagar_120_mil_donos_escola_base//?print=1); [Consultor Jurídico, 24 May 2006](https://www.conjur.com.br/2006-mai-24/sbt_condenado_pagar_900_mil_indenizacao//?print=1)). In 2012 the teacher and coordinator Paula Milhim was still waiting for an award of R$250,000 won in the São Paulo courts, according to a report republished by Bahia Notícias ([Bahia Notícias, 22 March 2012](https://www.bahianoticias.com.br/justica/noticia/43281-professora-da-escola-base-ainda-nao-sabe-quando-vai-receber-indenizacao)). By November 2022, when a documentary revisited the case, both owners had died ([Diário do Nordeste, 2022](https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/ultima-hora/pais/caso-escola-base-vira-tema-de-documentario-do-globoplay-relembre-historia-1.3298968)). When each payment was finally made is not shown in the sources read.

## What to remember

- Child sexual abuse is common, mostly committed by family members and acquaintances, and mostly unreported; in Brazil only about one in ten rapes reaches the police.
- The FBI's specialist found no evidence of organized Satanic cults in the hundreds of cases he reviewed, while noting that some abuse in those cases was corroborated.
- McMartin ended in 1990 without a conviction after seven years; in Kern County 20 people sent to prison were exonerated; New Zealand quashed Peter Ellis's convictions in 2022; Paraná's courts acquitted the Guaratuba defendants in 2023 after evidence of torture.
- Implantation studies show that suggestion can create false recollections in some people; how often they amount to full memories is disputed, and the dispute reached the raw data in 2025.
- In Escola Base, the STJ fixed R$250,000 per plaintiff against the state and lower amounts against the press, reasoning that the police were the direct cause and the press abused the right to inform; payments took years.
- Brazilian rules now make the presumption of innocence a journalistic principle, punish investigators who attribute guilt in the media before charges, and require protocol-based, single hearings of children.
