In the 1990s about 1,400 floor traders worked the trading floor of the São Paulo stock exchange, the Bolsa de Valores de São Paulo (Bovespa), and about 1,200 more the floor of the futures exchange, the Bolsa de Mercadorias & Futuros (BM&F); in April 2025 their successor, B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão), said an order took about 350 microseconds to travel through its trading system ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm); [B3, glossary, 23 July 2025](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/glossario/b3-s-a)). B3 also says the floor handled 10,000 to 15,000 trades a day, against an average of 8.4 million a day now, with a peak of 18 million ([B3 Bora Investir, 30 September 2025](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/noticias/fim-do-pregao-viva-voz-faz-20-anos-e-b3-celebra-historia-com-exposicao/)).

This article opens the series **Electronic trading**, which follows that change and the industry built around it. After reading, you will understand why each element of a modern market exists (the venues, the brokers, the investors whose orders move, the protocols, the networks, the vendors and the regulators), you will be able to place the key dates of the move from voices to machines, and you will know which part of the series treats each element in depth. Every fact below links to the document it comes from, with that document's date where it has one; claims made by companies about their own products are attributed to them.

## From voices to microseconds: the arc in dates

The story begins with people meeting face to face. The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) traces its origins to an agreement signed by 24 stockbrokers on 17 May 1792 that set commissions and rules for trading ([NYSE, history page, undated](https://www.nyse.com/history-of-nyse)); the historian Peter Eisenstadt calls the familiar account of that founding "largely legendary" ([Eisenstadt, Prospects, October 1994](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/prospects/article/how-the-buttonwood-tree-grew-the-making-of-a-new-york-stock-exchange-legend/68D4F4577C720CFC004C6BB6076D46F6)). The Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT) was founded on 3 April 1848 as a cash market for grain, and forward contracts began trading there almost immediately, according to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the United States (US) futures regulator ([CFTC, history before 1974, undated](https://www.cftc.gov/About/HistoryoftheCFTC/history_precftc.html)). The next part, [Open outcry: trading floors and the pregão](/learn/open-outcry-trading-floors-and-the-pregao), tells that floor era in detail.

Three numbers show the speed of the change. When the Globex system started in 1992 its operator announced responses within three seconds, and by 31 March 2002 the average time to get a message in and out of its match engine was 0.16 seconds ([CME, 25 June 2002](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/cme-celebrates-10th-anniversary-of-globexsup-sup-electronic-trading-on-heels-of--2011124/)). The NYSE's average execution time for small marketable orders fell from 10.1 seconds in January 2005 to 0.7 seconds in October 2009, according to the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), and by then some exchanges published average response times under one millisecond: 320 microseconds at BATS Exchange in June 2009 and 294 at Nasdaq in December 2009 ([SEC, concept release, 21 January 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)). B3's figure for its own trading system was 1.2 milliseconds in 2023 and about 350 microseconds in 2025, with a stated goal of under 300 microseconds by 2026 ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)).

## Why each element exists

A market is a meeting place, rules for who may meet there, a way to agree a price, and a way to make sure that what was agreed is delivered and paid. Every element of electronic trading answers one of those needs. The paragraphs below take them one at a time.

### Venues: where orders meet

A venue is where buy and sell interest meets and becomes a trade. On the old floors that meeting was physical: the NYSE assigned each stock to a trading post on an open floor ([NYSE, undated](https://www.nyse.com/history-of-nyse)), and in Chicago each commodity had its own pit ([StoneX, undated](https://futures.stonex.com/futures-options-101/trading-pit)). B3's own museum describes the exchange today as one of the world's main financial market infrastructure companies, responsible for trading systems and for clearing, settlement, depository and registration of assets ([MUB3, June 2025](https://mub3.org.br/docs/Caderno%20do%20Professor%20-%20Site.pdf)).

Electronic trading did not keep one venue per market. The SEC described the US equity market of 2009 as "dispersed and complex": registered exchanges executed about 63.8% of share volume, with no single exchange above 19.4%, electronic communication networks (ECNs) about 10.8%, dark pools about 7.9% and more than 200 broker-dealers trading internally about 17.5% ([SEC, 21 January 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)). The same document records that the NYSE executed about 79.1% of consolidated volume in its own listed stocks in January 2005 and 25.1% in October 2009 ([SEC, 21 January 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)). How the screens took over and why venues multiplied are the subjects of [The screens arrive](/learn/the-screens-arrive-electronic-exchanges-from-nasdaq-to-globex) and [The rules that rewired markets](/learn/the-rules-that-rewired-markets-reg-ats-reg-nms-mifid-and-brazil).

### Brokers and dealers: who may touch the market

Most investors cannot send an order straight to an exchange; a member firm does it for them. B3's museum defines brokerage firms (corretoras) as companies authorized to mediate the purchase and sale of financial products on the exchange ([MUB3, June 2025](https://mub3.org.br/docs/Caderno%20do%20Professor%20-%20Site.pdf)). Before 1986 the London Stock Exchange kept two roles strictly apart, brokers acting as agents for investors and dealers called jobbers making prices from their own books, an arrangement known as single capacity ([Bank of England, December 1985](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1985/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf); [Bank of England, February 1987](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1987/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf)). At the NYSE, US law allowed a member registered as a specialist to act as both broker and dealer, under an obligation to help maintain a fair and orderly market ([SEC, 24 October 2008](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2008-10-29/pdf/E8-25797.pdf)).

The broker's gatekeeping role became more important when the orders were machines'. On 1 August 2012 a routing system at Knight Capital, a US broker-dealer, sent millions of orders in 45 minutes while processing 212 small retail orders, and the firm lost over $460 million; the SEC found that Knight had broken the rule requiring brokers with market access to control its risks, a rule adopted in November 2010, and fined it $12 million ([SEC, 16 October 2013](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf)). A later part, on pre-trade risk and market access, returns to that rule.

### The buy side: the investors whose orders move

On the other side of the broker are the investors, above all institutions that manage money, such as pension funds and fund managers ([Bank of England, February 1987](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1987/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf)): the buy side, in the industry's vocabulary. The FIX Trading Community, the standards body of the industry, lists its membership as "the buy side, sell side, market infrastructure operators, financial technology firms, consultants, regulators, and other industry associations" ([FIX Trading Community, undated](https://www.fixtrading.org/who-we-are/)). Their weight drove change: the Bank of England traced the pressure on London's old rules partly to investors seeking an international spread, citing the US Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 and the end of British exchange controls in 1979 ([Bank of England, December 1985](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1985/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf)). After the Big Bang, its survey found commissions on equity deals of £100,000 to £1 million had fallen from about 0.4% to 0.2%, and many institutions dealt directly with market makers on a net-of-commission basis, paying no separate commission ([Bank of England, February 1987](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1987/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf)).

### Protocols: a common language

Once orders travelled between computers, firms needed a common format for the messages: the FIX Trading Community recalls that trades used to be executed by telephone and manual processes ([FIX Trading Community, 16 July 2024](https://www.marketsmedia.com/fix-trading-community-celebrates-30-years/)). It says the FIX protocol was developed by Bob Lamoureux and Jacques Perold of Fidelity Investments and Chris Morstatt and Jim Leman of Salomon Brothers to carry equity trading data electronically, that the first public version, FIX 2.7, came out in 1994, and that it later spread to derivatives, fixed income and foreign exchange ([FIX Trading Community, 16 July 2024](https://www.marketsmedia.com/fix-trading-community-celebrates-30-years/)). Kevin Houstoun's account in The Trade dates the first production use to 1993 and the release of FIX 2.7 to 1995, and says it began as an experiment between Salomon Brothers and Fidelity ([The Trade, 29 October 2020](https://www.thetradenews.com/the-long-journey-to-frictionless-electronic-trading/)). The FIX Trading Community describes itself as the custodian of the protocol and says it is used by well over 10,000 firms sending billions of messages a day ([FIX Trading Community, undated](https://www.fixtrading.org/who-we-are/)).

Speed then pushed exchanges towards compact binary formats: B3 credits part of its 2024 latency cut to adopting an international binary protocol ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)). The FIX parts of this series start with [The FIX protocol: origins, versions and the community](/learn/the-fix-protocol-origins-versions-and-the-community) and [Anatomy of a FIX message and the session layer](/learn/anatomy-of-a-fix-message-and-the-session-layer).

### Networks: getting the message there

A protocol needs a path, and vendors sell hubs and private networks that put many counterparties behind one connection. Raptor Trading Systems, for example, sells what it calls "a single, cross-asset FIX hub connecting networks and counterparties" ([Raptor Trading Systems, undated](https://raptortrading.com/)); Pico says its managed infrastructure and private network, PicoNet, connect more than 900 exchange and venue products across more than 55 data centres ([Pico, undated](https://www.pico.net/)). For speed, distance matters too: B3 offers co-location, hosting clients' equipment very close to its data centre in São Paulo state ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)). [FIX networks: hubs and managed connectivity](/learn/fix-networks-hubs-and-managed-connectivity) and [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency) cover these layers.

### Vendors: who builds the machinery

Vendors sell much of this machinery to the firms that trade, and the firms the series looks at describe themselves this way, on their own pages read on 15 November 2026:

- **Raptor Trading Systems** says it "delivers the highest-performing electronic trading tools to buy-side and sell-side firms worldwide", with order and execution management, routing, connectivity, pre-trade and post-trade risk checks, reporting and surveillance, and that it has spent "over two decades" doing so ([Raptor Trading Systems, undated](https://raptortrading.com/)).
- **ICAP** calls itself "a world-leading intermediary in wholesale financial, energy, and commodities markets" that combines voice brokers with electronic platforms, operating in 22 countries; its site carries a TP ICAP copyright notice ([ICAP, undated](https://icap.com/)).
- **Pico** says it was founded in 2009, is headquartered in New York and grew through acquisitions including Corvil (network analytics) and Redline Trading Solutions (low-latency software); it describes Corvil as its packet-level analytics platform, which "captures every packet" and computes nanosecond-resolution latency for every order ([Pico, undated](https://www.pico.net/)).

Brazilian technology providers appear in the part on Brazil, as their own pages describe them. A later part covers order management systems (OMS) and execution management systems (EMS) and the firms that sell them, and another covers network-derived analytics and monitoring.

### Clearing and settlement: making sure the trade completes

A trade agreed on a floor or in a matching engine is a promise; clearing and settlement turn it into delivered securities and paid money, with the clearing house standing between the parties as central counterparty in the systems that matter most ([Banco Central do Brasil, November 2002](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/ref/200211/RELESTAB2002-ref200201c3p.pdf)). Chicago had a clearing organization for CBOT contracts in 1883 ([CFTC, undated](https://www.cftc.gov/About/HistoryoftheCFTC/history_precftc.html)). Paper failed when volume rose: in the late 1960s back offices of US brokers could not keep up, failures to deliver soared, and about 160 NYSE members failed in that period, according to a study by Seligman (2003) cited by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) ([BIS, December 2007](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt0712z.htm)). The Depository Trust Company (DTC), today part of DTCC, was created in 1973 to solve that paperwork crisis ([DTCC, 2023](https://www.dtcc.com/insights/2023/celebrating-dtccs-50th-anniversary)). In Brazil the reform of the payments system put interbank transfers on real-time gross settlement from 22 April 2002 and required systemically important clearing houses to act as central counterparty, with equity trades settling up to three business days after the trade ([Banco Central do Brasil, November 2002](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/ref/200211/RELESTAB2002-ref200201c3p.pdf)).

### Regulators: who writes the rules

The floors regulated themselves first: the NYSE's constitution of 1817 imposed fines "to keep disorderly brokers in check" ([NYSE, undated](https://www.nyse.com/history-of-nyse)), and an Illinois charter of 1859 gave the CBOT self-regulatory authority over its members ([CFTC, undated](https://www.cftc.gov/About/HistoryoftheCFTC/history_precftc.html)). Public regulators followed. Congress created the SEC in 1934 ([NYSE, undated](https://www.nyse.com/history-of-nyse)) and the CFTC by an act signed in October 1974 ([CFTC, 1970s history, undated](https://www.cftc.gov/About/HistoryoftheCFTC/history_1970s.html)); Brazil's securities regulator, the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM), was created on 7 December 1976 by Law 6.385 ([CVM, undated](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao-cvm/institucional/sobre-a-cvm)), and the Banco Central do Brasil oversees the payments and settlement system ([Banco Central do Brasil, November 2002](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/ref/200211/RELESTAB2002-ref200201c3p.pdf)). In the European Union, the second Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID II) requires trading venues and their members to synchronize the business clocks they use to record reportable events ([EU, Directive 2014/65/EU, 15 May 2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065)) and has applied since 3 January 2018 ([EU, Directive 2016/1034, 23 June 2016](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016L1034)).

## What the machines changed, and what is still argued

Some results of automation are not in dispute. Brokers' commissions and the cost of searching for a price fell in London after 1986: the Bank of England found commissions halved for large institutional deals and said the firm prices on the Stock Exchange Automated Quotations (SEAQ) screens lowered the cost and risk of searching for the best deal ([Bank of England, February 1987](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1987/change-in-the-stock-exchange-and-regulation-of-the-city.pdf)). Capacity rose by orders of magnitude, as the B3 figures at the top of this article show ([B3 Bora Investir, 30 September 2025](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/noticias/fim-do-pregao-viva-voz-faz-20-anos-e-b3-celebra-historia-com-exposicao/)).

Other effects are measured differently by different studies. Kumar Venkataraman compared similar stocks on the automated Paris Bourse and the floor-based NYSE and found execution costs higher in Paris, by 14 basis points after controls, concluding that automated systems "may not be able to fully replicate the benefits of human intermediation on a trading floor" ([Venkataraman, Journal of Finance, 2001](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:56:y:2001:i:4:p:1445-1485)). Terrence Hendershott and Pamela Moulton found that the NYSE's Hybrid Market of late 2006, which cut execution time for market orders from 10 seconds to under one, raised bid-ask spreads through adverse selection but made prices more efficient ([Hendershott and Moulton, Journal of Financial Markets, November 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v14y2011i4p568-604.html)).

Speed also brought new failure modes: on 6 May 2010 major US indices "plummeted a further 5-6% in a matter of minutes before rebounding almost as quickly", after a large trader started an automated sale of 75,000 E-Mini futures contracts worth about $4.1 billion, according to the staffs of the CFTC and the SEC ([CFTC and SEC staffs, 30 September 2010](https://www.sec.gov/news/studies/2010/marketevents-report.pdf)). High-frequency traders are central to that argument: B3 says they account for about 40% of trades in the Brazilian equity market and <Claim status="attributed" by="by B3">play a relevant role in generating liquidity</Claim> ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)), while the European Union's MiFID II says high-frequency trading may "prompt investors to choose to execute trades in venues where they can avoid interaction with high-frequency traders" ([EU, Directive 2014/65/EU, 15 May 2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065)). [High-frequency trading: what it is and the dispute](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) sets out the evidence on both sides.

Even the floor is not fully gone: after the NYSE closed its floor on 23 March 2020 and traded fully electronically, it said data showed that <Claim status="attributed" by="by the NYSE">operating with the Trading Floor provides investors the highest level of market quality</Claim>, and it partially reopened the floor on 26 May 2020 ([NYSE, undated](https://www.nyse.com/history-of-nyse)). CME Group kept its Eurodollar options pit open when it decided in May 2021 not to reopen its other pits ([CME Group, 4 May 2021](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2021/5/04/cme_group_to_permanentlyclosemostopenoutcrytradingpitseurodollar.html)).

## The Brazilian thread

Brazil went through the same change on its own dates: Bovespa's open-outcry floor, whose history B3 dates to 1895, closed on 30 September 2005 ([B3 Bora Investir, 30 September 2025](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/noticias/fim-do-pregao-viva-voz-faz-20-anos-e-b3-celebra-historia-com-exposicao/)). At BM&F, the electronic system introduced in 2000 handled just over 3% of contracts that year, passed the floor in 2006 with 57.59% and, after side-by-side trading began in 2007, reached 72.95%; by June 2009 it carried over 98% of the 22 million contracts traded, and the exchange then ended floor trading ([BM&FBOVESPA, 30 June 2009](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/trading-in-the-bmandf-segment-at-bmandfbovespa-to-become-fully-electronic-as-of--2011124/)). The last floor trade was ten Ibovespa futures contracts, sold at 5:14 p.m. on 30 June 2009 ([BM&FBOVESPA, 1 July 2009](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/the-last-open-outcry-transaction-is-carried-out-at-bmandfbovespa-2011124/)). A B3 article of 2025 places the end of BM&F's floor "em meados de 2006", in mid-2006 ([B3, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)), which does not match the exchange's own announcements of 2009 quoted above.

The same 2009 announcement promised three levels of direct market access (DMA) by the end of that year, co-location among them ([BM&FBOVESPA, 30 June 2009](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/trading-in-the-bmandf-segment-at-bmandfbovespa-to-become-fully-electronic-as-of--2011124/)). Bovespa and BM&F combined in 2008 and joined Cetip in 2017 to form B3 ([B3, 23 July 2025](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/glossario/b3-s-a)). [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access) follows the platforms, the access models and the participants.

## What to remember

- A market needs a place to meet, rules for who may enter, a way to agree prices and a way to complete trades; venues, brokers, protocols, networks, clearing houses and regulators each answer one of those needs.
- The move from floors to screens took decades and happened market by market: London in 1986, Bovespa in 2005, the NYSE in 2006, BM&F in 2009, most CME futures pits in 2015.
- Response times fell from seconds (Globex's three-second promise in 1992, the NYSE's 10.1 seconds in 2005) to microseconds (B3's 350 in 2025).
- Automation cut commissions and raised capacity; its effect on spreads, price efficiency and stability is measured differently by different studies and remains argued.
- Company claims about their own products are claims; this series attributes them.

<ReaderQuestions>

- If a floor made every trader visible to every other, what replaced that visibility when trading moved into matching engines, and is it enough?

- When B3 measures its latency in microseconds, which investors gain from each microsecond saved, and which do not?
- The NYSE says its floor still improves market quality: what evidence would settle whether people on a floor add something machines cannot?
- Fragmentation gave investors more venues to choose from and more places to look for a price: is that more competition or more complexity, and for whom?
