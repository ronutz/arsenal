The two-exam path to F5 Certified Technology Specialist, BIG-IP Local Traffic Manager (F5-CTS, LTM) is 301A - Architect, Setup and Deploy (blueprint 301a.62021) and 301B - Maintain and Troubleshoot (blueprint 301b.102017), each 90 minutes. The newer track (beta blueprint F5CTSLTM.032026.BETA) divides the same ground into six modular exams. Lay the 301 blueprints beside the new one and the verdict is almost boring: this is a faithful restructure. Thirteen of the twenty 301A objectives reappear in the new Base Configuration and Virtual Server exams; its upgrade, high availability group, user role and Virtualized Clustered Multiprocessing (vCMP) objectives moved to the Upgrades exam, its iRule and iApp objectives to the iRules exam, and its security-requirements objective was folded into the objectives on securing Self IPs, virtual servers and administrative access. Twenty-one of the twenty-seven 301B objectives reappear in the iRules, Upgrades, and the two Packet Capture exams; its profile, consolidation and monitor objectives went to the Virtual Server exam, and its log-interpretation objective to Base Configuration. Example bullets came along, often word for word. If you prepared for the 301 pair, you prepared for this track.

## Where the 301 pair stands

Read on 26 October 2026, F5's own pages do not call 301A and 301B retired. F5's exam catalogue lists both as bookable, test-centre proctored, 90-minute exams, each still described as one of the two exams of the F5-CTS, LTM certification, with no end date ([301a exam page](https://education.f5.com/exams/big-ip-ltm-specialist-architect-setup-and-deploy-301a), [301b exam page](https://education.f5.com/exams/big-ip-ltm-specialist-maintain-and-troubleshoot-301b), both read 26 October 2026). Both pages still link their exam blueprints (301a.62021 and 301b.102017), which describe the same two-exam route: 301A awards no credential on its own and is the prerequisite to 301B, and neither blueprint states an end date. The same [exam catalogue](https://education.f5.com/exams) lists the six beta exams, F5CTSLTM1-B to F5CTSLTM6-B, each described as one of six exams required for that same credential. F5's pages therefore contradict each other on what the credential requires, and they do not say how the two routes relate or whether the 301 pair will be withdrawn. The one related change F5 does state is about renewal: passing 301B is no longer required to renew the credential, which now has its own recertification exam (F5CTSLTMR). Until F5 says more, treat both routes as listed and confirm on F5's exam pages before booking.

Which makes the handful of genuine differences worth naming precisely.

## What the new track dropped

**Serial and hardwired failover.** 301A asked you to describe the differences between network failover and hardware failover; 301B asked for the relationship between serial and network failover. Both bullets are gone - the new track discusses only network failover, unicast versus multicast. The removal mirrors the platforms: the dedicated failover cable belongs to hardware generations that are aging out. But paired appliances with serial failover still exist in the field, and knowing that a hardwired pair detects peer death independently of the network - and what that means when the network itself is the thing failing - remains a sharp diagnostic instinct. The [failover article](/learn/bigip-failover-states-and-operations) keeps the concept alive alongside the modern material.

**ssldump.** 301B names three protocol analyzers - Wireshark, tcpdump, ssldump; the new blueprint names two. ssldump, the classic on-box tool for reading Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) handshakes from a capture and key file, fell off the list as modern Transport Layer Security (TLS) and forward secrecy narrowed what it can decrypt and Wireshark's decryption workflow matured. It still earns its keep for handshake-level triage on the device - reading the hello exchange, the chosen cipher, the alert that killed the connection - none of which needs decryption at all. Capture safely per the [tcpdump safety article](/learn/bigip-tcpdump-safety), and let the [tcpdump builder](/tools/f5-bigip-tcpdump-builder) shape the capture.

**The three-technologies synthesis.** 301A closed its access-control objective with a bullet the new track dropped: summarize how user roles, administrative partitions, and route domains can be used together. The individual pieces all survive as separate objectives, but the synthesis - roles scope the verbs, partitions scope the objects, route domains scope the packets, and the three compose into full multi-tenancy - was the most instructive sentence in the old blueprint. The [route domains article](/learn/bigip-route-domains) preserves exactly that composition.

## What the new track added

The additions are the modern platform showing through: F5OS tenants get their own objective parallel to vCMP guests; In-TMM monitoring joins the monitor-troubleshooting objective; Telemetry stands alongside Application Visibility and Reporting (AVR) in the analytics exam; and the templates objective grows from iApps alone to iApps plus F5 Application Services Templates (FAST). Each addition is covered by the current study guides on this site, objective by objective.

## How to use the 301 blueprints

For this track, the 301 blueprints are corroboration more than supplement: they confirm the new exams test the same craft, reorganized. The three dropped items above are the entire meaningful delta - small enough to study in an afternoon, field-relevant enough that you should. Everything else the 301 pair asked, the new six ask too.
