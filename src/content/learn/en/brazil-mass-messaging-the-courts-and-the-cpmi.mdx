In October 2018, ten days before Brazil's presidential run-off, a newspaper reported that companies were buying packages of bulk WhatsApp messages against one of the two candidates. The report led to lawsuits before the electoral court that took three years to decide, to a joint congressional committee of inquiry, to lawsuits by WhatsApp against bulk-messaging firms, to new electoral rules and to a bill that stalled in the Chamber of Deputies. It also crossed paths with a Supreme Court investigation, opened in March 2019, whose evidence the electoral court later used. This part of the series opened by [the map from the small lie to the campaign](/learn/psychological-operations-from-the-small-lie-to-the-campaign) follows that record in order. The subject is politically contested in Brazil: every claim below is attributed, every court finding is given in the court's terms, and every named party's published answer is reported beside the claim. On fake accounts and bots in general, see [the previous part](/learn/bots-sockpuppets-and-astroturfing); on Brazil's internet law, see [Brazil's internet governance](/learn/brazil-internet-governance).

## A private app used at scale

WhatsApp describes itself as built for private conversation. In a white paper of 6 February 2019 it wrote that "WhatsApp was built for private messages", that it provides end-to-end encryption by default, "which means only the sender and recipient can see the content of messages", and that "Our service is not a broadcast platform"; it added that "Approximately 90% of the messages sent on WhatsApp are from one person to another, and the majority of groups have fewer than ten people" ([WhatsApp, Stopping Abuse: How WhatsApp Fights Bulk Messaging and Automated Behavior, 6 February 2019](https://www.internetlab.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/WA_StoppingAbuse_Whitepaper_020618-Final-1.pdf)). Encryption has been the default since 2016, the paper says, so the company focuses "on the behavior of accounts and user-reported content". It said <Claim status="reported" by="by WhatsApp">"We remove over two million accounts per month for bulk or automated behavior"</Claim>, "over 75% without a recent user report", and that these efforts "are particularly important during elections where certain groups may attempt to send messages at scale". Because sending mass messages from a phone is slow, it explained, bulk senders "often build hardware or software to try and automate the process".

Two design facts shape everything that follows. First, content travels encrypted between phones, so neither the company nor a court can read what was sent unless a participant hands it over. Second, the platform's limits on group size and forwarding are the main brakes on reach, and they changed during the period this part covers.

| Date | Limit or label announced by WhatsApp |
|---|---|
| 2018, before the changes | Groups of up to 256 members; a message could be forwarded to up to 20 people or groups, as the Folha de S.Paulo described the limits in October 2018 |
| 19 July 2018 | "Today, we're launching a test to limit forwarding that will apply to everyone using WhatsApp"; in India, a lower limit of five chats |
| 21 January 2019 | "all users on the latest versions of WhatsApp can now forward to only five chats at once" |
| 7 April 2020 | Highly forwarded messages, "labeled with double arrows", "can only be forwarded to one chat at a time" |

Sources: [Folha de S.Paulo, 18 October 2018](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/2018/10/empresarios-bancam-campanha-contra-o-pt-pelo-whatsapp.shtml); [WhatsApp, More changes to forwarding, 19 July 2018, updated 21 January 2019, read through a fetch tool](https://blog.whatsapp.com/more-changes-to-forwarding?lang=en); [WhatsApp, Keeping WhatsApp Personal and Private, 7 April 2020, read through a fetch tool](https://blog.whatsapp.com/Keeping-WhatsApp-Personal-and-Private).

WhatsApp said in January 2019 that "The forward limit significantly reduced forwarded messages around the world", and in April 2020 that after the earlier limit "we saw a 25% decrease in total message forwards globally". The architecture also limits evidence. WhatsApp told the electoral court on 20 November 2019 that it keeps user records for six months at most, and then only partially, so the court concluded that the content of messages, their reach and how they were received could not be established ([Tribunal Superior Eleitoral, judgment of 28 October 2021, headnote item 25](https://sjur-servicos.tse.jus.br/sjur-servicos/rest/download/pdf/2673321)).

## The Folha report, and the answers

On 18 October 2018 Patrícia Campos Mello reported in the Folha de S.Paulo that <Claim status="reported" by="by the Folha de S.Paulo">companies were buying packages of mass WhatsApp messages against the Partido dos Trabalhadores (PT, Workers' Party) and preparing a large operation for the week before the 28 October run-off, with contracts reaching R$12 million each and the retailer Havan among the buyers</Claim>; the newspaper wrote that the practice was illegal because it amounted to an undeclared campaign donation by companies, which electoral law forbids ([Folha de S.Paulo, 18 October 2018](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/2018/10/empresarios-bancam-campanha-contra-o-pt-pelo-whatsapp.shtml)). According to the report, companies supporting Jair Bolsonaro, then of the Social Liberal Party (PSL), bought a service called "disparo em massa" (mass sending), using either the candidate's own supporter lists or databases sold by digital agencies, the latter also illegal; it named Quickmobile, Yacows, Croc Services and SMS Market among the agencies, quoted prices of R$0.08 to R$0.12 per message to the candidate's own lists and R$0.30 to R$0.40 with an agency's list, and said agencies segmented by region and sometimes income. Former employees and clients told the newspaper that the campaign's digital team used automatically generated foreign numbers to run groups and escape WhatsApp's spam filters and limits; Folha wrote that it found no sign that the campaign's agency had contracted mass sending, and that estimates of the number of anti-PT groups, from 20,000 to 300,000, were "muito vagas" (very vague) because closed groups cannot be counted.

The answers were printed with the report or followed it:

- **Havan.** Its owner, Luciano Hang, asked whether he had used mass sending, said he did not know "o que é isso" (what that is): "Não temos essa necessidade", we have no such need; a live broadcast of his had reached 1.3 million people without paid boosting.
- **The agencies.** A partner of QuickMobile, Peterson Rosa, said the company was not working in politics that year and denied any contract for political content; a representative of Yacows said he would not comment; SMS Market did not respond. A partner of Croc Services, Pedro Freitas, first said "Quem tem de saber da legislação eleitoral é o candidato, não sou eu" ("it is the candidate who has to know electoral law, not me"), then said his firm sold mass-sending packages only to a candidate's own lists, which is legal.
- **The campaign's agency.** Marcos Aurélio Carvalho, a partner in AM4, the only digital firm in Bolsonaro's declared accounts (R$115,000), said "Os grupos são criados e nutridos organicamente", groups are created and nurtured organically by thousands of volunteers, and denied that his firm segmented users or tailored content (all in the same report).
- **The candidate.** Bolsonaro wrote on Twitter that "Apoio voluntário é algo que o PT desconhece e não aceita. Sempre fizeram política comprando consciências", voluntary support is something the PT neither knows nor accepts; it has always done politics by buying consciences ([Exame (Reuters), 18 October 2018](https://exame.com/brasil/apos-acusacao-bolsonaro-diz-que-pt-desconhece-apoio-voluntario/)). His campaign then asked the electoral court to investigate the newspaper and the PT ticket, arguing that the report cited R$12 million in contracts "sem apontar onde, como e com quem teriam sido firmados" (without saying where, how and with whom they were signed) and had been written to "instalar o caos no processo eleitoral"; the newspaper's lawyer, Luís Francisco Carvalho Filho, called the action "uma fantasia ridícula", an act of intimidation against a free press ([Exame, October 2018](https://exame.com/brasil/bolsonaro-entra-com-acao-no-tse-apos-denuncia-sobre-uso-do-whatsapp)).
- **The opponent.** The PT candidate, Fernando Haddad, said he would use every legal mechanism, alleged crimes including undeclared campaign funds and criminal organization, and said that "Em qualquer lugar do mundo isso seria um escândalo de proporções avassaladoras", anywhere in the world this would be an overwhelming scandal ([Exame (Reuters), 18 October 2018](https://exame.com/brasil/apos-acusacao-bolsonaro-diz-que-pt-desconhece-apoio-voluntario/)).

Three days later a judge of the electoral court, Sérgio Banhos, refused the Bolsonaro coalition's request to take the report down or grant a right of reply: "O simples fato de a referida matéria ser investigativa não altera a sua natureza jornalística", the mere fact that the article is investigative does not change its journalistic nature, and "não se deve, em regra, suprimir o direito à informação dos eleitores" ([Consultor Jurídico, 21 October 2018](https://www.conjur.com.br/2018-out-21/tse-nega-pedido-bolsonaro-tirar-ar-materia-folha/)). The same decision noted that the Prosecutor General's Office, the Procuradoria-Geral da República (PGR), had asked the Federal Police to investigate the possible use of professional schemes "por parte das campanhas de Jair Bolsonaro e Fernando Haddad", by both campaigns.

WhatsApp acted that month too: it sent extrajudicial notices to Quickmobile, Yacows, Croc Services and SMS Market and banned the accounts associated with them, according to a later summary of its actions ([A Gazeta, 9 June 2019](https://www.agazeta.com.br/brasil/whatsapp-vai-tomar-medidas-legais-contra-envio-de-mensagens-em-massa-0619)). In June 2019 the Folha reported that Brazilian companies had bought software from a Spanish firm, Enviawhatsapps, to send mass messages for Bolsonaro; its owner told the newspaper he did not know his software was used in Brazilian campaigns until WhatsApp cut his lines, and WhatsApp said it had sent the firm a cease-and-desist notice. Asked about it, President Bolsonaro said "Teve milhões de mensagens a favor da minha campanha, e talvez alguns milhões contra também" ("there were millions of messages for my campaign and perhaps some millions against it too") ([Diário de Pernambuco, 18 June 2019](https://www.diariodepernambuco.com.br/noticia/politica/2019/06/disparos-em-massa-pelo-whatsapp-foram-a-favor-e-contra-minha-campanha.html)).

## WhatsApp's 2019 statement and its lawsuits

In October 2019, at the Gabo journalism festival in Colombia, WhatsApp's manager for public policy and global elections, Ben Supple, said that <Claim status="attributed" by="by WhatsApp">during Brazil's 2018 election mass-messaging providers had violated WhatsApp's terms of use to reach large numbers of people</Claim>, according to the Folha de S.Paulo; asked whether campaigns broke the rules by using WhatsApp, he said they did not as long as the terms, which forbid automation and mass sending, were respected, and that everyone was subject to the same criteria, whether a presidential candidate or a farmer in rural India ([Folha de S.Paulo, English edition, 9 October 2019](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/internacional/en/brazil/2019/10/whatsapp-admits-to-illegal-mass-messaging-in-brazils-2018.shtml); [A Gazeta, 8 October 2019](https://www.agazeta.com.br/brasil/whatsapp-confirma-envio-ilegal-de-mensagens-em-massa-nas-eleicoes-1019)). He also criticized public groups joined through links used as broadcast lists for political content.

The company had already warned, in June 2019, that from 7 December 2019 it would take legal action against those engaged in or assisting abusive mass or automated messaging, even when the evidence came from outside the platform, such as advertisements for such services ([A Gazeta, 9 June 2019](https://www.agazeta.com.br/brasil/whatsapp-vai-tomar-medidas-legais-contra-envio-de-mensagens-em-massa-0619)). It then sued in Brazilian courts:

- **SallApp**, the first such suit in Brazil, and **Yacows**, the second. In April 2020 a São Paulo business court judge, Eduardo Palma Pellegrinelli, granted WhatsApp a preliminary injunction barring Yacows and related firms from offering or performing mass sending through WhatsApp and from using its marks, finding the unauthorized use of the marks "especialmente associado ao envio de mensagens em massa aos usuários" (especially tied to mass messaging of users) enough to show the probability of WhatsApp's right ([Consultor Jurídico, 10 April 2020](https://www.conjur.com.br/2020-abr-10/yacows-proibida-enviar-mensagens-massa-whatsasp/)); Yacows had 15 days to respond and had not commented when contacted ([A Gazeta, 9 April 2020](https://www.agazeta.com.br/brasil/justica-proibe-agencia-de-fazer-disparo-em-massa-pelo-whatsapp-0420)).
- **Message Flow**, and in November 2020 **Autland** and **VB Marketing**, which preliminary rulings barred from mass sending and from using WhatsApp's marks within 24 hours, under a daily fine of R$50,000; neither replied to the trade site that reported the decisions ([Mobile Time, 13 November 2020](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/13/11/2020/justica-whatsapp-disparo/)).

These are civil rulings on trademark, software and terms of service, most of them preliminary; they did not decide anything about any campaign.

## The electoral court: what it found and did not find

An ação de investigação judicial eleitoral (AIJE, electoral judicial investigation action) asks the Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE, Superior Electoral Court) to find abuse of economic power or misuse of the media under article 22 of Lei Complementar (supplementary law) 64/1990, which can lead to removal from office and ineligibility. Four AIJEs reached the court over the 2018 mass messaging. Two were judged on 9 February 2021 and dismissed; the headnote of one, quoted in a later vote, reads <Claim status="established" by="by the TSE">"ACUSAÇÃO AMPARADA EM CONJECTURAS. AUSÊNCIA DE PROVAS SEGURAS A VINCULAR A CAMPANHA ELEITORAL AOS SUPOSTOS DISPAROS. IMPROCEDÊNCIA"</Claim>: an accusation resting on conjecture, no secure evidence linking the campaign to the alleged sendings, dismissed ([TSE, judgment of 28 October 2021, vote quoting AIJE 0601779-05](https://sjur-servicos.tse.jus.br/sjur-servicos/rest/download/pdf/2673321)).

The other two, AIJEs 0601968-80 and 0601771-28, brought by the coalition O Povo Feliz de Novo, formed by the PT, the Partido Comunista do Brasil (PCdoB) and a third party, against President Bolsonaro, Vice-President Hamilton Mourão, Hang, the agencies and others, were judged together on 28 October 2021, with Justice Luis Felipe Salomão as rapporteur. The judgment, in the court's own headnote, contains both findings and a refusal.

**What the court said was proven.** WhatsApp Inc. told the court that in October 2018 it found the four agencies offering mass sending in breach of its terms, with advertised capacity of up to 75,000 sends a day, and identified concrete mass-sending behaviour by two of them during the campaign, leading to bans (item 14). Evidence shared from two Supreme Court inquiries (inquéritos, numbered INQ 4781 and INQ 4828) indicated, in the court's words, that "no mínimo desde 2017, pessoas próximas ao hoje Presidente da República atuavam de modo permanente, amplo e constante na mobilização digital de eleitores", with attacks on opponents and institutions as their method (item 15). And <Claim status="established" by="by the TSE">"O conjunto probatório das AIJEs 0601968-80 e 0601771-28 não deixa margem para dúvidas de que a campanha dos vencedores das eleições presidenciais de 2018 assumiu caráter preponderante nos meios digitais, mediante utilização indevida, dentre outros, do aplicativo de mensagens whatsapp para promover disparos em massa em benefício de suas candidaturas"</Claim>: the evidence left no doubt that the winning campaign relied mainly on digital means and misused WhatsApp, among others, for mass sending, through an organized structure of supporters and people close to the first defendant (item 17).

**What the court said was not proven.** Abuse requires "a gravidade das circunstâncias", the gravity of the circumstances. The evidence did not allow the court to assess (a) the content of the messages, including whether they were negative propaganda or false, (b) how they were received by voters, (c) their reach, (d) the candidates' degree of participation and (e) whether companies financed the campaign for that purpose (item 24). Although there were "indícios de ciência" (signs of knowledge) by the president, the lack of evidence on content and reach undermined that point (item 27); company financing could not be established "com segurança" (item 28); and most of the allegations rested on journalism which, "não obstante sua qualidade e seriedade", despite its quality and seriousness, did not by itself carry the force of proof for an electoral conviction (item 32). The court, unanimously, rejected the preliminary objections and dismissed both actions.

**The rule it set for the future.** By majority, with Justice Carlos Horbach dissenting on this point, the court approved a thesis: <Claim status="established" by="by the TSE, by majority">"o uso de aplicações digitais de mensagens instantâneas visando promover disparos em massa, contendo desinformação e inverdades, em prejuízo de adversário e em benefício de candidato, pode configurar abuso de poder econômico e o uso indevido dos meios de comunicação social"</Claim>, in the wording of the judgment: mass sending through messaging apps with disinformation and untruths against an opponent and in favour of a candidate can amount to abuse of economic power and misuse of the media.

The court's president, Luís Roberto Barroso, explained the result as he proclaimed it: there had been divergence on the reasoning, not on the outcome; "Parte dos ministros entenderam que havia prova dos fatos ilícitos, mas não havia provas da gravidade desses fatos, e alguns entenderam que não se conseguiu fazer um elo necessário entre os fatos graves e a chapa impugnada", some justices found proof of unlawful acts but not of their gravity, others found no necessary link between the acts and the ticket.

**The defences, as the judgment records them.** President Bolsonaro argued in December 2019 that WhatsApp's information was irrelevant, concerning only how marketing firms advertised their services, and asked for dismissal given an "absoluta carência de provas", a complete lack of evidence. Hang argued that WhatsApp's information showed he had no link with the agencies and that he never bought mass-sending packages. Two partners of Yacows denied taking part in the campaign or forwarding false news, and argued that evidence from INQ 4781 was void because that inquiry was unlawful (an argument taken up below). Other defendants said the actions rested on a single newspaper report.

## The CPMI das Fake News

The Comissão Parlamentar Mista de Inquérito (CPMI, joint parliamentary committee of inquiry) das Fake News was created by request RQN 11/2019 and installed on 4 September 2019, with a mandate to investigate, "no prazo de 180 dias", "os ataques cibernéticos que atentam contra a democracia e o debate público" and "a utilização de perfis falsos para influenciar os resultados das eleições 2018", as well as cyberbullying and the grooming of children for hate crimes and suicide ([Congresso Nacional, CPMI das Fake News committee page, read through a fetch tool](https://legis.senado.leg.br/comissoes/comissao?codcol=2292)). It was chaired by Senator Angelo Coronel, of the Partido Social Democrático (PSD), with Deputy Lídice da Mata, of the Partido Socialista Brasileiro (PSB), as rapporteur ([Congresso em Foco, 22 April 2020](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/legislativo/cpi-das-fake-news-e-prorrogada-por-tempo-indeterminado/)).

Among its sessions:

- **December 2019.** Deputy Joice Hasselmann alleged that a group she called the "Gabinete do Ódio" (hate office), made up of presidential aides and guided by the president's sons, spread false news and attacks, and urged the committee to "siga o rastro do dinheiro", follow the money ([IstoÉ, 4 December 2019](https://istoe.com.br/em-cpmi-joice-acusa-carlos-e-eduardo-bolsonaro-de-controlar-milicias-virtuais/)). The presidential palace said it would not comment ([A Gazeta (Folhapress), 4 December 2019](https://www.agazeta.com.br/brasil/joice-vai-a-cpi-e-diz-ter-ouvido-algo-sobre-abin-paralela-no-planalto-1219)). In 2021 the former head of the presidential communications secretariat, Fábio Wajngarten, told a Senate inquiry: "O único gabinete que eu conheço é o gabinete da Secretaria Especial de Comunicação" ("the only office I know is the communications secretariat's") ([DOL (Diário Online), 8 June 2021](https://dol.com.br/noticias/politica/656955/gabinete-do-odio-assessores-de-bolsonaro-admitem-atuacao)).
- **March 2020.** The committee heard the three partners of AM4 and received a document from Facebook that, as Congresso em Foco reported, pointed to the creation of an attack page from Eduardo Bolsonaro's office. Allied deputies attacked the committee's work: Caroline de Toni said "Estamos aqui perdendo o nosso tempo, jogando o dinheiro público no lixo" (we are wasting our time and public money), Carlos Jordy said Joice Hasselmann "não tem credibilidade alguma", and Éder Mauro asked what credibility "esses administradores esquerdistas do Facebook" (those leftist Facebook administrators) could have ([Congresso em Foco, 5 March 2020](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/18386/com-eduardo-na-mira-aliados-desqualificam-cpmi-das-fake-news)).
- **The Yacows partners.** The rapporteur later said that one of them had <Claim status="alleged" by="by the CPMI's rapporteur">"confessou à CPMI" the development of tools for mass messaging</Claim> ([Consultor Jurídico, 10 April 2020](https://www.conjur.com.br/2020-abr-10/yacows-proibida-enviar-mensagens-massa-whatsasp/)); before the electoral court the same partners denied taking part in the campaign or forwarding false news, saying their testimony to the CPMI had shown it ([TSE, judgment of 28 October 2021](https://sjur-servicos.tse.jus.br/sjur-servicos/rest/download/pdf/2673321)).

Sessions stopped on 17 March 2020 when the pandemic restricted access to Congress. In April 2020 the Senate accepted the chair's request to suspend the committee's deadline from 20 March until regular activity returned, a decision that came two days after Eduardo Bolsonaro asked the Supreme Court to block the committee's extension ([Congresso em Foco, 22 April 2020](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/legislativo/cpi-das-fake-news-e-prorrogada-por-tempo-indeterminado/)). Two years later it was still inactive. In March 2022 its chair said "Quando a eleição de 2018 era um dos temas da pauta, a CPMI fervia. Mas, depois que o TSE arquivou tudo, ela esfriou muito", when the 2018 election was on the agenda the committee was boiling, but after the electoral court shelved everything it cooled a great deal ([O Antagonista, 13 March 2022](https://oantagonista.com.br/?p=737128)). The rapporteur, speaking of "o enterro da CPMI" (its burial), said its practical result was the public debate it set off ([BNews, 18 April 2022](https://www.bnews.com.br/noticias/politica/lidice-da-mata-entende-que-cpmi-das-fake-news-teve-importancia-para-alem-do-legislativo.html)). The Congress page lists the committee as "Encerrada" (closed), records the suspension of its deadline as its last event and shows no vote on a final report.

## The Supreme Court's inquérito das fake news

On 14 March 2019 the president of the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF, Federal Supreme Court), Dias Toffoli, opened an inquiry on his own initiative into "notícias fraudulentas (fake news), denunciações caluniosas, ameaças e infrações revestidas de animus caluniandi, diffamandi ou injuriandi, que atingem a honorabilidade e a segurança do Supremo Tribunal Federal, de seus membros e familiares, extrapolando a liberdade de expressão", citing the court's internal rules, and designated Justice Alexandre de Moraes to lead it ([Tribuna do Paraná, 16 April 2019](https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/politica/raquel-arquiva-inquerito-sobre-fake-news-e-ofensas-contra-membros-do-stf/)). It became INQ 4781. Its scope is wider than the 2018 election, but it meets this story twice: its evidence was shared with the electoral court, and its legality was argued by defendants in the mass-messaging cases.

**April 2019.** The Prosecutor General, Raquel Dodge, informed the court that she was archiving the inquiry, arguing that measures had been taken without the prosecution service and that "O sistema penal acusatório é uma conquista antiga das principais nações civilizadas" (the accusatory system is a long-standing achievement of civilized nations), from which "não podemos abrir mão", we cannot give way (Tribuna do Paraná). Moraes rejected the request: it was not lawful, he wrote, to seek a generic archiving "sob o argumento da titularidade da ação penal pública impedir qualquer investigação que não seja requisitada pelo Ministério Público", on the argument that the prosecution's ownership of criminal action barred any investigation it had not requested ([InfoMoney, 16 April 2019](https://www.infomoney.com.br/politica/alexandre-de-moraes-rejeita-decisao-de-dodge-e-mantem-inquerito-sobre-ofensas-ao-stf-aberto/)). Within the inquiry Moraes had ordered the magazine Crusoé and the site O Antagonista to remove a report on Toffoli; the order, the Associated Press (AP) wrote, "had been widely criticized by other judges and by the nation's attorney general", and three days later Moraes reversed it, saying he had confirmed that the document behind the report existed ([Associated Press, 19 April 2019, as republished by KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/46534991)). Toffoli said the same day that "Não existe censura quando a decisão é a posteriori" (there is no censorship when the decision comes after publication) ([Tribuna do Paraná, 18 April 2019](https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/politica/nao-houve-mordaca-nem-censura-diz-toffoli/amp/)).

**June 2020.** In a case brought by the Rede Sustentabilidade party, the court's plenary <Claim status="established" by="by the STF, 10 votes to 1">upheld the inquiry's constitutionality</Claim>. The rapporteur, Justice Edson Fachin, held that the prosecution service and the Federal Police had failed to investigate attacks on judicial independence and threats to judges, so the internal rule could serve as an instrument of institutional defence; Justice Marco Aurélio, the only dissent, said the prerogative belonged to the PGR and that "Não pode a vítima instaurar inquérito", the victim cannot open an inquiry, lest the country have "um juízo de exceção", an exceptional court ([Agência Brasil, 18 June 2020](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/justica/noticia/2020-06/por-10-votos-1-stf-valida-inquerito-sobre-fake-news)).

| Argument | Who made it, in the sources read |
|---|---|
| The court may defend itself when the authorities fail to act against threats to its members | Toffoli in opening it; Fachin and nine justices in upholding it (2020) |
| Prior restraint is not censorship if it acts after publication | Toffoli and Moraes (April 2019) |
| The accusatory system requires the prosecution to lead investigations | Dodge (April 2019); Marco Aurélio (2020) |
| A victim cannot investigate and judge its own case; the rapporteur was designated without lottery | Marco Aurélio (2020); defendants in the TSE cases (2020) |
| Removing a news report was censorship | Crusoé, other judges and the attorney general, as the AP reported (2019) |

The sources read for this part do not record the inquiry's end.

## The rules for 2022, 2024 and 2026

The TSE writes the detailed rules for each election into its consolidated Resolution 23.610/2019 ([TSE, consolidated text, read through a fetch tool](https://www.tse.jus.br/legislacao/compilada/res/2019/resolucao-no-23-610-de-18-de-dezembro-de-2019)).

**For 2022.** Resolution 23.671/2021 allowed candidates to campaign on blogs and social networks "desde que não contratem disparos em massa de conteúdo" (art. 28, IV, a), banned electronic propaganda "por meio de disparo em massa de mensagens instantâneas sem consentimento da pessoa destinatária" (art. 34, II), and required senders to identify themselves fully and offer a way to unsubscribe (art. 33). It also inserted an article 9-A barring the spread of "fatos sabidamente inverídicos ou gravemente descontextualizados" (facts known to be untrue or gravely decontextualized); the consolidated text marks that article as revoked by Resolution 23.714/2022.

**For 2024.** Resolution 23.732/2024, approved on 27 February 2024 on proposals by Justice Cármen Lúcia ([CNN Brasil, 27 February 2024](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/politica/tse-proibe-deep-fakes-nas-eleicoes-e-amplia-responsabilidade-de-big-techs/)), added:

- a duty to state, "de modo explícito, destacado e acessível", when campaign content is "conteúdo sintético multimídia gerado por meio de inteligência artificial" (synthetic content generated by artificial intelligence) (art. 9-B, as later worded);
- a ban on chatbots and avatars that simulate conversation "com a pessoa candidata ou outra pessoa real" (art. 9-B, § 3);
- a ban on synthetic audio or video, "ainda que mediante autorização, para criar, substituir ou alterar imagem ou voz de pessoa viva, falecida ou fictícia (deep fake)", to harm or favour a candidacy (art. 9-C, § 1), whose breach "configura abuso do poder político" and can lead to "cassação do registro ou do mandato" (art. 9-C, § 2);
- duties for providers and joint civil and administrative liability when they fail to act in listed cases (arts. 9-D and 9-E);
- a definition of mass sending as a "estratégia coordenada de envio, compartilhamento ou encaminhamento de um mesmo conteúdo" (art. 37, XXI).

The court's president, Alexandre de Moraes, said the court had approved "uma das normatizações mais modernas do mundo em relação ao combate a desinformação e ao uso ilícito da inteligência artificial" (CNN Brasil).

**For 2026.** Resolution 23.755/2026 rewrote the labelling rule, required providers in listed cases to make unlawful content unavailable "independentemente de determinação judicial", without a court order (art. 28, § 4-B), and allowed providers to delete profiles only when they are fake, automated or bot profiles (art. 38-A, § 2).

No published criticism of these resolutions by a named party was retrieved for this part; the arguments over platform duties that the next section records were made about a bill, not about the resolutions.

## PL 2630, the "PL das Fake News"

Projeto de Lei (PL, bill) 2630/2020, the "Lei Brasileira de Liberdade, Responsabilidade e Transparência na Internet", was presented by Senator Alessandro Vieira. On 30 June 2020 the Senate approved it by 44 votes to 32 with two abstentions, under rapporteur Angelo Coronel; the government's leadership, the PSL and one other party directed their senators to vote against ([Congresso em Foco, 30 June 2020](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/legislativo/veja-como-os-senadores-votaram-o-pl-das-fake-news/)). In the Chamber, where it arrived on 3 July 2020, an urgency request failed narrowly in April 2022; on 25 April 2023 urgency was approved by 238 to 192, with the Partido Liberal and the Partido Novo against, and Deputy Orlando Silva, of the PCdoB, as rapporteur ([Congresso em Foco, 25 April 2023](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/29483/camara-aprova-urgencia-do-pl-das-fake-news); [Câmara dos Deputados, bill record](https://www.camara.leg.br/proposicoesWeb/fichadetramitacao?idProposicao=2256735)). On 2 May 2023 the bill was "Retirado de pauta, de ofício", withdrawn from the agenda by the chair, after the rapporteur asked for time; the Chamber's record still lists it as ready for the plenary agenda.

The Chamber's president, Arthur Lira, said in April 2024 that the bill was "contaminado" by ideological dispute and created a working group to start again, appointed in June 2024; in 2023, Agência Brasil recalled, he had attributed the lack of agreement to the action of the big technology companies ([Agência Brasil, 6 June 2024](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/politica/noticia/2024-06/apos-57-dias-lira-cria-grupo-para-discutir-regras-para-redes-sociais)).

**The case for the bill**, as its supporters put it:

- Orlando Silva said the aim was to unify the Chamber "num movimento de combater desinformação, garantir liberdade de expressão, responsabilidade para as plataformas e transparência na internet" ([Agência Câmara, 2 May 2023](https://www.camara.leg.br/noticias/957823-lira-adia-votacao-do-projeto-das-fake-news/)); Carlos Veras, of the PT, said it was urgent "dar um basta na irresponsabilidade que têm tido as plataformas"; Fernanda Melchionna, of the Partido Socialismo e Liberdade (PSOL), spoke of fighting "os crimes e a desinformação que custam vidas" (same source).
- The presidential communications secretariat's digital policy secretary, João Brant, cited the European Union's Digital Services Act as a reference and said platforms' administrative responsibility should grow without treating them as traditional media liable for every post; the influencer Felipe Neto called the bill "uma espécie de Constituição digital" and said that today platforms ban accounts "quando elas entendem necessário" and refuse to when they do not ([Agência Câmara, 23 April 2024](https://www.camara.leg.br/noticias/1055341-orlando-silva-cobra-do-governo-negociar-proposta-minimalista-para-regulacao-das-plataformas-digitais/)).

**The case against**, as its opponents put it:

- Google's director of government relations in Brazil, Marcelo Lacerda, wrote that the company supported the bill's goals but feared it would "mude a internet que você conhece para pior"; he argued it would protect producers of disinformation by shielding any company constituted "para fins jornalísticos", undermine free licensing of user content, give a government body broad powers with little oversight, and, through a preventive "dever de cuidado" with heavy fines, push platforms to remove legitimate speech, "uma nova forma de censura" ([Google Brasil blog, 27 April 2023](https://blog.google/intl/pt-br/novidades/iniciativas/como-o-pl-2630-pode-piorar-a-sua-internet/)). The Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB) Brasil, whose members include Google, Meta and TikTok, said the proposed rules "podem acabar por criar mais problemas, ao invés de mitigá-los" ([Congresso em Foco, 24 April 2023](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/amp/noticia/29494/google-pressiona-por-adiamento-de-votacao-do-pl-das-fake-news)).
- Speaking for the Partido Liberal, Deputy Giovani Cherini said "ele é a censura no Brasil" (Congresso em Foco, 25 April 2023); Deputy Bia Kicis, of the same party, said approving it would "legitimar a perseguição, a censura e o rompimento com o devido processo legal"; Hugo Motta, leader of Republicanos, said the debate had been "contaminada pela polarização" and by narratives (Agência Câmara, 2 May 2023).

## What research on WhatsApp groups can and cannot show

Researchers cannot see private chats, so most quantitative work studies public groups, those anyone can join through a chat.whatsapp.com link.

- **The 2018 monitoring.** From 16 August to 7 October 2018 the Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (UFMG) and the Universidade de São Paulo (USP) monitored 347 public political groups; the fact-checking agency Lupa analysed 50 of the most shared images ([Lupa, 18 October 2018](https://piaui.folha.uol.com.br/lupa/2018/10/18/imagens-falsas-whatsapp-presidenciaveis-lupa-ufmg-usp/)). Three of the researchers, Cristina Tardáguila, Fabrício Benevenuto and Pablo Ortellado, wrote that "It is difficult to establish to what extent these misinformation campaigns are affiliated with political parties or candidates", but that the tactics relied on "a combined pyramid and network strategy" in which producers broadcast content to regional and local activists who spread it to public and private groups; they called their 347 groups "just a small sample of the estimated hundreds of thousands" ([The New York Times, opinion, 17 October 2018](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/17/opinion/brazil-election-fake-news-whatsapp.html)).
- **The peer-reviewed study.** Gustavo Resende and colleagues, at the WWW conference of 2019, studied 141 groups during the 2018 truckers' strike and 364 during the campaign. As Rafael Evangelista and Fernanda Bruno summarize their results, 85 images identified as misinformation were shared eight times more often than 69,590 other images; misinformation was about 1% of images but appeared in 44% of the groups; and while 95% of unchecked images appeared on the web before WhatsApp, only 45% of misinformation images did and 35% appeared first on WhatsApp, which the authors read as WhatsApp acting "as a source of images with misinformation" ([Evangelista and Bruno, Internet Policy Review, 31 December 2019](https://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/whatsapp-and-political-instability-brazil-targeted-messages-and-political)). The original paper could not be downloaded for this part, so these figures are as Evangelista and Bruno report them.
- **An interpretation.** Evangelista and Bruno argue that these patterns indicate "a more centralised and less distributed dissemination structure", and that "as the sample collected by Resende et al. (2019) suggests, there is no evidence that the left coalition has employed the same tactics as Bolsonaro's in secretly managing multiple WhatsApp chat groups"; they also note that "There are reports that Haddad's campaign has also used WhatsApp to deliver messages to voters". Their claim about the left is drawn from public groups and is theirs; the PGR's 2018 request to investigate both campaigns and Bolsonaro's statement that millions of messages also circulated against him are the other parties' positions on the record.

The limits are stated by the researchers and the platform themselves. Kiran Garimella and Gareth Tyson, who built a method for collecting public-group data, call public groups a "radical shift from the bilateral nature of SMS" that "warrant study in their own right", list the privacy considerations, and set out to examine bias and representativeness ([Garimella and Tyson, 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01473)). WhatsApp's own figure that about 90% of messages go from one person to another and most groups have fewer than ten people means that large public groups are not the typical use. Evangelista and Bruno call WhatsApp's "closed, encrypted architecture" the feature that most restricts visibility for researchers and authorities. And the electoral court found that, more than a year after the election, neither the content nor the reach of the mass sending could be reconstructed.

## What defenders and voters can do

- **The platform's levers** are behavioural: bans for bulk and automated behaviour, forwarding limits and labels, and a prompt to block or report when a message comes from someone outside the address book (WhatsApp, 2019 and 2020).
- **The electoral rules** give recipients rights: the sender of campaign messages must identify itself and offer a way to unsubscribe, and mass sending without consent is banned (Resolution 23.610, arts. 33 and 34).
- **Evidence has a shelf life.** The TSE's judgment shows why: records kept for six months could not answer questions asked a year later. Preserving messages, screenshots and sender numbers at the time is what lets a complaint be assessed.
- **Reading habits** help: the [human attack surface](/learn/the-human-attack-surface-why-repetition-works) explains why repeated messages from trusted contacts feel true, and the planned parts on verification and prebunking cover the countermeasures.

## What to remember

- WhatsApp is encrypted end to end and built for private chats; it says about 90% of messages are one to one, and it removed over two million accounts a month for bulk or automated behaviour in early 2019.
- Forwarding was cut from 20 chats to five worldwide in January 2019 and to one for highly forwarded messages in April 2020.
- The Folha de S.Paulo reported in October 2018 that companies bought bulk messaging against the PT; Havan's owner, the agencies and the campaign's agency denied it or declined to comment, and Bolsonaro attributed his support to volunteers.
- WhatsApp said in 2019 that mass-messaging providers had broken its terms in the 2018 election, and won preliminary injunctions against several Brazilian firms.
- The TSE unanimously dismissed all four lawsuits: it found mass sending through WhatsApp by the winning campaign's structure, but not the content, reach, gravity or company funding needed for abuse; by majority it ruled that such conduct can be abuse in future.
- The CPMI das Fake News heard testimony from 2019, stopped in March 2020 and closed with no final report vote on record.
- The STF upheld its own fake-news inquiry 10 to 1 in 2020; the PGR and the dissenting justice argued that a victim cannot investigate its own case.
- TSE rules now ban mass sending without consent, deepfakes in campaigns and chatbots that impersonate people, and require labels on content generated by artificial intelligence.
- PL 2630 passed the Senate in 2020 and stalled in the Chamber in 2023 amid arguments over censorship and platform responsibility.
- Research on public WhatsApp groups found misinformation concentrated and sometimes seeded on WhatsApp, but public groups are a small, untypical slice of a private platform.
