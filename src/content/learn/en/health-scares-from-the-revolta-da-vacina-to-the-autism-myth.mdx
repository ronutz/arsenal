On 10 November 1904, the day after the rules for enforcing compulsory smallpox vaccination were made public, Rio de Janeiro rose in a revolt that lasted about a week and left 30 dead, 110 wounded and 945 arrested, according to the Oswaldo Cruz Foundation (Fiocruz) ([Fiocruz, Oswaldo Cruz biography, The Battle against Smallpox](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/en/biography/scientific-career/general-directorate-of-public-health/the-battle-against-smallpox)). On 28 February 1998 The Lancet published a paper on twelve children in which parents had linked the onset of behavioural symptoms to the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine in eight cases; the paper was retracted in 2010 ([PubMed record of Wakefield et al., The Lancet, 28 February 1998](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=9500320)). In April 2020 mobile phone masts were set on fire in England by people who linked 5G networks to coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) ([BBC News, 4 April 2020](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-52164358)). And on 20 November 2025 a page of the United States (US) Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) began to say that the claim "vaccines do not cause autism" is "not an evidence-based claim" ([CDC, Autism and Vaccines, updated 22 July 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccine-safety/about/autism.html)).

This part of the series opened by [the map of mass hysteria](/learn/mass-hysteria-how-groups-come-to-believe-anything) follows those scares through their records: what happened in Rio de Janeiro and why historians still disagree about it, how the 1998 paper came apart and what the studies that followed found, how measles returned, what the masts episode shows about speed, how Brazil's vaccination coverage fell and recovered, and how real adverse events of vaccines are found. After reading, you can separate a documented risk from an invented one, know which systems detect real problems, and check a new health scare against the same kind of evidence.

No one is described here as guilty of anything a court or professional tribunal did not find, and the answer of each person or body accused is given where the record holds one. One of the judges below found that a family who claimed that vaccines caused their child's autism had acted "in good faith". Portuguese-language sources are quoted verbatim with an English gloss.

## What vaccine hesitancy means

The Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE) of the World Health Organization (WHO) defines vaccine hesitancy as "delay in acceptance or refusal of vaccination despite availability of vaccination services", a behaviour "complex and context specific, varying across time, place and vaccines" and shaped by "complacency, convenience and confidence" ([MacDonald and the SAGE Working Group, Vaccine, August 2015, abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=25896383)). In 2019 the WHO listed it among ten threats to global health, noting that vaccination "currently prevents 2-3 million deaths a year" and that measles cases had risen 30% worldwide, while adding that "The reasons for this rise are complex, and not all of these cases are due to vaccine hesitancy" ([WHO, Ten threats to global health in 2019](https://www.who.int/news-room/spotlight/ten-threats-to-global-health-in-2019)).

Hesitancy is not the same as illness caused by fear. How expectation produces real symptoms is examined in [the part on the mechanisms](/learn/conformity-cascades-and-contagion-the-mechanisms), and group reactions after vaccination at schools in Colombia and Brazil in [the part on mass psychogenic illness](/learn/mass-psychogenic-illness-when-symptoms-spread); what helped end such episodes is in [What ends an outbreak, and what helps](/learn/what-ends-an-outbreak-and-what-helps). This part follows the scares that turned on a claim about the vaccine itself.

## Rio de Janeiro, November 1904

### An epidemic, a law and a name for it

In the winter of 1904 a smallpox epidemic killed about 3,500 people in Rio de Janeiro, then the capital ([Fiocruz, Luta contra a varíola](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/biografia/trajetoria-cientifica/na-diretoria-geral-de-saude-publica/luta-contra-a-variola)). Vaccination had been compulsory on paper since the nineteenth century, for children from 1837 and adults from 1846, but had never been enforced ([de Oliveira and colleagues, Revista da Sociedade Brasileira de Medicina Tropical, May 2022, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9131779/fullTextXML); [Fiocruz, Luta contra a varíola](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/biografia/trajetoria-cientifica/na-diretoria-geral-de-saude-publica/luta-contra-a-variola)). Oswaldo Cruz, head of public health, proposed a law that gave sanitary authorities broad powers, including "a aplicação de multas aos refratários" (fines on those who refused) and the requirement of vaccination certificates to enrol in school, enter public service, marry or travel; the public called it the "Código de Torturas" (Torture Code) (same). Fiocruz's English page says the powers included the power "to lower fines on the recalcitrant", an apparent mistranslation of the Portuguese ([Fiocruz, The Battle against Smallpox](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/en/biography/scientific-career/general-directorate-of-public-health/the-battle-against-smallpox)).

Opponents came from almost every group: army officers, monarchists, workers, Positivists, students, journalists and physicians; many held that compulsion violated individual liberty, many believed the vaccine itself spread the disease, and a moral objection asked how heads of families could let their wives and daughters "desnudar braços e coxas" (bare arms and thighs) for the inoculation ([Fiocruz, Luta contra a varíola](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/biografia/trajetoria-cientifica/na-diretoria-geral-de-saude-publica/luta-contra-a-variola)). The Senate voted the bill on 20 July and the Chamber gave final approval on 26 October ([Arca Fiocruz, Salgado, A Revolta contra a vacina, 2018, record](https://www.arca.fiocruz.br/handle/icict/31112)); the law, No. 1,261, is dated 31 October 1904 ([Wang and colleagues, Health and Human Rights, June 2021, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8233032/fullTextXML)). A draft of the enforcing regulation appeared first in the newspaper A Notícia on 9 November and the next day in the Correio da Manhã and the Gazeta de Noticias ([Salgado, master's dissertation, Casa de Oswaldo Cruz, 2018](https://www.arca.fiocruz.br/bitstreams/26341b37-b79e-4a87-a224-563e196417fd/download)). How intensely the press covered the bill in the months before is summarized in [the opening part](/learn/mass-hysteria-how-groups-come-to-believe-anything).

### What the opposition said

According to an Estadão article of 2020, the opposition spread claims that the vaccine caused "gangrena, epilepsia, meningite, tuberculose e sífilis" (gangrene, epilepsy, meningitis, tuberculosis and syphilis), and a story that those vaccinated could take on the features of cattle, a distortion of the vaccine's origin in cowpox ([Estadão Conteúdo via IstoÉ Dinheiro, 22 October 2020](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/desinformacao-ja-era-forte-na-revolta-da-vacina-de-1904)). On 28 September 1904 the paper had printed a Senate speech by Senator Barata Ribeiro accusing the government of letting the epidemic grow "afim de obrigar o povo a estender o braço à lanceta homicida" (so as to force the people to hold out their arm to the murderous lancet) (same). The same article records that vaccinations in the city rose from about 8,200 in May to 23,021 in July, then fell to 6,036 in August and 1,138 at the end of October, as the debate on compulsion grew (same). The 2020 article calls the campaign "orquestrada" (orchestrated) by opponents of the government; whether it was orchestrated is one of the points historians weigh below.

### About a week of revolt

The revolt began on 10 November. For about a week thousands took to the streets, shops closed and public transport stopped; in the middle of it a military insurrection tried to depose President Rodrigues Alves, the parade of 15 November was cancelled, and a state of siege declared on 16 November restored control ([Fiocruz, Luta contra a varíola](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/biografia/trajetoria-cientifica/na-diretoria-geral-de-saude-publica/luta-contra-a-variola)). The military rising came on 14 November from the Military School at Praia Vermelha, whose roughly 300 cadets followed General Silvestre Travassos, with the senator and lieutenant colonel Lauro Sodré among the leaders ([Castro and Gagliardi, Militares e Política, July to December 2009](https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/mp/article/download/33819/18952)). On 15 November the newspaper O Estado de S. Paulo reported: "A cidade apresenta um aspecto tristíssimo, de verdadeira praça de guerra" (the city looks most sorrowful, a true battlefield) ([Estadão Conteúdo via IstoÉ Dinheiro, 15 November 2020](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/115-anos-depois-stf-volta-a-debater-revolta-da-vacina)).

### What happened to the law

Fiocruz writes that Rodrigues Alves, though victorious, "anunciou o fim da vacinação obrigatória" (announced the end of compulsory vaccination), that smallpox deaths in Rio fell to nine in 1906, and that a new epidemic in 1908 raised them above 6,500: "A revogação da obrigatoriedade cobrava o seu preço" (the revocation of compulsion took its toll) ([Fiocruz, Luta contra a varíola](https://oswaldocruz.fiocruz.br/index.php/biografia/trajetoria-cientifica/na-diretoria-geral-de-saude-publica/luta-contra-a-variola)). Tania Fernandes, a retired researcher at Fiocruz's Casa de Oswaldo Cruz, gives a different account of the law itself: it "não foi revogada, mas se manteve sem exigência até 1908" (was not revoked, but was kept without being enforced until 1908), when a new epidemic led to its gradual acceptance ([Fernandes, interview, História, Ciências, Saúde-Manguinhos, 2026, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13450908/fullTextXML)). Both agree that enforcement stopped and that smallpox came back.

The courts were also asked where the state's powers ended. In early 1905 the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF), Brazil's Supreme Court, granted habeas corpus to a resident of Rio Comprido who refused entry to a sanitary inspector sent to disinfect against yellow fever, holding that forced entry into homes had to be authorized by a law passed by Congress, not by a government regulation ([Estadão Conteúdo via IstoÉ Dinheiro, 15 November 2020](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/115-anos-depois-stf-volta-a-debater-revolta-da-vacina)). On 20 February 1905 the US Supreme Court, in Jacobson v. Massachusetts, upheld a five-dollar fine for refusing smallpox vaccination, writing that "There are manifold restraints to which every person is necessarily subject for the common good"; the argument of the plaintiff's counsel, as the report gives it, noted that "In 1904 there were riots in Brazil arising from attempts to enforce vaccination" ([Jacobson v. Massachusetts, 197 U.S. 11, 1905](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep197/usrep197011/usrep197011.pdf)).

Brazil's later history ran the other way. Gilberto Hochman, of the Casa de Oswaldo Cruz, describes a "culture of immunization" built through campaigns and mass vaccination, with the smallpox eradication campaign of 1966 to 1973 as a turning point ([Hochman, Ciência & Saúde Coletiva, February 2011, abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=21340314)), and Fernandes says smallpox vaccination, like other immunization, remained compulsory and was accepted "sem questionamentos significativos ao longo do século XX" (without significant challenge throughout the twentieth century) ([Fernandes, 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13450908/fullTextXML)).

### Why it happened: what historians argue

Aline Salgado's dissertation summarizes the main explanations ([Salgado, 2018](https://www.arca.fiocruz.br/bitstreams/26341b37-b79e-4a87-a224-563e196417fd/download)):

| Explanation | Who proposed it, as Salgado summarizes |
| --- | --- |
| Draconian regulations and a military-style campaign against smallpox, yellow fever and plague | Gilberto Hochman (2012) |
| Urban reform already under way, including the demolition of tenements such as the Cabeça de Porco in 1892, which unsettled the poor of the centre | the reference literature Salgado cites |
| A defence of citizens' right "de não serem arbitrariamente tratados pelo governo" (not to be arbitrarily treated by the government), with "várias revoltas dentro da revolta" (several revolts within the revolt) | José Murilo de Carvalho (1987) |
| A coalition of "integrantes do Apostolado Positivista, oficiais descontentes do exército, monarquistas e líderes operários" (members of the Positivist Apostolate, discontented army officers, monarchists and labour leaders) gathered in the League against Compulsory Vaccination, who tried a coup | Jaime Benchimol (2000) |
| A wider plan for a coup against Rodrigues Alves behind the "ódio à vacina" (hatred of the vaccine) | Nicolau Sevcenko (1984) |

Salgado records that the senator Lauro Sodré founded the League against Compulsory Vaccination on 5 November at the Centro das Classes Operárias (same). Celso Castro and Juliana Gagliardi argue that for the Military School rising the popular revolt was "apenas um pretexto" (only a pretext), set within a project of "refundação" (refounding) of the republic of 1889 ([Castro and Gagliardi](https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/mp/article/download/33819/18952)). Fernandes adds that the episode was also known as the "Revolta dos Lampiões" (Revolt of the Street Lamps) and reflected a wider social discontent ([Fernandes, 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13450908/fullTextXML)). <Claim status="disputed" by="by historians of the revolt">How far the revolt was steered by politicians and officers, and how far it was a spontaneous protest against the vaccine and the reform of the city</Claim> remains open; that both elements were present is common ground in these accounts.

## 1998: the Lancet paper and what followed

### The paper and the press conference

The paper by Andrew Wakefield and twelve co-authors at the Royal Free Hospital in London began: "We investigated a consecutive series of children with chronic enterocolitis and regressive developmental disorder"; twelve children, eleven of them boys, had been referred to a paediatric gastroenterology unit, and "Onset of behavioural symptoms was associated, by the parents, with measles, mumps, and rubella vaccination in eight of the 12 children"; the authors concluded that the disease and regression they found were "generally associated in time with possible environmental triggers" ([PubMed record of Wakefield et al., 1998](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=9500320)). The abstract does not state that the vaccine caused autism.

What reached the public went further than the paper. According to the High Court judgment of 2012 on one of the co-authors, at a press conference convened to accompany publication, which the senior clinician John Walker-Smith did not attend, Wakefield said that he could no longer support the giving of MMR vaccine; Walker-Smith and Simon Murch had written to him on 21 January 1998 that it was inappropriate to emphasize the vaccine in publicity, and they issued a press release supporting "present public health policy concerning MMR" ([High Court of England and Wales, Walker-Smith v General Medical Council, [2012] EWHC 503 (Admin), 7 March 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf)). The judge added: "Dr. Wakefield's statement and subsequent publicity had a predictable adverse effect upon the take up of MMR vaccine of great concern to those responsible for public health" (same).

### How it came apart

| Date | Event | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| December 1996 | the Legal Aid Board provides £25,000 towards the costs of an "MMR investigation" for litigation proposed by a law firm; in February 1997 Walker-Smith writes that "the link with MMR is so far unproven" | [High Court, 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf) |
| March 2004 | ten of the twelve co-authors publish a "retraction of an interpretation": "no causal link was established between MMR vaccine and autism as the data were insufficient"; The Lancet acknowledges that financial interests were not disclosed but, at that stage, clears the authors of ethical violations and scientific misconduct | [PubMed record of Murch et al., 2004](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=15016483); [Rao and Andrade, Indian Journal of Psychiatry, 2011](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3136032/fullTextXML) |
| 28 January 2010 | the General Medical Council (GMC) panel gives its findings of fact, after a hearing that began on 16 July 2007 and ran 149 days | [High Court, 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf) |
| 6 February 2010 | The Lancet fully retracts the paper | [PubMed record of the retraction](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=20137807) |
| 24 May 2010 | the panel finds Wakefield and Walker-Smith guilty of serious professional misconduct, but not Murch, and orders both names erased from the medical register; Wakefield later abandons his appeal | [High Court, 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf) |
| January 2011 | The BMJ (formerly British Medical Journal) publishes articles by the journalist Brian Deer and editorials titled "Secrets of the MMR Scare" and "The Fraud Behind the MMR Scare" | [Texas Court of Appeals, 19 September 2014](https://cases.justia.com/texas/third-court-of-appeals/2014-03-12-00576-cv.pdf) |
| 7 March 2012 | the High Court quashes the findings against Walker-Smith for "inadequate and superficial reasoning and, in a number of instances, a wrong conclusion" | [High Court, 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf) |
| 19 September 2014 | a Texas appeals court affirms the dismissal of Wakefield's defamation suit against the BMJ, Deer and the editor Fiona Godlee for lack of jurisdiction | [Texas Court of Appeals, 2014](https://cases.justia.com/texas/third-court-of-appeals/2014-03-12-00576-cv.pdf) |

According to a review by T. S. Sathyanarayana Rao and Chittaranjan Andrade, the 2010 retraction followed findings of ethical violations, invasive investigations without the necessary ethical clearance, and of misrepresentation, since the paper reported as consecutive a sample that had been selected ([Rao and Andrade, 2011](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3136032/fullTextXML)). <Claim status="established" by="by the General Medical Council panel in 2010; Wakefield abandoned his appeal">Wakefield was found guilty of serious professional misconduct and struck off</Claim>. <Claim status="denied" by="by Andrew Wakefield">The BMJ's charge of fraud</Claim> was contested by Wakefield, whose petition in Texas said the articles "contained unfair, incorrect, inaccurate and unjust criticisms of findings previously reported by Dr. Wakefield and 12 other co-authors"; the Texas courts dismissed the suit for want of jurisdiction and did not rule on whether the articles were true ([Texas Court of Appeals, 2014](https://cases.justia.com/texas/third-court-of-appeals/2014-03-12-00576-cv.pdf)).

Clearing Walker-Smith did not mean endorsing the hypothesis: the judge who quashed the findings against him also wrote: "There is now no respectable body of opinion which supports his hypothesis, that MMR vaccine and autism/enterocolitis are causally linked" ([High Court, 2012](https://www.hempsons.co.uk/app/uploads/2020/01/Walker-Smith.pdf)). The hypothesis there is Wakefield's.

### What the studies found

| Study | Population | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Taylor and colleagues, The Lancet, 1999](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=10376617) | 498 cases of autism in eight North Thames health districts | no "step-up" in cases after MMR was introduced in the United Kingdom (UK) in 1988; "Our analyses do not support a causal association" |
| [Madsen and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2002](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=12421889) | 537,303 Danish children born 1991 to 1998 | relative risk of autistic disorder 0.92 (95% confidence interval 0.68 to 1.24) in vaccinated children |
| [Taylor, Swerdfeger and Eslick, Vaccine, 2014](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=24814559) | five cohort studies with 1,256,407 children and five case-control studies with 9,920 | no relationship with autism (odds ratio 0.99) or with MMR (0.84), thimerosal or mercury |
| [Hviid and colleagues, Annals of Internal Medicine, 2019](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=30831578) | 657,461 Danish children, 6,517 diagnosed with autism | hazard ratio 0.93 (0.85 to 1.02), also in children with an autistic sibling or other risk factors |
| [Di Pietrantonj and colleagues, Cochrane review, 2021](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=34806766) | 138 studies, 23,480,668 participants | no association with autism spectrum disorders (rate ratio 0.93, 0.85 to 1.01; 1,194,764 children); measles prevented in 95% after one dose and 96% after two |

The same Cochrane review documents the vaccine's real risks: febrile seizures, estimated at one per 1,700 to one per 1,150 doses; immune thrombocytopenic purpura, about one case per 40,000 doses, fewer than after natural infection; and aseptic meningitis with vaccines containing the Urabe and Leningrad-Zagreb mumps strains, but not the Jeryl Lynn strain ([Di Pietrantonj et al., 2021](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=34806766)). The Danish study of 2019 lists its funders, the Novo Nordisk Foundation and the Danish Ministry of Health, and its main limitation, that individual medical charts were not reviewed ([Hviid et al., 2019](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=30831578)).

### In the vaccine court

In the US, more than 5,000 families brought claims to the federal vaccine compensation programme alleging that vaccines had caused autism, and the special masters heard selected test cases for the families' main causation theories ([United States Court of Federal Claims, Cedillo v. Secretary of Health and Human Services, No. 98-916V, 12 February 2009](https://www.uscfc.uscourts.gov/sites/cfc/files/hastings_cedillo_decision.pdf)). In the first, Special Master George Hastings concluded that "the evidence was overwhelmingly contrary to the petitioners' contentions" and that "This case, however, is not a close case"; he also found that the family had filed "in good faith", had been "misled by physicians who are guilty, in my view, of gross medical misjudgment", and deserved "deep sympathy and admiration" (same). The Federal Circuit affirmed on 27 August 2010 ([United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, 27 August 2010](https://www.uscfc.uscourts.gov/sites/cfc/files/federal_circuit_cedillo_decision.pdf)).

## 2025 and 2026: a health agency reopens the question

On 20 November 2025 the CDC page on autism and vaccines kept its heading "Vaccines do not cause Autism", now with an asterisk, and added that the statement "is not an evidence-based claim because studies have not ruled out the possibility that infant vaccines cause autism" and that "Studies supporting a link have been ignored by health authorities"; the version updated on 22 July 2026 cites the Data Quality Act and says the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is evaluating "plausible biologic mechanisms" ([CDC, Autism and Vaccines](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccine-safety/about/autism.html)). FactCheck.org reported that the asterisk refers to an agreement with Senator Bill Cassidy, who backed Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s nomination as health secretary after Kennedy promised not to remove statements that vaccines do not cause autism ([FactCheck.org, 20 November 2025](https://www.factcheck.org/2025/11/revised-cdc-website-about-autism-and-vaccines-is-not-evidence-based/)).

The positions on the record:

- HHS: its communications director, Andrew Nixon, called the change "a common-sense update that brings CDC's website in line with our commitment to transparency and gold standard science" (same).
- Cassidy: "What parents need to hear right now is vaccines for measles, polio, hepatitis B and other childhood diseases are safe and effective and will not cause autism. Any statement to the contrary is wrong, irresponsible, and actively makes Americans sicker" (same).
- The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP): its president, Susan Kressly, said that "independent researchers across seven countries have conducted more than 40 high-quality studies involving over 5.6 million people" and found no link (same).
- Former CDC staff: Demetre Daskalakis, who resigned in August 2025, said the change "blindsided" career scientists, who "did not participate in its creation" ([NPR, formerly National Public Radio, via Oregon Public Broadcasting, 20 November 2025](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/11/20/the-cdc-revives-debunked-link-between-childhood-vaccines-and-autism/)).
- Medical bodies: more than 60 organizations publicly countered the new statements ([Chief Healthcare Executive, 25 November 2025](https://www.chiefhealthcareexecutive.com/view/dozens-of-medical-groups-denounce-cdc-s-new-statements-on-vaccines-and-autism)).

A second dispute concerned a Danish cohort of 1,224,176 children, published in July 2025, which found no increase in 50 chronic disorders with the aluminium in early childhood vaccines, and a hazard ratio of 0.93 (0.90 to 0.97) for any neurodevelopmental disorder per milligram ([Andersson and colleagues, Annals of Internal Medicine, 2025, abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&rettype=abstract&retmode=text&id=40658954)). Kennedy asked for its retraction in an article of 1 August 2025; the journal's editor, Christine Laine, answered that "retraction is warranted only when serious errors invalidate findings or there is documented scientific misconduct, neither of which occurred here" ([Scientific American, from Nature, 25 August 2025](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/rfk-jr-demanded-study-on-vaccines-and-aluminum-be-retracted-the-journal-said/)). The authors answered the criticisms one by one: they did not compare with unvaccinated children because only 1.2% (15,200) had received no aluminium-containing vaccine before age two, and they acknowledged that the study did not test whether any exposure at all increased risk; a secondary analysis showing a 67% higher risk of Asperger syndrome per milligram for children born after 2007, which Kennedy cited, should, they said, be read cautiously because its data were incomplete (same). <Claim status="disputed" by="by HHS and its secretary, against the AAP, more than 60 medical organizations and the studies above">Whether science has excluded a link between vaccines and autism</Claim> is now argued between a federal agency and most of the medical profession; [Why misbeliefs persist](/learn/why-misbeliefs-persist) explains why a correction can be weakened when an authority revives the original claim.

## Measles comes back

### The United Kingdom

In late June 2008 the Health Protection Agency (HPA) of England and Wales announced, as Eurosurveillance reported on 3 July, that measles was endemic again, fourteen years after local transmission had stopped, stating that after almost a decade of low MMR coverage "the number of children susceptible to measles is now sufficient to support the continuous spread of measles"; 461 cases had been confirmed in England and Wales that year, and a 17-year-old with a congenital immunodeficiency had died ([Eurosurveillance, 3 July 2008](https://www.eurosurveillance.org/content/10.2807/ese.13.27.18919-en)). In January 2026 the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) reported that the WHO had confirmed the loss of the UK's elimination status on 2024 data, after "a decade of declining vaccination rates", with second-dose MMR coverage at five years of 83.7% and 2,911 laboratory-confirmed cases in England in 2024; the WHO European Region had 127,350 cases that year ([UKHSA, Health Protection Report, 29 January 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/health-protection-report-volume-20-2026/hpr-volume-20-issue-1-news-29-january-2026)).

### The Americas and Brazil

On 10 November 2025 the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) announced that the Americas had lost their measles-free status after Canada's endemic transmission was confirmed: 12,596 cases in ten countries to 7 November 2025, 30 times more than in 2024, 28 deaths, and 89% of cases among the unvaccinated or people of unknown status ([PAHO, 10 November 2025](https://www.paho.org/en/news/10-11-2025-paho-calls-regional-action-americas-lose-measles-elimination-status)). The CDC counted 2,289 cases in the US in 2025 and 5,412 in 2026 as of 12 November ([CDC, Measles Cases and Outbreaks](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html)). PAHO scheduled the review of US and Mexican status for its commission's meeting in November 2026 ([PAHO, 2 March 2026](https://www.paho.org/en/news/2-3-2026-update-review-measles-elimination-status)); Reuters reported on 11 November 2026 that the decision was expected to be made public the following week, and quoted HHS as saying national vaccination rates had declined before Kennedy took office ([Reuters via the Jefferson City News Tribune, 11 November 2026](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/nov/11/us-faces-loss-of-measles-free-status-as-experts-meet-for-review/)). <Claim status="reported" by="by KFF Health News, published by NPR, citing researchers with direct knowledge of the deliberations">In the week of 14 November 2026, PAHO concluded that measles had re-established regular transmission in the United States</Claim>, and it had planned a public announcement for 17 November ([NPR and KFF Health News, 14 November 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/14/nx-s1-6041119/us-measles-elimination-status-vaccines-cdc-paho)).

Brazil went through the same cycle earlier. According to PAHO, it re-established endemic measles transmission in 2019, interrupted it by mid-2022 and regained its measles-free status in 2024 ([PAHO, measles and rubella elimination: regional monitoring and re-verification commission](https://www.paho.org/en/measles-rubella-elimination-regional-monitoring-re-verification-commission)). Between August 2019 and July 2020 Brazil recorded 24,956 cases and topped the WHO list of measles incidence ([Matos, Barbieri and Couto, Revista de Saúde Pública, 2020, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7647464/fullTextXML)).

## 2020: masts on fire

The claim that 5G networks caused or spread COVID-19 has been traced to comments by a Belgian doctor in January 2020; at least 20 masts were vandalized in the UK between 2 and 6 April 2020, including one serving the Nightingale hospital in Birmingham ([Ahmed and colleagues, Journal of Medical Internet Research, 6 May 2020, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7205032/fullTextXML)). In a sample of 233 tweets under the hashtag #5GCoronavirus, 34.8% linked 5G and the virus, 32.2% denounced the theory and 33.0% were neutral, and the authors found "a lack of an authority figure who was actively combating such misinformation" (same).

The response was immediate. The Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport said "there is absolutely no credible evidence" of a link; the minister Michael Gove called the theories "just nonsense, dangerous nonsense as well"; and Stephen Powis, medical director of the National Health Service (NHS) in England, said: "I'm absolutely outraged and disgusted that people would be taking action against the infrastructure we need to tackle this emergency" ([BBC News, 4 April 2020](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-52164358)). The industry body Mobile UK said key workers had suffered abuse and threats (same); by June operators had recorded around 100 attacks on their towers ([ISPreview, 9 June 2020](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2020/06/covid-19-5g-conspiracy-arsonist-gets-3-years-for-burning-mast.html)). A 47-year-old man from Knowsley who set fire to a mast at Kirkby on 5 April was sentenced at Liverpool Crown Court to three years; the mast served 4G, not 5G, and the judge found "a high degree of planning and premeditation" ([The Register, 9 June 2020](https://www.theregister.com/2020/06/09/arsonist_sentencing/)).

The regulator Ofcom measured radio emissions at 22 5G sites in ten cities and found that the highest level from 5G signals was "0.039% of the maximum" set in the international guidelines of the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP), and wrote that "There is no scientific basis or credible evidence for these claims" ([Ofcom, Clearing up the myths around 5G and the coronavirus](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/spectrum/electromagnetic-fields/clearing-up-myths-5g-and-coronavirus)). In a preregistered study of 601 Britons, Daniel Jolley and Jenny Paterson found that belief in 5G and COVID-19 theories went with state anger, which in turn went with greater justification of violence, "strongest for those highest in paranoia" ([Jolley and Paterson, British Journal of Social Psychology, 2020, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7323354/fullTextXML)). The 5G theory later entered the QAnon narrative recorded by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, described in [Conspiracy theories at scale](/learn/conspiracy-theories-at-scale); experiments on symptoms attributed to sham radio exposure are in [the part on the mechanisms](/learn/conformity-cascades-and-contagion-the-mechanisms), and the platforms' role in [the part on social media](/learn/social-media-and-the-contagion-of-symptoms-and-beliefs).

## Brazil: coverage falls and recovers

Ana Paula Sato, of the University of São Paulo, wrote in 2018 that childhood coverage had been above 95% since the 1990s but had been falling by about 10 to 20 percentage points since 2016; she listed among the causes the weakening of the public health system, the Sistema Único de Saúde (SUS), a new immunization information system and social and cultural factors, while noting that in a 67-country survey only 0.7%, 6.1% and 4.5% of Brazilians disagreed that vaccines were important, safe and effective ([Sato, Revista de Saúde Pública, 2018, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6284490/fullTextXML)). Camila Matos, Carolina Barbieri and Marcia Couto called the decline "multifactorial": the complexity of the National Immunization Programme (Programa Nacional de Imunizações, PNI), shortages, limited opening hours of vaccine rooms, under-financing of the SUS, the dismantling of primary care, rising hesitancy and fake news ([Matos, Barbieri and Couto, 2020](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7647464/fullTextXML)).

The Ministry of Health reported in January 2025 that the number of municipalities reaching the 95% target for the second dose of the MMR vaccine rose from 855 in 2022 to 2,408 in 2024, and attributed the recovery to its national vaccination movement launched in 2023; its surveillance secretary, Ethel Maciel, said: "Conseguimos vacinar mais pessoas em 2023 do que nos quatro anos anteriores" (we managed to vaccinate more people in 2023 than in the previous four years) ([Agência Gov, via the Ministry of Health, 22 January 2025](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202501/cresce-numero-de-municipios-com-mais-de-95-de-cobertura-vacinal)). The international series also shows a dip in 2025 after the 2024 peak; the reasons were not given in the sources read.

In December 2020 the STF ruled on compulsory vaccination again, 116 years after the revolt. It held that "A vacinação compulsória não significa vacinação forçada" (compulsory vaccination does not mean forced vaccination), that it may be enforced by indirect measures provided by law, and, in an appeal on refusal for philosophical or religious convictions, that compulsory immunization with a registered vaccine in the national plan does not violate "liberdade de consciência e de convicção filosófica dos pais ou responsáveis" (the parents' freedom of conscience and philosophical conviction) ([STF, 17 December 2020](https://noticias.stf.jus.br/?p=52224)). Justice Cármen Lúcia said that "A Constituição não garante liberdades às pessoas para que elas sejam soberanamente egoístas" (the Constitution does not guarantee liberties so that people can be sovereignly selfish); Justice Nunes Marques, partly dissenting, would allow compulsion against COVID-19 only after the Ministry of Health had been heard and as a last measure (same).

## Real adverse events, and how they are found

Brazil's Ministry of Health states that vaccines are among the safest medicines but, like any medicine, "não são isentas de riscos" (are not free of risk) ([Ministério da Saúde, Eventos Supostamente Atribuíveis à Vacinação ou Imunização](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/vacinacao/esavi)). It defines an ESAVI (evento supostamente atribuível à vacinação ou imunização, event supposedly attributable to vaccination or immunization) as "qualquer ocorrência médica indesejada temporalmente associada à vacinação, não possuindo necessariamente uma relação causal com o uso de uma vacina" (any unwanted medical occurrence temporally associated with vaccination, not necessarily causally related to the use of a vaccine) ([Ministério da Saúde, Nota Técnica nº 88/2025](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/vacinacao/esavi/notas-tecnicas/nota-tecnica-no-88-2025-cgfam-dpni-svsa-ms)). Any health professional can report an event in the e-SUS Notifica system, and the data are shared with the health regulator, the Agência Nacional de Vigilância Sanitária (Anvisa) ([Ministério da Saúde, ESAVI](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/vacinacao/esavi)). The distinction between an event that follows a vaccine and one caused by it is the core of the method; the cases below are those in which the systems found a real effect.

## What to remember

- Vaccine hesitancy, as the WHO defines it, is delay or refusal despite available vaccines; it varies by place, time and vaccine, and not every fall in coverage is caused by it.
- Rio de Janeiro rose in November 1904 against compulsory smallpox vaccination: about a week of revolt, 30 dead, hundreds arrested and deported; sources differ on the numbers and on whether the law was revoked or merely left unenforced, and historians differ on how far politicians and officers steered it.
- The 1998 Lancet paper described twelve children and did not state that the vaccine caused autism; the call to stop using MMR came at the press conference. The paper was retracted in 2010, Wakefield was struck off, and studies of millions of children found no link.
- In 2025 a US federal agency reopened the autism question; the AAP, more than 60 medical organizations and a US senator who supports vaccines rejected the change, and HHS defended it.
- Measles returned where coverage fell: the UK lost its elimination status twice, the Americas in 2025, and Brazil between 2019 and 2024.
- Masts were burned in 2020 over a 5G theory with no evidence; one of them was a 4G mast.
- Brazil's coverage fell from 2016, for many reasons, and recovered from 2023; the STF ruled in 2020 that compulsory vaccination does not mean forced vaccination.
- Vaccines have real, rare risks, and the surveillance systems that measured them are the same ones that tested the autism claim and found nothing.
