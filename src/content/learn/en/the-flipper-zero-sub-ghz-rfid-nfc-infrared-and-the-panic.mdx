A handheld that greets its owner with what the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) called an "adorable cyber-dolphin on its monochrome 128x64 pixel screen" ([EFF, 9 March 2023](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2023/03/flipper-zero-devices-being-seized-brazils-telecom-agency)), sold for US$169, has been seized by Brazil's telecommunications regulator, removed from Amazon as a "card skimming device" and named by a Canadian minister in a plan to ban car-theft tools. Its maker, Flipper Devices, says on its own documentation that "Flipper Zero can help you learn if your security is compromised" ([Flipper Docs, Sub-GHz](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz)). This part of the series opened by [Hacking devices: from the blue box to the Flipper Zero](/learn/hacking-devices-from-the-blue-box-to-the-flipper-zero) reads the device from its maker's documentation, firmware and filings, then follows each restriction in order. After reading, you will be able to describe its radios and what each one does, read its sub-GHz frequency table against the rules of three regulators, say what it can and cannot do against rolling codes and modern cards, explain why community firmware changes the picture, and name the defences that make most of the fuss irrelevant. The device's cheaper relatives, built on Espressif's ESP32 chips and including Brazil's High Boy, are the subject of the next part, [High Boy, ESP32 Marauder and the Flipper's alternatives](/learn/high-boy-esp32-marauder-and-the-flipper-alternatives).

## On Kickstarter, 2020

Flipper Devices launched the campaign at the end of July 2020. On 30 July 2020 the team wrote that "We made it in just 8 minutes", that so many backers arrived in the first seconds that the payment processing system was lagging, and that a Bluetooth stretch goal was reached in three hours ([Flipper blog, "Funded in 8 Minutes, New Outfit & Bluetooth Unlocked", 30 July 2020](https://blog.flipper.net/funded-in-8-minutes/)). The stretch goals had been set at US$100,000 for new colours, US$300,000 for Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) and US$700,000 for near-field communication (NFC); "all the stretch goals were unlocked just in 15 hours", the company wrote, and the first day brought "a whopping $1 million" ([Pavel Zhovner, "20 hours till the end of campaign!", Flipper Zero project log, Hackaday.io, 28 August 2020](https://hackaday.io/project/170875-flipper-zero-multi-tool-device-for-hackers/log/182857-20-hours-till-the-end-of-campaign); [Flipper blog, "NFC is Here", 31 July 2020](https://blog.flipper.net/nfc-is-here/)). The campaign ran 30 days and closed at the end of August 2020 ([Flipper blog, "Campaign is over. What's next?", 30 August 2020](https://blog.flipper.net/campaign-is-over/)). The company's own page gives the total: "In 2020, we've launched a crowdfunding Kickstarter campaign and raised $4.8M" ([Flipper Devices, company page](https://flipperdevices.com/)). The Kickstarter page itself refused automated reading; its figures are listed for checking in a browser.

In the campaign's first week the team answered the question that would follow the device for years. "Is this legal?" it asked, and replied: "Official firmware will not have any kind of potentially illegal features, such as jamming, brute force, etc.", adding that, since the firmware is open source, users could implement any feature or use third-party firmware: "In this case, all responsibility is on you" ([Flipper blog, "Under the pressure of unexpected fame", 3 August 2020](https://blog.flipper.net/under-the-pressure/)). That sentence, written before a single unit shipped, is the hinge of everything that follows.

Delivery took longer than planned: in August 2020 the team expected to start shipping in February 2021 ([Flipper blog, 3 August 2020](https://blog.flipper.net/under-the-pressure/)), and the first units left in January 2022, to backers in "over 120 countries" ([Flipper blog, "Shipping Started!", 21 January 2022](https://blog.flipper.net/shipping-started/)). In March 2021 the company reported that its first sub-1 GHz design had failed certification "due to parasitic harmonics exceeding acceptable levels" and that the whole antenna path had to be redesigned to obtain the certificates needed to import into the European Union, the United States, Japan and Australia ([Flipper blog, "Tiresome certification, opening sources, and new cool stuff", 23 March 2021](https://blog.flipper.net/certification-and-opening-sources/)). The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) granted the equipment authorization for FCC identifier 2A2V6-FZ on 14 January 2022 ([FCC grant, Part 15 Security/Remote Control Transmitter, 14 January 2022, published by Flipper Devices](https://cdn.flipper.net/FCC_-_2A2V6-FZGrant-DSC.pdf)), and a certification body issued the Canadian technical acceptance certificate, IC 27624-FZ, on 21 January 2022 ([Eurofins Product Service, Technical Approval Certificate DE-04-01522W, 21 January 2022](https://cdn.flipper.net/ISED_27624-FZ_Technical_Acceptance_Certificate_FZ.1.pdf)). By 13 May 2022 the company counted 23,000 devices shipped, and its co-founder Pavel Zhovner wrote in an unedited afterword: "My worst nightmare is being the person who failed, didn't live up to their commitments, and scammed 40k backers" ([Flipper blog, "23,000 Devices Shipped!", 13 May 2022](https://blog.flipper.net/quick-start-guide-and-shipping-update/)). On 4 July 2022 nearly 90% of Kickstarter devices had been sent ([Flipper blog, "90% of Kickstarter Orders Shipped", 4 July 2022](https://blog.flipper.net/most-kickstarter-orders-shipped/)), and three days later the shop opened pre-orders for buyers in the United States, Canada, Australia, the European Union and the United Kingdom ([Flipper blog, "Shop is Now Open", 7 July 2022](https://blog.flipper.net/shop-is-open/)).

### The company

The United States trademark registration of 1 March 2022 names the owner as "Flipper Devices Inc. (DELAWARE CORPORATION)" with an office address in Moscow, and describes the product as a handheld security testing device "for use by IT security professionals, computer hardware and software engineers, and electronics hobbyists" (IT standing for information technology) ([USPTO, registration 6,660,671, FLIPPER ZERO](https://cdn.flipper.net/Flipper_Zero_US_Trademark__90121012.pdf)). In June 2023 TechCrunch reported that the company "started in Russia in 2020, left the country at the start of the war" and was on track to sell US$80 million of products that year after US$25 million the year before; a company spokesperson then told the publication that the company is not Russian, that its chief executive is Ukrainian, that it is a distributed company building its headquarters in London and that it does not ship devices to Russia ([TechCrunch, "Flipper hacking device on track to make $80M worth of sales", 26 June 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/flipper-sales/)). Those are the company's statements. In November 2026 its contact page gives an address in Claymont, Delaware ([Flipper Devices, company page](https://flipperdevices.com/)). In July 2026 the company wrote that "the total number of Flipper Zero users passed one million" ([Flipper blog, "Future of Flipper Zero development", 1 July 2026](https://blog.flipper.net/future-of-flipper-zero-development/)).

The inspiration came from another hacking gadget. The manufacturer wrote, as quoted by Ars Technica, that "Flipper was inspired by pwnagotchi project", the Wi-Fi hacking pet described in the next part ([Ars Technica, 2 November 2023](https://arstechnica.com/security/2023/11/flipper-zero-gadget-that-doses-iphones-takes-once-esoteric-attacks-mainstream/)).

Two details matter for what follows. The 125 kHz reader has no chip of its own: "The STM32WB55 microcontroller unit is used for the 125 kHz RFID functionality" ([Flipper Docs, 125 kHz RFID](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/rfid)). And the radio chip is ordinary. Texas Instruments lists the CC1101 as an active "Low-power Sub-1 GHz wireless transceiver" covering 300 to 348, 387 to 464 and 779 to 928 MHz with a maximum transmit power of 12 dBm ([Texas Instruments, CC1101 product page](https://www.ti.com/product/CC1101)); Flipper Devices notes that it "has been widely used since its release in 2007" in radio toys and educational kits ([Flipper blog, "Our Response to the Canadian Government", 19 March 2024](https://blog.flipper.net/response-to-canadian-government/)). The Flipper's own power figure, -20 dBm, is far below what the chip can do. Every capability described below exists in other products; what the Flipper changed was putting them in one case with a game-like interface.

## Sub-GHz: what the radio actually does

The application "can receive and transmit radio frequencies in the range of 300-928 MHz" to read, save and emulate remotes used with "gates, barriers, radio locks, remote control switches, wireless doorbells, smart lights, and more"; the built-in antenna's maximum range is 50 metres ([Flipper Docs, Sub-GHz](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz)). Its functions are reading by known protocol, Read RAW for unknown ones, a frequency analyser, manual creation of a virtual remote, and a region information screen.

**It is not a software-defined radio (SDR).** The documentation says so in as many words: "Because Flipper Zero is not a software-defined radio, you need to configure the right modulation before scanning the signal, otherwise, you will not receive the correct data" ([Flipper Docs, Reading signals](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz/read)). The presets are AM270 and AM650 (amplitude modulation, 270 and 650 kHz bandwidth) and FM238 and FM476 (frequency modulation with deviations of about 2.38 and 47.6 kHz). The frequency analyser reports the strongest signal above -90 dBm, and hopping mode stops on any frequency that crosses that threshold for one second (same source). For the tools that sample the whole band, see [Radio for hackers: SDR, HackRF, RTL-SDR and rolling codes](/learn/radio-for-hackers-sdr-hackrf-rtl-sdr-and-rolling-codes).

**Static and dynamic remotes.** The supported-vendors page sorts remotes into two classes. A static protocol is "not encrypted": the Flipper "can decode, save, and playback" it. A dynamic protocol is "encrypted": the Flipper "can decode signals from these radio remotes. For security reasons, the save function is disabled" ([Flipper Docs, Supported Sub-GHz vendors](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz/supported-vendors)). Many of the gate brands listed there fall in the dynamic class: FAAC's RC, XT and SLH remotes, CAME's ATOMO and TOP series, Nice's FLOR-S and One, DoorHan, and Chamberlain's openers at 310 to 390 MHz. With firmware 1.0 the official firmware decoded 89 radio protocols and accepted an external CC1101 module for a more sensitive antenna ([Flipper blog, "Firmware 1.0 Released", 10 September 2024](https://blog.flipper.net/released-firmware-1/)).

The company explains the difference in its own words: "A static code is a fixed set of numbers access control systems use to grant access. Every time the user presses a button on a remote, the same set of numbers is sent. It is enough to capture such a signal once, which can then be endlessly replayed to grant access", while with a rolling code "Simply capturing a signal and replaying it wouldn't provide access since this set of numbers has already been used" ([Flipper blog, 19 March 2024](https://blog.flipper.net/response-to-canadian-government/)). The weakness of static codes is old: the same post shows a static 433 MHz remote recorded through "a piece of wire" plugged into a computer's microphone input. That is the [replay attack](/glossary/replay-attack) in its simplest form.

The device ships with transmission disabled "until the device is updated", and the update determines the region and unlocks only the frequencies "allowed for civilian use" there; outside them the screen reads "Transmission is blocked. Transmission on this frequency is restricted in your region" ([Flipper Docs, Frequencies](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz/frequencies)). Its table, set against the regulators' own texts:

The region lock therefore does what the law asks of the device. It is also the first thing community firmware removes, as described below.

## RFID, NFC, infrared and iButton

**125 kHz cards.** These are low-frequency (LF) cards, and Flipper Devices puts it plainly: "Unlike NFC cards, LF RFID cards don’t usually provide high levels of security" ([Flipper Docs, 125 kHz RFID](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/rfid)). A 125 kHz card "stores a unique identification number" and transmits it when scanned; the Flipper reads about two dozen formats, alternating amplitude-shift and phase-shift keying every three seconds ([Flipper Docs, Reading 125 kHz RFID cards](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/rfid/read)), and can write what it saved onto rewritable T5577 blanks ([Flipper Docs, Writing data to T5577 cards](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/rfid/write-data)). A badge that only says its number can be copied by anything that can listen.

**13.56 MHz cards.** NFC cards "have complex protocols and support encryption, authentication, and full-fledged two-way data transfer" ([Flipper Docs, NFC](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/nfc)). What the Flipper can take depends on the card ([Flipper Docs, Reading NFC cards, updated 16 October 2026](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/nfc/read)):

- **MIFARE Classic.** To read the sectors it must find all 32 keys of a 1K card (80 for a 4K card), first from a dictionary. Where the dictionary fails, the documentation describes MFKey32, which "exploits weaknesses in the Crypto-1 encryption algorithm": the Flipper collects nonce pairs from a reader that tries to authenticate a card, and the keys are computed offline, on a phone, a computer or slowly on the device itself; "card-only" nested and hardnested attacks work on some cards ([Flipper Docs, Recovering MIFARE Classic keys](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/nfc/mfkey32)). The weakness behind this is the 2008 break of the MIFARE Classic, told in [Badges and cards: RFID, NFC, the Proxmark and MIFARE](/learn/badges-and-cards-rfid-nfc-the-proxmark-and-mifare).
- **MIFARE Ultralight and NTAG.** Open pages are read; password-protected pages need the password, or a dictionary for Ultralight C.
- **MIFARE DESFire.** The Flipper "reads and saves unprotected applications and files with data", which leaves the protected ones out.
- **Unknown cards.** "When reading an unknown NFC card, Flipper Zero reads and saves only the UID, SAK, and ATQA", that is, the identifiers a card announces before any authentication. For some cards it can emulate only the identifier.

Bank cards are the line the company drew in public. When Amazon delisted the device in April 2023, Flipper Devices' chief executive, Pavel Zhovner, told BleepingComputer that "the device is not capable of skimming bank cards" ([BleepingComputer, 7 April 2023](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/flipper-zero-banned-by-amazon-for-being-a-card-skimming-device-/)). That is the maker's statement; the documentation lists no payment-card function.

**Infrared.** The Flipper learns and replays remote-control buttons and carries universal remotes that iterate over a dictionary of known codes, "also known as a brute force attack" in the documentation's own phrase ([Flipper Docs, Infrared](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/infrared)). Ars Technica's list of what people do with it begins there: they "covertly change the channels of a TV at a bar" ([Ars Technica, 2 November 2023](https://arstechnica.com/security/2023/11/flipper-zero-gadget-that-doses-iphones-takes-once-esoteric-attacks-mainstream/)).

**iButton.** These small contact keys speak the 1-Wire protocol and, in the documentation's words, "are used for access control, temperature measurements, humidity measurements, storing cryptographic keys, etc." The Flipper reads, writes and emulates Dallas, Cyfral and Metakom access keys through three spring-loaded pins: one side of the pad reads a key, the other presents the Flipper to a reader as if it were the key ([Flipper Docs, iButton](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/ibutton)).

**USB and the rest.** Over USB or BLE the Flipper can act as a keyboard and type a DuckyScript payload, which its documentation says can "open backdoors, retrieve data, initiate reverse shells, or do basically anything that can be achieved with physical access" ([Flipper Docs, Bad USB](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/bad-usb)); that technique has its own part, [Keystroke injection: the Rubber Ducky, the Bash Bunny, BadUSB and the O.MG Cable](/learn/keystroke-injection-rubber-ducky-bash-bunny-badusb-and-the-om-g-cable). It can also serve as a Universal 2nd Factor (U2F) security key, and its GPIO pins turn it into a USB converter for the serial interfaces found on circuit boards ([Flipper Docs, U2F](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/u2f); [Flipper Docs, GPIO & Modules](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/gpio-and-modules)).

## The official firmware

The firmware is published under the GNU General Public License version 3 ([flipperzero-firmware, LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flipperdevices/flipperzero-firmware/dev/LICENSE)) and "is written in C, with some bits and pieces written in C++ and armv7m assembly languages" ([flipperzero-firmware, README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flipperdevices/flipperzero-firmware/dev/ReadMe.md)). Its history is one of running out of room. Flipper Devices says only about 700 KB of flash is available for the firmware, a limit reached early; the answer was loading applications from the microSD card as Flipper Application Package (FAP) files ([Flipper blog, 1 July 2026](https://blog.flipper.net/future-of-flipper-zero-development/)). Firmware 1.0, on 10 September 2024, finished features "that have been in development for 3 years", added an Apps Catalog of open-source community applications, a JavaScript engine based on mJS that needs "less than 50k of flash space and 2k of RAM" (random-access memory), and an NFC subsystem rewritten from scratch whose dictionary key matching became "2.7 times faster" ([Flipper blog, "Firmware 1.0 Released", 10 September 2024](https://blog.flipper.net/released-firmware-1/)).

The stable release on 16 November 2026 is 1.5.1, published on 15 October 2026 ([Flipper update server, firmware directory](https://update.flipperzero.one/firmware/directory.json)). Its notes add "two new protocols (Elplast and Cardin S449 KeeLoq)" in sub-GHz and "a plugin that identifies some hotel card systems" in NFC (same source). In July 2026 the company had to reassure users that it had not stopped developing the firmware: after the community reacted strongly, it allocated resources again, moved feature requests to voting in GitHub Discussions, tightened its rules for pull requests, "especially to AI-generated code that touches low-level libraries", AI meaning artificial intelligence, and published its integration test cases ([Flipper blog, "Future of Flipper Zero development", 1 July 2026](https://blog.flipper.net/future-of-flipper-zero-development/)).

## Community firmware

Because the firmware is open source, forks appeared. Each describes itself in its own repository:

| Xtreme | "an extensive overhaul of the Official Firmware" | "Xtreme is no longer in development" |

Sources: [Unleashed README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DarkFlippers/unleashed-firmware/dev/ReadMe.md); [Xtreme README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Flipper-XFW/Xtreme-Firmware/dev/ReadMe.md); [Momentum README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Next-Flip/Momentum-Firmware/dev/ReadMe.md); [RogueMaster README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RogueMaster/flipperzero-firmware-wPlugins/420/ReadMe.md), all read on 16 November 2026. Each carries a disclaimer; Unleashed's says it "is intended solely for experimental purposes and is not meant for any illegal activities" and that it "is not affiliated with Flipper Devices".

This is where the 2020 promise meets practice. The official firmware locks transmission to legal bands and refuses to save dynamic codes; the most popular forks remove the region lock and add saving and sending for rolling-code protocols. The company's position since 2020 has been that the responsibility then lies with the user. Whether that line is enough is the dual-use question this series keeps returning to, and community firmware is what most of the viral videos ran.

## The panic, in order

**Brazil, March 2023.** Buyers reported from the start of 2023 that parcels were diverted to the Agência Nacional de Telecomunicações (Anatel) for lack of certification. A letter they received, in BleepingComputer's English rendering, said the device "has been used in the country by malicious users in facilitating a crime or criminal misdemeanor" and that, under item II of article 60 of the homologation regulation, Anatel "has rejected all homologation requests for the product in question" ([BleepingComputer, 11 March 2023](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/brazil-seizing-flipper-zero-shipments-to-prevent-use-in-crime/)). Article 60 orders refusal when "o produto se prestar a fins ilícitos, ou concorrer à facilitação de crime ou contravenção penal" ([Anatel, Resolução 715, 23 October 2019, article 60](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2019/1350-resolucao-715)). By 17 March 2023 Anatel, working with the Receita Federal, had seized more than 340 units; it said that "o Flipper Zero e produtos similares podem possuir potencial lesivo aos usuários", citing the maker's own site ([Convergência Digital, 17 March 2023](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/telecom/anatel-apreendeu-mais-de-340-flipper-zero-importados-por-suposto-uso-criminoso)). On 29 March 2023 the agency published a note explaining that homologation by declaration of conformity was not appropriate for the Flipper, that the same criterion applies to signal jammers and to TV boxes, and that security bodies and other public agencies could still homologate it for institutional use through its Gerência de Certificação e Numeração ([Minha Operadora, 29 March 2023](https://www.minhaoperadora.com.br/2023/03/anatel-divulga-nota-sobre-certificacao-do-flipper-zero.html); [Itatiaia, 30 March 2023](https://www.itatiaia.com.br/ciencia-e-tecnologia/anatel-esclarece-proibicao-do-flipper-zero/)). The EFF objected that the device "doesn’t introduce any fundamentally new technologies" and that "Banning trade tools will only make security systems more vulnerable" ([EFF, 9 March 2023](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2023/03/flipper-zero-devices-being-seized-brazils-telecom-agency)). In April 2023 Anatel's inspection superintendent, Hermano Tercius, said the maker had approached the agency and that "Estamos em negociações para explicar os requisitos técnicos que o dispositivo precisa atender para que as vendas sejam permitidas no país", that the adjustments discussed included lower power and sensitivity, that seizures continued, and that companies or researchers could import it for scientific purposes with Anatel's authorization; Mercado Livre and OLX had removed listings ([Minha Operadora, citing TeleSíntese, 7 April 2023](https://www.minhaoperadora.com.br/?p=113265)). No Brazilian homologation followed in the sources read: on 16 November 2026 Flipper's region selector listed Brazil with neither direct sale nor a reseller ([Flipper shop, region selector, 16 November 2026](https://flipper.net/pages/compliance)), and its compliance page lists certificates for the United States, Canada, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Japan, Australia and the Eurasian Economic Union, none from Anatel (same source).

**Amazon, April 2023.** Sellers received notices that "This product has been identified as a card skimming device. Amazon policy prohibits the sale or listing of card skimming devices", with 48 hours to remove other restricted listings; card skimmers sit in Amazon's restricted category next to key duplicating devices ([BleepingComputer, 7 April 2023](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/flipper-zero-banned-by-amazon-for-being-a-card-skimming-device-/)). Zhovner said the company had asked Amazon to reconsider (same source). Amazon had not commented when the story ran.

**Phones on a train, November 2023.** The episode that made the Flipper look like a weapon involved community firmware. Jeroen van der Ham, a security researcher, told Ars Technica that on a Dutch train his iPhone showed pop-ups "every few minutes and then my phone would reboot", and that the passenger responsible was "blithely working on some kind of app on his Macbook" ([Ars Technica, 2 November 2023](https://arstechnica.com/security/2023/11/flipper-zero-gadget-that-doses-iphones-takes-once-esoteric-attacks-mainstream/)). The device ran the Xtreme firmware, whose BLE setting labelled "iOS 17 attack" floods nearby phones with fake pairing announcements; the researcher could not crash iPhones running versions before iOS 17 (same source). After iOS 17.2, MacRumors reported, the pop-ups still appeared but no longer in a deluge capable of disabling an iPhone ([MacRumors, 15 December 2023](https://www.macrumors.com/2023/12/15/apple-flipper-zero-bug-fix/)).

**Canada, February 2024.** On 8 February 2024 a federal summit on auto theft announced that the government would be "Pursuing all avenues to ban devices used to steal vehicles by copying the wireless signals for remote keyless entry, such as the Flipper Zero" ([Public Safety Canada, 8 February 2024; read again 17 November 2026 in a browser](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-safety-canada/news/2024/02/government-of-canada-hosts-national-summit-on-combatting-auto-theft.html)). The industry minister, François-Philippe Champagne, posted: "Today, I announced we are banning the importation, sale and use of consumer hacking devices, like flippers, used to commit these crimes"; Flipper Devices' chief operating officer, Alex Kulagin, answered that the device "can't be used to hijack any car, specifically the ones produced after the 1990s, since their security systems have rolling codes" ([BleepingComputer, 9 February 2024](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/canada-wants-to-ban-the-flipper-zero-to-stop-surge-in-car-thefts/)). ## The law, briefly

The rules are read in full in a later part of this series, on owning hacking gear legally. The essentials, as read on 16 November 2026:

- **Brazil.** "É vedada a utilização de equipamentos emissores de radiofreqüência sem certificação expedida ou aceita pela Agência" ([Lei 9.472 of 16 July 1997, article 162, paragraph 2, original text at the Senado](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/551348/publicacao/15754971)). Restricted-radiation equipment, the low-power class that includes remote controls, "devem possuir certificação emitida ou aceita pela Anatel" ([Anatel, Resolução 680, 27 June 2017, article 4](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2017/936-resolucao-680)). Any natural or legal person may request homologation "quando para uso próprio" ([Resolução 715, article 20, III](https://informacoes.anatel.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/2019/1350-resolucao-715)), but article 60, II, closes that door when the product lends itself to unlawful ends. Separately, article 154-A of the Código Penal, added by Lei 12.737 of 2012, punishes whoever "produz, oferece, distribui, vende ou difunde dispositivo ou programa de computador com o intuito de permitir a prática" of invading a device: the offence turns on intent, not on the object ([Lei 12.737, 30 November 2012, at the Senado](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/588113/publicacao/15773241)); Lei 14.155 of 2021 raised the penalty for the invasion itself to one to four years ([Lei 14.155, 27 May 2021, at the Senado](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/34016289/publicacao/34016562)).
- **United States.** The Flipper is an authorized Part 15 device; a remote transmitter must respect 15.231's limits, and using it on someone else's system is a matter for other laws, read in the later legal part.
- **Canada.** Certified radio apparatus; since June 2024 the minister may restrict devices by order under the amended Radiocommunication Act, with exemptions for legitimate users.
- **European Union and United Kingdom.** Sold under declarations of conformity listed on its compliance page ([Flipper, compliance documents](https://flipper.net/pages/compliance)), within the ERC/REC 70-03 bands shown above.

This is a summary, not legal advice.

## How defenders make it irrelevant

Nearly every Flipper trick depends on a system that trusts something it should not. The defences follow the radios:

- **Remotes and gates.** Replace static-code remotes with rolling-code or two-way systems; Flipper Devices' own list shows which brands use dynamic protocols ([Flipper Docs, Supported Sub-GHz vendors](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz/supported-vendors)). A static code can be captured "with a piece of wire", so the Flipper is the least of the problem ([Flipper blog, 19 March 2024](https://blog.flipper.net/response-to-canadian-government/)).
- **Badges.** A 125 kHz badge that only announces its number is copyable by design; MIFARE Classic keys fall to known attacks. Card systems that authenticate cryptographically, and readers configured to require it, are the subject of [Badges and cards](/learn/badges-and-cards-rfid-nfc-the-proxmark-and-mifare).
- **Phones.** Keep systems updated: iOS 17.2 ended the pop-up crash. Ars Technica listed the settings that silence BLE pairing prompts on Android (the "show notification" option of Nearby Share) and Windows ("Show notifications to connect using Swift Pair") ([Ars Technica, 2 November 2023](https://arstechnica.com/security/2023/11/flipper-zero-gadget-that-doses-iphones-takes-once-esoteric-attacks-mainstream/)).
- **Computers.** Treat any USB or Bluetooth keyboard as untrusted until approved; the controls are in [Keystroke injection](/learn/keystroke-injection-rubber-ducky-bash-bunny-badusb-and-the-om-g-cable).
- **Detection.** The Flipper advertises itself over BLE, and cheap tools look for it: the ESP32 Marauder firmware lists detection of "Flipper" devices among its Bluetooth sniffers ([ESP32 Marauder wiki, Applications; read again 17 November 2026 in a browser](https://github.com/justcallmekoko/ESP32Marauder/wiki/applications)).
- **Distance.** The built-in antenna's range is 50 metres for sub-GHz and a few centimetres for cards; an attacker must be close, which is why physical security and observation still matter ([Flipper Docs, Sub-GHz](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/sub-ghz)).

## What to remember

- The Flipper Zero puts ordinary chips (STM32WB55, CC1101, ST25R3916) in one game-like case; nothing in it is unique, which is both the company's defence and the regulators' worry.
- Official firmware locks transmission to each region's legal bands and refuses to save rolling codes; community forks remove the lock and add rolling-code features, and most viral demonstrations used them.
- Its sub-GHz table maps onto FCC 15.231, ERC/REC 70-03 and Anatel's Ato 14448, and Brazil's periodic-operation rule is nearly a copy of the American one.
- It reads 125 kHz identifiers easily, MIFARE Classic through the published Crypto-1 weaknesses, and only the unprotected parts of DESFire; the maker says it cannot skim bank cards or steal modern cars, and independent researchers broadly agree about cars.
- Brazil refused homologation under Resolução 715, article 60, II, Amazon delisted it in 2023, and Canada announced a ban in 2024, then narrowed it to illegitimate users and enacted the power to restrict devices without, as of 16 November 2026, a found order naming the Flipper.
