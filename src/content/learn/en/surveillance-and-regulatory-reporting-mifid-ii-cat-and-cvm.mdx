An order on an electronic market is a message, and every message leaves a record: in the venue's matching engine, in the firm's order management system, in the drop copies sent to its risk desk, and, increasingly, in a regulator's own database. Market surveillance is the business of reading those records for abuse, and regulatory reporting is the obligation to hand them over. This part of the series opened by [Electronic trading: from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) follows [Market data: consolidated feeds and direct feeds](/learn/market-data-consolidated-feeds-and-direct-feeds), which described what the market sees; here the subject is what the watchers see. The controls that stop an order before it reaches the market belong to [Pre-trade risk and market access](/learn/pre-trade-risk-and-market-access-controls), the capture and time stamping of the traffic to [Network-derived analytics: watching trading on the wire](/learn/network-derived-analytics-and-trading-monitoring), and the clocks that stamp every record were covered in [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency). After reading, you will be able to say what the law in the European Union (EU), the United States and Brazil calls manipulation, recognize spoofing and layering in order book data and explain why intent decides the case, follow a suspicious order from a firm's alert to a regulator's file, read the fields of a European transaction report, explain what the United States Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT) collects, what it costs and what it stopped collecting, describe how BSM Supervisão de Mercados and the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM), Brazil's securities regulator, watch B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão), and weigh what surveillance can and cannot see.

## Three watchers and their records

Surveillance happens in three places, and each holds different data. The EU's rule on surveillance systems draws the line between the first two: a venue's systems must analyse each transaction and each order "placed, modified, cancelled or rejected in the systems of the trading venue", while a firm that arranges or executes transactions must also cover its trading "outside a trading venue" ([Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/957, Article 3(1)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0957)). The venue therefore sees its whole book but nothing else, and the firm sees its clients across venues but not the rest of the market. The regulator sees what the others report or what it asks for, and in the United States it now holds a single audit trail that tracks orders "throughout their life cycle" ([Consolidated Audit Trail, home page](https://www.catnmsplan.com/)). Each of the three has an obligation in law. In the EU, the Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) requires venue operators to "establish and maintain effective arrangements, systems and procedures aimed at preventing and detecting" market abuse and to report suspicious orders and transactions, and requires "Any person professionally arranging or executing transactions" to do the same for what passes through it ([Regulation (EU) No 596/2014, Article 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)). In Brazil, an intermediary must "monitorar continuamente as operações por ele intermediadas", continuously monitor the trades it intermediates, to identify those that seek an undue advantage or harm third parties ([Resolução CVM 35, 26 May 2021, Article 33, item IX](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/cvm_institucional/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/001/resol035.pdf)).

The regulator is no longer only a reader of what others send. The United Kingdom's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) explained in 2020 that it "ingests order book data from the leading UK equity trading venues and then runs surveillance algorithms, designed to identify potentially abusive behaviours, across that consolidated data set", and that the trading of the portfolio manager it was fining had been identified by its internal surveillance systems ([FCA, FCA fines and prohibits hedge fund Chief Investment Officer for market abuse, 15 December 2020](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-and-prohibits-hedge-fund-chief-investment-officer-market-abuse)). The records themselves are the drop copies, execution reports and allocations described in [The life of an order in FIX](/learn/the-life-of-an-order-in-fix), the Financial Information eXchange (FIX) protocol being the common language most of them travel in.

## What the law calls manipulation

### Europe: signals, effects and named practices

MAR defines market manipulation by its effect on the market's information. It includes placing an order or any other behaviour which "gives, or is likely to give, false or misleading signals as to the supply of, demand for, or price of, a financial instrument", or which secures a price at "an abnormal or artificial level", unless the person establishes that it acted for legitimate reasons and in conformity with an accepted market practice ([Regulation (EU) No 596/2014, Article 12(1)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)). It names the machine age explicitly: manipulation includes placing, cancelling or modifying orders "by any available means of trading, including by electronic means, such as algorithmic and high-frequency trading strategies", where this disrupts or delays the venue's systems, makes it "more difficult for other persons to identify genuine orders", or creates a false or misleading signal "in particular by entering orders to initiate or exacerbate a trend" (Article 12(2)(c), same regulation).

The Commission's delegated regulation on indicators gives the practices their market names. One is "Submitting multiple or large orders to trade often away from the touch on one side of the order book in order to execute a trade on the other side of the order book. Once the trade has taken place, the orders with no intention to be executed shall be removed", which it says is usually known as layering and spoofing; another is "Entering large number of orders to trade and/or cancellations and/or updates to orders to trade so as to create uncertainty for other participants, slowing down their process and/or to camouflage their own strategy", quote stuffing; a third, momentum ignition, is entering orders "likely to start or exacerbate a trend and to encourage other participants to accelerate or extend the trend" ([Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/522, Annex II](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0522)). For momentum ignition it adds a measurable sign, "the high ratio of cancelled orders (e.g. order to trade ratio)" (same annex). The sanctions are set as minimum maxima for member states to adopt: for a legal person that manipulates the market, at least EUR 15 million or 15% of annual turnover, and for a natural person at least EUR 5 million ([Regulation (EU) No 596/2014, Article 30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)).

### The United States: intent written into the statute

The United States approached the same conduct through a statute that names it. In the words of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, Congress "criminalized" spoofing in 2010 in the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, and the Commodity Exchange Act now proscribes conduct "commonly known to the trade as, 'spoofing' (bidding or offering with the intent to cancel the bid or offer before execution)" ([Seventh Circuit, United States v. Coscia, No. 16-3017, 7 August 2017, reproduced in the appendix to the petition in Supreme Court docket 17-1099](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/17/17-1099/34287/20180202161853965_Coscia%20Petition%20Appendix.pdf)). The definition puts intent at the centre: the same order, placed and cancelled, is lawful or criminal depending on what its sender meant when he placed it.

### Brazil: four prohibited practices

Brazil's rule is Resolução CVM 62 of 19 January 2022, in force from 1 February 2022, which replaced Instrução CVM 8 of 1979 ([Resolução CVM 62, 19 January 2022](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/001/resol062.pdf)). It defines four practices: artificial conditions of demand, supply or price, created when participants "por ação ou omissão dolosa provocarem, direta ou indiretamente, alterações no fluxo de ordens de compra ou venda", by intentional act or omission cause changes in the flow of buy or sell orders; price manipulation, the use of "qualquer processo ou artifício destinado, direta ou indiretamente, a elevar, manter ou baixar a cotação de um valor mobiliário, induzindo terceiros à sua compra e venda", any process or device meant to raise, hold or lower a security's price by inducing others to trade; fraudulent operations; and unfair practices (Article 2, same resolution). Article 3 prohibits all four to listed companies' managers and shareholders, intermediaries and every other market participant, and Article 4 makes a breach a serious infraction (same resolution). Manipulation is also a crime: the CVM's report on its sanctioning activity lists market manipulation under article 27-C of Lei 6.385 among the crimes it must communicate to prosecutors, with whom it has had a cooperation agreement since 2008 ([CVM, Relatório da Atividade Sancionadora, October to December and full year 2025](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora-cvm-2025-4o-trimestre-versao-integral.pdf)). The use of social media to move prices, which the CVM has also punished, is told in [How manipulators harness collective panic](/learn/how-manipulators-harness-collective-panic).

## Spoofing and layering in the record

### One trader, two jurisdictions

The clearest picture of layering in the public record is the FCA's Final Notice to Michael Coscia, an experienced trader who traded commodity futures on ICE Futures Europe (ICE), a Recognised Investment Exchange in the FCA's words, from the United States through a direct market access provider. The FCA found that between 6 September and 18 October 2011 he "deliberately engaged in a form of manipulative trading known as 'layering'", using an algorithm of his own design, and fined him US$903,176 after a 30% settlement discount ([FCA, Final Notice to Michael Coscia, 3 July 2013](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/final-notices/coscia.pdf)). The notice prints one sequence of his orders millisecond by millisecond, and it is the best worked example of what surveillance looks for.

Source: [FCA, Final Notice to Michael Coscia, paragraphs 11, 15 and 17](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/final-notices/coscia.pdf); the FCA says the times are notional, to illustrate the sequence. Computed for this article by two independent programs, in Python and in JavaScript, which agree: the large orders on each side added up to 261 and 309 lots against a genuine order of 17, ratios of 15.4 and 18.2 to 1; they lived 234, 139 and 34 milliseconds on the first leg and 293, 184 and 43 on the second; and the whole round trip lasted 609 milliseconds. The FCA puts the profit on this example at US$340, which is what 17 lots gain from two cents if each lot is 1,000 barrels, the size of ICE's Brent contract ([ICE, Brent Crude Futures, contract specifications](https://www.ice.com/products/219/Brent-Crude-Futures)). The patterns that gave it away are in the notice: of his large Brent orders "only 0.1% were ever fully executed compared to 44.5% of his small orders"; his orders typically rested less than a second; the market's average displayed order was 2 lots and his 40; and his large orders were "consistently over 50% of the market depth within five ticks of the best bid and best offer price at the time that they are placed and typically represent 75% of the market depth" (same notice). The venue saw it first: ICE investigated the trading and "used a monitoring tool to identify the layering trading pattern", which was "often repeated hundreds of times within a day", with typically over a thousand large orders placed and cancelled in a day (same notice).

The same trader was then prosecuted in the United States for trading in 2011 on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) and ICE. The Seventh Circuit recorded that a jury convicted him of spoofing and commodities fraud, that he was sentenced to 36 months in prison, and that the trial evidence showed his cancellations "represented 96% of all Brent futures cancellations on the Intercontinental Exchange during the two-month period", that on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange "35.61% of his small orders were filled, whereas only 0.08% of his large orders were filled", and that the process was "repeated tens of thousands of times, resulting in over 450,000 large orders, and earning Mr. Coscia $1.4 million"; it affirmed on 7 August 2017 ([Seventh Circuit, United States v. Coscia, No. 16-3017](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/17/17-1099/34287/20180202161853965_Coscia%20Petition%20Appendix.pdf)). The defence's case, as his petition to the Supreme Court put it, was that "all of Coscia's orders were bona fide, executable orders subject to legitimate market risk", that during the same weeks "more than 50% of orders placed by other market participants were open for less than one second, and 90.2% of those orders were cancelled in full", and that his indictment "marked the first-ever criminal prosecution under the 'anti-spoofing' provision" ([Coscia v. United States, petition for a writ of certiorari, 2 February 2018](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/17/17-1099/34287/20180202161844684_Coscia%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari.pdf)). The court of appeals answered the vagueness argument that "high-frequency traders cancel 98% of orders before execution" by holding that a person whose conduct is clearly proscribed cannot complain of vagueness as applied to others, and the Supreme Court denied the petition on 14 May 2018 ([Supreme Court, docket 17-1099](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/17-1099.html)).

The FCA reached a similar conclusion about a portfolio manager in equity contracts for difference (CFDs): between 20 January and 15 May 2017 he placed "large misleading orders" he did not intend to execute while placing smaller orders he did intend to execute on the opposite side, aware of the risk that this might be manipulation but going ahead "recklessly", in the FCA's word; the FCA fined him £100,000 and prohibited him from regulated activity, and his reference to the Upper Tribunal was withdrawn on 10 November 2020 ([FCA, 15 December 2020](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-and-prohibits-hedge-fund-chief-investment-officer-market-abuse)). The FCA's 2013 release on Coscia recalled an earlier layering case against Swift Trade, in which the Upper Tribunal had found in January 2013 "as a matter of fact that the layering strategy adopted was abusive" ([FCA, FCA fines US based oil trader US$903K for market manipulation, 22 July 2013](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-us-based-oil-trader-us-903k-market-manipulation)).

### A bank's desks, eight years and a record penalty

Spoofing is not only the work of lone algorithms. On 29 September 2020 the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) ordered JPMorgan to pay US$920.2 million, "the largest amount of monetary relief ever imposed by the CFTC", for spoofing and manipulation that spanned at least eight years and "involved hundreds of thousands of spoof orders in precious metals and U.S. Treasury futures contracts"; the order found that traders on both desks, "including the heads of both desks", placed orders "with the intent to cancel those orders prior to execution" ([CFTC, release 8260-20, 29 September 2020](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8260-20)). For surveillance the most telling finding is about the firm's own controls: the futures commission merchant failed to stop the conduct "despite numerous red flags, including internal surveillance alerts, inquiries from CME and the CFTC, and internal allegations of misconduct from a JPM trader" (same release). The Department of Justice entered a deferred prosecution agreement with the bank the same day, and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) settled parallel charges (same release). Individual traders were prosecuted too. On 20 August 2025 the Seventh Circuit affirmed the convictions of Gregg Smith, Michael Nowak and Christopher Jordan, precious metals futures traders who "were all convicted of various crimes in connection with this practice"; the opinion records that "Nowak and Smith worked at JPMorgan from 2008 to 2016", and on one argument the court said it had "already addressed and rejected this same argument in Chanu", its 2022 decision in United States v. Chanu ([Seventh Circuit, United States v. Smith, Nos. 23-2840, 23-2846 and 23-2849, decided 20 August 2025](https://media.ca7.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/OpinionsWeb/processWebInputExternal.pl?Submit=Display&Path=Y2025/D08-20/C:23-2840:J:Kirsch:aut:T:fnOp:N:3413241:S:0)). The case of the London trader whose spoofing the CFTC linked to the Flash Crash of 6 May 2010 is told in [High-frequency trading: what it is and the dispute](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute), and a broker that relaxed its layering controls for a client is a case in [Pre-trade risk and market access](/learn/pre-trade-risk-and-market-access-controls).

### Brazil: the first spoofing and layering judgments, and a case lost

The CVM judged its first spoofing case in March 2018 and its first layering case on 1 October 2019. The magazine Capital Aberto reported that the 2019 case involved at least 8,000 operations by an individual investor between 2013 and 2017, about R$1 million obtained by layering according to the prosecution, and a unanimous conviction for price manipulation under the old Instrução CVM 8, following the vote of the CVM's then chairman, Marcelo Barbosa ([Capital Aberto, CVM condena investidor por manipulação de mercado em caso de layering, 11 October 2019](https://www.capitalaberto.com.br/regulacao/2019/10/cvm-condena-investidor-por-manipulacao-de-mercado-em-caso-de-layering)). The same report sets out both sides: the defence argued that placing orders on both sides of the book and cancelling them soon after are permitted conduct, and that the sample of operations, which came from BSM's surveillance, had not been selected on precise criteria; the rapporteur answered that the conduct, "quando observadas num contexto global", seen in its overall context, showed a strategy, and cited the investor's having continued after warnings from his intermediaries and from BSM (same report). The CVM's own decisions in these two sanctioning proceedings, numbered RJ2016/7192 and 19957.006019/2018-26 in the same report, were not found on its site for this article.

Proving manipulation from order data alone is not automatic. On 25 November 2025 the CVM's board acquitted Cortel Holding S.A. of price manipulation in units of a real estate fund, a charge based on the theory that its orders, executed in a "concentrada e agressiva" way, concentrated and aggressive, were meant to "limpar" the book, to clean it, up to target prices; two individuals in the same case were fined for unfair practices ([CVM, Relatório da Atividade Sancionadora, October to December and full year 2025](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora-cvm-2025-4o-trimestre-versao-integral.pdf)).

## Suspicious transaction and order reports

### What the rule requires

The EU's technical standard on surveillance turns MAR's duty into system requirements. Firms that arrange or execute transactions must monitor "all orders received and transmitted and all transactions executed", venues all the orders they receive and transactions they execute, and the systems of both must "allow for the analysis, individually and comparatively, of each and every transaction executed and order placed, modified, cancelled or rejected" and "produce alerts indicating activities requiring further analysis"; venues must use "software capable of deferred automated reading, replaying and analysis of order book data, and such software shall have sufficient capacity to operate in an algorithmic trading environment" ([Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/957, Articles 2 and 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0957)). Software is not enough on its own: the same article requires "an appropriate level of human analysis" in monitoring and detection (Article 3(4), same regulation). When a reasonable suspicion is formed, the suspicious transaction and order report (STOR) goes to the competent authority "without delay", on the regulation's template, and the person reported, and anyone who does not need to know, must not be told (Articles 5 to 7, same regulation). A firm that fails in these duties faces its own sanction: for a legal person, MAR sets the maximum for breaches of Article 16 at no less than EUR 2.5 million or 2% of annual turnover ([Regulation (EU) No 596/2014, Article 30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)). Replaying the book is a networking problem as much as a compliance one: it needs the venue's order data with time stamps fine enough to put events in order, which is why the clock rules in [The rules that rewired markets](/learn/the-rules-that-rewired-markets-reg-ats-reg-nms-mifid-and-brazil) and the capture of the data on the wire matter to surveillance.

The 2024 amendments to MAR, part of the EU Listing Act, add cross-border sharing of order books. Authorities supervising venues with a significant cross-border dimension must, by 5 June 2026, set up "a mechanism to permit the ongoing and timely exchange of order data" collected from those venues, may delegate it to the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA), and must make it operational for bonds and futures, after shares, by 5 June 2028; the venues themselves must have the systems to permit that exchange by 5 June 2026 ([Regulation (EU) 2024/2809 of 23 October 2024, Article 2, inserting Article 25a into MAR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2809)). Whether the mechanism was operating on 16 November 2026 was not found in the sources read.

### How many, and about what

ESMA collects the numbers each year. In 2024 national authorities received 5,981 STORs, 8% fewer than in 2023, with Germany receiving 30% and France 16%; 57% of notifications concerned alleged insider trading and 41% market manipulation; and comparisons with earlier years are distorted by the departure of the United Kingdom, whose FCA had received 50% and 43% of all STORs in 2017 and 2018 ([ESMA, Report on Suspicious Transaction and Order Reports, 19 December 2025](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-12/ESMA74-268544963-1554_Report_on_Suspicious_Transaction_and_Order_Reports__STORs_.pdf)). The FCA alone received 4,528 STORs in 2024, 3,945 about insider dealing, 581 about market manipulation and 2 about other matters ([FCA, Number of STORs received: 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/markets/how-report-suspected-market-abuse-firm-or-trading-venue/number-stors-received-2024)), and 3,806 in 2025, 3,124 about insider dealing, 679 about market manipulation and 3 about other matters ([FCA, Number of STORs received: 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/markets/how-report-suspected-market-abuse-firm-or-trading-venue/number-stors-received-2025)). Adding up the monthly rows of the two tables gives 4,193 equity STORs in 2024 and 3,421 in 2025, about 93% and 90% of the totals (computed for this article from the same pages).

The FCA says the reports matter. In April 2025 Therese Chambers, its joint executive director of enforcement and market oversight, said that "over 70% of our current market abuse investigations" originate from a STOR and that organized crime groups account "for around 25% of all STORs" ([FCA, Our agenda to combat market abuse, speech delivered 28 April 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/our-agenda-combat-market-abuse)). The same speech, published "as drafted" with a note that it may differ from the delivered version, says "Last year, we received 4,528 STORs: 3,495 related to insider dealing and 581 to market manipulation"; the FCA's own table gives 3,945 for insider dealing, and 3,945 + 581 + 2 makes the 4,528 total while 3,495 does not, so the speech's figure appears to transpose two digits (same speech; [FCA, STORs 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/markets/how-report-suspected-market-abuse-firm-or-trading-venue/number-stors-received-2024)). The mix changes over time. In 2024 insider dealing led in both the EU and the United Kingdom, but ESMA notes that from 2019 to 2023 most EU notifications had concerned market manipulation, around 52% each year ([ESMA, 19 December 2025](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-12/ESMA74-268544963-1554_Report_on_Suspicious_Transaction_and_Order_Reports__STORs_.pdf)).

## Transaction reporting under MiFIR

### The obligation

STORs report suspicion; transaction reports report everything. Under the Markets in Financial Instruments Regulation (MiFIR), investment firms that execute transactions must report "complete and accurate details of such transactions to the competent authority as quickly as possible, and no later than the close of the following working day", for instruments admitted to trading or traded on a venue and instruments whose underlying is one of them or an index or basket of them, whether or not the transaction itself took place on a venue; the report must include, among other things, "a designation to identify the persons and the computer algorithms within the investment firm responsible for the investment decision and the execution of the transaction" ([Regulation (EU) No 600/2014, Article 26](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0600)). Firms must keep the relevant data on all orders and transactions for five years, and venues must keep their order data, linked to the resulting transactions, for at least five years (Article 25, same regulation). The detailed record-keeping rules for firms' and venues' order data, and the time stamps that make them usable, are covered in [Network-derived analytics: watching trading on the wire](/learn/network-derived-analytics-and-trading-monitoring).

The volumes are large. The FCA says it receives "over 7 billion transaction reports a year" covering "over 20 million reportable financial instruments" ([FCA, Discussion Paper DP24/2, Improving the UK transaction reporting regime, November 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/discussion/dp24-2.pdf)), and in 2025 it said that on Monday 7 April alone it received 76 million transaction reports across asset classes, more than double its daily average of 30 million ([FCA, speech of 28 April 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/our-agenda-combat-market-abuse)).

### Worked example: one trade as a report

The regulatory technical standard (RTS) known as RTS 22 sets the content of a report in 65 fields ([Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/590, Annex I, Table 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0590)). The trade below is invented: an investment firm's algorithm buys 1,000 shares for the firm's own account on a regulated market that uses a central counterparty (CCP), and the seller's identity is not disclosed. The fields and their formats are the standard's.

| Field | Name | Format in RTS 22 | Value for this trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report status | 'NEWT' new, 'CANC' cancellation | NEWT |

## The Consolidated Audit Trail

### Why it was built

The United States audit trail grew out of a reconstruction that took too long. When the SEC adopted Rule 613 in July 2012, it explained that "it took Commission staff nearly five months to complete an accurate representation of the order books of the equity markets for May 6, 2010", and that even then the reconstruction "only contained an estimated 90% of trade and order activity for that day" ([SEC, Consolidated Audit Trail, Release 34-67457, 18 July 2012](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2012/34-67457.pdf)). Until then regulators had relied on separate trails: the Order Audit Trail System (OATS) that the National Association of Securities Dealers, now the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA), established in 1996, the New York Stock Exchange's Order Tracking System of 1999, options trails and the Electronic Blue Sheets system, for which "complete responses from each broker-dealer may take days or weeks"; the SEC had no direct access to OATS and other trails, and requests to the self-regulatory organizations (SROs) could take "days or weeks" (same release). The proposal had carried a cost estimate of about US$4 billion, which the final release described as based on building very large systems from scratch (same release). The flash crash itself is told in [High-frequency trading: what it is and the dispute](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute).

Rule 613 did not build the trail; it required the exchanges and FINRA to propose a plan. The SEC approved the CAT National Market System (NMS) Plan on 15 November 2016 ([SEC, Release 34-79318, 15 November 2016](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2016/34-79318.pdf)). The trail is run by Consolidated Audit Trail, LLC (CAT LLC), jointly owned on an equal basis by its participants, the exchanges and FINRA ([SEC, Release 34-105003, 16 March 2026](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-105003.pdf)); its site describes its purpose as tracking "orders throughout their life cycle" and identifying "the broker-dealers handling them" ([CAT NMS Plan, home page, read 16 November 2026](https://www.catnmsplan.com/)); the system itself is operated under a plan processor agreement by FINRA CAT, which publishes the alerts to reporting firms ([SEC, Release 34-105107, Federal Register, 1 April 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-01/pdf/2026-06255.pdf); [CAT Alert 2025-03, 14 August 2025](https://www.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2025-08/08.14.25-CAT-Alert_2025-03.pdf)). The clock tolerances it imposes, 100 microseconds for exchanges and 50 milliseconds for broker-dealers, are in [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency).

### What it costs

The cost became the CAT's central dispute. In 2016 the SEC estimated the annual cost of running the central repository at about US$55.8 million; by its own account in 2026, "CAT operating costs have far exceeded these estimates due largely to increases in trading activity", the 2025 budget initially approved was about US$249 million, revised in May 2025 to about US$228 million, and CAT LLC reported cloud fees of US$136 million for 109 trillion events in 2022, US$128 million for 116 trillion in 2023 and US$135 million for 154 trillion in 2024 ([SEC, Release 34-105107, Federal Register, 1 April 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-01/pdf/2026-06255.pdf)). The United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit summarized the gap: the cost of setting up the system "exceeded the Commission's 2016 estimate by eight times", the annual running cost was likely to exceed it "by nearly four times", and "it took over $500 million to build the CAT and will likely cost about $200 million a year to run it" ([Eleventh Circuit, American Securities Association v. SEC, No. 23-13396, 25 July 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca11-23-13396/pdf/USCOURTS-ca11-23-13396-0.pdf)). The budget for 2027 foresees expenses of about US$136.3 million, of which US$75.2 million is cloud hosting, and revenue of US$148.0 million, two thirds from industry members and one third from the SROs, the difference going to a liquidity reserve ([CAT LLC, 2027 Financial and Operating Budget, posted November 2026](https://www.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2026-11/11.13.26-CAT-2027-Budget.pdf)).

Who pays was decided in court. The original plan anticipated that the costs would be shared by the SROs and their members, but the SEC's 2023 funding order "expressly allows self-regulatory organizations to pass along all of their CAT costs to their members"; a trade association, the American Securities Association, and a broker-dealer, Citadel Securities, challenged it, and on 25 July 2025 the Eleventh Circuit vacated it as arbitrary and capricious, because the SEC had not justified the pass-through and "Although the Commission underestimated the cost of building and maintaining the CAT, it declined to update that previous economic analysis with real-world numbers in its 2023 Funding Order"; the court stayed its judgment, so fees continued in the meantime ([Eleventh Circuit, No. 23-13396](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca11-23-13396/pdf/USCOURTS-ca11-23-13396-0.pdf); [CAT Alert 2025-03](https://www.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2025-08/08.14.25-CAT-Alert_2025-03.pdf)). The plan's participants filed a revised funding model on 5 September 2025, and the SEC approved it, with modifications, on 16 March 2026 ([SEC, Release 34-105003, 16 March 2026](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-105003.pdf)). CAT LLC said it welcomed "the Chairman's call for a comprehensive review of the CAT" and would work with the Commission to reduce its costs ([CAT Alert 2025-03](https://www.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2025-08/08.14.25-CAT-Alert_2025-03.pdf)).

### What it knows about people

The other dispute is privacy. A database that links every order to a customer is a target, and the plan has narrowed what it holds. The SEC describes the structure: personal customer information is stored separately, and "The transaction database contains only anonymized order and event data, including anonymized customer identifiers" ([SEC, Release 34-104586, 13 January 2026](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-104586.pdf)). In 2020 it granted relief that limited the personal information reported to name, address and birth year and let the plan processor generate a CAT customer identifier (CCID) through a two-phase transformation, so that Social Security numbers (SSNs) are not reported to the CAT; in February 2025 it exempted the reporting of names, addresses and years of birth for natural persons reported with transformed identifiers, concluding that "the regulatory benefit of collecting the names, addresses and years of birth for natural persons reported with transformed SSNs no longer justified the associated risks" (same order). On 13 January 2026 it approved an amendment to the Customer and Account Information System (CAIS) that eliminates the reporting of names, addresses and dates of birth for all customers and requires the deletion of those already reported, with estimated savings of US$7 to US$9 million a year ([SEC, Release 34-105107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-01/pdf/2026-06255.pdf)). A regulator that needs the person behind a CCID can still ask: it uses the firm designated identifiers linked to it and requests the name, address or year of birth from the broker-dealers that reported them ([SEC, Release 34-104586](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-104586.pdf)).

The two sides of the privacy argument appear in the record. Removing personal data, the SEC stressed when it granted the 2025 exemption, left the system of generating CCIDs, the anonymized identifiers linked to every order event, unchanged: it "would not be impacted", and regulators keep the ability to identify individuals through the firms (same order). Some commenters wanted more: one, objecting to the 2026 amendment, stated that neither the SEC nor the SROs have rights above the United States Constitution and invoked the Fourth Amendment's protection against unwarranted search or seizure (same order). How other surveillance systems are restrained, by statute, courts and oversight bodies, is discussed in [Surveillance capability and restraint](/learn/surveillance-capability-and-restraint).

### Reports the public can read

Not every United States report goes only to regulators. Rule 605, adopted in 2000 as Rule 11Ac1-5, requires market centres to make public standardized monthly reports of execution quality statistics for the orders in NMS stocks they receive; amendments adopted on 6 March 2024 extended it to broker-dealers that introduce or carry 100,000 or more customer accounts, added a summary report, and brought in fractional share, odd-lot and larger orders ([SEC, Disclosure of Order Execution Information, Release 34-99679, 6 March 2024](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-99679.pdf)). Rule 606, amended on 2 November 2018, requires broker-dealers to publish quarterly order routing reports for held orders in NMS stocks, describing among other things "any terms of payment for order flow arrangements and profit-sharing relationships with certain venues that may influence their order routing decisions", to keep them online for three years, and to give a customer, on request, the routing and execution details of its not held orders for the prior six months ([SEC, Disclosure of Order Handling Information, Release 34-84528, 2 November 2018](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2018/34-84528.pdf)). These are the reports that brokers' systems produce alongside their CAT submissions, as [OMS and EMS: order and execution management systems](/learn/oms-and-ems-order-and-execution-management-systems) notes; they measure execution quality and routing, not abuse.

## Brazil: BSM and the CVM

### BSM, the self-regulator

In Brazil the organized markets are run by B3, and a single body watches all of them. BSM Supervisão de Mercados calls itself "principal autorreguladora do mercado de capitais brasileiro", the main self-regulator of the Brazilian capital market, "Uma associação civil, pessoa jurídica de direito privado", a civil association under private law, created to supervise the organized markets B3 runs, with administrative and budgetary autonomy and regulated by the CVM ([BSM, Quem somos](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/quem-somos)). B3's regulatory filing adds that BSM is a non-profit association, that it is not consolidated in B3's accounts, and that B3, as its maintaining entity, contributed about R$272 million to its activities between its creation in 2007 and 31 December 2023 ([B3, Formulário de Referência 2024, version 16](https://api.mziq.com/mzfilemanager/v2/d/5fd7b7d8-54a1-472d-8426-eb896ad8a3c4/8d8a1c1b-991e-904f-e699-a9cb7cc6d9e5?origin=1)). The CVM's rule on organized markets requires each market operator to keep a self-regulation department, or to exercise self-regulation through an association, charged with supervising the trades on its markets, its own market activities and its participants ([Resolução CVM 135, 10 June 2022, consolidated text, Articles 47 and 48](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/100/resol135consolid.pdf)).

BSM says it monitors everything: "Monitoramos, diariamente, 100% das ofertas e operações realizadas nos mercados da B3", every day, 100% of the offers and trades on B3's markets, with statistical models developed in-house and, in 2025, more use of big data, artificial intelligence and machine learning ([BSM, Relatório Anual 2025](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/documents/1266368/1502039/Relat%C3%B3rio+Anual+2025.pdf/726df4f5-35da-1573-e2be-e699df474f06?version=1.0&t=1774992665214)). BSM also teaches. Its alert guide, version 2.0 of September 2024, defines layering as inserting or modifying supposedly fictitious offers "sem a intenção real de executá-la(s), formando camadas no livro", without a real intention to execute them, forming layers in the book, to trade on the opposite side at better prices, and walks through order book tables with millisecond time stamps ([BSM, Guia de Alertas BSM, version 2.0, September 2024](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/documents/d/guest/guia-de-alertas-2024)). Each month it publishes layering and spoofing benchmarks so that intermediaries "possam comparar seus próprios padrões operacionais com os parâmetros observados no mercado", can compare their own patterns with those observed in the market, and use them to build alerts ([BSM, benchmarks for October 2026](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/w/benchmarks-de-layering-spoofing-e-transfer%C3%AAncia-de-recursos-de-outubro-dispon%C3%ADveis-1)); the glossary of those benchmarks defines 48 fields per instrument and month: identifiers and the number of trading days, then the time a spread stays unchanged, the time between trades, order sizes and spread values, each summarized over the month's days as averages, standard deviations, medians and quartiles ([BSM, Glossary Layering Spoofing](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/documents/1266368/1723361/Glossary-LS.xlsx/f7d625fa-8a02-9615-ad16-b132dc76921d?version=1.0&t=1725906243685)). BSM's public page on market supervision illustrates spoofing with a case in mini index futures: at 10:27:02.942 a client entered an offer to sell 10 contracts at R$94,690; three seconds later, an offer to buy 80 contracts at R$94,685; a few milliseconds after that false offer another investor entered a buy order for 20 contracts, generating trades; and once the sale was executed the client deleted the false buy offer ([BSM, Supervisão de mercado](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/supervisao-de-mercado)). The case, it says, was identified because the investor "sempre abria ofertas de lotes expressivos do lado oposto", always opened large offers on the opposite side, with a high cancellation rate and short resting times (same page).

### The CVM, behind BSM

Many of BSM's cases end at the CVM: when a hypothesis is corroborated, BSM says, it sends the case "para o regulador de competência, normalmente à CVM", to the competent regulator, normally the CVM, and assesses the conduct of the participant and its staff for its own enforcement ([BSM, Supervisão de mercado](https://www.bsmsupervisao.com.br/supervisao-de-mercado)); in 2025 that meant 49 of the 122 closed cases, and 2 more sent to both (table above). The CVM judges them under Resolução CVM 62, as in the cases described earlier, and passes signs of crime to prosecutors: in 2025 it sent 95 such communications, 28 to state prosecutors and 67 to the federal prosecution service, against 325 in 2020 ([CVM, Relatório da Atividade Sancionadora, October to December and full year 2025](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora-cvm-2025-4o-trimestre-versao-integral.pdf)). The evidence comes from records the intermediaries must keep under [Resolução CVM 35](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/cvm_institucional/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/001/resol035.pdf), orders with their times, recorded calls and the origin of direct market access orders, and from clocks that [Resolução CVM 135, Article 17](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/100/resol135consolid.pdf) requires market operators to synchronize to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), with an accuracy the CVM is to set; those rules are set out in [Network-derived analytics](/learn/network-derived-analytics-and-trading-monitoring), and the direct access rules in [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access).

## What surveillance can and cannot see

The cases above show both the reach and the limits. Order book data shows patterns with great precision: lifetimes in milliseconds, sizes against the market's average, cancellation ratios by order size, shares of depth. It does not show intent, and intent is what every one of the three legal systems turns on: the United States statute defines spoofing by "the intent to cancel", MAR exempts conduct with legitimate reasons, and Resolução CVM 62 speaks of "ação ou omissão dolosa". In the Coscia case the government proved intent with the design of the program and the testimony of its programmer, not with the order book alone ([Seventh Circuit, No. 16-3017](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/17/17-1099/34287/20180202161853965_Coscia%20Petition%20Appendix.pdf)); in the JPMorgan case alerts existed and were not acted on ([CFTC, release 8260-20](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8260-20)); in the Cortel case the CVM's board acquitted the company of the price manipulation charge built on its orders ([CVM, 2025](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora/relatorio-de-atividade-sancionadora-cvm-2025-4o-trimestre-versao-integral.pdf)). Each venue and each regulator sees only part of the market, which is why the EU is building an order data exchange between authorities ([Regulation (EU) 2024/2809](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2809)) and the United States built the CAT after finding that each SRO had direct access only to its own audit trail ([SEC, Release 34-67457](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2012/34-67457.pdf)). A consolidated database is expensive and holds sensitive data, and the CAT has been cut back on both counts ([SEC, Release 34-105107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-01/pdf/2026-06255.pdf); [SEC, Release 34-104586](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-104586.pdf)). Surveillance reads the same order books whose public face is the market data of [the previous part](/learn/market-data-consolidated-feeds-and-direct-feeds).

## What to remember

- Three watchers read the same records: the venue sees its whole book, the firm sees its clients, and the regulator sees what is reported to it or, in the United States, a single audit trail across all venues.
- MAR defines manipulation by false or misleading signals and names layering, spoofing, quote stuffing and momentum ignition; the United States statute defines spoofing as bidding or offering with the intent to cancel; Resolução CVM 62 forbids artificial conditions, price manipulation, fraudulent operations and unfair practices.
- The FCA's 2013 notice and the Seventh Circuit's 2017 decision on the same trader show what layering looks like in data, large orders cancelled within milliseconds after a small one fills, and how intent was proved.
- In the EU firms file suspicious order reports without delay and transaction reports by the next working day, in 65 fields that identify the algorithm responsible; ESMA counted 5,981 STORs in 2024, the FCA 4,528 in 2024 and 3,806 in 2025.
- The CAT was built after a five-month reconstruction of the Flash Crash; it cost over US$500 million to build, its running cost far exceeded the 2016 estimate, its 2023 funding order was vacated in 2025, and a 2026 amendment ended the reporting of customers' names, addresses and dates of birth.
- In Brazil BSM monitors all of B3's offers and trades, publishes monthly layering and spoofing benchmarks for intermediaries and sent 51 of the 122 cases it closed in 2025 to the CVM, alone or with its own enforcement; the CVM judged its first spoofing case in 2018 and its first layering case in 2019.
