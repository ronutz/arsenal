Psychological operations (PSYOP), in military doctrine, are planned efforts to change what a chosen audience perceives, feels and does. The same aim runs through a rumour repeated until it sounds true, a fake bank clerk's call, a scam sent to millions and a covert account network run for a government or a client. All work on perception, can travel through digital networks and raise the question of who decides what is harmful. This map follows that range in six steps.

## What the words cover

United States (US) doctrine defines military information support operations (MISO) as "Planned operations to convey selected information and indicators to foreign audiences to influence their emotions, motives, objective reasoning, and ultimately the behavior of foreign governments, organizations, groups, and individuals" ([Joint Publication 3-13.2](https://www.bits.de/NRANEU/others/jp-doctrine/JP3-13.2C1%2811%29.pdf)). It replaced PSYOP in 2010, and a memo of 2 December 2025 reverses that: "Effective immediately, the Department of War (DoW) will use the term Psychological Operations (PSYOP) instead of Military Information Support Operations (MISO)" ([memo, 2025](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Dec/08/2003839440/-1/-1/0/CHANGING-THE-TERM-MILITARY-INFORMATION-SUPPORT-OPERATIONS-BACK-TO-PSYCHOLOGICAL-OPERATIONS.PDF); [DefenseScoop, 2025](https://defensescoop.com/2025/12/09/hegseth-memo-psyop-miso-psychological-operations/)). Army Techniques Publication (ATP) 3-53 (April 2026) defines them as "operations to influence the behavior of targeted foreign audiences in support of a commander's objectives" ([ATP 3-53](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN46327-ATP_3-53-000-WEB-1.pdf)). The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) speaks of activities "directed at approved audiences in order to influence perceptions, attitudes and behaviours" ([NATO doctrine, lexicon](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6525459d244f8e00138e7343/AJP_10_Strat_Comm_Change_1_web.pdf)). For Claire Wardle and Hossein Derakhshan, misinformation is shared with no harm meant, while "Dis-information is when false information is knowingly shared to cause harm" ([Council of Europe, 2017](https://firstdraftnews.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/PREMS-162317-GBR-2018-Report-de%CC%81sinformation-1.pdf)).

Doctrine's term is narrow; this series extends it to any deliberate attempt, by a fraudster, a company or a state, to change belief or behaviour through perception, since all exploit the same human tendencies and raise the same questions. **[PSYOP, propaganda and disinformation: the words](/learn/psyop-propaganda-and-disinformation-the-words)** sets the definitions side by side, Soviet active measures and Meta's coordinated inauthentic behaviour (CIB) among them; **[A short history of propaganda and psychological warfare](/learn/a-short-history-of-propaganda-and-psychological-warfare)** runs from the First World War to today.

## The small lie, repeated

The smallest unit is a claim that feels true because it is familiar. Henderson, Simons and Barr define the illusory truth effect: "Repeated statements are rated as subjectively truer than comparable new statements, even though repetition alone provides no new, probative information"; in their study of 567 participants it was still present a month later ([Henderson et al., 2021](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.161)). A meta-analysis of 32 studies found that corrections do not entirely remove misinformation's influence ([Walter and Tukachinsky, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650219854600)), while <Claim status="disputed">the backfire effect, a correction that strengthens the false belief</Claim>, was overstated in its generality, says Brendan Nyhan, co-author of the study that described it ([Nyhan, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912440117)). See **[The human attack surface: why repetition works](/learn/the-human-attack-surface-why-repetition-works)** and **[Why misbeliefs persist](/learn/why-misbeliefs-persist)**.

Social engineering aims the same tendencies at one person. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines it as "an attempt to trick an individual into revealing information or taking an action that can be used to breach, compromise, or otherwise adversely impact a system" ([NIST 800-53, AT-2(3)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf)). See [Kevin Mitnick's case](/learn/kevin-mitnick-the-myth-and-the-man) and **[Social engineering: pretexting, vishing and baiting](/learn/social-engineering-pretexting-vishing-and-baiting)**.

Scams industrialize the trick through volume. Cormac Herley modelled why a message costing almost nothing per recipient can pay: <Claim status="reported" by="by its author">"By sending an email that repels all but the most gullible the scammer gets the most promising marks to self-select"</Claim> ([Herley, 2012](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/WhyFromNigeria.pdf)), a model the anthropologist Radu Umbres faulted for lacking data on success rates ([Umbres, 2013](https://cognitionandculture.net/blogs/radu-umbres/why-do-scammers-persist-in-saying-they-are-from-nigeria/index.html)). The Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) received 1,008,597 complaints in 2025, with $20.877 billion in reported losses ([IC3, 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). In Brazil, fraud notifications on Pix instant payments averaged <Claim status="reported" by="by Estadão Conteúdo">0.007% of monthly transactions from April 2023</Claim>, according to central bank data the press obtained ([Estadão Conteúdo, 2025](https://www.bemparana.com.br/noticias/economia/fraudes-no-pix-disparam-acima-de-390-mil-por-mes-em-2024-mostram-dados-do-bc/)). The United Nations human rights office cites estimates of at least 300,000 people working in Southeast Asian scam operations ([UN News, 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/02/1167012)). See **[Scams at scale: why a tiny success rate pays](/learn/scams-at-scale-why-a-tiny-success-rate-pays)** , **[Scam compounds and "pig butchering"](/learn/scam-compounds-and-pig-butchering)**, **[Business email compromise and impersonation](/learn/business-email-compromise-and-impersonation)** and **[Voice clones and deepfakes in fraud](/learn/voice-clones-and-deepfakes-in-fraud)**.

## Campaigns: many actors, different aims

The ABC framework sorts influence campaigns by actors, behaviours and content ([François, 2019](https://www.ivir.nl/publicaties/download/ABC_Framework_2019_Sept_2019.pdf)), and DISARM (Disinformation Analysis and Risk Management) catalogues their tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs) ([DISARM Foundation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks/main/README.md)). Meta said in 2022 that CIB networks it had removed since 2017 came from 68 countries, two-thirds aimed wholly or partly at domestic audiences ([Meta, 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/12/metas-2022-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-enforcements/)).

For Russia's Internet Research Agency (IRA), the US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) found that <Claim status="attributed" by="by the SSCI">"the IRA sought to influence the 2016 U.S. presidential election by harming Hillary Clinton's chances of success and supporting Donald Trump at the direction of the Kremlin"</Claim> ([SSCI, 2019](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Report_Volume2.pdf)). The Kremlin's spokesman said the Special Counsel's report "contains no proven evidence that Russia allegedly interfered" ([tass.com, 2019](https://tass.com/politics/1054567)); in 2023 Yevgeny Prigozhin said he had invented, created and run the agency ([The Moscow Times, 2023](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2023/02/14/wagner-founder-prigozhin-admits-he-was-behind-russias-infamous-troll-farms-a80228)). A 2016 Twitter panel study found no meaningful link between exposure and changes in attitudes or voting ([Eady et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9)).

In 2023 Meta found <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">links between the Spamouflage network and individuals associated with Chinese law enforcement</Claim> ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)); China's foreign ministry spokesperson replied that "Some people and institutions have launched one 'rumor campaign' after another against China" ([Chinese foreign ministry, 2023](https://adelaide.china-consulate.gov.cn/eng/wjbfyrth/202308/t20230830_11135713.htm)). In 2022 Meta found <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">links to individuals associated with the US military</Claim> in a network ([Meta, 2022b](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/11/metas-adversarial-threat-report-q3-2022/)), after the Pentagon ordered an audit of its clandestine information operations and pledged to enforce its safeguards, according to The Washington Post ([Stars and Stripes, 2022](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2022-09-19/pentagon-clandestine-psychological-operations-7388658.html)). In 2024 Meta linked a network to STOIC, a Tel Aviv political marketing firm ([Meta, 2024](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q1-2024-Adversarial-threat-report)); The New York Times reported <Claim status="reported" by="by the newspaper">that Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs was paying STOIC $2 million</Claim> ([npr.org, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/06/05/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign-gaza)); the ministry said it "does not engage in disinformation campaigns" ([thejc.com, 2024](https://www.thejc.com/news/israel/israeli-minister-denies-reports-the-government-ran-covert-influence-campaign-on-us-politicians-uy5gj4fq)), and STOIC did not respond to interview requests.

In Brazil, Meta removed a network in July 2020 that it linked to <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">individuals associated with the Social Liberal Party (PSL) and to employees of the offices of five politicians: Jair, Flávio and Eduardo Bolsonaro and the Rio de Janeiro state deputies Anderson Moraes and Alana Passos</Claim> ([Meta, 2020](https://about.fb.com/news/2020/07/removing-political-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior/)). The party denied responsibility, attributing the accounts to the lawmakers' own aides; Senator Flávio Bolsonaro said "it is impossible to evaluate what kind of profile was banned and if the platform crossed or not the limits of censorship", and Eduardo Bolsonaro did not comment ([Associated Press, 2020](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/international/facebook-removes-false-accounts-linked-to-brazils-bolsonaro/)). Passos said she could not answer for posts by people who had worked in her office; Moraes, that the removed account was a real aide's ([Congresso em Foco, 2020](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/31287/facebook-remove-contas-ligadas-ao-psl-e-a-familia-bolsonaro)); President Jair Bolsonaro challenged the press to show a hateful text of his ([ABCdoABC, 2020](https://www.abcdoabc.com.br/apos-acao-do-facebook-bolsonaro-desafia-imprensa-e-adversarios/)). In October 2021 the electoral court, the Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE), unanimously dismissed both cases brought against Jair Bolsonaro and Hamilton Mourão's 2018 campaign over mass messaging on WhatsApp: as the result was proclaimed, <Claim status="established" by="by the TSE">some justices found the unlawful acts proved but not their gravity, and others found no necessary link between the acts and the ticket</Claim>, a link the defence disputed; by majority it held that such messaging with disinformation can be abuse of economic power ([TSE, 2021](https://sjur-servicos.tse.jus.br/sjur-servicos/rest/download/pdf/2673321)). **[Anatomy of an influence operation: ABC, DISARM and kill chains](/learn/anatomy-of-an-influence-operation-abc-disarm-and-kill-chains)**, **[State campaigns on the record](/learn/state-influence-campaigns-on-the-record)**, **[Influence for hire](/learn/influence-for-hire-the-firms-that-sell-influence-operations)**, **[Bots, sockpuppets and astroturfing](/learn/bots-sockpuppets-and-astroturfing)** and **[Brazil: mass messaging, the courts and the CPMI](/learn/brazil-mass-messaging-the-courts-and-the-cpmi)** (Comissão Parlamentar Mista de Inquérito) go further.

## The amplifier: what digital reach changed

A study of about 126,000 fact-checked stories on Twitter from 2006 to 2017 found that <Claim status="disputed">"Falsehood diffused significantly farther, faster, deeper, and more broadly than the truth in all categories of information"</Claim>, driven by humans rather than bots ([Vosoughi et al., 2018](https://politics.media.mit.edu/papers/Vosoughi_Science.pdf)); a reanalysis found the structural differences almost vanish when equal-sized cascades are compared ([Juul and Ugander, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609637/)).

Experiments with Meta during the 2020 US election cut exposure to like-minded sources, or switched users to a chronological feed, for three months without measurable change in polarization ([Nyhan et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w.pdf); [Guess et al., 2023](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=37498999&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)). Meta read this as <Claim status="disputed">little evidence that its platforms alone cause harmful polarization</Claim> ([Meta, 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/07/research-social-media-impact-elections/)); Science's editor-in-chief wrote that the journal disputed that reading as overstating the evidence ([Thorp, 2024](https://holdenthorp.substack.com/p/more-doubts-for-facebook-on-polarization)). A Science editorial noted 63 emergency measures Facebook applied during the experiment, so "the default algorithm was in flux"; the authors held to their conclusions, Meta said it had informed the researchers, and Science did not correct the paper ([Science editorial, 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adt2983)); errata in 2024 and 2026 revised Instagram measures, saying experimental estimates were unaffected or likely so ([Science, 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adu8261); [Science, 2026](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aeh2575)). A 2025 experiment that reranked X feeds without the platform's help shifted partisan animosity by more than 2 points on a 100-point scale ([Piccardi et al., 2025](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=41308156&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)). Outside the US and Western Europe, Ceren Budak and colleagues note, research and data are scant and harms may be more severe ([Budak et al., 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/s41586-024-07417-w%22&format=json&resultType=core)). **[From the lodge to the network: how reach changed](/learn/from-the-lodge-to-the-network-how-reach-changed)**, **[Echo chambers and filter bubbles: the evidence and the dispute](/learn/echo-chambers-and-filter-bubbles-the-evidence-and-the-dispute)** and **[Recommendation and profiling: what platforms disclose](/learn/recommendation-and-profiling-what-platforms-disclose)** go further.

## Arbitration: what counts as harmful, and who decides

Platforms arbitrate at industrial volume: in the 180 days to 9 November 2026 they filed 4,794,678,524 statements of reasons for moderation decisions under the European Union (EU) Digital Services Act (DSA), 43% for fully automated decisions ([DSA Transparency Database](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/)).

Section 230 of the US Communications Decency Act says "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker" of others' information ([47 U.S.C. 230](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/html/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5-subchapII-partI-sec230.htm)). An appeals court defended it on free-expression grounds: liability on notice would chill speech ([Zeran, 1997](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/Opinions/Published/971523.P.pdf)); the Department of Justice argued in 2020, for harm prevention, that broad readings left platforms immune for much illicit activity ([Department of Justice, 2020](https://www.justice.gov/file/1286331/download)). The DSA makes the largest platforms assess systemic risks, including to civic discourse and elections ([DSA, art. 34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)). Republican staff of the House Judiciary Committee alleged that <Claim status="alleged" by="by committee Republicans">"The DSA is the EU's comprehensive digital censorship law"</Claim> ([House Republicans, 2025](https://judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/republicans-judiciary.house.gov/files/2025-07/DSA_Report&Appendix%2807.25.25%29.pdf)); the committee's Democrats replied that there is "no evidence that the European Commission classifies political debate" as hate speech ([House Democrats, 2025](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/debunking-republicans-misleading-report-on-the-eu-s-digital-services-act-dsa.pdf)), and a Commission spokesman said its first DSA fine, on X, concerned transparency, not freedom of speech, while Elon Musk answered with an expletive and vowed a court battle ([Creatives Unite, 2025](https://creativesunite.eu/article/eu-fines-x-million-musk-and-top-us-officials-respond-with-fury)).

Brazil's Marco Civil da Internet made platforms liable for users' content only after a specific court order, "Com o intuito de assegurar a liberdade de expressão e impedir a censura", to protect expression and prevent censorship ([Marco Civil, art. 19](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2014/lei-12965-23-abril-2014-778630-normaatualizada-pl.html); [Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l12965.htm)). On 26 June 2025 the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF) held by 8 votes to 3 that this rule is partially unconstitutional ([STF, 2025](https://sipla.ip.mpg.de/fileadmin/Files/Files/STF_Responsabilidade_de_plataformas_digitais_por_conteudo_de_terceiros.pdf)); under the thesis confirmed in June 2026, platforms can be liable after notice for crimes and unlawful acts, while offences against honour still need a court order ([STF, 2026](https://noticias-stf-wp-prd.s3.sa-east-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/wpallimport/uploads/2026/06/17180658/MCI_tese.pdf)). Justice Alexandre de Moraes, in the majority, said "a liberdade de expressão não é liberdade de agressão", freedom of expression is not freedom of aggression ([Moraes, 2025](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/06/transcricao-voto-AlexandreMoraes-STF-responsabilizacaodasredes-12-jun-2025.pdf)), and, as Agência Estado reported, "Aqui não se discute nenhuma limitação ao exercício da liberdade de expressão, aqui se discute a responsabilização pelo abuso criminoso da expressão", that the case was about liability for the criminal abuse of expression ([O Povo, from Agência Estado, 13 June 2025](https://www.opovo.com.br/noticias/politica/2025/06/13/moraes-em-seu-voto-diz-que-big-techs-tem-ideologia-e-autorregulacao-faliu.html)); Justice Edson Fachin, dissenting, held a court order the only constitutionally adequate way to reconcile free expression with liability ([Fachin, 2025](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/06/proposta-tese-EdsonFachin-MarcoCivilInternet-25-jun-2025-1.pdf)), and said the rule "não é um deixa fazer desenfreado", is not an unbridled laissez-faire ([Teletime, 25 June 2025](https://teletime.com.br/25/06/2025/fachin-vota-pela-legalidade-do-art-19-do-marco-civil-da-internet/)).

Beneath moderation lies the capability to inspect traffic, which the Marco Civil restrains by forbidding connection providers to monitor or analyse packet content ([Marco Civil, art. 9](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2014/lei-12965-23-abril-2014-778630-normaatualizada-pl.html); [Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l12965.htm)). When the PRISM programme was disclosed in 2013, The Guardian reported that <Claim status="reported">"The National Security Agency has obtained direct access to the systems of Google, Facebook, Apple and other US internet giants"</Claim>, and The Washington Post that the agency and the FBI were "tapping directly into the central servers" of nine US internet companies ([Guardian, 2013](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2013/jun/06/us-tech-giants-nsa-data); [Washington Post, 2013](https://www.washingtonpost.com/investigations/us-intelligence-mining-data-from-nine-us-internet-companies-in-broad-secret-program/2013/06/06/3a0c0da8-cebf-11e2-8845-d970ccb04497_story.html)). Google said the US government had no direct access to its data centres ([Google, 2013](https://blog.google/technology/safety-security/what/)); a US oversight board later found PRISM, in which providers are compelled to hand over communications matching selectors such as e-mail addresses, authorized by statute ([oversight board report, 2014](https://documents.pclob.gov/prod/Documents/OversightReport/823399ae-92ea-447a-ab60-0da28b555437/702-Report-2.pdf)). Platforms profile users under their own terms: Google's privacy policy says it may, depending on settings, personalize ads by interests and activity ([Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy?hl=en)). Brazil's Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (LGPD) lets people request review of automated decisions that define their profile ([LGPD, art. 20](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2018/lei-13709-14-agosto-2018-787077-normaatualizada-pl.html); [Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)). **[Content moderation at scale](/learn/content-moderation-at-scale)**, **[The law: Section 230, the DSA, the Marco Civil and the rest](/learn/platform-law-section-230-the-dsa-and-the-marco-civil)** and **[Surveillance capability and restraint](/learn/surveillance-capability-and-restraint)** go further.

## Defences, and what is known about them

Fact-checks reduce false belief: experiments in Argentina, Nigeria, South Africa and the United Kingdom measured a fall of at least 0.59 points on a 5-point scale ([Porter and Wood, 2021](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1073/pnas.2104235118%22&format=json&resultType=core)), though Nyhan notes such effects often decay ([Nyhan, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912440117)). Ending its US fact-checking programme in 2025, Meta said <Claim status="alleged" by="by Meta">"A program intended to inform too often became a tool to censor"</Claim> ([Meta, 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/meta-more-speech-fewer-mistakes/)); the International Fact-Checking Network (IFCN) answered "This is false, and we want to set the record straight", noting that Meta never let fact-checkers remove content ([IFCN, 2025](https://www.poynter.org/ifcn/2025/an-open-letter-to-mark-zuckerberg-from-the-worlds-fact-checkers-nine-years-later/)).

Professional fact-checkers read laterally, leaving a site to see what others say about it ([Wineburg and McGrew, 2017](https://www.hendrix.edu/uploadedFiles/Academics/Faculty_Resources/Teaching_and_Learning/EvaluatingDigitalInformation.pdf)), and short prebunking videos raised recognition of manipulation techniques by about 5% in a YouTube field study funded by Google Jigsaw ([Roozenbeek et al., 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9401631/fullTextXML)); <Claim status="disputed">whether such tools improve the ability to tell true from false news</Claim> is argued between [Modirrousta-Galian and Higham (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36996156/) and [Simchon et al. (2025)](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/a-signal-detection-theory-meta-analysis-of-psychological-inoculat/). The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) records where a file came from, but notes that "provenance information alone cannot tell you whether the digital content is true" ([C2PA explainer 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html)). On generative artificial intelligence (AI), OpenAI's first threat report, in May 2024, rated none of five covert operations above category 2 of 6 for reach ([OpenAI, 2024](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/kftzwdyauwt9/5IMxzTmUclSOAcWUXbkVrK/3cfab518e6b10789ab8843bcca18b633/Threat_Intel_Report.pdf)). **[Verification and fact-checking](/learn/verification-and-fact-checking)**, **[Prebunking and inoculation](/learn/prebunking-and-inoculation)** and **[Generative AI and influence operations](/learn/generative-ai-and-influence-operations)** go further.

## What to remember

- PSYOP and MISO name military activity aimed at foreign (US) or approved (NATO) audiences; this series uses PSYOP for any deliberate attempt to change belief through perception.
- Repetition makes claims feel truer; corrections reduce but do not erase misinformation's influence; the backfire effect has not held up as a general rule.
- Scams pay through volume: a nearly free message needs only a tiny success rate; the IC3 logged 1,008,597 complaints and $20.877 billion in losses for 2025.
- Influence operations are documented from many origins, Russian, Chinese, American, Israeli and Brazilian among them; measured reach is often small.
- False news spread farther on Twitter, but whether ranking algorithms polarize is disputed and depends on platform, period and method.
- Who decides what is harmful differs by jurisdiction: Section 230 shields platforms, the DSA imposes risk duties, and Brazil's STF narrowed article 19 of the Marco Civil in 2025.
- Defences help modestly: fact-checks lower belief, prebunking's benefit is disputed, and provenance shows origin, not truth.
