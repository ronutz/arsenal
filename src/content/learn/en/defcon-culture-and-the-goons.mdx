## The party that did not end

In 1993 **Jeff Moss** - Dark Tangent - planned a send-off in Las Vegas for a friend leaving the country, and for the bulletin-board network they both ran. The friend left early; the party happened anyway, with about a hundred people. That accident became **DEF CON**, and it now draws tens of thousands each summer.

The design decisions made in those early years are why it never turned into an ordinary trade show. **Cash at the door, no registration** - no attendee database to subpoena, leak or sell. A deliberately hostile network everyone is warned never to trust. And a culture of contests rather than keynotes, so the centre of gravity stays with people doing things rather than people announcing things.

Its commercial sibling **Black Hat**, founded by Moss in 1997, exists so the same research can be presented to people whose employers need an invoice. The pairing is the industry's honest compromise: one event you can expense, one you cannot.

## The Goons

Nothing about a gathering this size works by itself. **The Goons** are DEF CON's volunteer staff, identifiable by their **red shirts**, and they are the load-bearing structure of the entire event.

The role is worth describing precisely, because it is routinely mistaken for a job. Goons are **not paid**. They buy their own flights, pay for their own rooms, and in most years buy their own badge - then work punishing shifts across four days. Positions are earned rather than applied for: goons are drawn from people who have already given years to the community, invited into departments that run everything from Info Booth and A/V to Speaker Operations, Contests and Villages, Network Operations and Safety.

Their most visible work is crowd management, and it is theatre with a purpose. A line of five thousand people moving into a room at a fire-code-limited venue is a genuine safety problem, and it gets solved by someone with a voice and the authority to use it, willing to be shouted at, willing to be the villain for ten minutes so that nobody gets hurt. The shouted line calls are famous, occasionally funny, and entirely functional.

Their least visible work matters more. Goons carry the conference's unwritten rules to people encountering them for the first time: what you do not photograph, what you do not plug in, how the code of conduct is actually enforced, where to go if something happens to you. They de-escalate. They find lost people. They handle medical situations, harassment reports, and the moments where a very large gathering of very tired humans goes wrong. A convention that prides itself on distrusting authority is, in practice, held together by a group of volunteers everyone chooses to trust - which is only possible because the trust was earned in public over years.

That is why the red shirt is treated as an honour rather than a uniform, and why the phrase *once a Goon, always a Goon* means something specific to the people who have worn one.

## Contests, villages, badges

The programme reflects the same values. **Capture the Flag** is the field's most demanding team sport and the origin of a whole training discipline. The **Wall of Sheep** projects credentials it sniffs off the conference network in the clear - education by public embarrassment, and a quiet historical record of encryption's spread, since it used to fill in minutes and now struggles. **Spot the Fed** turned the presence of federal agents into a game rather than a panic, which is a diplomatic achievement disguised as a joke. The **badges** are hardware puzzles with their own competitive subculture. The **villages** - lockpicking, cars, aviation, voting machines, biohacking - are where whole research areas were born in public, the [Jeep hack](/glossary/the-jeep-hack) and voting-machine work among them.

## What the conference is for

DEF CON is where the field's arguments happen in the same room. [The L0pht presented here before the Senate called them](/learn/l0pht-cdc-and-the-road-to-the-industry). The Cult of the Dead Cow released Back Orifice from this stage. [Barnaby Jack made two ATMs spray cash](/glossary/barnaby-jack) at its commercial sibling and changed an industry with a single demonstration. Vendors have been embarrassed here, careers have started here, and researchers have been arrested here - Marcus Hutchins on his way home in 2017 among them, which is why the community's advice about legal counsel is not paranoia.

Strip away the neon and the point is simple: a subculture that was told for two decades it was a criminal problem built its own institution, ran it on volunteer labour and cash, and made it good enough that governments now send people to listen. The red shirts are how that keeps working.
