A message that seems to have reached the wrong number, a friendship or romance that grows over weeks, then an investment platform showing steady gains until the money cannot be withdrawn: this is the fraud known as pig butchering, much of it worked from guarded compounds in Southeast Asia by people trafficked and forced to scam. After [Scams at scale](/learn/scams-at-scale-why-a-tiny-success-rate-pays), this part of the series opened by [the map from the small lie to the campaign](/learn/psychological-operations-from-the-small-lie-to-the-campaign) follows one victim over months and the industry behind it. After reading, you can describe the scheme, explain the dispute over its name, read the estimates within their limits, tell sanctions and indictments from court findings, and name the defences.

## How the scheme works

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) of the United States (US) Treasury set out four stages in its alert FIN-2023-Alert005 of 8 September 2023 ([FinCEN, 2023](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/FinCEN_Alert_Pig_Butchering_FINAL_508c.pdf)):

1. **Contact.** A text or social media message presented as a wrong number or an attempt to reconnect with an old friend, followed by conversation until trust exists.
2. **The pitch.** A virtual currency opportunity on a legitimate-looking site or app the scammer controls.
3. **The returns.** Fabricated returns, and sometimes a small withdrawal to build confidence; some victims borrow against their homes to invest more.
4. **The end.** Invented losses to cover, then taxes or fees to withdraw; when the victim stops paying, the scammer cuts contact and keeps everything.

The Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) calls these sophisticated long-term scams built on psychological manipulation ([IC3, 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)), and the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) says they often last months ([Treasury, 5 May 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0129)).

## The name and the case against it

FinCEN says the scams "resemble the practice of fattening a hog before slaughter": scammers call victims pigs, fatten them with invented personas and speak of butchering them once their assets are taken; the Chinese original, Sha Zhu Pan, translates loosely the same way ([FinCEN, 2023](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/FinCEN_Alert_Pig_Butchering_FINAL_508c.pdf)).

On 17 December 2024 the International Criminal Police Organization (INTERPOL) proposed romance baiting instead, because "INTERPOL argues that the term 'pig butchering' dehumanizes and shames victims of such frauds, deterring people from coming forward to seek help and provide information to the authorities" ([INTERPOL, 17 December 2024](https://www.interpol.int/en/News-and-Events/News/2024/INTERPOL-urges-end-to-Pig-Butchering-term-cites-harm-to-online-victims)). US agencies kept the old name; Treasury in October 2025 wrote of scams "commonly known as 'pig butchering' scams" ([Treasury, 14 October 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0278)). No US reply to INTERPOL appears in the sources.

## The compounds

The human rights office of the United Nations (UN), the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), reported in August 2023 that "Credible sources indicate that at least 120,000 people across Myanmar may be held in situations where they are forced to carry out online scams", and at least 100,000 in Cambodia, numbers it called hard to estimate given the operations' secrecy ([OHCHR, 2023](https://data.opendevelopmentcambodia.net/km/dataset/2fa4481b-09ab-407a-bfe0-7bff943781c6/resource/58af8cca-675e-4f22-9177-059364bf932b/download/online-scam-operations-2582023.pdf)). Centres were allegedly in Shwe Kokko and elsewhere in Myawaddy, on the Thai border, with others in Laos's Golden Triangle Special Economic Zone and the Philippines. The High Commissioner, Volker Türk, said of the workers: "They are victims. They are not criminals" ([UN News, 29 August 2023](https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/08/1140187)).

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) described recruitment through advertisements for well-paid office jobs, then up to 15 hours a day of scamming under force and artificial debts of up to US$25,000 ([UNODC, 2023](https://www.unodc.org/roseap/uploads/documents/Publications/2023/TiP_for_FC_Summary_Policy_Brief.pdf)).

OHCHR's report A wicked problem, of 20 February 2026, cites "Recent credible estimates of the scam workforce" of at least 300,000 people from 66 countries and says that "All victims described receiving and/or witnessing severe mistreatment amounting to torture within scam compounds" ([OHCHR, 2026](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/trafficking/report-a-wicked-problem.pdf)). It reports experts' warnings that <Claim status="alleged" by="by OHCHR">"criminal syndicates are benefiting from collusion with government officials, politicians, local law enforcement, and influential business figures"</Claim>. States' factual comments, it says, "have been made available online as relevant"; none was found published beside it.

### Brazilians inside

Brazil's foreign ministry calls Southeast Asia "o principal foco de tráfico de cidadãos brasileiros para exploração laboral", the main focus of trafficking of Brazilians for labour exploitation ([Itamaraty, consular alert](https://www.gov.br/mre/pt-br/assuntos/portal-consular/alertas%20e%20noticias/alertas/trafico-de-pessoas-para-o-sudeste-asiatico)). The Ministry of Justice and Public Security (MJSP) counts 49 Brazilian trafficking victims abroad notified by the foreign ministry in 2024, against two in 2023, and says most cases of international trafficking for slave-like labour, 35 occurrences, "envolveu trabalho em plataformas digitais de aposta", online betting platforms, in countries such as the Philippines, Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos ([MJSP, Relatório Nacional 2024](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/sua-protecao/trafico-de-pessoas/Dados%20e%20estatisticas/relatorio-nacional-de-dados-2024/relatorio-nacional-de-dados-sobre-trafico-de-pessoas-dados-2024-v3-1.pdf)). UNODC's statement that <Claim status="reported" by="by UNODC">such cases are "the vast majority of all trafficking in persons cases reported by Brazilian authorities involving the country's nationals in recent years"</Claim> cites a Royal Thai Police meeting instead ([UNODC, 2025](https://www.unodc.org/roseap/uploads/documents/Publications/2025/Inflection_Point_2025.pdf)). In May 2026 Brazil's Superior Tribunal de Justiça set a final sentence of 23 years and 10 months for a Brazilian who took 12 compatriots to scam at KK Park, according to the Ministério Público Federal (MPF), the federal prosecution service ([MPF, 26 May 2026](https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/procuradoria-geral-da-republica-pgr/noticias/brasileiro-e-condenado-a-23-anos-de-prisao-por-trafico-e-exploracao-de-pessoas-no-sudeste-asiatico)), and in July 2026 the Polícia Federal searched premises in a case of recruitment for scam centres on the Cambodia-Vietnam border ([Polícia Federal, 14 July 2026](https://www.gov.br/pf/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026/07/pf-combate-trafico-internacional-de-brasileiros-para-centros-de-golpes-no-sudeste-asiatico)). On fraud aimed at Brazilians at home, see [Brazilian cybercrime](/learn/brazilian-cybercrime-from-boleto-to-pix).

## Losses, and what the estimates can say

UNODC estimated losses of US$18 billion to US$37 billion in 2023 from scams targeting East and Southeast Asia ([UNODC, 7 October 2024](https://www.unodc.org/roseap/en/2024/10/cyberfraud-industry-expands-southeast-asia/story.html)). The range divides the losses reported in 12 countries and territories by each one's estimated reporting rate ([UNODC, 2024](https://www.unodc.org/roseap/uploads/documents/Publications/2024/TOC_Convergence_Report_2024.pdf)). A second method, from the workforce and proceeds per worker, gives US$27.4 billion to US$36.5 billion a year, with "uncertainties regarding both the estimated number of trafficked victims and the average amount of money generated by each individual". OHCHR calls the industry's size hard to estimate and cites claims of some US$64 billion a year in global profits ([OHCHR, 2026](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/trafficking/report-a-wicked-problem.pdf)).

US figures rest on different bases and years and cannot be added. IC3 recorded 61,559 complaints of cryptocurrency investment fraud in 2025 with $7.228 billion in losses and says <Claim status="attributed" by="by the FBI">such scams are "largely perpetrated by organized criminal enterprises based in Southeast Asia using victims of human trafficking as forced labor"</Claim> ([IC3, 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). Treasury cited an unnamed government estimate that Americans lost at least $10 billion to Southeast Asia-based scam operations in 2024 ([Treasury, 14 October 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0278)).

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) cites a 2021 study by K. B. Anderson "showing that only 4.8 percent of victims of mass-market consumer fraud complained to a Better Business Bureau or a government entity" ([FTC, 25 March 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf)), a finding about consumer fraud in general. The Department of Justice (DOJ) says the IC3 figures understate real losses because most victims never go to the police ([DOJ, 23 April 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/scam-center-strike-force-takes-major-actions-against-southeast-asian-scam-centers-targeting)).

## How the money moves

FinCEN says victims buy virtual currency, sometimes at kiosks, or wire money abroad, and what they deposit on the fake platform goes to addresses and accounts the scammers control ([FinCEN, 2023](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/FinCEN_Alert_Pig_Butchering_FINAL_508c.pdf)). UNODC says victim funds pass from wallet to wallet among scammers and launderers, then are often swapped for stablecoins at a few large exchanges, Tether on the Tron blockchain being the syndicates' preferred choice according to regional authorities; it also describes organized money mule networks and job scams that make people "unwitting money mules" ([UNODC, 2024](https://www.unodc.org/roseap/uploads/documents/Publications/2024/TOC_Convergence_Report_2024.pdf)).

In October 2025 FinCEN cut the Cambodia-based Huione Group off from the US financial system, finding that <Claim status="attributed" by="by FinCEN">it laundered at least $4 billion of illicit proceeds between August 2021 and January 2025</Claim> ([Treasury, 14 October 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0278)). Counsel for Huione Pay argued in June 2025 that the company was independent and remedying compliance issues; the final rule calls the claim "unpersuasive" ([FinCEN, 16 October 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-10-16/pdf/2025-19571.pdf)). A Huione spokesperson told the business site Under30CEO in December 2025: "we disagree with the FinCEN ruling and reject any suggestion that Huione is involved in illegal or illicit activities" ([Under30CEO, 11 December 2025](https://www.under30ceo.com/huione-group-increases-security-protocols-amid-treasury-notice/)).

## Sanctions, prosecutions and the replies

An OFAC designation is an administrative decision that blocks property under US jurisdiction; of an indictment, the DOJ itself writes: "An indictment is merely an allegation". Neither is a court's finding of guilt.

- **Ly Yong Phat, Cambodia.** OFAC designated the businessman and his O-Smach Resort on 12 September 2024 for serious human rights abuse against trafficked workers forced to scam ([Treasury, 12 September 2024](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2576)). His group did not immediately answer Reuters; Cambodia's foreign ministry called the sanctions politically motivated and contrary to "the principles of sovereign equality and non-interference" ([The Star (Reuters), 13 September 2024](https://thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2024/09/13/cambodia-says-us-sanctions-against-tycoon-unjust-politically-motivated)).
- **Saw Chit Thu, Myanmar.** OFAC designated his Karen National Army militia on 5 May 2025, saying <Claim status="attributed" by="by OFAC">it leases land to scam syndicates and guards compounds</Claim> ([Treasury, 5 May 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0129)). Months earlier he had called such accusations baseless ([Eleven Media, 18 February 2025](https://elevenmyanmar.com/news/kayins-bgf-denies-involvement-in-illegal-online-scam-operations-in-shwe-kokko)) and told Nation Thailand: "I declare, in all seriousness, that I have no involvement with the call-centre gangs" ([Nation, 3 March 2025](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/policy/40046962)). No response to the designation itself was found in the press.
- **Chen Zhi and Prince Group, Cambodia.** On 14 October 2025 OFAC sanctioned 146 targets of what Treasury calls the Prince Group criminal organization ([Treasury, 14 October 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0278)). The DOJ unsealed an indictment for wire fraud and money laundering conspiracy alleging that <Claim status="alleged" by="by the DOJ">Chen Zhi directed forced-labour scam compounds across Cambodia and had the proceeds laundered through cryptocurrency</Claim>, and sought forfeiture of about 127,271 bitcoin ([DOJ, 14 October 2025](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chairman-prince-group-indicted-operating-cambodian-forced-labor-scam-compounds-engaged)). The company replied: "The Prince Group categorically rejects the notion that it or its Chairman, Chen Zhi, has engaged in any unlawful activity" ([Malay Mail, 12 November 2025](https://malaymail.com/news/money/2025/11/12/cambodias-prince-group-denies-scam-links-after-us15b-asset-seizures-of-founder-chen-zhi/198117)); in March 2026 a spokesperson told the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ): "This indictment is simply air cover for a giant cash grab" ([ICIJ, 18 March 2026](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coin-laundry/questions-swirl-around-us-plans-for-record-15b-prince-group-crypto-seizure/)). China's National Computer Virus Emergency Response Center (CVERC) alleged that <Claim status="alleged" by="by China's CVERC">the coins were stolen from Chen Zhi in a 2020 attack on the LuBian mining pool, probably by a nation-state</Claim> ([The Register, 12 November 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/12/cverc_prince_grou_scam_bitcoin/)); SC Media reported that the DOJ denied it ([SC Media, 13 November 2025](https://www.scworld.com/brief/us-dismisses-chinese-accusation-of-extensive-lubian-mining-pool-hack)), while ICIJ says the DOJ declined to say how it obtained the coins. In January 2026 Cambodia revoked Chen Zhi's citizenship and sent him to China, where police placed him under compulsory criminal measures, according to the Cable News Network ([Cable News Network, 7 January 2026](https://kvia.com/news/us-world/cnn-world/2026/01/07/alleged-cybercrime-kingpin-arrested-and-extradited-to-china-cambodia-says/)). The public copy of the criminal docket records no filing after the October 2025 unsealing ([CourtListener, criminal docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71639335/united-states-v-zhi/)); the forfeiture case goes on, with Chen Zhi, Prince Holding Group and LuBian among the claimants and briefing on a government motion to strike other claims set to end on 15 January 2027 ([CourtListener, forfeiture docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71639393/united-states-v-approximately-127271-bitcoin-btc-previously-stored-at/)).
- **Scam Center Strike Force.** Set up in November 2025, it announced in April 2026 charges against two compound managers and the restraint of more than $700 million in cryptocurrency it alleges is tied to laundering scam proceeds ([DOJ, 23 April 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/scam-center-strike-force-takes-major-actions-against-southeast-asian-scam-centers-targeting)). No statement from the two accused was found in the press.

## What the governments say

**Cambodia.** Amnesty International identified "at least 53 scamming compounds" in June 2025; the government's anti-trafficking committee, it reports, "did not substantively respond" but cited crackdowns on 28 locations ([Amnesty, 26 June 2025](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/ASA2394472025ENGLISH.pdf)). In June 2026 Amnesty found <Claim status="denied" by="by Cambodia's government">state interventions at only 24 of 86 compounds, "often reactive, ineffective or undermined by apparent collusion between compound managers and police"</Claim> ([Amnesty, 8 June 2026](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ASA2310932026ENGLISH.pdf)). Chhay Sinarith, of the Commission for Combating Online Scams, replied that "The core operating systems, command technologies and especially the financial chains, money laundering flows and real masterminds are outside Cambodia's jurisdiction" ([The Star, 10 June 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/06/10/the-masterminds-are-abroad-cambodia-responds-to-damning-amnesty-scam-report)). Prime Minister Hun Manet, rejecting allegations of government connivance, told Agence France-Presse (AFP) that "most of the proceeds do not go into the government of Cambodia" ([The Star (AFP), 26 February 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/02/26/scam-centres-039destroying039-cambodia039s-economy-pm-tells-afp)). In October 2026 Amnesty found that <Claim status="alleged" by="by Amnesty">serving soldiers and police took part in compound security</Claim>; the Interior Ministry replied that the law allows prosecuting complicit officials and that from July 2025 to August 2026 authorities acted against 832 scam sites and sent 3,477 suspects to court ([Amnesty, October 2026](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/official-business-en.pdf)).

**Myanmar.** The military government raided the KK Park compound in October 2025 and said 413 of its buildings had been demolished by 13 December; state media, as the Associated Press (AP) summarized them, called the scams "the work of foreign criminal networks that have taken root in lawless borderlands controlled by insurgents" ([The Star (AP), 18 December 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/12/18/myanmar-declares-a-039zero-tolerance039-policy-for-cyberscams-but-the-fraud-goes-on)). The Centre for Information Resilience (CIR) found that <Claim status="disputed" by="by the CIR">the first wave of demolition, which the government says is complete</Claim>, flattened 31 structures and partly damaged at least 78 (same report), and experts quoted by AFP said "the junta raids are likely limited, choreographed and intentionally publicised" ([The Star (AFP), 9 November 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/11/09/myanmar-junta-says-demolishing-150-scam-hub-buildings)).

**Laos and the Philippines.** Laos's state news agency reported the arrest in August 2024 of 771 suspects from 15 Asian and African countries in the Golden Triangle zone ([Lao News Agency, 27 August 2024](https://kpl.gov.la/EN/detail.aspx?id=85386)). The Philippines' Executive Order No. 74 of 5 November 2024 ordered offshore gaming operators to close by 31 December 2024, citing a Department of Finance study on the risks of crime and exploitation ([Executive Order No. 74, 2024](https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo2024/eo_74_2024.html)). No answer from Myanmar, Laos or the Philippines to the collusion allegations was found.

## The defences FinCEN and the police recommend

FinCEN's red flags, written for banks, trace what victims go through: a new, unsolicited contact recommending a high-return virtual currency investment; savings or home loans spent on virtual currency; a small deposit coming back before much larger transfers out; and platforms on misspelled or newly registered domains imitating another business ([FinCEN, 2023](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/FinCEN_Alert_Pig_Butchering_FINAL_508c.pdf); see [deceptive URLs](/learn/deceptive-urls)).

IC3 advises speed after a fraudulent transfer: ask the bank at once to recall the funds and file a complaint whatever the amount, since IC3 may help freeze money ([IC3, 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). It warns of recovery scams that target victims again: 10,516 complaints and $1.4 billion in 2025, a sum that may include earlier losses.

## What to remember

- FinCEN's four stages: contact disguised as a wrong number or an old friend, trust built over time, fabricated returns on a platform the scammer controls, then fees and silence.
- INTERPOL asked in December 2024 for romance baiting to replace the name, which it says shames victims and deters reporting.
- OHCHR cited credible estimates of at least 120,000 people held in Myanmar and 100,000 in Cambodia in 2023, and at least 300,000 from 66 countries in 2026.
- Brazil's Ministry of Justice recorded 49 Brazilian trafficking victims notified from abroad in 2024; a recruiter for KK Park received a final sentence in 2026.
- Sanctions are administrative and indictments are allegations: Prince Group denies all unlawful activity, Chen Zhi is in China, and the US forfeiture case is still open.
- Defences: distrust an unsolicited contact who steers towards crypto investing, call the bank and IC3 at once after a fraudulent transfer, and distrust offers to recover losses.
