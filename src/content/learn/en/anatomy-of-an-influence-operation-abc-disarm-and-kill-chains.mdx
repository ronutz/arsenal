Analysts describe influence operations in a vocabulary borrowed from network defence: tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs), phases and kill chains. After the [series opener](/learn/psychological-operations-from-the-small-lie-to-the-campaign) and [the article on the words](/learn/psyop-propaganda-and-disinformation-the-words), this part sets out ABC (actors, behaviour, content), Disinformation Analysis and Risk Management (DISARM), two kill chains and the European External Action Service (EEAS) approach to foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI), then walks a Russian and a Chinese operation through them. After reading, you can place a documented activity in a phase and say what a takedown or an attribution shows.

## Borrowed from cybersecurity

The EEAS defines TTPs as "patterns of behaviour used by threat actors to manipulate the information environment with the intention to deceive" ([EEAS, 2023, glossary](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)). Ben Nimmo and Eric Hutchins of Meta trace the vocabulary to cyber-espionage models such as the Intrusion Kill Chain, which Hutchins co-authored at Lockheed Martin, and MITRE's Adversarial Tactics, Techniques and Common Knowledge (ATT&CK) framework ([Nimmo and Hutchins, 2023](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/202303-Nimmo_Hutchins_Online_Ops.pdf); [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/adversarial_tactics_techniques_and_common_knowledge)), on which DISARM says its style is based ([DISARM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks/main/README.md)). On intrusions, see [adversary behaviour in a security operations centre](/learn/fortinet-soc-architecture-and-adversary-behaviour) and [detection engineering](/learn/siem-and-detection-engineering).

## ABC, and the letters added to it

In September 2019 Camille François, of Graphika and Harvard's Berkman Klein Center, set out three vectors of viral deception ([François, 2019](https://www.ivir.nl/publicaties/download/ABC_Framework_2019_Sept_2019.pdf)): "Manipulative actors, by definition, engage knowingly and with clear intent in viral deception campaigns"; deceptive behaviour, from bot armies to troll farms, lets a few actors look like many; and content is the most visible vector, she wrote, likely why regulators focus on it.

Alexandre Alaphilippe of EU DisinfoLab argued in 2020 that "the ABC framework should be expanded to include a 'D,' for information Distribution", citing recommendation systems, advertising and encrypted messaging, key, he said, to disinformation around Brazil's presidential elections ([Alaphilippe, 2020](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/adding-a-d-to-the-abc-disinformation-framework/)). James Pamment's ABCDE (actor, behaviour, content, degree, effect), proposed in 2020 for European Union (EU) institutions, adds degree, the distribution of content and the audiences it reaches, and effect, how much of a threat a case poses ([Pamment, 2020](https://carnegieendowment.org/files/Pamment_-_Crafting_Disinformation_1.pdf)). The EEAS adopted ABCDE as a checklist and uses DISARM to make its behaviour component measurable ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)).

For effect, Ben Nimmo proposed the Breakout Scale in 2020: six categories, from one, confined to one community on one platform, to six, which "triggers a policy response or some other form of concrete action" or includes a call for violence. A higher category, he warns, "does not necessarily equate to actual impact" ([Nimmo, 2020](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Nimmo_influence_operations_PDF.pdf)).

## DISARM: four phases and a catalogue of tactics

"DISARM is a framework designed for describing and understanding disinformation incidents", its README says, with a Red framework of the creators' TTPs and a Blue framework of countermeasures; it took its present form in 2022 with teams from the MITRE Corporation and others ([DISARM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks/main/README.md)). Its phases are Plan, Prepare, Execute and Assess, the last evaluating effectiveness for future plans ([DISARM phases](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks/main/generated_pages/phases_index.md)). Its 16 tactics are numbered TA01 to TA18 (TA03 and TA04 are absent), among them Target Audience Analysis (TA13), Establish Assets (TA15), Establish Legitimacy (TA16) and Maximise Exposure (TA17) ([DISARM tactics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks/main/generated_pages/tactics_index.md)). The Blue framework includes censorship as observed under authoritarian governments, and its maintainers write "we advise caution to anyone using it".

The EEAS chose DISARM as the only framework meeting all its criteria, open source among them. Coding 100 incidents detected from October to December 2022, it recorded 308 occurrences of TTPs, 72 of them unique techniques: 67% in preparation, 20% in execution and 13% in planning; it says the sample is not comprehensive ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)).

## Kill chains: Schneier, Meta and the EEAS

Bruce Schneier proposed an information operations kill chain in Lawfare in April 2019, updating seven steps drawn from a 1980s campaign, Operation INFEKTION, into eight: finding social divisions, seeding alternative narratives, wrapping them in kernels of truth, building audiences, concealing the source, cultivating people who amplify, denying involvement and playing the long game ([Schneier, 2019](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/toward-information-operations-kill-chain)). Against concealment, he wrote, "Here the answer is attribution, attribution, attribution", but "Public attribution of information attacks must be accompanied by convincing evidence". Unlike the cyber model, he added, "The information operations kill chain is fuzzier. Steps overlap. They can be conducted out of order".

Nimmo and Hutchins's Online Operations Kill Chain of March 2023, built on cases from espionage to scams, has ten links: acquiring assets, disguising assets, gathering information, coordinating and planning, testing platform defences, evading detection, indiscriminate engagement, targeted engagement, compromising assets and enabling longevity ([Nimmo and Hutchins, 2023](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/202303-Nimmo_Hutchins_Online_Ops.pdf)). Breaking one tactic, they write, can disrupt a whole operation, and the model records only observable behaviour, not strategic goals.

The EEAS reads the DISARM phases as a kill chain, so named because denying one step kills the attack, and says reused combinations of techniques form a "behavioural fingerprint" that aids attribution ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)). In its view, official rebuttals suit early stages, fact-checking and debunking an attack that has matured along the chain.

## Doppelganger, phase by phase

EU DisinfoLab named Doppelganger on 27 September 2022: clones of at least 17 media outlets carrying fake articles, videos and polls. "Our investigation does not lead to a formal attribution to a specific actor", it wrote, citing domains bought through a Russian registrar, and "we cannot entirely exclude the possibility of a false flag operation" ([EU DisinfoLab, 2022](https://www.disinfo.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Doppelganger-1.pdf)). Meta removed the network that day as Russian in origin ([Meta, 2022](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CIB-Report_-China-Russia_Sept-2022-1-1.pdf)) and in December <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">attributed it to Structura National Technology and Social Design Agency (SDA), two Russian companies</Claim> ([Meta, 2022b](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/12/metas-2022-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-enforcements/)). In June 2023 France's foreign minister said its Service de vigilance et protection contre les ingérences numériques étrangères (VIGINUM) ([SGDSN](https://www.sgdsn.gouv.fr/notre-organisation)) had found <Claim status="attributed" by="by France">Russian or Russian-speaking individuals and several Russian companies behind the campaign, with Russian state or state-affiliated entities spreading some content</Claim> ([Colonna, 2023](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/declaration-de-catherine-colonna-ingerences-numeriques-etrangeres-detection-par-la-france-d-une)). In September 2024 a Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) affidavit for seizing 32 domains, by its own account showing only probable cause, stated that <Claim status="alleged" by="by the FBI">SDA, Structura and the organization Dialog acted under the direction of the Russian Presidential Administration, in particular Sergei Kiriyenko</Claim> ([FBI affidavit, 2024](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-09/doppelganger_affidavit_9.4.24.pdf)). Russia's embassy in Paris met France's statement with bewilderment and disappointment, saying French services had found "ничего нового, кроме безобидной карикатуры", nothing new beyond a harmless cartoon, Gazeta.Ru reported ([Gazeta.Ru, 2023](https://www.gazeta.ru/politics/news/2023/06/14/20661044.shtml)). Answering the United States (US) announcements, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said "Well of course it’s nonsense. We’re not interfering" ([The Washington Post, 2024](https://adn.com/nation-world/2024/09/05/russia-dismisses-charges-of-election-meddling-putin-claims-he-backs-harris)). The firms' sanctioned leaders, Ilya Gambashidze and Nikolai Tupikin, declined to comment to Voice of America ([Voice of America, 2024](https://www.voanews.com/a/investigation-who-is-ilya-gambashidze-the-man-the-us-government-accuses-of-running-a-kremlin-disinformation-campaign-/7604052.html)); no statement by SDA or Structura was found.

**Through ABCDE.** Actor: the chain above. Behaviour: 1,633 Facebook accounts, 703 Pages, 29 Instagram accounts and around $105,000 of advertising, combining sophistication and brute force, Meta wrote ([Meta, 2022](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CIB-Report_-China-Russia_Sept-2022-1-1.pdf)). Content: "Ukraine is a failed state" and "Winter is coming", in EU DisinfoLab's summary, and a fake French foreign ministry communiqué announcing a tax to fund support for Ukraine ([VIGINUM, 2023](https://www.sgdsn.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/20230719_NP_VIGINUM_RAPPORT-CAMPAGNE-RRN_EN.pdf)). Degree: from February 2023, VIGINUM found more than 160 Facebook pages running more than 600 sponsored posts whose links, after at least two redirects, showed content by the reader's location. Effect: most engagement came from fake likes the operators bought, EU DisinfoLab found, and OpenAI placed the activity tied to its models in category 2 of the Breakout Scale ([OpenAI, 2024](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/kftzwdyauwt9/5IMxzTmUclSOAcWUXbkVrK/3cfab518e6b10789ab8843bcca18b633/Threat_Intel_Report.pdf)).

**Plan.** The affidavit cites an SDA document, "The Good Old U.S.A. Project", aimed among others at voters in six swing states, and an SDA list of more than 2,800 people in 81 countries identified as influencers (Target Audience Analysis; gathering information); an SDA manual split campaigns into monitoring, analytics, content production and content delivery ([FBI affidavit, 2024](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-09/doppelganger_affidavit_9.4.24.pdf)).

**Prepare.** Lookalike domains, copied designs, the Reliable Recent News (RRN) brand and same-name accounts backing one another fit Establish Assets and Establish Legitimacy, or acquiring and disguising assets ([Meta, 2022](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CIB-Report_-China-Russia_Sept-2022-1-1.pdf)). Meta says its automated systems removed most accounts, Pages and ads before its investigation began; the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated SDA, Structura and the two men on 20 March 2024 ([FBI affidavit, 2024](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-09/doppelganger_affidavit_9.4.24.pdf)).

**Execute.** The affidavit lists influencers, paid advertisements, some made with artificial intelligence (AI) tools, and fake profiles posing as Americans that posted comments with links (Maximise Exposure; targeted engagement). Nimmo and Hutchins cite geo-restricted sites (evading detection) and comments on the war under posts about sport or fashion (indiscriminate engagement) ([Nimmo and Hutchins, 2023](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/202303-Nimmo_Hutchins_Online_Ops.pdf)). Blocked domains gave way to backups, redirects and likely compromised sites (Persist; enabling longevity); Meta blocked over 2,000 but wrote that blocking on each platform can only go so far while the websites keep operating ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). France says VIGINUM's early detection allowed protective measures ([Colonna, 2023](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/declaration-de-catherine-colonna-ingerences-numeriques-etrangeres-detection-par-la-france-d-une)), and the Department of Justice (DOJ) seized the 32 domains ([German Marshall Fund, 2024](https://gmfus.org/news/doj-vs-doppelganger-four-takeaways-us-governments-action-against-russian-election-interference)).

**Assess.** An SDA manual set key performance indicators (KPIs) such as 10,000,000 "encounters" a month for comments in Ukraine's regions ([FBI affidavit, 2024](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-09/doppelganger_affidavit_9.4.24.pdf)). Meta noted that such firms have an incentive to exaggerate their effectiveness, so impact must be judged "based on evidence, not on the actors’ own claims or their persistence" ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). A 2025 report for Sweden's Psychological Defence Agency, drawing on 3,100 leaked SDA documents, assessed with medium confidence that <Claim status="reported" by="by its authors">"Discourse in Western target countries around the exposure of Doppelgänger contributed to SDA receiving more funding to conduct more information influence campaigns"</Claim> ([Pamment and Tsurtsumia, 2025](https://mpf.se/download/18.7cffbee41969f6d83e115221/1747230166207/Beyond%20Operation%20Doppelganger.pdf)).

## Spamouflage: a second state, the same vocabulary

In 2020 Graphika reported Twitter's attribution to operators linked to Chinese state actors, while "Graphika has not to date been able to establish a connection between Spamouflage Dragon and government entities" ([Graphika, 2020](https://public-assets.graphika.com/reports/graphika_report_spamouflage_dragon_goes_to_america.pdf)). In April 2023 the DOJ charged 34 officers of the Ministry of Public Security (MPS) of the People's Republic of China (PRC), assigned to a "912 Special Project Working Group": <Claim status="alleged" by="by US prosecutors">"The complaint alleges how members of the Group created thousands of fake online personas on social media sites"</Claim> ([DOJ, 2023](https://static.poder360.com.br/2023/04/DOJ-policiais-china-presos.pdf); [DOJ, archived release](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/40-officers-china-s-national-police-charged-transnational-repression-schemes-targeting-us)); OpenAI later wrote that the FBI had attributed some of Spamouflage's activity to a unit of that ministry ([OpenAI, 2024](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/kftzwdyauwt9/5IMxzTmUclSOAcWUXbkVrK/3cfab518e6b10789ab8843bcca18b633/Threat_Intel_Report.pdf)). Asked the next day about the US cases, foreign ministry spokesperson Wang Wenbin said China "firmly opposes" the "blatant prosecution of Chinese law enforcement and cyber administration officials" ([PRC foreign ministry, 2023a](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/nanhai/eng/fyrbt_1/202304/t20230418_11061179.htm)). In August 2023 Meta <Claim status="attributed" by="by Meta">found links to individuals associated with Chinese law enforcement</Claim> and removed 7,704 Facebook accounts, 954 Pages, 15 Groups and 15 Instagram accounts ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). On Meta's findings Wang said "I’m not aware of what you mentioned" and that the company should "avoid applying double standards, truly differentiate rumors and lies from truth and facts" ([PRC foreign ministry, 2023b](https://adelaide.china-consulate.gov.cn/eng/wjbfyrth/202308/t20230830_11135713.htm)).

Meta describes operators across China working shifts in Beijing hours behind shared proxies and posting identical "personal" comments on more than 50 platforms, from Pages likely bought from spam operators, with followers mainly in Vietnam, Bangladesh and Brazil, none of them targets (acquiring assets) ([Meta, 2023](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-2023-Adversarial-threat-report)). To claim that the pandemic began in the US, the network posted a 66-page "research paper", then videos, articles and links (Develop Content; Establish Legitimacy). Nimmo and Hutchins cite posts without hashtags or mentions (indiscriminate engagement) and a persona rebuilt after a takedown, "This is my new account" (enabling longevity) ([Nimmo and Hutchins, 2023](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/202303-Nimmo_Hutchins_Online_Ops.pdf)). For Assess, the DOJ alleges the group rewarded members who ran personas undetected. Meta found that "Spamouflage consistently struggled to reach beyond its own (fake) echo chamber"; Google's Threat Analysis Group (TAG), which calls it DRAGONBRIDGE, reported over 175,000 instances disrupted ([Google TAG, 2024](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/google-disrupted-dragonbridge-activity-q1-2024/)).

## The same lens, another origin

In June 2024 Reuters <Claim status="reported" by="by Reuters">identified at least 300 X accounts matching former US military officials' descriptions of a 2020 to 2021 operation questioning China's Sinovac vaccine in the Philippines</Claim>, accounts meant to impersonate Filipinos ([Reuters via Al Jazeera, 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/6/14/pentagon-ran-secret-anti-vax-campaign-to-undermine-china-during-pandemic)). A Pentagon message to Manila, as reviewed by Reuters, said "It is true that the (Department of Defense) did message Philippines audiences questioning the safety and efficacy of Sinovac"; spokesman Pete Nguyen said an initial review "found that the U.S. military was not responsible for the troubling social media content related to the Philippines" cited by Reuters ([Reuters, 2024](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/politics-issues/2024/07/29/280555/u-s-told-philippines-it-made-missteps-in-secret-anti-vax-propaganda-effort/)).

## What defenders and platforms do at each phase

| Phase and links | Documented defender actions |
|---|---|
| Plan; gathering information | Little is visible; plans surfaced through legal process and leaks |
| Prepare; acquiring and disguising assets | Automated removal of fake accounts, domain blocking and seizure, sanctions |
| Execute; engagement, evasion, longevity | Takedowns, public attribution, rebuttals, debunking, limiting channels, sharing indicators |
| Assess | Rating impact on evidence, as the Breakout Scale does, not on operators' claims |

The EEAS found that 80% of the incidents it studied drew no response; 20 incidents drew 28 countermeasures, half of them statements of refutal ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)). Exposure can end a front: the fake journalist Alice Donovan and the PeaceData site both stopped once exposed, OpenAI notes ([OpenAI, 2026](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-ai-enabled-false-front-operations/)).

## Limits and criticisms

The authors list limits: Schneier's chain is fuzzy, Meta's leaves strategy out, and the EEAS notes important differences between computer systems and the information environment ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)). Schneier's defence against kernels of truth assumed that debunking amplifies fake news ([Schneier, 2019](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/toward-information-operations-kill-chain)); Brendan Nyhan reported in 2021 an emerging consensus that "corrective information is typically at least somewhat effective at increasing belief accuracy when received by respondents" ([Nyhan, 2021, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912440117)).

Pamment and Tsurtsumia argue that a focus on narratives and TTPs distracts from strategic analysis and that "These narratives and TTPs are derived from insufficiently diverse data, which leads to false inferences regarding the influence of SDA’s operations" ([Pamment and Tsurtsumia, 2025](https://mpf.se/download/18.7cffbee41969f6d83e115221/1747230166207/Beyond%20Operation%20Doppelganger.pdf)); their evidence is SDA's own reporting to its client, and the publishing agency leaves responsibility for the conclusions with the authors.

Researchers depend on figures that platforms choose to release, Alaphilippe noted ([Alaphilippe, 2020](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/adding-a-d-to-the-abc-disinformation-framework/)), and François and evelyn douek found in 2021 that ambiguities in platform enforcement distort public understanding of influence operations ([François and douek, 2021](https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/download/17/8/15)). François warned that "Governments’ appetite to regulate viral deception through the content lens risk further eroding protections to freedom of expression online" ([François, 2019](https://www.ivir.nl/publicaties/download/ABC_Framework_2019_Sept_2019.pdf)), and the EEAS calls the choice of responses explicitly normative ([EEAS, 2023](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/rr9q9n-yuiux/2023-02-00-EU-First-EEAS-Report-Foreign-Manipulation-Interference-Threats-via-website.pdf)).

## What to remember

- ABC sorts a case into actors, behaviour and content; later extensions added distribution, degree and effect.
- DISARM, styled on MITRE ATT&CK, divides an incident into Plan, Prepare, Execute and Assess, with 16 numbered tactics.
- Kill chains assume that breaking one link disrupts an operation; Schneier notes that influence steps overlap and run out of order.
- Doppelganger went from no formal attribution in 2022 to attributions by Meta and France and an FBI affidavit in 2024; Russia's embassy in Paris and the Kremlin rejected the accusations.
- Meta linked Spamouflage to Chinese law enforcement and US prosecutors charged police officers; China's foreign ministry opposed the prosecution and urged Meta to avoid double standards.
- Platforms measured little real reach for either operation, and Meta warns that impact must rest on evidence, not on operators' claims.
- Critics say TTP frameworks miss strategy and depend on platform data, and responses involve value choices.
