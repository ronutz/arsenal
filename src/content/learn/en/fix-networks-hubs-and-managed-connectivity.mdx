The Financial Information eXchange (FIX) protocol gave the trading industry a common language, but a language does not lay cables, test counterparties or answer the telephone at three in the morning when a session drops. That work fell to FIX networks: companies that sit between investment firms, brokers and trading venues and sell one managed connection in place of many. This part of the series opened by [Electronic trading: from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) builds on [The FIX protocol: origins, versions and the community](/learn/the-fix-protocol-origins-versions-and-the-community) and [Anatomy of a FIX message and the session layer](/learn/anatomy-of-a-fix-message-and-the-session-layer). After reading, you can explain why bilateral FIX links multiply, what a hub does with the header fields that carry routing, who owns the main networks after two decades of sales, what a financial extranet is, and how an order travels from a client through a broker to an exchange, with Brazil's exchange, B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão), as the worked case ([B3 Bora Investir glossary, B3 S/A](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/glossario/b3-s-a)).

## Why point-to-point FIX multiplies

A FIX session, in the words of the FIX Trading Community's session-layer page, is a bidirectional stream of ordered messages between two peers, numbered in a continuous sequence that begins with 1, and one session can survive several successive connections ([FIX Trading Community, FIX Session Layer](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fixsession/)). The two ends name themselves in every message header: SenderCompID (tag 49) identifies the firm sending and TargetCompID (tag 56) the firm receiving, both fields present since FIX 2.7 ([FIX Latest, SenderCompID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/SenderCompID); [FIX Latest, TargetCompID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/TargetCompID)). B3's own specification restates the rule with a two-line table: when A sends directly to B, A is the sender and B the target, and the roles swap for the reply ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)).

So every pair of firms that trades electronically needs its own session, with its own sequence numbers, its own network path and its own agreement about how the messages will be filled in. The arithmetic is unkind. If every one of *n* firms connected directly to every other, the number of sessions would be *n*(*n* − 1)/2: 45 for ten firms, 4,950 for a hundred, 499,500 for a thousand. A hub turns that mesh into a star, in which each firm keeps one connection to the network and the network keeps the rest.

Real communities sit between those extremes, because no asset manager trades with every broker, but the counts are still large. When NYSE Euronext agreed to buy NYFIX in 2009, it described the NYFIX Marketplace as more than 1,000 global trading counterparties connected to one another through more than 9,000 fully managed FIX messaging channels ([NYSE Euronext, third quarter 2009 results, exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K, 30 October 2009](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001368007/000119312509219091/dex991.htm)).

Each of those channels carries more than a network circuit. The FIX Trading Community notes that "It is common for parties to encode their FIX messages slightly differently, possibly using user-defined tags", which is why it built Orchestra, a machine-readable way to publish a firm's rules of engagement ([FIX Trading Community, Orchestra](https://fixtrading.org/standards/orchestra/)). A broker taking a new client therefore has to agree the dialect, test it, certify it, monitor it and support it. When Transaction Network Services (TNS) launched a FIX hub in 2016, its managing director said it was designed "to eliminate the burden associated with setting up and managing multiple FIX connections" ([TNS release reproduced by Mondo Visione, 10 November 2016](https://mondovisione.com/news/tns-launches-fix-hub-to-streamline-trading-globally-20161110/)). Eight years later a Broadridge release put the business case more bluntly: "In an industry where FIX connectivity has become commoditized, outsourcing this critical infrastructure is becoming increasingly beneficial" ([Broadridge, 5 September 2024](https://s203.q4cdn.com/209299927/files/doc_news/FIA-Tech-Leverages-Broadridges-COMS-and-NYFIX-Network-to-Expand-its-TDN-Capabilities-2024.pdf)).

## What a FIX network does

### Routing on behalf of someone else

FIX has had fields for third-party routing since version 4.0. OnBehalfOfCompID (tag 115) identifies the firm that originated a message when a third party delivers it: the third party's identifier goes in SenderCompID and the originator's in tag 115 ([FIX Latest, OnBehalfOfCompID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/OnBehalfOfCompID)). DeliverToCompID (tag 128) works the other way, naming the firm that should finally receive a message while the third party sits in TargetCompID ([FIX Latest, DeliverToCompID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/DeliverToCompID)). Companion fields narrow the address to a trader, OnBehalfOfSubID (116) and DeliverToSubID (129), or to a location or desk, OnBehalfOfLocationID (144, added in FIX 4.1) ([FIX Latest, OnBehalfOfSubID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/OnBehalfOfSubID); [FIX Latest, DeliverToSubID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/DeliverToSubID); [FIX Latest, OnBehalfOfLocationID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/OnBehalfOfLocationID)).

The worked example below follows one order through a hub. The firm names are invented for the illustration; the field rules are the ones just cited. An asset manager, AMGR, sends a NewOrderSingle (MsgType 35=D), the message an order submitter uses to place an order with a counterparty ([FIX Latest, NewOrderSingle](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/messages/NewOrderSingle)), to a broker, BRKR, through a network whose own identifier is NETX.

| Hop | Message | SenderCompID (49) | TargetCompID (56) | OnBehalfOfCompID (115) | DeliverToCompID (128) | MsgSeqNum (34) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Asset manager to network | NewOrderSingle (35=D) | AMGR | NETX |  | BRKR | next number on the AMGR to NETX session |
| Network to broker | NewOrderSingle (35=D) | NETX | BRKR | AMGR |  | next number on the NETX to BRKR session |
| Broker to network | ExecutionReport (35=8) | BRKR | NETX |  | AMGR | next number on the NETX to BRKR session |
| Network to asset manager | ExecutionReport (35=8) | NETX | AMGR | BRKR |  | next number on the AMGR to NETX session |

Three consequences follow from the definitions. First, the hub runs two separate sessions, so each leg has its own sequence numbers, the integer counter in tag 34 ([FIX Latest, MsgSeqNum](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/MsgSeqNum)). Second, because the header changes on each leg, the hub has to compute a new BodyLength (tag 9), the byte count up to the checksum, and a new CheckSum (tag 10), the three-character trailer that closes every message ([FIX Latest, BodyLength](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/BodyLength); [FIX Latest, CheckSum](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/CheckSum)). Third, the business content of the order, ClOrdID (11), Symbol (55), Side (54), OrderQty (38), OrdType (40) and Price (44), is defined in the same repository and travels untouched unless the hub has been told to translate it ([FIX Latest, ClOrdID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/ClOrdID); [FIX Latest, Symbol](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/Symbol); [FIX Latest, Side](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/Side); [FIX Latest, OrderQty](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/OrderQty); [FIX Latest, OrdType](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/OrdType); [FIX Latest, Price](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/Price)).

Not every network uses the third-party fields. Broadridge says its NYFIX network can be configured "for hub-and-spoke or point-to-point" ([Broadridge, NYFIX Order Routing Network](https://www.broadridge.com/capability/front-office-solutions/connectivity/order-routing-network)), and an exchange may refuse them altogether: B3 states that it used to include DeliverToCompID on order entry and drop copy sessions and no longer supports the field in any scenario ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)). In a pure point-to-point design the network carries each session through to the other side and keeps the mapping in its own tables instead of in the header.

### Translation and normalization

Because counterparties fill in FIX differently, a hub often rewrites messages on the way through. The FIX Trading Community describes the case directly: an Orchestra file that describes each variant of FIX "can inform a translator to normalize them into a common format for processing" ([FIX Trading Community, Orchestra](https://fixtrading.org/standards/orchestra/)). Vendors sell this as a feature. Raptor Trading Systems lists "Dynamic FIX message transformation, normalization and translation across counterparties" for its FIX Hub ([Raptor Trading Systems, products](https://raptortrading.com/products/)), and Iress advertises "Normalised and predictable FIX RoE (FIX 4.2 and FIX 4.4) compliance for fast certification" for its cloud FIX Hub, RoE being rules of engagement ([Iress, FIX Hub](https://www.iress.com/software/trading-and-market-data/fix/)).

### Onboarding, testing and certification

Before a counterparty may send live orders, its messages are tested against the receiving firm's rules. Broadridge describes a testing and simulation tool "with AI-automated client certification" and "automated onboarding and instant certification of FIX counterparties through our web-based interface" ([Broadridge, FIX Technology and Client Connectivity Services](https://www.broadridge.com/financial-services/capital-markets/trading-and-connectivity/fix-technology-and-client-connectivity-services)). When TNS launched its hub it said new connections could "usually be established within 24 hours" ([TNS release reproduced by Mondo Visione, 10 November 2016](https://mondovisione.com/news/tns-launches-fix-hub-to-streamline-trading-globally-20161110/)). Those are vendors' own claims, and they describe the commercial promise of a network: the second broker a client adds should be cheaper than the first. Exchanges run their own certification on top. B3 allows only certified software to trade, subject to inspection by its self-regulatory arm, and checks a version string of the form M.m.R.B carried in the message a connection sends to log in; a change in M, a material change, requires new certification ([B3, Manual de Procedimentos Operacionais de Negociação, 1 July 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/CF/D0/64/95/753D0A105BF9020AAC094EA8/MPO%20de%20Negociacao%20da%20B3.pdf)).

### Monitoring, resilience and support

A network sells availability as much as reach. Broadridge's NYFIX overview promises regional hubs in the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Africa and Asia-Pacific, support "during all major market hours", and round-the-clock multilingual help ([Broadridge, NYFIX solution overview, undated](https://www.broadridge.com/_assets/pdf/broadridge-nyfix-solutions.pdf)). TNS said in 2016 that its core network had no single point of failure, more than 125 points of presence and monitoring from several network operating centres ([TNS release reproduced by Mondo Visione, 10 November 2016](https://mondovisione.com/news/tns-launches-fix-hub-to-streamline-trading-globally-20161110/)). Broadridge also sells session monitoring with "proactive alerts" to firms that run their own engines ([Broadridge, FIX Technology and Client Connectivity Services](https://www.broadridge.com/financial-services/capital-markets/trading-and-connectivity/fix-technology-and-client-connectivity-services)).

### Discovery: indications of interest

Networks also distribute what brokers want to advertise. The FIX indication of interest (IOI) message (35=6) exists to "market merchandise which the broker is buying or selling in either a proprietary or agency capacity", with the warning that another firm may react first ([FIX Latest, IOI](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/messages/IOI)). The London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) says its Autex IOI service processes approximately four million IOI messages a day ([LSEG, Autex IOI fact sheet](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/data-analytics/en_us/documents/fact-sheets/lseg-autex-ioi-fact-sheet.pdf)), and Bloomberg tells users of its execution system that they can "Surface relevant IOIs, filter by strategy or security" ([Bloomberg, listed electronic trading](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/trading/electronic-markets/listed/)).

### Drop copies

A drop copy is a copy of an execution report sent to a third system, typically a risk or back-office platform. FIX 4.4 added CopyMsgIndicator (tag 797), which "Indicates whether or not this message is a drop copy of another message" ([FIX Latest, CopyMsgIndicator](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/CopyMsgIndicator)). B3 sells FIX 4.4 drop copy sessions "for confirmation of orders and trades" separately from order entry ([B3, Commercial Policy for Connectivity Services](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/47/92/29/A6/EED1F910ADC36BE9AC094EA8/Commercial%20Politcy%20for%20Connectivity%20Services.pdf)), and its CompID scheme gives broker and client drop copy connections their own prefixes, D and Z ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)).

### NYFIX: from a community to Broadridge

NYSE Euronext agreed in 2009 to buy NYFIX, Inc. for $144 million including preferred stock consideration ([NYSE Euronext, exhibit 99.1, 30 October 2009](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001368007/000119312509219091/dex991.htm)). When the deal closed, the trade press reported that NYFIX's FIX business, the NYFIX Marketplace and its FIX software, became part of NYSE Technologies, while the American agency execution business and the Millennium alternative trading system were being sold to ConvergEx; NYSE Technologies' chief executive counted "more than 450 buy-side institutions and more than 600 sell-side institutions" on the Marketplace ([Hedgeweek, 1 December 2009](https://www.hedgeweek.com/nyse-euronext-completes-acquisition-nyfix/)).

By 2014 NYSE Technologies belonged to Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the owner of the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE). On 23 June 2014 ICE agreed to sell NYFIX and Metabit, its Japanese connectivity unit, to Ullink, describing the NYFIX Marketplace as "a global community of more than 1,000 trading counterparties" ([ICE, 23 June 2014](https://s2.q4cdn.com/154085107/files/doc_news/archive/3788db55-d9b4-47d1-afac-8b4a27230fb9.pdf)); the sale closed on 19 September 2014 on undisclosed terms ([ICE, 22 September 2014](https://s2.q4cdn.com/154085107/files/doc_news/archive/7bf1af5f-a571-40b5-b369-192e1a97bfe2.pdf)). The 2017 announcement described Ullink, founded in 2001, as the owner of NYFIX, "one of the industry's largest FIX based trading communities" ([Nordic Capital, 28 November 2017](https://www.nordiccapital.com/news-views/press-releases/nordic-capital-backed-itiviti-announces-the-intention-to-combine-with-ullink-to-create-a-new-global-force-in-capital-markets-technology-and-infrastructure)).

That announcement was Nordic Capital's plan to combine Ullink with Itiviti, a Swedish company formed from Orc Group, CameronTec and Tbricks ([Nordic Capital, 28 November 2017](https://www.nordiccapital.com/news-views/press-releases/nordic-capital-backed-itiviti-announces-the-intention-to-combine-with-ullink-to-create-a-new-global-force-in-capital-markets-technology-and-infrastructure)); the merger was completed on 14 March 2018 ([Itiviti release reproduced by Mondo Visione, March 2018](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/itiviti-and-ullink-complete-merger-to-build-a-global-technology-force-in-the-cap-2018315/)). On 29 March 2021 Broadridge agreed to buy Itiviti from Nordic Capital in an all-cash transaction valued at €2.143 billion, about $2.5 billion, citing Itiviti's "FIX connectivity and network offerings" and recurring revenue of about €210 million in 2020 ([Broadridge, 29 March 2021](https://broadridge.com/press-release/2021/broadridge-agrees-to-acquire-itiviti); [Nordic Capital, 29 March 2021](https://www.nordiccapital.com/news-views/press-releases/nordic-capital-to-sell-itiviti-a-leading-trading-technology-and-service-provider-to-broadridge-a-global-fintech-leader)). The deal closed on 12 May 2021 ([Broadridge, 12 May 2021](https://www.broadridge.com/press-release/2021/broadridge-completes-acquisition-of-itiviti)), and Itiviti now trades as Broadridge Trading and Connectivity Solutions ([Broadridge, NYFIX solution overview, undated](https://www.broadridge.com/_assets/pdf/broadridge-nyfix-solutions.pdf)).

Broadridge's own figures for the network do not agree with one another. Its undated overview says NYFIX "Connects more than 1,700 buy-side and sell-side firms globally" and services "more than one-third of all spoke-and-hub ORN traffic", ORN meaning order routing network, and calls it "the original FIX order routing network" ([Broadridge, NYFIX solution overview, undated](https://www.broadridge.com/_assets/pdf/broadridge-nyfix-solutions.pdf)); its product page, read on 15 November 2026, offers connectivity "to over 2,200 buy- and sell-side participants" in one place and "1,800+ buy-side and sell-side firms" in another ([Broadridge, NYFIX Order Routing Network](https://www.broadridge.com/capability/front-office-solutions/connectivity/order-routing-network)). The difference may come from what is counted, firms or participants, but the pages do not say.

### Radianz: Reuters, BT, and now TNS

Radianz was founded by Reuters and Equant in June 2000; in October 2004 Reuters agreed to buy Equant's 49% interest for $110 million in cash while it negotiated with BT ([Thomson Reuters investor relations, 21 October 2004](https://ir.thomsonreuters.com/node/12746)). On 10 March 2005 BT agreed to pay $175 million for Radianz plus the cash on its balance sheet, alongside a network services contract under which Reuters was "expected to spend in the region of $3 billion over eight and a half years" ([Thomson Reuters investor relations, 10 March 2005](https://ir.thomsonreuters.com/node/11296)).

Two decades later BT sold it on. On 2 September 2025 BT and TNS announced that TNS would acquire the Radianz business, which BT described as providing "financial information exchange networks" connecting brokers, institutions, exchanges and clearing and settlement houses, and which generated revenue of about £142 million in the 2024/25 financial year ([BT, 2 September 2025](https://newsroom.bt.com/bt-enters-agreement-with-tns-for-the-sale-of-its-radianz-business/)). BT announced completion on 2 February 2026, after regulatory clearance ([BT, 2 February 2026](https://newsroom.bt.com/bt-completes-sale-of-its-radianz-business-to-tns/)). The TNS release said Radianz would join its Financial Markets offering and that its network links thousands of market participants and financial applications, without a precise count or price ([TNS release on Business Wire, 2 February 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260202066868/en/)).

### TNS and Waypoint

TNS has done business since 1990 ([Siris Capital, 1 June 2021](https://siris.com/news/koch-equity-development-completes-acquisition-of-transaction-network-services)). On 1 June 2021 an affiliate of Koch Equity Development, an investment and acquisition subsidiary of Koch Industries, completed its purchase of TNS from affiliates of Siris Capital Group, which TNS's chief executive thanked for an "eight-year partnership", making TNS a wholly owned Koch subsidiary based in Reston, Virginia ([Siris Capital, 1 June 2021](https://siris.com/news/koch-equity-development-completes-acquisition-of-transaction-network-services); [PR Newswire, 1 June 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/koch-equity-development-completes-acquisition-of-transaction-network-services-301302702.html)).

Its financial markets network has been described with different counts over the years: more than 2,000 endpoints and 125 points of presence when it launched its Secure Trading Hub in 2016 ([TNS release reproduced by Mondo Visione, 10 November 2016](https://mondovisione.com/news/tns-launches-fix-hub-to-streamline-trading-globally-20161110/)); over 2,800 financial community endpoints and access to more than 60 exchanges in an April 2022 release ([TNS, 26 April 2022](https://tnsi.com/resource/waypoint/tns-announces-market-data-coverage-for-all-us-equity-options-groups-press-release/)); and 5,000 secure extranet endpoints, connectivity to more than 140 exchanges and over 1,000 financial markets clients in a 2022 fact sheet ([TNS, Financial Markets "About Us" sheet, 2022](https://tnsi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/FM_AboutUs_GBL_MAR2022.pdf)). In 2026 TNS formed Waypoint Trading Solutions "following the acquisition of Radianz" and describes it as combining a low-latency trading platform, "the world's largest financial extranet" and managed market data; that superlative is the company's own ([TNS, Waypoint](https://tnsi.com/solutions/waypoint/)).

### IPC Systems and Connexus

IPC Systems, which sells trading communications including trader turrets, introduced Connexus, a "financial extranet service", on 21 November 2011, saying its Financial Markets Network reached more than 4,000 market participant locations in over 700 cities in more than 58 countries over a Multiprotocol Label Switching (MPLS) platform ([IPC, PR Newswire, 21 November 2011](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ipc-systems-introduces-connexus-financial-extranet-service-134230928.html)). On 1 December 2014 IPC announced its sale by Silver Lake Partners to Centerbridge Partners for about $1.2 billion in total deal value ([Silver Lake, 1 December 2014](https://silverlake.com/ipc-systems-to-be-acquired-by-centerbridge-partners)), a purchase completed in February 2015 ([NJBIZ, 9 February 2015](https://njbiz.com/centerbridge-wraps-up-deal-for-jersey-city-network-services-company/)).

In October 2021 an out-of-court recapitalization restructured more than $1 billion of debt, cut it by more than $400 million and brought $125 million of new capital ([Kirkland & Ellis, October 2021](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2021/10/kirkland-advises-ipc-systems-recap)), with funds managed by Strategic Value Partners (SVP) becoming a substantial equity holder and Centerbridge remaining a significant one ([ABF Journal, 4 October 2021](https://www.abfjournal.com/ipc-systems-enters-agreement-to-reduce-debt-by-more-than-400mm/)). On 9 January 2024 SVP announced that its funds had acquired the remaining 40% of IPC, having owned 60% since 2021 ([SVP, PR Newswire, 9 January 2024](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/svp-acquires-remaining-40-of-ipc-resulting-in-full-ownership-302029676.html)).

### Bloomberg, LSEG and the order routing networks inside trading systems

Some of the largest FIX networks are built into the screens traders already use. Bloomberg says its execution management system, EMSX, connects users "to 3,700+ global broker destinations via FIX with no additional connectivity fees" ([Bloomberg, listed electronic trading](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/trading/electronic-markets/listed/)). LSEG says its Autex Trade Route connects "over 1,000 buy-side firms and 600 brokers" and processes "over 4 billion shares per day" through a single FIX connection ([LSEG, Autex Trade Route](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-analytics/products/autex-trade-route)). These figures are the vendors' own, read on 15 November 2026, and are not comparable with one another: a destination, a broker and a firm are different units.

### Exchange networks, hubs and the newer entrants

Exchanges built networks too. In September 2016 ICE combined its Secure Financial Transaction Infrastructure (SFTI) with the 7ticks network into a business it called Connectivity, including "the SFTI Direct Market Access (DMA) network of over 150 venues" ([ICE, 8 September 2016](https://s2.q4cdn.com/154085107/files/doc_news/archive/1c1734d7-d16f-44cc-b7f4-e38a87b3781b.pdf)). Software vendors sell hubs that a firm runs for itself or consumes as a service: Raptor Trading Systems, founded in 2003, private and headquartered in New York, offers a FIX Hub and a connectivity network it calls RaptorNet ([Raptor Trading Systems, company](https://raptortrading.com/company/)), and Iress offers its cloud-native FIX Hub ([Iress, FIX Hub](https://www.iress.com/software/trading-and-market-data/fix/)). Pico, which describes itself as a technology partner for capital markets since 2009, runs a fibre network it calls PicoNet ([Pico, about](https://www.pico.net/about/)) and says it connects "900+ exchange and venue products across 55+ global data centers" ([Pico](https://www.pico.net/)); its Corvil Analytics measurement business belongs to the later part on network-derived analytics and to [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency).

## Extranets and financial community networks

A financial extranet is a private network shared by a community of firms, which connect once to the provider and reach the other members and their services over it; IPC presented Connexus as access to a broad range of trade lifecycle services "through a single connection" ([IPC, PR Newswire, 21 November 2011](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ipc-systems-introduces-connexus-financial-extranet-service-134230928.html)). TNS described its Secure Trading Extranet as "a private closed network with strong network security measures" ([TNS release reproduced by Mondo Visione, 10 November 2016](https://mondovisione.com/news/tns-launches-fix-hub-to-streamline-trading-globally-20161110/)), and IPC built Connexus on MPLS ([IPC, PR Newswire, 21 November 2011](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ipc-systems-introduces-connexus-financial-extranet-service-134230928.html)), the label-switched technology explained in [MPLS: labels, stacks, and the BGP-free core](/learn/mpls-primer). FIX itself does not care what carries it, which is why sessions also run over the internet inside a virtual private network (VPN) or Transport Layer Security; the FIX Trading Community's FIX-over-TLS standard, FIXS, is "considered a requirement as it introduces a basic level of security and standard options for interoperability" ([FIX Trading Community, FIXS](https://fixtrading.org/standards/fixs/)). The trade-offs of tunnels and of two-sided certificates are covered in [VPN fundamentals](/learn/vpn-fundamentals) and [Mutual TLS](/learn/mutual-tls).

### Brazil: the networks around B3

B3 publishes its network rules in a manual. It offers four ways in: its own network, the RCB (Rede de Comunicação B3); VPN over the internet; local network access inside its co-location area; and an agreement with Rede de Telecomunicações para o Mercado (RTM), a Brazilian financial network company ([B3, Manual de Acesso à Infraestrutura Tecnológica, version 8.5, 13 February 2026](https://b3.com.br/data/files/B9/F0/1F/4F/DC7CF91094DCD8F9AC094EA8/2026%20-%20Manual%20de%20Acesso%20a%20Infraestrutura%20Tecnologica%20da%20B3.pdf)). The same manual sets the RCB's terms:

- institutions buy the circuits from telecommunications operators that B3 lists, using dark fibre, wavelength services, Ethernet carried over older telecom transport, or metro Ethernet, up to 10 Gbps;
- the customer's routers must support BGP and first-hop redundancy, and multicast where they take market data, because B3's Unified Market Data Feed (UMDF) is multicast and is generated independently by both data centres as Feed A and Feed B, which customers may use at the same time;
- brokers, banks and securities distributors, and the providers that connect brokers' trading desks, must contract two different network providers using physically distinct routes, to avoid a single point of failure;
- the active data centre is in Santana de Parnaíba, in greater São Paulo, and the disaster recovery site is on Rua Quinze de Novembro in central São Paulo.

The manual's revision history also records a Brazilian community network that no longer exists: on 13 March 2015 B3 discontinued the Rede de Comunicação da Comunidade Financeira (RCCF) ([B3, Manual de Acesso à Infraestrutura Tecnológica, version 8.5, 13 February 2026](https://b3.com.br/data/files/B9/F0/1F/4F/DC7CF91094DCD8F9AC094EA8/2026%20-%20Manual%20de%20Acesso%20a%20Infraestrutura%20Tecnologica%20da%20B3.pdf)). The RCCF still appears beside the RCB in the network diagram of B3's FIX specification, which shows DMA providers reaching the broker community over either network ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)).

RTM says it was founded in 1997 by Andima, today the Associação Brasileira das Entidades dos Mercados Financeiro e de Capitais (ANBIMA), to interconnect the financial sector and cut telecommunications costs, that Cetip, now part of B3, later became a shareholder, and that it is today a company of ANBIMA and B3 ([RTM, institutional page](https://rtm.net.br/institucional/); [B3 Bora Investir glossary, ANBIMA](https://borainvestir.b3.com.br/glossario/anbima-associacao-brasileira-das-entidades-dos-mercados-financeiro-e-de-capitais/)). The same page puts its reach at more than 850 institutions in 21 states in one paragraph and more than 750 in 20 states and the Federal District in another ([RTM, institutional page](https://rtm.net.br/institucional/)). B3's manual says RTM's members reach B3 trading, post-trading and Banco B3 services through the agreement, while the reverse path for B3 users has been discontinued for new contracts ([B3, Manual de Acesso à Infraestrutura Tecnológica, version 8.5, 13 February 2026](https://b3.com.br/data/files/B9/F0/1F/4F/DC7CF91094DCD8F9AC094EA8/2026%20-%20Manual%20de%20Acesso%20a%20Infraestrutura%20Tecnologica%20da%20B3.pdf)).

## How a broker connects a client to an exchange

The table follows an order from an asset manager to the exchange and back, with B3 as the exchange. Each row is sourced in the paragraphs after it.

| Step | Who acts | What crosses the wire | Where the rule comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asset manager's order management system (OMS) | NewOrderSingle (35=D) on the session to its network or broker | FIX Latest |
| 2 | FIX network, if one is used | Same order on a new session, header rewritten or session mapped | FIX Latest third-party fields; the network's rules |
| 3 | Broker's OMS and risk system | Order checked against the client's limits and identified by origin | Resolução CVM 35 of the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM), article 16 |
| 4 | Broker's gateway to B3 | Order on the broker's EntryPoint session, FIX 4.4, with a CompID and Sender Location for its access category | B3 EntryPoint guidelines |
| 5 | B3's matching engine | Execution or rest in the book | B3 trading rules |
| 6 | Back along the chain | ExecutionReport (35=8), plus drop copies to risk and back office | FIX Latest; B3 drop copy service |

The asset manager's system sends a NewOrderSingle to its broker, directly or through a network ([FIX Latest, NewOrderSingle](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/messages/NewOrderSingle)). In Brazil the broker that receives orders by direct market access (DMA) must, under the securities regulator's intermediation rule, try to identify where each order came from and keep a pre-trade risk control system with operational limits and parameters to catch erroneous orders, able to adjust orders that exceed each client's limits ([CVM, Resolução CVM 35, articles 12 and 16, 26 May 2021](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/cvm_institucional/export/sites/cvm/legislacao/resolucoes/anexos/001/resol035.pdf)). B3 provides a pre-trade risk tool, Line EntryPoint, through which brokers set limits for clients using the exchange's DMA 3 and DMA 4 models ([B3, EntryPoint](https://www.b3.com.br/en_us/solutions/platforms/puma-trading-system/for-developers-and-vendors/entrypoint-8AA8D0CC95C8AC5601961178692278BE)).

The broker then sends the order to B3 over EntryPoint, the exchange's order entry interface, "based on the 4.4 version of the Financial Information Exchange ("FIX") Protocol" ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)). B3 assigns the CompIDs, and their first letter says what kind of connection is speaking: S or C for trading desks, depending on whether the front end is B3's or a vendor's, R for give-up agents, C, P, Y and X for the four DMA categories, D and Z for broker and client drop copies. Every order also carries a Sender Location as a party role, a code that identifies the access category, and "DMA Providers (DMA2), sponsored by multiple Firms, must use different FIX sessions to submit orders from each Firm" ([B3, EntryPoint Messaging Guidelines 2.9.31, 16 December 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/FF/84/5F/93/DB81D8103152D4C8AC094EA8/EntryPointMessagingGuidelines.pdf)). The FIX field behind the idea, SenderLocationID (tag 142), has identified a message originator's location since FIX 4.1 ([FIX Latest, SenderLocationID](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/fields/SenderLocationID)).

B3 also forbids one connection from being shared by more than one trading participant, so each broker's order flow stays segregated ([B3, Manual de Procedimentos Operacionais de Negociação, 1 July 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/CF/D0/64/95/753D0A105BF9020AAC094EA8/MPO%20de%20Negociacao%20da%20B3.pdf)). The ExecutionReport (35=8) carries the result back ([FIX Latest, ExecutionReport](https://orchimate.org/fixtrading/fix-latest/messages/ExecutionReport)), and drop copy sessions send copies to the systems that need them ([B3, Commercial Policy for Connectivity Services](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/47/92/29/A6/EED1F910ADC36BE9AC094EA8/Commercial%20Politcy%20for%20Connectivity%20Services.pdf)).

There is a shorter path. Under B3's sponsored direct connection, a client sends its own proprietary orders straight to the trading system, always under the responsibility of a broker, within pre-trade limits the broker sets ([B3, Manual de Procedimentos Operacionais de Negociação, 1 July 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/CF/D0/64/95/753D0A105BF9020AAC094EA8/MPO%20de%20Negociacao%20da%20B3.pdf)). How B3's access models evolved, and what co-location adds, is the subject of [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access); the controls that sit in step 3 in other markets belong to the later part on pre-trade risk and market access.

## What to remember

- Every pair of FIX counterparties needs its own session; a full mesh of *n* firms needs *n*(*n* − 1)/2 sessions, a hub needs *n* connections.
- OnBehalfOfCompID (115) and DeliverToCompID (128), present since FIX 4.0, let a third party carry messages for others; some networks map sessions instead, and B3 no longer supports tag 128.
- Networks sell routing, translation, onboarding and certification, monitoring, IOI distribution and drop copies, not just circuits.
- NYFIX went from NYFIX, Inc. to NYSE Euronext (2009), Ullink (2014), Itiviti (2018) and Broadridge (2021, €2.143 billion for Itiviti).
- Radianz went from Reuters and Equant (2000) to BT (2005) and TNS (completed 2 February 2026), now inside TNS's Waypoint; IPC's Connexus belongs to SVP funds since January 2024.
- In Brazil, B3 is reached through its RCB network, internet VPN, co-location or RTM, and its FIX 4.4 EntryPoint identifies each access category by CompID prefix and Sender Location.
