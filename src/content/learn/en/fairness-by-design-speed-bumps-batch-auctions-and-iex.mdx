On 29 May 2026 a United States (US) federal appeals court opened its opinion with a number: "The dispute in this case centers on 350 microseconds, about one-third of one-thousandth of a second" ([US Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, opinion in No. 25-13631, 29 May 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)). The 350 microseconds are the delay that the Investors Exchange (IEX) adds to every incoming order with a coil of optical fibre, and the case was the second time in four years that a large market maker had asked a federal court to overturn the US Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) approval of an IEX design; both courts refused ([US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, Citadel Securities LLC v. SEC, No. 20-1424, 29 July 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf); [Eleventh Circuit, 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)).

This last part of the series opened by [the map from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) is about designs that change the rules of a market instead of the speed of the people in it. [High-frequency trading: what it is and the dispute](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) set out the race to pick off stale quotes and the estimates of its cost, and [Colocation, microwaves and the race for latency](/learn/colocation-microwaves-and-the-race-for-latency) the cables and clocks behind it; this part does not repeat them. After reading, you will be able to explain three families of designs (delays that apply to everyone, delays that hold back only the orders that take liquidity, and auctions that batch the orders that arrive together), follow one stale-quote race through each of them, retell how IEX became an exchange and what regulators and courts decided since, compare the delays that other venues tried and sometimes abandoned, read the evidence on each side, and place the Brazilian debate on retail order flow. Every fact links to the document it comes from, with that document's date where it has one; what companies say about their own designs is attributed to them.

## The problem each design answers

In a continuous limit order book the exchange processes messages one at a time, in order of arrival. Eric Budish, Peter Cramton and John Shim, whose argument [the high-frequency trading part](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) summarizes, put the consequence in one line: when public news moves a price, liquidity providers trying to update their quotes and fast traders trying to take them race, and because the book handles messages serially "liquidity-providing HFTs usually lose this race", HFTs being high-frequency traders ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, American Economic Review: Papers and Proceedings, May 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)). The cost, they argue, is passed on to investors as wider spreads and thinner markets, and it does not shrink as markets get faster: they date the race at the scale of 0.01 seconds in 2005, 0.001 seconds by 2008 and 0.0001 seconds by 2014 ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)).

Exchanges and their critics have tried three kinds of answer, and the rest of this part follows them in turn.

| Family | What changes | Who is slowed | Examples in this part |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symmetric delay | Every incoming message waits the same fixed time; the exchange's own view of other markets does not | Everyone equally, including the provider's cancellation | IEX (2016), NYSE American, an exchange of the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) group (2017 to 2019) |
| Asymmetric delay | Only orders that would trade on arrival wait; resting orders and cancellations do not | Liquidity takers | Chicago Stock Exchange's proposal (2017), TSX Alpha (2015), ICE Futures U.S. (2019), Eurex (2019), Cboe EDGA's proposal (2019), the London Metal Exchange's platform |
| Discrete time | Orders are collected for a short interval and matched together at one price | Nobody; arrival order inside the interval stops mattering | Frequent batch auctions (Budish, Cramton and Shim), Taiwan until 2020, Europe's periodic auction books |

Sources for the table: [SEC, Release 34-78101, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf); [SEC, Release 34-80700, 16 May 2017](https://docs.regulations.justia.com/entries/2017-05-22/2017-10304.pdf); [SEC, Release 34-81913, 19 October 2017](https://docs.regulations.justia.com/entries/2017-10-25/2017-23122.pdf); [Ontario Securities Commission, Staff Notice 21-712, 2 February 2018](https://OSC.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/20180202_21-712_sn-alpha-impact.pdf); [Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), 15 May 2019](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7928-19); [Eurex, Passive Liquidity Protection](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/trade/eurex-plp); [SEC, Release 34-88261, 21 February 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-02-27/pdf/2020-03915.pdf); [London Metal Exchange (LME), LMEselect v10 Functional Definition, version 1.5.1](https://www.lme.com/-/media/files/trading/new-initiatives/new-trading-platform/access/documents-september-2026/lmeselect10-functional-definition-v1-5.pdf); [Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf); [Indriawan, Pascual and Shkilko, working paper, 3 March 2021](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/jhasbrou/SternMicroMtg/Old/SternMicroMtg2021/Papers/Effects%20of%20continuous%20trading.pdf); [European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA), 11 June 2019](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma70-156-1035_final_report_call_for_evidence_periodic_auctions.pdf).

## One race, five market designs

A worked example makes the differences concrete. The setting is the one the District of Columbia Circuit borrowed from IEX's brief: "A stock price may be in the process of changing from $10.00 to $10.01 across different exchanges. A high-speed trader might swoop in and buy at $10.00 from an investor that cannot update its quote fast enough, and then sell at $10.01" ([D.C. Circuit, 29 July 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf)). An investor has an offer to sell resting at $10.00; news reaches the fast trader and the investor at almost the same time; the fast trader sends a buy order, the investor a cancellation. The table follows that race through each design; each row cites the document that describes the rule.

| Design | What happens to the two messages | Who wins | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous book, no delay | The exchange processes whichever message arrives first; the fast trader usually arrives first | The fast trader buys at $10.00 | [Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf) |
| IEX, an ordinary displayed limit order (2016 rules) | Both messages enter at IEX's point of presence (POP) and both traverse the 350-microsecond coil, so "the race simply takes place at the POP" | Unchanged: the fast trader usually wins | [SEC, Release 34-78101, footnote 212](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf) |
| IEX, a pegged or D-Limit order | IEX reads other exchanges' prices without delay; when its signal predicts the move, it reprices the resting sell order a penny higher while the buy order is still in the coil | The buy order arrives to find no offer at $10.00 | [D.C. Circuit, 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf) |
| Asymmetric delay (Eurex, ICE, LME) | The buy order, which would trade on arrival, is held for a few milliseconds; the cancellation is processed at once | The investor's cancellation | [ICE Futures U.S., Submission 19-119, 1 February 2019](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule022019iceusdcm001.pdf) |
| Frequent batch auction | Both messages arrive in the same interval; during the interval orders "can be freely submitted, modified, or withdrawn", and they are matched together at its end | Nobody wins on speed; the buy order trades only if someone still offers at its price | [Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf) |

The second row is the subtle one. A delay that applies to everyone protects nobody in particular: the SEC itself pointed out, in a footnote to its approval of IEX, that a displayed order at $10 is in "the same position on IEX as they would be on other markets without intentional access delays", because the fast trader's order and the investor's cancellation both wait in the same coil ([SEC, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). IEX's delay protects only the orders whose price IEX itself moves while the others wait. The economist Eric Budish made the same point in his comment supporting IEX's application: the design "only addresses latency arbitrage in a limited way" ([Budish, comment letter to the SEC, 5 February 2016](https://ericbudish.org/files/iex_comment_letter.pdf)). IEX's later order types, below, extended the protection to displayed orders.

## IEX: from a dark pool to a protected exchange

### Origins

IEX's own history page says its co-founders met at the Royal Bank of Canada in 2009, that the company was founded in 2012 and began work on an alternative trading system (ATS) that "debuts one year later", and that its "origin story is chronicled in Michael Lewis's New York Times Best Seller 'Flash Boys'" in 2014 ([IEX, Company history, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/about)). The SEC's approval order records that the delay was described in IEX's Form ATS, published on its website since it began operating as an ATS in October 2013 ([SEC, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). In 2014, IEX says, it created the formula it calls the Signal and the Discretionary Peg (D-Peg) order type built on it ([IEX, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/about)).

### The coil and the point of presence

On 21 August 2015 IEX applied to the SEC on Form 1 to register as a national securities exchange; it amended the application five times, and the Commission received 474 comments ([SEC, Release 34-78101, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). The order describes the design in physical terms. Users connect through a point of presence (POP) in Secaucus, New Jersey; every message then "must physically traverse the IEX 'coil,' which is a box of compactly coiled optical fiber cable equivalent to a prescribed physical distance of 61,625 meters (approximately 38 miles)", and continues to the trading system in Weehawken, New Jersey; together, coil and distance equate to 350 microseconds of latency ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). Outbound confirmations and IEX's proprietary data feed come back through the same coil ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)).

Two flows skip the coil, and they are the point of the design: market data from other trading centres and from the consolidated feeds comes into IEX without delay, and IEX's quotes and trades go out to the securities information processors without delay ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). (How consolidated and direct feeds differ, and why the gap between them matters, is the subject of [Market data: consolidated feeds and direct feeds](/learn/market-data-consolidated-feeds-and-direct-feeds).) The SEC summarized the purpose as "an intentional buffer that slows down incoming orders to allow IEX's matching engine to update the prices of resting 'pegged' orders when away prices change", and quoted IEX's own aim: to "ensure that no market participants can take action on IEX in reaction to changes in market prices before IEX is aware of the same price changes on behalf of all IEX members" ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). The SEC offered an analogy: the coil is equivalent to placing the matching engine a fixed distance from its nearest user, or not letting any user locate closer than that ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)).

The coil has since grown. IEX's technology page says the speed bump is now "44 miles* of coiled optical fiber", and explains in the footnote that it was historically 38 miles and was lengthened after IEX relocated its primary data centre in 2024, "to maintain the same 350-microsecond delay" ([IEX, Technology, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/technology)). The court opinions of 2022 and 2026 still describe the 38-mile coil of the approval records ([D.C. Circuit, 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf); [Eleventh Circuit, 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)).

### The objections

The exchanges and trading firms that opposed the application, among them the NYSE, Nasdaq, BATS, Citadel Securities, the Futures Industry Association (FIA) and Hudson River Trading, raised three main objections, as the SEC's order records them ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)):

- **Protected quotes.** Regulation NMS (National Market System) protects only "automated" quotations, and the 2005 adopting release had interpreted "immediate" to preclude "any coding of automated systems or other type of intentional device that would delay the action taken with respect to a quotation"; opponents said a 350-microsecond delay was such a device ([SEC, Release 34-78102, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14876.pdf)). [The rules that rewired markets](/learn/the-rules-that-rewired-markets-reg-ats-reg-nms-mifid-and-brazil) explains the order protection rule itself.
- **Dark orders favoured.** Because only pegged orders benefit, several commenters argued that IEX would attract hidden orders at the expense of displayed ones; the NYSE predicted that IEX's book "would be composed primarily, or entirely, of these dark, pegged orders" ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)).
- **Routing.** As first filed, orders that IEX's own routing broker sent to other markets would not have traversed the coil; in Amendment No. 2 IEX redesigned routing so that routable orders go first to its routing logic, which reaches IEX's book through an equivalent 350-microsecond delay, "to place its outbound routing function on parity with competing broker-dealers" ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)).

### Approval, and a new reading of "immediate"

The SEC approved the application on 17 June 2016, with Chair White and Commissioner Stein voting for it and Commissioner Piwowar "concurring in part and dissenting with respect to Sections III.C.7 and III.C.8", the sections on whether IEX's quotations are protected and whether other market participants must treat them as protected ([SEC, Release 34-78101, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). It found that the inbound delay "is not unfairly discriminatory and does not impose an unnecessary or inappropriate burden on competition" ([SEC, 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14875.pdf)). The same day it issued an interpretation: "immediate" in Regulation NMS now "precludes any coding of automated systems or other type of intentional device that would delay the action taken with respect to a quotation unless such delay is de minimis" ([SEC, Release 34-78102, 17 June 2016](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14876.pdf)).

The proposal had suggested that delays under one millisecond may be de minimis; the final interpretation declined to set the line, finding that "establishing a bright line de minimis threshold is not appropriate at this time", and said every proposed delay would still be reviewed individually, with particular concern for delays "imposed only on certain market participants" ([SEC, Release 34-78102](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14876.pdf)). The Commission's staff separately published guidance the same day: "delays of less than a millisecond are at a de minimis level that would not impair fair and efficient access to a quotation" ([SEC, Division of Trading and Markets, Staff Guidance on Automated Quotations under Regulation NMS, 17 June 2016](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions-2)). The interpretation also promised that "The Staff will also conduct a study within two years regarding the effects of intentional access delays on market quality" ([SEC, Release 34-78102](https://s3.amazonaws.com/public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2016-14876.pdf)); no such staff report was found for this article.

Budish, who supported the application, also argued that the de minimis line opened the door to other designs: frequent batch auctions with intervals under a millisecond, asymmetric delays and random delays ([Budish, comment letter on Release 34-77407, 14 April 2016](https://ericbudish.org/files/comment_letter_proposed_commission_interpretation_one_millisecond_de_minimis.pdf)). The rest of this part shows exchanges trying each of them.

### The Signal and D-Limit

The coil alone buys time; something must decide what to do with it. IEX calls that something the Signal, "also known as the Crumbling Quote Indicator, or CQI": a formula that, reading the sequence of price updates from other exchanges, predicts that the national best bid is about to fall or the national best offer about to rise; when it fires it "is 'on' for 2 milliseconds, or until the price changes" ([IEX, Technology, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/technology)). IEX's D-Peg, a hidden order type, is willing to trade up to the midpoint when the price is stable and, when the Signal is on, is priced one tick (one cent for most stocks) less aggressively than the national best bid or offer ([IEX, 2026](https://www.iex.io/technology)).

On 16 December 2019 IEX asked to apply the same protection to a displayed order, the Discretionary Limit (D-Limit), and the SEC approved it on 26 August 2020 ([SEC, Release 34-89686, 26 August 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-09-01/pdf/2020-19204.pdf)). The numbers in the approval are the core of the case: IEX showed that the indicator was on for 1.64 seconds per symbol per day on average, 0.007% of regular trading hours, yet in that time it received 33.7% of marketable orders, and 24% of the displayed volume on IEX executed while the indicator was on ([SEC, 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-09-01/pdf/2020-19204.pdf)). Citadel Securities objected that the indicator also catches orders it sends for retail investors; it said that "over 50% of our trading activity on IEX is on behalf of retail investors" ([SEC, 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-09-01/pdf/2020-19204.pdf)).

Citadel petitioned for review. On 29 July 2022 the District of Columbia Circuit denied the petition, holding that the 24%-in-0.007% disparity "is substantial evidence that short-term investors engage in latency arbitrage during the same 'small increments of time' that the crumbling quote indicator turns on", and noting that "No one in this case has alleged that latency arbitrage is unlawful"; the issue was only whether the SEC could let IEX innovate ([D.C. Circuit, No. 20-1424, 29 July 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf)). The court accepted the SEC's point that brokers sweeping several exchanges can time their orders to reach IEX at the same moment as the others and so avoid triggering the indicator ([D.C. Circuit, 2022](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2022/07/20-1424-1956972.pdf)).

### IEX Options and the Eleventh Circuit

IEX filed on 10 January 2025 to open an options exchange with the same speed bump and an optional quote type for market makers, the Options Risk Parameter (ORP), which cancels or reprices a quote when the price of the underlying stock signals that the quote is about to go stale ([SEC, Release 34-103998, 18 September 2025](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-18380.pdf); [IEX, The Options Risk Parameter, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/technology/options-risk-parameter)). The SEC approved it on 18 September 2025 ([SEC, Release 34-103998](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-18380.pdf)). The Eleventh Circuit summarized why the SEC found the problem sharper in options: U.S. exchanges list about 135 times more options than equities, market makers account for "more than 99 percent of open orders and quotes", each move in one stock can require repricing hundreds or thousands of options, and options trade only on exchanges, so the SEC called latency arbitrage there "especially acute" ([Eleventh Circuit, 29 May 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)).

Citadel Securities, which the court describes as accounting for "30 percent to be precise" of US consolidated equity options volume, petitioned for review on 17 October 2025 ([Eleventh Circuit, 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)); NYSE American, NYSE Arca and the exchange operator MEMX filed amicus briefs on its side, and the advocacy group Better Markets on the SEC's ([Better Markets, amicus brief, 6 February 2026](https://bettermarkets.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Citadel-v.-SEC-Better-Markets-amicus-brief-as-filed-2-6-26.pdf)). On 29 May 2026 the court denied the petition: IEX's data showed the ORP would affect quotes "significantly less than 0.001% of the trading day" on average, the SEC had expressly considered Citadel's figure of up to 0.2% for some options, and the court would "not second-guess" that technical judgement ([Eleventh Circuit, 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)). The opinion repeats, citing the United Kingdom's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), that investors lose around $5 billion a year to latency arbitrage ([Eleventh Circuit, 2026](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202513631.pdf)), the estimate whose method [the high-frequency trading part](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) sets against the industry's objections.

The options market's data processor told its subscribers on 1 May 2026 that IEX Options "plans to begin trading options on October 2, 2026" ([Options Price Reporting Authority (OPRA), 1 May 2026](https://cdn.opraplan.com/documents/notices/IEX_Options_New_Participant_100226.pdf)); IEX's frequently asked questions, read on 16 November 2026, still describe symbol rollouts "tentatively planned" in six tranches from 2 to 30 October ([IEX, IEX Options Information Hub, read 16 November 2026](https://www.iex.io/options/resources)).

## The others who slowed down

IEX was the first US exchange with a deliberate delay, not the last. The venues below each published the rule they adopted or proposed, and several later published what happened.

### NYSE American: a copy, and a retreat

In January 2017 NYSE MKT, now NYSE American, proposed an intentional delay "designed to create a competitive trading model to IEX": 350 microseconds added by software rather than by fibre, on inbound and outbound order messages; the SEC approved it on 16 May 2017 ([SEC, Release 34-80700, 16 May 2017](https://docs.regulations.justia.com/entries/2017-05-22/2017-10304.pdf)). On 4 November 2019 the exchange filed to remove it. "We have not had any issuers interested in listing because of the Delay Mechanism", the filing says, and "market participants have not increased their trading volume on the Exchange as a result of adding the Delay Mechanism" ([NYSE American, Release 34-87550, 15 November 2019](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-11-21/pdf/2019-25207.pdf)). It published its own comparison of exchange-listed securities in the first half of 2017 against July 2017 to September 2019:

| Measure on NYSE American | Before the delay | With the delay |
|---|---|---|
| Average quoted spread (basis points) | 208.2 | 292.4 |
| Average quoted shares at the best bid and offer | 2,762 | 1,197 |
| Average quoted notional at the best bid and offer | $13,342 | $9,549 |
| Share of the trading day quoting at the national best bid or offer | 71.3% | 68.4% |
| Market share | 12.1% | 11.5% |

Source: [NYSE American, Release 34-87550, November 2019](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-11-21/pdf/2019-25207.pdf). The comparison is the exchange's own, before and after a change that coincided with its move from a floor-based model to a fully automated platform in 2017, which the same filing describes; it is not a controlled study ([NYSE American, 2019](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-11-21/pdf/2019-25207.pdf)).

### The Chicago Stock Exchange's LEAD: approved, stayed, withdrawn

On 10 February 2017 the Chicago Stock Exchange (CHX) proposed the Liquidity Enhancing Access Delay (LEAD): 350 microseconds on all new orders, cancels and cancel/replace messages, except liquidity-providing orders and cancels from a new class of market makers with heightened quoting obligations ([SEC, Release 34-81913, 19 October 2017](https://docs.regulations.justia.com/entries/2017-10-25/2017-23122.pdf)). The SEC's staff approved it under delegated authority on 19 October 2017; on 24 October the Commission stayed that approval for review; CHX withdrew the proposal on 25 July 2018, and on 2 October 2018 the Commission set the staff's order aside ([SEC, Release 34-84337, 2 October 2018](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2018-10-09/pdf/2018-21761.pdf)).

### Cboe EDGA's four milliseconds: disapproved

In June 2019 Cboe EDGA proposed the Liquidity Provider Protection (LP2) delay: up to four milliseconds on every incoming order that would remove liquidity, with no delay on market data, and with EDGA's quotes downgraded to "manual", unprotected ones ([SEC, Release 34-88261, 21 February 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-02-27/pdf/2020-03915.pdf)). The exchange justified the length by the journey from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange's data centre to its own in New Jersey: about 7.75 milliseconds over fibre and 4.005 milliseconds over microwave, so that four milliseconds would cancel the microwave users' head start ([SEC, 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-02-27/pdf/2020-03915.pdf)). On 21 February 2020 the SEC disapproved it: "the Commission does not believe that the Exchange has supported its assertions and demonstrated that the LP2 delay mechanism is appropriately tailored to address latency arbitrage and not permit unfair discrimination"; the exchange had not shown why the delay should apply to all its securities rather than those linked to futures traded in Chicago, nor "the extent to which latency arbitrage is a problem on its market" ([SEC, 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-02-27/pdf/2020-03915.pdf)).

### Canada: TSX Alpha's random delay, reviewed by its regulator

In September 2015 TSX Alpha Exchange introduced "a randomized 1-3 millisecond order processing delay applicable to all orders except those marked as 'post only'", which cannot remove liquidity and must meet a minimum size ([Ontario Securities Commission (OSC), Staff Notice 21-712, 2 February 2018](https://OSC.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/20180202_21-712_sn-alpha-impact.pdf)). The OSC approved it in April 2015 on two conditions: Alpha's displayed orders would not be protected under Canada's order protection rule, and Alpha would supply data on the impact ([OSC, 2018](https://OSC.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/20180202_21-712_sn-alpha-impact.pdf)). One of the commenters' most common concerns was "quote fade": post-only providers could cancel without delay when quotes changed elsewhere, so delayed takers would miss fills ([OSC, 2018](https://OSC.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/20180202_21-712_sn-alpha-impact.pdf)).

The OSC's staff review of 2018 is the closest thing to a regulator's verdict on an asymmetric delay. Its own statistics showed spreads and other market quality measures "did not materially change for Alpha or other Canadian marketplaces"; two academic studies disagreed, Chen, Foley, Goldstein and Ruf finding wider spreads, less market-wide liquidity and significant quote fade, Anderson, Andrews, Devani, Mueller and Walton finding no evidence of harm to market-wide liquidity or trading costs; dealers reported lower fill rates for large orders split across venues and better results for small ones; and the staff concluded that "we are unable to conclude that the Alpha Amendments have negatively impacted market quality such that regulatory changes need to be made", while calling the debate "polarizing" ([OSC, Staff Notice 21-712](https://OSC.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/20180202_21-712_sn-alpha-impact.pdf)). In March 2023 Alpha proposed replacing the random delay with a static one, 1 millisecond on Alpha and 10 milliseconds on two new order books ([Alpha Exchange, Notice of Proposed Amendments, 2 March 2023](https://tsx.com/en/resource/2974/proposed-amendments-re-alpha-x-alpha-drk-and-order-processing-delay-2023-03-02-en.pdf)); TMX's page now describes "a static 1-millisecond order processing delay" for orders without a post-only instruction ([TMX, TSX Alpha order processing delay, read 16 November 2026](https://tsx.com/trading/tsx-alpha-exchange/order-types-and-features/order-processing-delay)).

### Futures: ICE's gold and silver, and a dissent

The first US designated contract market to certify a speed bump was ICE Futures U.S., on 1 February 2019: its Passive Order Protection holds an incoming order that would trade against a resting one for 3 milliseconds, initially in its Gold Daily and Silver Daily futures, while resting orders can still be modified ([ICE Futures U.S., Submission 19-119, 1 February 2019](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule022019iceusdcm001.pdf)). The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's (CFTC) Division of Market Oversight stayed it for a 90-day review, at the end of which, on 14 May 2019, it took effect; the Division noted that "currently, there is limited data and empirical analysis on the impact of speed bumps generally", that these markets had "little or no order book trading", and that any other product or a change of delay would need a new filing ([CFTC, Release 7928-19, 15 May 2019](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7928-19)). Commissioner Dan Berkovitz disagreed: "Asymmetric speed bumps, such as POP, that purposely disadvantage particular trading entities, strategies, or technologies, are discriminatory, anti-competitive, and facially inconsistent with the fundamental objectives of the Commodity Exchange Act" ([CFTC, Statement of Commissioner Dan M. Berkovitz, 15 May 2019](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/SpeechesTestimony/berkovitzstatement051519)).

### Eurex: Passive Liquidity Protection

Eurex, the derivatives exchange whose history [The screens arrive](/learn/the-screens-arrive-electronic-exchanges-from-nasdaq-to-globex) tells, took the asymmetric delay furthest among the venues in this part. Under Passive Liquidity Protection (PLP), "all aggressive orders, (orders that are executable upon arrival at the matching engine), will be delayed by a product segment-specific deferral time", while passive orders enter the book at once ([Eurex, Passive Liquidity Protection initiative page](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/support/initiatives/archive/passive-liquidity-protection)). It started with all foreign exchange (FX) products on 27 May 2019 and German (1 millisecond) and French (3 milliseconds) equity options on 3 June 2019 ([Eurex, initiative page](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/support/initiatives/archive/passive-liquidity-protection)); after a six-month pilot it extended PLP from 24 August 2020 to the remaining equity options (3 milliseconds) and the DAX index option (1.5 milliseconds) ([Eurex, Circular 032/20](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/circulars/Passive-Liquidity-Protection-Further-rollout-to-remaining-equity-options-and-DAX-index-option-1997394)), to the FTSE 100 index option (3 milliseconds) from 31 May 2021 ([Eurex, Circular 045/21](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/circulars/circular-2653444)) and, since October 2022, to the EURO STOXX 50 index option family ([Eurex, PLP page, read 16 November 2026](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/trade/eurex-plp)).

Eurex's case was explicitly about who quotes. In June 2019 its executive board member Randolf Roth wrote that "in some market segments, the speed race has reached its limit because it no longer increases liquidity or the efficiency of pricing" ([Eurex, Could slow be the better speed?, 28 June 2019](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/news-center/news/Could-slow-be-the-better-speed--1576216)); after the pilot he said the results were <Claim status="attributed" by="by Eurex">"very encouraging"</Claim>, with more liquidity providers at the best bid and offer, and added: "What we have not yet seen is that, as a result, the buy side changes their behavior and moves more activity in the order book" ([Eurex, interview, 13 May 2020](https://www.eurex.com/ec-en/find/news/Strengthening-liquidity-successful-pilot-phase-for-Eurex-s-Passive-Liquidity-Protection--1963602)). He also recorded the opposition: "some large tech-savvy clients" feared both the extension to other products and other exchanges following ([Eurex, 2020](https://www.eurex.com/ec-en/find/news/Strengthening-liquidity-successful-pilot-phase-for-Eurex-s-Passive-Liquidity-Protection--1963602)). Optiver, a market maker, warned in 2020 that "Liquidity takers may no longer be willing to do certain trades if the proposed delay of 1.5 milliseconds on aggressing orders in DAX options leads to a loss of control over orders" ([The TRADE, 21 August 2020](https://www.thetradenews.com/eurexs-incoming-options-speed-bump-could-disadvantage-liquidity-takers-hft-firm-warns/)); by October 2022 it wrote that <Claim status="attributed" by="by Optiver, a liquidity provider">"competition at the top of the order book has increased and liquidity has improved"</Claim> ([Optiver, 11 October 2022](https://optiver.com/insights/the-verdict-is-in-for-eurexs-passive-liquidity-protection/)).

### The London Metal Exchange and foreign exchange

The LME's specification for its new trading platform includes the same mechanism as a configurable feature: "The LME can specify a predefined delay to incoming orders submitted by traders", set in milliseconds per market segment, applied only to orders that would execute on arrival; "Passive orders that rest in the order book, order cancellations, stops and mass quotes are exempt" ([LME, LMEselect v10 Functional Definition, version 1.5.1, 2026](https://www.lme.com/-/media/files/trading/new-initiatives/new-trading-platform/access/documents-september-2026/lmeselect10-functional-definition-v1-5.pdf)). The document says that "In specific markets" orders can be subject to it, without listing which; no LME notice naming the markets or the delay was found for this article.

Foreign exchange got there first. A Bank for International Settlements (BIS) report records that in 2013 several large dealer banks supported the launch of the ParFX platform, "which applied randomised pauses (measured in milliseconds), known as latency floors", and that other electronic platforms, including the primary venues, then followed with their own delays and randomization ([BIS Markets Committee, Monitoring of fast-paced electronic markets, September 2018](https://www.bis.org/publications/monitoring-fast-paced-electronic-markets.pdf)). [Interdealer brokers and electronic fixed income and FX](/learn/interdealer-brokers-and-electronic-fixed-income-and-fx) covers those platforms.

## Frequent batch auctions: changing the clock instead of the cable

### The design

Budish, Cramton and Shim proposed a different cure: "uniform-price sealed-bid double auctions conducted at frequent but discrete time intervals", which transform the nature of competition "from competition on speed to competition on price" ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, American Economic Review: Papers and Proceedings, May 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)). [The high-frequency trading part](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) gives the theory and the measurements behind it; their implementation paper of 2014 describes the machinery, in three stages ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)):

1. **Order submission.** Orders look like today's limit orders (direction, price, quantity), can be freely submitted, modified or withdrawn during the interval, carry over to the next auction if not filled, and are not displayed: "This is important to prevent gaming, and is why we describe the auction as 'sealed bid.'"
2. **Auction.** At the end of the interval the exchange adds up demand and supply. If they do not cross, nothing trades. If they cross, everything trades at one price; orders priced exactly at the clearing price are rationed "pro-rata with time priority across batch intervals but not within batch intervals", so resting orders keep their reward for patience while arriving a microsecond earlier inside the interval earns nothing.
3. **Reporting.** The price, the quantity and the aggregate demand and supply curves are published; each trader learns privately what happened to its own orders.

How long should the interval be? The 2014 paper speaks of slowing markets "to the blink of an eye", roughly half a second, while stressing that even much shorter intervals cut the value of tiny speed advantages by orders of magnitude ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)); Budish later told the European regulator that "the interval could be 1 millisecond, or potentially sub-millisecond, or potentially a bit slower" ([Budish, response to ESMA, 11 January 2019](https://ericbudish.org/files/response_esmas_call_evidence_periodic_auctions.pdf)). For fragmented US markets the paper proposes sweeping in better-priced quotes from continuous exchanges when the auction would otherwise clear through the national best bid or offer ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2014](https://ericbudish.org/files/implementation_details_frequent_batch_auctions.pdf)).

### A worked auction

The numbers below are illustrative; the rules are those of the 2014 paper. In one interval the book holds these orders for a stock (E1 is left over from an earlier interval; the rest arrived in this one):

| Side | Order | Shares | Limit price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy | A | 300 | $10.02 |
| Buy | B | 200 | $10.01 |
| Buy | C | 400 | $10.00 |
| Sell | D | 250 | $9.99 |
| Sell | E1 (earlier interval) | 100 | $10.01 |
| Sell | E2 | 200 | $10.01 |
| Sell | E3 | 100 | $10.01 |
| Sell | F | 200 | $10.02 |

At each candidate price, demand is the sum of buy orders willing to pay at least that price and supply the sum of sell orders willing to accept it; the tradable quantity is the smaller of the two.

| Price | Demand | Supply | Tradable |
|---|---|---|---|
| $9.99 | 900 | 250 | 250 |
| $10.00 | 900 | 250 | 250 |
| $10.01 | 500 | 650 | 500 |
| $10.02 | 300 | 850 | 300 |

The market clears at $10.01 with 500 shares. Buyers A and B are filled in full, both at $10.01 (A, willing to pay $10.02, pays less). Seller D is filled in full at $10.01 although it asked only $9.99. The remaining 250 shares come from the sellers at exactly $10.01, who offer 400 between them: E1, from an earlier interval, is filled first (100), and the last 150 are split pro rata between E2 and E3, which arrived in the same interval, 100 to E2 and 50 to E3, whichever of them arrived first. C, bidding $10.00, does not trade and carries over. Run the race from the previous section through this auction and the fast trader's microseconds buy nothing: the investor's cancellation and the trader's order are in the same batch, and only price decides.

The tie-break is where designs differ. The call auctions of B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão), Brazil's exchange, also pick the price that maximizes traded quantity first, then the one with the smallest imbalance, then the one closest to the last trade; but in B3's rules "Não há rateio para ofertas ao mesmo preço", there is no pro-rata at the same price, and limit orders keep chronological priority ([B3, Manual de Procedimentos Operacionais de Negociação, 1 July 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/CF/D0/64/95/753D0A105BF9020AAC094EA8/MPO%20de%20Negociacao%20da%20B3.pdf)). Under that rule E2 would receive all 150 shares if it reached the exchange first: in a time-priority auction, arriving early still pays. Cboe's European periodic auctions use a similar four-step price rule (maximum executable volume, minimum surplus, market pressure, reference price) and let brokers opt into priority for their own clients' orders ([Cboe Europe, Periodic Auctions Book, product sheet, 2020](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/participant_resources/BATSEuro_PeriodicAuctions.pdf)).

### Where batch auctions have run

**Taiwan.** Until March 2020 the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TWSE) matched most trading in call auctions held every five seconds; it launched continuous trading on 23 March 2020, a move that one study describes as the opposite of the one the batch-auction literature recommends, and a rare one ([Indriawan, Pascual and Shkilko, On the Effects of Continuous Trading, working paper, 3 March 2021](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/jhasbrou/SternMicroMtg/Old/SternMicroMtg2021/Papers/Effects%20of%20continuous%20trading.pdf)). The same event produced opposite readings, and the switch also coincided with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, which both analyses try to control for with a comparison market:

| Question | Indriawan, Pascual and Shkilko (2021) | Citadel Securities, in the World Federation of Exchanges (WFE) Focus (2022) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Difference in differences against stocks on the Korea Exchange | Before and after, normalized against the Hong Kong exchange |
| Trading costs | "continuous trading is associated with significantly greater adverse selection, a sizeable reduction in displayed liquidity, and an increase in trading costs" | Spreads about 8% narrower in the most liquid names |
| Liquidity | Less displayed liquidity | Depth in the top five levels roughly doubled, from about 600,000 to about 1.3 million shares |
| Volume | Uninformed volume falls, toxic volume rises, total volume up | Volume up 15% beyond the pandemic-driven rise seen in Hong Kong |
| Conclusion | Continuous trading reduced gains from trade for some investors | "continuous trading results in better liquidity provision, lower bid-ask spreads, more stable prices and enhanced price discovery, as well as higher trading volumes" |
| Source | [Working paper, March 2021](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/jhasbrou/SternMicroMtg/Old/SternMicroMtg2021/Papers/Effects%20of%20continuous%20trading.pdf) | [Matthew Steinert, Citadel Securities, WFE Focus, April 2022](https://focus.world-exchanges.org/articles/citadel-trading-auctions) |

The study is a working paper and the article is written by a market maker whose business is continuous trading; the WFE notes that its Focus articles do not necessarily reflect its own views ([WFE Focus, April 2022](https://focus.world-exchanges.org/articles/citadel-trading-auctions)). Neither reading is a regulator's.

**Europe.** Under the second Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID II), European venues launched intraday periodic auction books, which the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) called frequent batch auctions (FBAs): auctions lasting "only some milliseconds" and triggered by incoming orders rather than scheduled ([ESMA, Final Report, Call for Evidence on Periodic Auctions, 11 June 2019](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma70-156-1035_final_report_call_for_evidence_periodic_auctions.pdf)). ESMA's concern was not speed but transparency: their share jumped from about 0.5% of on-venue equity trading in January 2018 to 2.4% in August 2018, after the first suspensions of dark trading under the double volume cap, before settling around 1.7%, and ESMA examined whether they were being used to circumvent the cap ([ESMA, 2019](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma70-156-1035_final_report_call_for_evidence_periodic_auctions.pdf)). Budish's response distinguished these books from his proposal: he agreed that they last milliseconds but said that his auctions "would ideally be run at frequent, scheduled intervals" as a replacement for the continuous book, not as a side venue ([Budish, response to ESMA, 11 January 2019](https://ericbudish.org/files/response_esmas_call_evidence_periodic_auctions.pdf)). Cboe, which runs one such book, describes it as <Claim status="attributed" by="by Cboe Europe">"4,000x slower than continuous books, prioritizing size over speed"</Claim>, with randomized durations ([Cboe Europe, Periodic Auctions Book, read 16 November 2026](https://www.cboe.com/europe/equities/trading/periodic_auctions_book/)); its maximum duration is 100 milliseconds, and from 21 September 2026 it is trialling an undisclosed minimum duration for Danish, Finnish and Swedish stocks ([Cboe Europe, release note, 2026](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/release_notes/2026/Cboe-Europe-Equities-Announces-PAB-Minimum-Auction-Duration.pdf)).

### Why exchanges have not switched

If batch auctions are better, why does nearly every exchange still run a continuous book? Budish, Robin Lee and Shim's answer, published in the Journal of Political Economy in 2024, is about incentives: "exchange trading fees are competitive, but exchanges earn economic rents from selling speed", and those rents "create a wedge between private and social incentives to innovate" ([Budish, Lee and Shim, A Theory of Stock Exchange Competition and Innovation, Journal of Political Economy, April 2024](https://ericbudish.org/files/A-Theory-of-Stock-Exchange-Competition-and-Innovation.pdf)). Other theory treats the two cures as substitutes: Markus Baldauf and Joshua Mollner find that the trade-off between liquidity and information production can be restored by "delaying all orders except cancellations or implementing frequent batch auctions" ([Baldauf and Mollner, Journal of Finance, 2020, abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:3:p:1495-1526)). The designers have since gone further: in 2023 Budish, Cramton, Jeongmin Lee, Albert Kyle and David Malec proposed "flow trading", in which traders submit demand curves for portfolios as flows of shares per second and "Batch auctions clear all asset markets jointly in discrete time" ([Budish, Cramton, Lee, Kyle and Malec, Flow Trading, 24 March 2023](https://ericbudish.org/files/Flow-Trading.pdf)).

## Brazil: auctions, the RLP and the internalization debate

B3 runs a continuous book, and no B3 or Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM) document proposing a speed bump or frequent batch auctions for continuous trading was found for this article. Batch matching is not foreign to it, though. B3 forms its opening price in a pre-opening phase under auction rules, closes with a closing call that uses the same fixing rules, and holds call auctions (leilões) when an order breaches its price tunnels, with the fixing rules described above, a free-cancellation period in the first seconds and automatic extensions when the theoretical price or quantity changes ([B3, Manual de Procedimentos Operacionais de Negociação, 1 July 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/CF/D0/64/95/753D0A105BF9020AAC094EA8/MPO%20de%20Negociacao%20da%20B3.pdf)). [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access) covers the trading platform.

Brazil's own fairness-by-design debate is about who may trade against retail orders. In 2019 B3 created the Retail Liquidity Provider (RLP) offer: an order inside its trading system, pegged to the best bid or offer, that can be hit only by orders of the same broker's clients flagged as retail; when the spread is two ticks or more it must improve the price by at least one tick; and it has priority over all other brokers' orders at the same price but not over the same broker's other clients ([B3, Ofício Circular 019/2019-VOP, 10 June 2019](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/91/32/1A/FF/B534B610F94E03B6AC094EA8/OC%20019-2019-VOP%20%20RLP-V9%20-%2020190503%20limpo_REVISADO%20%28003%29_ANEXO_FORMATADO_R.pdf)). The CVM's board approved the rules on 21 and 28 May 2019, for an experimental period on the mini Ibovespa and mini dollar futures ([B3, 2019](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/91/32/1A/FF/B534B610F94E03B6AC094EA8/OC%20019-2019-VOP%20%20RLP-V9%20-%2020190503%20limpo_REVISADO%20%28003%29_ANEXO_FORMATADO_R.pdf)).

The high-frequency traders were at the centre of the argument from the start. A respondent to B3's consultation warned that brokers serving both retail and high-frequency clients could not use the RLP, because the high-frequency clients' orders, always at the top of the book, would take priority; B3 asked to let institutional clients waive that priority; the CVM's market supervision staff opposed it, seeing no benefit to the client and an evident one to the broker; and the board approved the waiver unanimously for the experimental year, relying on a flag that lets the CVM, B3, BSM Supervisão de Mercados and the broker monitor it ([CVM, decision of 28 May 2019](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/decisoes/2019/20190528_R1/20190528_D1410.html); [CVM, Memorando 20/2019-CVM/SMI, 23 May 2019](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/cvm_institucional/export/sites/cvm/decisoes/anexos/2019/20190528/1410.pdf)). After twelve months the staff found spreads unchanged ("não afetou o baixo nível de spread", it did not affect the low spreads), more investors and trades, no expressive gains or losses for investors and "um excelente resultado para os intermediários", an excellent result for brokers; RLP use was capped at 15% of the volume of the contracts ([CVM, Memorando 25/2020-CVM/SMI, 29 July 2020](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/export/sites/cvm/decisoes/anexos/2020/20200804/141020.pdf)).

The experiment kept being extended. In January 2023 the CVM's staff said the RLP was a form of internalization monitored as "um experimento controlado", a controlled experiment, and would continue until a board decision ([CVM, 31 January 2023](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2023/prorrogado-prazo-do-programa-experimental-relacionado-a-ofertas-rlp)); on 13 August 2024 the board approved the RLP permanently for the mini contracts and for another 18 experimental months in 20 stocks, with new rules from 3 November 2025 that require price improvement whenever the spread is open ([B3, Ofício Circular 038/2025-VNC, 30 September 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/17/B4/88/14/AEA99910F5D34899AC094EA8/OC%20038-2025-VNC%20NOVAS%20REGRAS%20DE%20FUNCIONAMENTO%20DE%20OFERTA%20RLP%20NO%20MERCADO%20DE%20ACOES%20E%20PARA%20OS%20MINICONTRATOS%20FUTUROS%20WIN%20E%20WDO_PT.pdf)). The CVM's economic analysis unit then studied internalization as a whole. Its final report, of February 2026, recommends keeping internalization inside exchange procedures such as the RLP, but removing the limits on which stocks (any cash-market stock, odd lots included) and how much retail volume may use it, evaluating the result within five years, and standardizing brokers' disclosures on best execution and conflicts of interest; its authors note that the opinions are theirs and not necessarily the CVM's ([CVM, Assessoria de Análise Econômica, Gestão de Riscos e Integridade (ASA), Internalização de Ordens, February 2026](https://www.gov.br/cvm/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/estudos/internalizacao-de-ordens_air_2026-02-11_asa.pdf/@@download/file)). The trade press reported that the current caps are 30% of retail volume in the 20 stocks and 15% in the mini dollar contract ([Capital Aberto, 11 February 2026](https://www.capitalaberto.com.br/economia-e-financas/2026/02/area-tecnica-da-cvm-recomenda-flexibilizacao-do-modelo-atual-de-internalizacao-de-ordens)).

The RLP is not a speed bump, but it answers the same question with a different tool: whose orders should meet whose, and on what terms. IEX and Eurex slow down the fast takers to protect resting liquidity; the RLP lets a broker's liquidity provider step in front of everyone else's resting orders for its own retail clients, in exchange for a better price.

## What to remember

- A continuous book processes messages one at a time, so news creates races; the designs in this part change the rules of the race instead of the racers.
- IEX delays every message by 350 microseconds while reading other markets without delay, which lets it reprice its own pegged and D-Limit orders before fast orders arrive; the SEC approved it in 2016 with a new reading of "immediate", and two federal appeals courts upheld its later designs in 2022 and 2026.
- A delay that applies to everyone does not protect an ordinary displayed order: the race simply moves to the entrance.
- Asymmetric delays hold back only liquidity-taking orders; Eurex, ICE Futures U.S. and TSX Alpha run them, the SEC disapproved one in 2020, and regulators and market makers disagree about whether they are fair.
- Frequent batch auctions collect orders for a short interval and clear them at one price, with pro-rata ties inside the interval; Taiwan ran five-second auctions until 2020, and the evidence from its switch is read both ways.
- In Brazil the debate is about retail flow: B3's RLP lets brokers' liquidity providers trade with their own retail clients, the CVM has extended it since 2019, and its economists recommended in 2026 lifting its limits.

<ReaderQuestions>

- IEX's delay protects orders whose price the exchange itself moves; is an exchange that is faster than its users a neutral referee or a participant with an advantage?
- If asymmetric delays help those who post liquidity and hurt those who take it, which group are the end investors in each market, and does the answer change between equity options and cash equities?
- Frequent batch auctions remove the reward for arriving a microsecond earlier only if ties are split pro rata; why might exchanges that already run call auctions, B3 among them, keep time priority?
- NYSE American's delay attracted no new order flow while IEX's share grew; what besides the delay itself could explain the difference?
- Brazil's RLP and America's speed bumps both decide whose orders meet whose: should such choices be made by each exchange and approved case by case, or set once by the regulator for all?

</ReaderQuestions>
