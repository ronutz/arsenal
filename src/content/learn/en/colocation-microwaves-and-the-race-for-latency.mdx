The race for speed described in [the previous part](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute) is fought with ordinary network engineering pushed to its physical limits: racks rented a few metres from an exchange's matching engine, cables of equal length so that no customer is closer than another, fibre routes dug through mountains, microwave towers that beat glass because radio travels faster through air, clocks kept within millionths of a second of Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), and taps that copy every packet so that each microsecond can be accounted for. This part of the series opened by [the map from the pit to the microsecond](/learn/electronic-trading-from-the-pit-to-the-microsecond) explains that infrastructure. After reading, you can explain why distance is time, describe the colocation offers of the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE), CME Group and B3 (Brasil, Bolsa, Balcão), retell the race between Chicago and New Jersey from the measurements, state the European and American clock rules, say how latency is measured on the wire, place Corvil and Pico, and explain field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) and kernel bypass in plain terms.

## Why distance is time

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) gives planning values for one-way delay of 5 microseconds per kilometre for a digital optical fibre system and 4 microseconds per kilometre for terrestrial coaxial cable or radio-relay ([ITU-T Recommendation G.114, May 2003](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.114-200305-I!!PDF-E&type=items)). Gregory Laughlin, Anthony Aguirre and Joseph Grundfest put the physics plainly: electromagnetic signals cross air, including microwaves, "a factor of 1/n faster than in fiber of refractive index n", and microwave paths can also run straighter than fibre, across water and terrain where cable is hard to lay ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, working paper, 21 November 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)). Between the Globex matching engine of the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) in Aurora, Illinois, and Nasdaq's matching engine in Carteret, New Jersey, 1,179 kilometres apart, light sets "a hard tmin = 3.93 ms minimum information propagation time" ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)).

The arithmetic follows from those numbers. Spread Networks' fibre route of 825 miles, about 1,328 kilometres, at the ITU's 5 microseconds per kilometre gives about 6.64 milliseconds one way, or 13.28 milliseconds there and back, close to the 13.33 milliseconds round trip the company quoted in 2010 ([Lightwave, 22 June 2010](https://lightwaveonline.com/network-design/article/16671350/spread-networks-to-debut-low-latency-new-york-to-chicago-dark-fiber-route); [ITU-T G.114](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.114-200305-I!!PDF-E&type=items)). Inside a data centre the same rule works at a smaller scale: at 5 microseconds per kilometre, every 200 metres of fibre costs about a microsecond ([ITU-T G.114](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.114-200305-I!!PDF-E&type=items)). A microsecond is what the fastest firms compete for: the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) wrote in 2010 that speed matters "in the relative sense of being faster than competitors, even if only by a microsecond" ([SEC, Release 34-61358, 14 January 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)).

## Colocation and proximity hosting

### What regulators mean by the terms

The SEC's 2010 concept release defined co-location as "a service offered by trading centers that operate their own data centers and by third parties that host the matching engines of trading centers", in which the operator "rents rack space to market participants that enables them to place their servers in close physical proximity to a trading center's matching engine" ([SEC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)). The SEC said colocation offered by registered exchanges is subject to the Securities Exchange Act, so exchanges must file rule changes and win approval before offering it, on terms that "must not be unfairly discriminatory" and with fees "equitably allocated and reasonable"; it then asked whether colocation gives proprietary firms an unfair advantage and whether its fees are "so high that they effectively create a barrier for smaller firms" ([SEC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)).

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) proposed in June 2010 a definition covering both the exchange's own service and nearby third-party hosting: "co-location/proximity hosting services" means "space, power, telecommunications, and other ancillary products and services made available to market participants for the purpose of enabling them to position their computer systems/servers in close proximity" to a market's trade and execution systems ([CFTC, proposed rule, Federal Register, 11 June 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-06-11/pdf/2010-13613.pdf)). The proposal required equal access for all eligible participants and third-party providers, uniform and non-discriminatory fees, and monthly public disclosure of "the longest, shortest, and average latencies for each connectivity option" ([CFTC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-06-11/pdf/2010-13613.pdf)). It noted that "for some trading firms, speed is now measured in microseconds" ([CFTC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-06-11/pdf/2010-13613.pdf)).

In the European Union (EU), the revised Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID II) says high-frequency trading "is facilitated by the co-location of market participants' facilities in close physical proximity to a trading venue's matching engine" and that it is "essential to require trading venues to provide such co-location services on a non-discriminatory, fair and transparent basis" ([Directive 2014/65/EU, recital 62](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065)). Article 48(8) makes that a duty of every regulated market, and the directive's definition of a high-frequency technique names "co-location, proximity hosting or high-speed direct electronic access" as its infrastructure test ([Directive 2014/65/EU, articles 4(1)(40) and 48(8)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065)).

### What exchanges offer

**NYSE, Mahwah.** When the SEC approved NYSE's colocation fees on 21 September 2010, the exchange had represented that it planned to operate a data centre in Mahwah, New Jersey, offering cabinets of 4 or 8 kilowatts; that colocated users get no access "separate from or superior to" that of others, since all orders enter "through the same order gateway"; that the Liquidity Center Network, a local network in the data centre, gives lower latency than its wider Secure Financial Transaction Infrastructure network; and that any difference among cabinet positions "does not create any material difference" ([SEC, Release 34-62960, 21 September 2010, Federal Register 27 September 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-09-27/pdf/2010-24061.pdf)). Today the operating policies of the facility, which they call "the NYSE Markets' data center in Mahwah, New Jersey", run to version 28 of April 2025 ([NYSE Technologies Connectivity, USLC Operating Policies and Procedures, April 2025](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/IGN_Colocation_Mahwah_Operating_Policies.pdf)).

**CME Group, Aurora.** On 26 June 2024 CME Group and Google Cloud announced plans to build a private Google Cloud region and a co-location facility in Aurora, Illinois, near CME's current data centre campus, where clients could choose self-managed infrastructure or Google's infrastructure service, "both with equal network latency to the exchange"; disaster recovery would be in Dallas, clients would get "at least 18 months advance notice" before markets move, and CME's cash markets would stay in their current facilities ([CME Group and Google Cloud, joint release, 26 June 2024](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2024-06-26-CME-Group-and-Google-Cloud-Announce-New-Chicago-Area-Private-Cloud-Region-and-Co-location-Facility-for-CME-Groups-Markets)).

**B3, Santana de Parnaíba.** B3's commercial policy for colocation, version 3.5 of 15 January 2024, describes a data centre in Santana de Parnaíba, inaugurated in 2017 and certified Tier III by the Uptime Institute, with the older XV de Novembro data centre, updated in 2008, as its disaster recovery site ([B3, Política Comercial de Serviços de Co-location, 15 January 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/POLITICA%20COMERCIAL%20COLOCATION%20-%20PORTUGUES.PDF)). The English edition, version 3.6 of 24 June 2024, whose change log adds a cross connect certification product, repeats these facts: the Santana de Parnaíba Data Center "was inaugurated in 2017" and has Tier 3 certification from the Uptime Institute ([B3, Co-location Services Commercial Policy, version 3.6, 24 June 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/Politica-Comercial-Colocation-24-06-24-EN.PDF)). The service links customers to the PUMA Trading System "de forma privilegiada obtendo a menor latência possível", in a privileged way with the lowest possible latency, because the colocation network sits in the same infrastructure as PUMA's order routing components ([B3, 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/POLITICA%20COMERCIAL%20COLOCATION%20-%20PORTUGUES.PDF)). For "isonomia", equal treatment, every cable inside the colocation area "possuem o mesmo comprimento, isto é, são equidistantes", has the same length ([B3, 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/POLITICA%20COMERCIAL%20COLOCATION%20-%20PORTUGUES.PDF)). From 15 January 2026 a half rack of 3 kilowatts for trading costs R$10,594.09 a month and a full high-density rack of 15 kilowatts R$54,931.15 a month on a three-year term, with prices adjusted every year by consumer price inflation measured from July to June ([B3, Tabela de Preços Co-location, 15 January 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/41/13/B1/7E/B1DDB9109B5E99B9AC094EA8/Tabela_de_precos_do_Colocation_REAL.pdf)).

Colocation is not the only route in: Reuters reported in 2021 that banks, brokers and fund managers may instead rent third-party data centres also connected to PUMA, but that orders from B3's own facility had risen to 39% of average daily volume in cash equities and 59% in derivatives in the first quarter of 2021 ([Forbes Brasil with Reuters, 19 April 2021](https://forbes.com.br/forbes-money/2021/04/b3-oferta-dispositivos-mais-potentes-em-meio-a-alta-das-operacoes-de-alta-frequencia/)). [Brazil: BM&F, Bovespa, B3 and electronic access](/learn/brazil-bmf-bovespa-b3-and-electronic-access) covers the market access models around it.

## Chicago to New Jersey: fibre, then microwave

The route is old. On 1 September 1949, Laughlin and his co-authors recall, AT&T added a 34-hop line-of-sight 4 GHz microwave relay between New York and Chicago, an 838-mile route through six states ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)).

**The straight fibre.** Spread Networks presented its dark fibre route in June 2010: 825 fibre miles, "the shortest route possible", with latency quoted at 13.33 milliseconds, equipment partners ADVA Optical Networking, Ciena, Infinera and JDSU, and certification of equipment that adds minimal latency ([Lightwave, 22 June 2010](https://lightwaveonline.com/network-design/article/16671350/spread-networks-to-debut-low-latency-new-york-to-chicago-dark-fiber-route)). The trade press reported in August that the line had gone live; the company said it had obtained permits "to blast rock, dig a new trench and install a completely new fiber optic cable", choosing "the shortest possible path, rather than following the traditional routes that telcos follow along rail lines", and used dispersion-compensating fibre to avoid equipment that adds latency ([WatersTechnology, 16 August 2010](https://www.waterstechnology.com/technology/1728158/spread-networks-lights-chicago-ny-link)). Its chairman was Jim Barksdale, a former chief executive of Netscape ([Converge Digest, 22 June 2010](https://convergedigest.com/spread-networks-delivers-133ms-latency/?amp=1)). Eric Budish, Peter Cramton and John Shim cite an estimated construction cost of $300 million ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, conference draft, 17 September 2013](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)).

**The towers.** Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest measured the race in market data: in April 2010 equity prices in New Jersey responded within 7.25 to 7.95 milliseconds of a price change in Chicago; Spread Networks' line cut that to about 6.65 milliseconds; from about March 2011 the response time fell by about another 2 milliseconds, which they attribute to custom microwave networks and possibly to algorithms that statistically anticipate Chicago prices ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)). From records of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) they documented at least 15 microwave networks licensed between Chicago and the New York area, mostly in the 6 to 11 GHz bands, with estimated latencies of 4.2 to 5.2 milliseconds, most of them "taking far more hops and less direct routes than necessary", and projected that better designs could reach about 4.03 milliseconds, 0.1 millisecond above the light limit ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)). They estimated infrastructure and five-year operating costs of these improvements at more than $500 million ([Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest, 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf)); the paper was published in the Financial Review in 2014 ([EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:finrev:v:49:y:2014:i:2:p:283-312)).

| Path between the Chicago and New Jersey markets | One-way time | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Light in a vacuum, Aurora to Carteret, 1,179 km | 3.93 ms | [Laughlin et al., 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf) |
| Best route before Spread Networks, measured as the equity response, April 2010 | 7.25 to 7.95 ms | [Laughlin et al., 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf) |
| Spread Networks fibre, from August 2010 | about 6.65 ms (13.33 ms quoted round trip) | [Laughlin et al., 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf); [Lightwave, 2010](https://lightwaveonline.com/network-design/article/16671350/spread-networks-to-debut-low-latency-new-york-to-chicago-dark-fiber-route) |
| Microwave networks licensed in 2011 and 2012 | 4.2 to 5.2 ms (estimated) | [Laughlin et al., 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf) |
| Well-designed microwave, projected | about 4.03 ms | [Laughlin et al., 2012](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/SSRN-id2227519.pdf) |

Microwave has a price besides money: Budish, Cramton and Shim note that it shaved round-trip time "from 13ms to 8.5ms" but is "less reliable, especially during adverse weather conditions" ([Budish, Cramton and Shim, 2013](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)). That weakness became a natural experiment: Andriy Shkilko and Konstantin Sokolov studied weather episodes that "temporarily remove the speed advantages of the fastest traders by disrupting their microwave networks" and found the disruptions associated with "lower adverse selection and lower trading costs" ([Shkilko and Sokolov, Journal of Finance, 2020, EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:6:p:2899-2927)). Their reading is that speed differentials in their setting harm liquidity, which supports one side of the dispute described in [the previous part](/learn/high-frequency-trading-what-it-is-and-the-dispute).

**Who builds the links now.** McKay Brothers, a network operator, says that many financial networks "are operated by trading firms" and markets itself against that model, saying it "is not a trading firm" and that it builds towers, radios and hollow-core fibre on routes including "Aurora to New Jersey" and between London, Frankfurt and Bergamo ([McKay Brothers, home page, read 15 November 2026](https://www.mckay-brothers.com/)). Those are the company's descriptions of itself.

## Time stamps and clock synchronization

### Why clocks matter

A regulator rebuilding a crash, or a firm proving best execution, has to put events from different machines in one order, and that is only possible if their clocks agree. The European rule is Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574, known as Regulatory Technical Standard (RTS) 25, which applies from 3 January 2018 ([Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574, article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)). Venues and their members must synchronize business clocks with UTC issued by the timing centres listed by the Bureau international des poids et mesures (BIPM), or with UTC disseminated by a satellite system "provided that any offset from UTC is accounted for and removed from the timestamp" ([article 1](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)). They must demonstrate traceability to UTC, "identify the exact point at which a timestamp is applied", and review compliance at least once a year ([article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)).

| Who | Activity | Maximum divergence from UTC | Timestamp granularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trading venue | Gateway-to-gateway latency above 1 millisecond | 1 millisecond | 1 millisecond or better |
| Trading venue | Gateway-to-gateway latency of 1 millisecond or less | 100 microseconds | 1 microsecond or better |
| Member or participant | High-frequency algorithmic trading technique | 100 microseconds | 1 microsecond or better |
| Member or participant | Voice trading, request for quote with human response, negotiated transactions | 1 second | 1 second or better |
| Member or participant | Any other trading activity | 1 millisecond | 1 millisecond or better |

Source: [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574, annex, tables 1 and 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574). The regulation defines gateway-to-gateway latency as the time from a message's arrival at a venue's outer gateway, through the order submission protocol and the matching engine, until an acknowledgement is sent back from the gateway ([article 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)).

In the United States (US) the Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT) plan requires exchanges and the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) to keep business clocks within 100 microseconds of the time maintained by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and broker-dealers within 50 milliseconds, with one second for manual events; the SEC found the 50-millisecond standard "acceptable for the initial phases of reporting" ([participants in the CAT plan, clock synchronization assessment, 15 May 2017](https://acquia.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2020-02/Clock-Synchronization-Assessment-Final-5.15.17.pdf)). The difference is large: Europe asks a high-frequency firm for 100 microseconds, the US plan asks a broker for 500 times that.

### How it is done: precision time and satellites

The tool is the Precision Time Protocol (PTP), defined in IEEE 1588; the current edition, IEEE 1588-2019, approved on 7 November 2019 and published on 16 June 2020, "provides precise synchronization of clocks in packet-based networked systems", supports accuracy and precision "in the sub-microsecond range", and adds that "Sub-nanosecond time transfer accuracy can be achieved in a properly designed network" ([IEEE Standards Association, IEEE 1588-2019](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1588/6825/)). [Time: the dependency nobody lists](/learn/ntp-family-history) tells the story of the Network Time Protocol (NTP), its predecessor for ordinary networks.

Exchanges sell time as a service. In NYSE's Mahwah facility, the technical specifications published by Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) offer NTP, direct PTP over Ethernet and a direct Global Positioning System (GPS) feed; the PTP service relies on a local clock synchronized through the GPS constellation, "uses rubidium holdovers and is verified by the National Institute of Standards and Technology", and lets customers "achieve sub 1µs precision" with a network interface card (NIC) that has an IEEE 1588 hardware clock ([ICE Global Network and Colocation US Technical Specifications, 2026](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/IGN_Colocation_Mahwah_Technical_Specs.pdf)). B3 lets customers install GPS antennas on the roof of its data centre "para recebimento de sinal e distribuição de serviços relacionados à sincronização de relógios", to receive the signal and distribute clock synchronization services, offers racks for providers distributing "sinal GPS, PTP e NTP", and from January 2026 charges R$227.01 a month for a GPS or PTP cross connect between customers ([B3, Política Comercial, 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/POLITICA%20COMERCIAL%20COLOCATION%20-%20PORTUGUES.PDF); [B3, Tabela de Preços, 2026](https://www.b3.com.br/data/files/41/13/B1/7E/B1DDB9109B5E99B9AC094EA8/Tabela_de_precos_do_Colocation_REAL.pdf)).

## How latency is measured on the wire

### One-way delay, and where to put the clock

The Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) defines one-way delay precisely: a packet's delay is dT if the source "sent the first bit of a Type-P packet to Dst at wire time* T" and the destination "received the last bit of that packet at wire time T+dT" ([IETF, RFC 7679, January 2016](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7679.txt)). Measuring it needs two synchronized clocks, which is why PTP and measurement belong together.

Where the timestamp is taken matters as much as the clock. For their study of latency races the Financial Conduct Authority's (FCA) researchers used messages "recorded and timestamped on the external side of this firewall using an optical TAP", a traffic analysis point in the paper's words, which they called the ideal location because "it represents the point at which messages are no longer under the control of market participants" ([Aquilina, Budish and O'Neill, FCA Occasional Paper 50, January 2020](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-50.pdf)). On a server, Linux can ask the network adapter itself to stamp packets: the kernel's timestamping interface offers flags that "Request rx timestamps generated by the network adapter" and the same for transmitted packets ([Linux kernel documentation, Timestamping](https://docs.kernel.org/networking/timestamping.html)). The RTS 25 duty to "identify the exact point at which a timestamp is applied" is the regulatory form of the same engineering question ([Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574, article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)).

### The numbers that people quote

Three measurements recur. Tick-to-trade is the time from a market data update to the order a firm sends in response; FIA MarketVoice, the news service of the Futures Industry Association (FIA), described the analytics of Corvil, a packet analytics company, as letting front-office staff measure "fill ratios and tick-to-trade latencies" and correlate them with network performance ([FIA MarketVoice, 26 July 2019](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/corvil-deal-bring-increased-transparency-data-pico-clients)). Gateway-to-gateway latency is the exchange's own round trip as RTS 25 defines it ([Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574, article 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574)). And the venue's order-to-execution figure is what B3 publishes: about 350 microseconds in 2025, down from 1.2 milliseconds in 2023 after it adopted "um protocolo internacional binário", an international binary protocol, with a target below 300 microseconds by 2026 ([B3, Avanço em tecnologia, 4 April 2025](https://www.b3.com.br/pt_br/noticias/avanco-em-tecnologia.htm)).

An order's life at B3, drawn from B3's own figures:

The firm's own tick-to-trade time sits between the second and third rows, inside its rack, and that is where FPGAs and kernel bypass come in.

## Network-derived analytics: Corvil and Pico

If every microsecond matters, someone has to measure them, and a market grew up for appliances that capture every packet, decode the trading protocols inside and report what happened to each order. Corvil, a Dublin company, was one of them; on 9 July 2019 Pico, a New York provider of technology services for financial markets, announced a definitive agreement to acquire it, with Corvil's chief executive, Donal Byrne, to become chief technology officer and the Corvil name kept for its products, including a new Corvil-as-a-Service offering ([Markets Media, 9 July 2019](https://www.marketsmedia.com/pico-to-acquire-corvil); [FIA MarketVoice, 26 July 2019](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/corvil-deal-bring-increased-transparency-data-pico-clients)). FIA MarketVoice reported on 26 July 2019 that Pico had installed Corvil extensively in its own environment before deciding to buy it, that closing was expected in early August, that terms were not disclosed because both companies were privately owned, and that Pico was minority-owned by a consortium including Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, UBS and Wells Fargo ([FIA MarketVoice, 26 July 2019](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/corvil-deal-bring-increased-transparency-data-pico-clients)). It also reported that Pico then had 35 data centre locations and connected to more than 200 exchanges and venues, B3 among the derivatives exchanges named ([FIA MarketVoice, 2019](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/corvil-deal-bring-increased-transparency-data-pico-clients)).

What Pico is today comes from its own pages and announcements. Golden Gate Capital's release of 13 July 2022, announcing a $200 million investment in Pico, says Pico "was founded in 2009", spans 55 data centres, serves more than 430 clients and acquired Redline Trading Solutions in January 2022, and names Goldman Sachs Asset Management, Wells Fargo Strategic Capital, J.P. Morgan, UBS, Intel Capital and others as existing investors ([Golden Gate Capital, 13 July 2022](https://goldengatecap.com/pico-signs-agreement-with-golden-gate-capital-for-200-million-investment-to-accelerate-next-phase-of-ma-activity/)). Pico announced the Redline acquisition on 4 January 2022, describing Redline as a provider of ultra-low latency software and common interfaces to more than 180 venues ([Pico, 4 January 2022](https://www.pico.net/about/news/pico-acquires-redline-trading-solutions/)). Its site now presents three lines, Corvil Analytics, Redline software and connectivity with colocation and market data "across 900+ exchange and venue products", and is copyrighted to Pico Quantitative Trading LLC ([Pico, home and about pages, read 15 November 2026](https://www.pico.net/about/)).

Those are the vendor's claims, not independent measurements. A later part of this series covers network-derived analytics and monitoring in depth.

## FPGAs and kernel bypass, in plain terms

### Kernel bypass

Normally the operating system stands between the network card and the application. AMD's user guide for Onload, its accelerated network middleware, describes the alternative: a TCP and UDP implementation linked into the application and "granted direct (but safe) access to the network-adapter hardware", so data moves "without involvement of the operating system. This technique is known as 'kernel bypass'" ([AMD, Onload User Guide UG1586 v1.2, 31 July 2023, copy published by Concurrent Real-Time](https://redhawk.concurrent-rt.com/docs/root/1Linux/1RedHawk/9.2/AMD-Onload-User-Guide-1.2.pdf)). The guide says it "avoids disruptive events such as system calls, context switches and interrupts" and lists market-data and trading applications first among its uses ([AMD, 2023](https://redhawk.concurrent-rt.com/docs/root/1Linux/1RedHawk/9.2/AMD-Onload-User-Guide-1.2.pdf)). The open-source Data Plane Development Kit (DPDK) takes a related approach with poll mode drivers "designed to work without asynchronous, interrupt-based signaling mechanisms" ([DPDK, Programmer's Guide, Overview](https://doc.dpdk.org/guides/prog_guide/overview.html)), and the Linux kernel's own AF_XDP sockets, "optimized for high performance packet processing", let a program hand frames directly to memory shared with user space ([Linux kernel documentation, AF_XDP](https://docs.kernel.org/networking/af_xdp.html)).

### FPGAs

An FPGA lets a firm run trading logic in hardware rather than in software on a processor. The FCA researchers listed "putting trading algorithms onto field programmable gate arrays (FPGAs, because hardware is faster than software)" among the sums spent on speed, beside microwave links and colocation ([FCA Occasional Paper 50, 2020](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-50.pdf)). AMD announced on 27 September 2023 an accelerator card for electronic trading that it says delivers "less than 3ns FPGA transceiver latency" and a "7X latency improvement over prior generation FPGA technology", on a Virtex UltraScale+ FPGA with 64 transceivers; its footnote says the measurement excludes protocol overhead, framing and programmable logic latency, and that the comparison is "Based on simulation" ([AMD, 27 September 2023](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1158/amd-unveils-purpose-built-fpga-based-accelerator-for)). The figure describes one component, the transceiver, not a complete tick-to-trade path.

## The dispute about speed infrastructure

The questions the SEC asked in 2010 are still the questions: whether colocation favours firms with "greater resources and sophistication", whether its fees bar smaller firms, and whether banning it would only push the same racks into third-party buildings next door, "outside the Commission's regulatory authority" ([SEC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-01-21/pdf/2010-1045.pdf)). Regulators' answer so far has been to require fair and transparent access rather than to forbid it: equal access and published latencies in the CFTC's 2010 proposal, non-discriminatory colocation under MiFID II, equal cable lengths at B3 and equal network latency in CME's planned cloud facility ([CFTC, 2010](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-06-11/pdf/2010-13613.pdf); [Directive 2014/65/EU, article 48(8)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065); [B3, 2024](https://sistemasweb.b3.com.br/normativos/POLITICA%20COMERCIAL%20COLOCATION%20-%20PORTUGUES.PDF); [CME Group and Google Cloud, 2024](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2024-06-26-CME-Group-and-Google-Cloud-Announce-New-Chicago-Area-Private-Cloud-Region-and-Co-location-Facility-for-CME-Groups-Markets)). Equal access inside the building does not equalize the links between buildings, which is where Spread Networks and the microwave towers competed. Economists who see the race as waste propose changing market design instead, and a later part on fairness by design covers speed bumps and batch auctions.

## What to remember

- Distance is time: about 5 microseconds per kilometre in fibre and 4 by radio in the ITU's planning values; 3.93 milliseconds is the light limit between Aurora and Carteret.
- Colocation is rack space beside a matching engine; proximity hosting is the same nearby, run by others; US, EU and Brazilian rules and practice require fair access, and B3 equalizes cable lengths.
- Spread Networks' straight fibre cut the Chicago to New Jersey trip to 13.33 milliseconds round trip in 2010; microwave networks licensed in 2011 and 2012 cut it towards the light limit, at the cost of rain fade.
- MiFID II's RTS 25 asks high-frequency firms for clocks within 100 microseconds of UTC and microsecond timestamps; the US CAT plan asks exchanges for 100 microseconds and brokers for 50 milliseconds.
- Latency is measured as one-way delay between synchronized clocks, ideally with passive taps and hardware timestamps; B3 publishes 11 and 22 microseconds inside colocation and about 350 microseconds for its trading system.
- Corvil, the Dublin packet analytics firm, has belonged to Pico since 2019; Pico, founded in 2009, also bought Redline in 2022 and took $200 million from Golden Gate Capital.
- Kernel bypass lets an application talk to the network card without the operating system; FPGAs put trading logic into hardware.

<ReaderQuestions>

- If every customer in a colocation hall gets cables of equal length but the fastest firms own private microwave links between cities, is the playing field level, and where should a regulator draw the line?
- The US plan accepts broker clocks 500 times looser than Europe's limit for high-frequency firms: which approach better serves a regulator trying to rebuild a crash?
- When the evidence from storms suggests that markets trade more cheaply while the fastest links are down, what should exchanges and regulators conclude about the value of speed?
- Should exchanges publish their latency distributions, as the CFTC proposed in 2010 and as B3 does in part, and who would use them?
- If a single vendor's appliances record the trading of hundreds of banks and exchanges, what does that mean for trust, independence and resilience in market measurement?

</ReaderQuestions>
